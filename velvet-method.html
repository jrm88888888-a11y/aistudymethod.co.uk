Why It Exists

Most students study hard.
Few study effectively.

The problem is rarely effort. It is strategy: diving into content without knowing what the exam asks, skipping any honest diagnosis of what you do not know, revising passively, and never reviewing how the session went.

AI closes that gap. Personalised diagnostics, adaptive questioning, instant feedback and unlimited unseen material used to require a private tutor. The Velvet Method is the order you do them in.

Step 1
Schema Theory & Learning Objectives

View the Bigger Picture

Before you study anything, use AI to map the whole specification: every topic, how they connect, where the marks sit, and which command words keep coming up.

This gives you the framework that later detail attaches to — and it shows you where the marks actually are, so revision time goes where the paper rewards it, not where you feel most comfortable.

💬 From the Prompt Library
Turn Spec Points into Exam Questions
"Take these specification statements for cell structure and, for each one, write the kinds of exam questions an examiner could realistically ask from it, including the likely command words and mark allocations."
E
Step 2
Formative Assessment & Pretesting

Evaluate Your Starting Point

Before investing hours in revision, get AI to quiz you across every topic at once. It pushes harder where you are solid and digs deeper where you are not.

You end up with a ranked list of weaknesses instead of vague dread. And testing yourself before you study makes the studying that follows more effective — even when you get the answers wrong.

💬 From the Prompt Library
Self-Explanation Check
"I'm going to explain cell structure to you in my own words. Listen, then identify every gap, error, vague statement, or missing link in my explanation, in order of importance."
L
Step 3
Multimodal Learning & the Generation Effect

Learn Creatively

Fill the gaps with AI as a teaching partner, matching the format to the material: a process wants a diagram, a mechanism wants an analogy, vocabulary wants a game, a concept you almost understand wants the Feynman technique.

What matters is that you produce the explanation rather than read someone else's. Information you generate yourself is retained better than information you simply receive — the generation effect.

💬 From the Prompt Library
Build Precise Vocabulary
"Generate the key technical terms for cell structure, each with a precise GCSE definition and a sentence showing correct use. Then test me by giving definitions and asking for the term, and vice versa."
V
Step 4
The Testing Effect & Retrieval Practice

Verify Your Knowledge

Feeling like you understand something is not the same as retrieving it under exam conditions. Get AI to set exam-style questions, mark your answers, and show you the marking points you missed.

Pulling something out of memory strengthens it far more than reading it again — the testing effect, one of the most reliably replicated findings in cognitive psychology.

💬 From the Prompt Library
Predict the Mark Scheme
"Show me a past-paper-style question on cell structure. Before answering, I'll predict what the mark scheme rewards. Then reveal a realistic mark scheme and tell me how accurate my prediction was."
E
Step 5
Higher-Order Thinking & Unseen Material

Explore Application & Analysis

Move from recall to application: unseen data, novel scenarios, cross-topic questions. In the sciences that means data and experimental interpretation; in the humanities, sources, arguments and interpretations.

Top grades come from applying what you know to material you have never seen before. Past papers run out. This does not.

💬 From the Prompt Library
Predict and Justify
"Set me a scenario about cell structure and ask me to predict what would happen, then justify my prediction with biology. Tell me whether my reasoning, not just my prediction, was sound."
T
Step 6
Metacognition & Spaced Retrieval

Transform Through Reflection

Close the loop. Ask AI what you actually got wrong today, what is secure, and which methods worked — then have it build your next fortnight of review.

Spreading review across days beats cramming it into one session, one of the largest and most consistent findings in the learning research. Reflecting on how you learn is what turns one good session into a habit.

💬 From the Prompt Library
Run a Metacognitive Check
"Ask me reflective questions about how I just studied cell structure: what worked, what I avoided, where I lost focus, and whether I tested myself or just re-read. Then suggest one change to my method."

Questions
Is using AI like this cheating?

No. Nothing in the Velvet Method asks AI to write your answers. Every step asks it to question you, mark you, or set you problems — the thinking stays yours. Coursework is different: follow your school's AI policy, which usually requires you to declare any use.

Do I need to pay for ChatGPT?

No. Every prompt on this page works on the free tiers of ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini. Paid tiers allow longer sessions and file uploads, which help when you upload a specification at the View step, but nothing here depends on them.

Which exam boards does it work with?

Any — you name your board and specification inside the prompt, as the examples above do. It works for GCSE, A-Level, IB and AP. AI does get things wrong, so check what it tells you against your own specification and mark schemes; the course covers exactly how to do that.
