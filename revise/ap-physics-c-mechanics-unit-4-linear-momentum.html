Key concepts

Linear momentum, p⃗The product of an object's mass and its velocity, p⃗ = mv⃗.
Net external force as rate of change of momentumThe net external force on a system equals the rate of change of the system's momentum, F⃗_net = dp⃗/dt.
SystemA collection of objects chosen for analysis and treated together.
Kinetic energy of a systemThe sum of the translational kinetic energies of the system's objects, K_sys = Σ ½m_i v_i².
Momentum from positionFor motion along the x-axis, the momentum at time t is p = m(dx/dt): the mass times the slope of the position–time graph at that instant, not the height of the graph.
Impulse, J⃗The integral of the net force over a time interval, J⃗ = ∫F⃗_net dt from t₁ to t₂.
Center-of-mass velocity, v⃗_cmThe velocity of a system's center of mass, v⃗_cm = (Σ p⃗_i)/(Σ m_i) = (Σ m_i v⃗_i)/(Σ m_i): the mass-weighted average of the parts' velocities, equal to the system's…
Elastic collisionA collision in which the system's total kinetic energy just after the collision equals its total kinetic energy just before.
Momentum and kinetic energyFor an object of mass m, K = (1/2)mv² = p²/(2m).
Direction of impulseImpulse is a vector with the direction of the net force over the interval (for a varying direction, the direction of the vector integral).
Constant center-of-mass velocityIf the net external force on a system is zero, its center-of-mass velocity is constant, however its parts move, collide or push on one another.
Transfer of kinetic energy in an elastic collisionIn an elastic collision only the system's total kinetic energy is unchanged.
Momentum as a vectorMomentum has magnitude mv and the direction of the velocity.
Impulse from a force–time graphThe impulse delivered by a net force is the area between the graph of F_net against t and the time axis; area below the axis counts as negative impulse.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Can an object's momentum be negative?✗ Momentum is an amount of motion with a size but no direction, so it is always positive.   ✓ Yes.
Can a system whose parts move with different velocities be described by a single velocity?✗ A system can be given a single velocity only when all of its parts move together with the same velocity.   ✓ Yes.
If momentum is conserved in a collision, is kinetic energy conserved too?✗ Whenever momentum is conserved in a collision, kinetic energy is conserved as well, so conservation of kinetic energy can be applied to any collision.   ✓ Not necessarily.
