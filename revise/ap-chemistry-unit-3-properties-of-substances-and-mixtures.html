Key concepts

London dispersion forcesAttractions between atoms or molecules that arise from the Coulombic interactions between temporary, fluctuating dipoles.
Vapor pressureThe pressure exerted by the vapor of a substance above its liquid (or solid) in a closed container at a given temperature.
Crystalline solidA solid in which the particles are arranged in a regular, repeating three-dimensional structure, for example an ionic solid such as KCl(s) or ice.
Ideal gasA model gas whose pressure, volume, temperature and amount are related exactly by PV = nRT.
Kinetic molecular theory (KMT)A particulate model of a gas: the gas consists of very many particles in continuous, random motion, and the macroscopic properties of the gas (pressure, volume,…
Ideal gasA model of a gas in which the particles have negligible volume and no attractions for one another.
Solution (homogeneous mixture)A mixture whose macroscopic properties, such as composition, density and color, do not vary throughout the sample.
Particulate representation of a solutionA drawing of a small region of a solution that shows its components as particles (molecules or ions), communicating the structure and properties of the solution through…
FiltrationPassing a mixture through a porous barrier such as filter paper to separate undissolved solid particles from a liquid.
MiscibleDescribes two liquids that mix in all proportions to form a single homogeneous phase, such as ethanol and water.
SpectroscopyThe study of matter through the electromagnetic radiation that it absorbs or emits.
PhotonA quantum (packet) of electromagnetic radiation.
Beer-Lambert lawA = εbc: the absorbance of a solution at a given wavelength equals the molar absorptivity of the absorbing species times the path length times its molar concentration.
Temporary (instantaneous) dipoleAn uneven distribution of the electron cloud of an atom or molecule that lasts only an instant because the electrons are always moving.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

What are the partial charges in a London dispersion attraction?✗ London dispersion forces are attractions between permanent partial charges that the particles carry all the time.   ✓ They are temporary.
Is every mixture of two substances a solution?✗ Any mixture of two substances is a solution, even if the components form layers or the properties of the mixture vary from place to place.   ✓ No.
Does a colored solution absorb light of its own color most strongly?✗ A colored solution absorbs light of the color it appears, so a blue solution absorbs blue light most strongly and absorbs little light of other colors.   ✓ No.
