Magnetism & Electromagnetism

Magnetic fields, electromagnets, the motor effect, electromagnetic induction and how transformers work.

Magnetism & Electromagnetism is a core part of GCSE Physics (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA and IGCSE). Use the key concepts and common misconceptions below to revise, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Magnetic fieldRegion around a magnet/current where a magnetic material feels a force; lines run N→S.
Induced vs permanent magnetPermanent keeps its magnetism; induced is magnetic only in a field.
ElectromagnetA coil carrying a current; stronger with more turns, current or an iron core.
Motor effectA current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences a force (Fleming's left hand).
Electromagnetic inductionMoving a wire through a field induces a potential difference.
TransformerChanges the size of an alternating voltage using two coils on an iron core.
Generator effectMoving a wire in a field induces a voltage
Right-hand ruleUsed for generators — same fingers, opposite hand
AlternatorAC generator — uses slip rings
DynamoDC generator — uses a split-ring commutator
Vp/Vs = Np/NsVoltage ratio equals turns ratio in a transformer
SolenoidCoil of wire — makes a uniform field like a bar magnet
F = B × I × LForce (N) = field (T) × current (A) × length (m)
Left-hand ruleThumb force, first finger field, second finger current

Common misconceptions to avoid

✗ A magnet that stays permanently magnetised once made. ✓ A material that becomes magnetic only while it is in a magnetic field.
✗ There is no field inside it, only outside. ✓ Strong and roughly uniform, like the field of a bar magnet.
✗ It stays permanently magnetic, making the electromagnet stronger forever. ✓ It magnetises strongly with current and loses its magnetism quickly when switched off.
✗ A wire with no current is pushed by a nearby magnet. ✓ A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field experiences a force.
✗ Hold the wire still inside a steady magnetic field. ✓ Move the wire through a magnetic field, or change the field through it.
