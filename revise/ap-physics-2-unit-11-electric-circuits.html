Key concepts

Electric current, IThe rate at which charge passes through a cross-sectional area of a wire, I = Δq/Δt.
Electric circuitOne or more electrical loops made of circuit elements, such as wires, batteries, resistors, lightbulbs, capacitors, switches, ammeters and voltmeters, through which…
Resistance, RA measure of the degree to which an object opposes the movement of electric charge through it.
Electric power, PThe rate at which energy is transferred, converted or dissipated by a circuit element: P = IΔV, where I is the current in the element and ΔV the potential difference…
Series connectionA connection in which any charge passing through one circuit element must pass through every element in the connection, with no other path available.
Change in electric potential energy, ΔU_E = qΔVWhen a charge q moves through a potential difference ΔV, the electric potential energy of the system changes by ΔU_E = qΔV.
Conservation of electric chargeElectric charge is neither created nor destroyed.
Equivalent capacitance, C_eqThe capacitance of a single capacitor that, connected in place of a group of capacitors, would store the same charge for the same potential difference across the group.
Charge carriersThe charged particles whose motion makes up a current: electrons in metal wires; positive and negative ions in a solution.
Circuit elementA component of a circuit, such as a wire, battery, resistor, lightbulb, capacitor, switch, ammeter or voltmeter.
Resistance of a uniform resistorR = ρℓ/A: proportional to the resistivity ρ of the material and to the length ℓ, and inversely proportional to the cross-sectional area A.
Power in a resistorUsing ΔV = IR, the power dissipated in a resistor is P = I²R = (ΔV)²/R.
Parallel connectionA connection in which charges may flow through any one of two or more paths joined between the same two points.
Emf of a battery, εThe energy per unit charge that a battery supplies to charge passing through it from its negative to its positive terminal.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Is current the amount of charge in a wire, or the amount that has passed through it?✗ Current is the amount of charge in a wire, or the amount that has passed through it, so more charge means more current.   ✓ No.
Can you tell whether elements are in series or in parallel from how they are drawn?✗ Elements drawn in a line, or around the outside of a diagram with the battery, are in series, and elements drawn side by side are in parallel.   ✓ No.
Does connecting more capacitors together always give a larger equivalent capacitance?✗ Capacitances simply add whatever the connection, because more capacitors can always store more charge.   ✓ No.
