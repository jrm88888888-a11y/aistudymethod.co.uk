Key concepts

Scalar quantityA quantity described completely by a magnitude (a number with a unit) and no direction.
Object modelA model in which an object's size, shape and internal configuration are ignored and the object is treated as a single point.
Motion diagramA diagram that shows an object's position at a series of instants separated by equal time intervals, drawn as dots (or images) along the line of motion.
Reference frameA coordinate system attached to an observer, with an origin and a chosen positive direction, relative to which positions, velocities and accelerations are measured.
Vector componentThe part of a vector along one axis of a coordinate system, written as a signed number (for example v_x = −3.0 m/s).
Vector quantityA quantity described by both a magnitude and a direction, such as 30 m east or 4 m/s in the −x direction.
Extensive propertyA property whose value for a whole object is the total for all its parts, such as mass (kg) and charge.

ObserverThe person or instrument making a measurement, at rest in its own reference frame.
Resultant of perpendicular componentsThe single vector equivalent to two perpendicular components placed tip to tail.
MagnitudeThe size of a quantity, with its unit and without any direction.
Position, xThe location of an object along an axis, measured from a chosen origin, with a sign that gives the side of the origin.
Constant (uniform) accelerationMotion in which the velocity changes by equal amounts in equal time intervals, so the acceleration a_x has the same value at every instant.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Is a vector component with a negative sign a smaller quantity than one with a positive sign?✗ A negative vector component is a smaller quantity than a positive one, as on a number line: −5 m/s is less motion than +2 m/s, and a vector in the negative direction is drawn with a shorter arrow.   ✓ No.
Does the shape of a motion graph look like the motion itself?✗ A motion graph is a picture of the motion: a line that rises and then falls shows an object going up and coming back down, and a level line shows an object at rest, whatever quantity is plotted.   ✓ No.
Is the magnitude of a vector the sum of the magnitudes of its perpendicular components?✗ Vector magnitudes add like ordinary numbers, so a vector's magnitude is the sum of its components' magnitudes (and one component is the magnitude minus the other).   ✓ No.
