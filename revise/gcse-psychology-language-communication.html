Key concepts

Sapir-Whorf hypothesisThe language we speak shapes the way we think; strong version claims language determines thought.
Piaget's viewThought develops first, language follows; children must understand a concept before naming it.
Vygotsky's viewLanguage and thought develop separately until age 2, then merge; inner speech guides thinking.
Carmichael (1932)Showed labels given to ambiguous figures altered how participants later reproduced them, supporting Whorf.
Animal communicationVon Frisch's bee waggle dance signals the direction and distance of food out of sight (displacement), but unlike human language it is fixed and lacks creativity.
Linguistic relativityWeaker Whorf claim that language influences but does not determine cognitive categories like colour.
