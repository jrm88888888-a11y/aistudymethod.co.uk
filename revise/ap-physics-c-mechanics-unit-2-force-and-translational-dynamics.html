Key concepts

SystemA chosen object or collection of objects that is analyzed together; everything outside it is the environment.
ForceA vector quantity that describes an interaction between two objects or systems.
Newton's third lawWhen object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts a force on object A of equal magnitude and opposite direction at the same time: F⃗_{A on B} = −F⃗_{B on A}.
Net force, F⃗_netThe vector sum of all the forces exerted on a system, F⃗_net = ΣF⃗_i, found by adding the forces' components along each axis.
Net force, F⃗_netThe vector sum of all the forces exerted on a system by objects outside it: F⃗_net = Σ F⃗.
Newton’s law of universal gravitationThe gravitational force between two objects or systems has magnitude |F⃗_g| = Gm₁m₂/r², where m₁ and m₂ are their masses and r is the distance between their centers of…
Kinetic friction, F⃗_f,kThe friction force exerted by a surface on an object when the two surfaces in contact move relative to each other (slide).
Ideal springA spring that has negligible mass and exerts a force proportional to the change in its length measured from its relaxed length.
Resistive force, F⃗_rA velocity-dependent force exerted on an object in the direction opposite to its velocity, such as the drag of air or water.
Centripetal acceleration, a_cThe component of an object’s acceleration directed toward the center of its circular path, in m/s².
Internal and external interactionsInteractions between objects inside a system are internal; interactions between a part of the system and something outside it are external.
Agent of a forceThe object that exerts a force.
Third-law pairThe two forces of one interaction.
Translational equilibriumThe configuration of forces for which the net force on a system is zero: ΣF⃗_i = 0, so the forces' components add to zero along every axis (ΣF_x = 0 and ΣF_y = 0).

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Is a solid made of particles that are each solid?✗ A property of a whole substance, such as being solid, hard or liquid, is also a property of each of its particles.   ✓ No.
In Newton’s law of gravitation, what distance is r for two spheres or for an object above a planet?✗ r is the distance between the surfaces of the objects, or the height of an object above a planet’s surface.   ✓ r is the distance between the centers of mass of the two objects.
Does an object moving in a circle at constant speed have an acceleration?✗ Acceleration is a change in speed only, so an object whose speed is constant, such as one in uniform circular motion, has zero acceleration, and only the speeding up or slowing down of an object counts as acceleration.   ✓ Yes.
