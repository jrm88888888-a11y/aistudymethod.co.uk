Unseen Poetry

How to analyse and compare unseen poems under exam conditions.

Unseen Poetry is a core part of GCSE English Literature. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Unseen poemA poem you meet for the first time in the exam
PEELPoint, Evidence, Explanation, Link
FormThe shape and type of poem
StructureHow a poem is built and progresses
LanguageWord choices and devices used
ToneThe attitude or feeling behind the poem
ImageryVivid descriptions creating pictures in the mind
MetaphorCalling one thing another to compare them
SimileComparison using 'like' or 'as'
PersonificationGiving human qualities to non-human things
VoltaA turn or shift in a poem's thought
AmbiguityWhen a word or image has more than one meaning
Thesis statementA clear argument answering the question
JuxtapositionPlacing two ideas side by side for contrast

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

What does analysing a poem actually involve?✗ Just saying what the poem is literally about.   ✓ Exploring how form, language and imagery create meaning and feeling.
Must all poems rhyme and keep a regular rhythm?✗ Yes, a real poem always rhymes with a fixed rhythm.   ✓ No — many use free verse or irregular form deliberately for effect.
Is the 'I' in a poem always the poet?✗ Yes, the speaker is always the poet themselves.   ✓ No — the speaker is a constructed voice that may differ from the poet.
