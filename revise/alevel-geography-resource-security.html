Resource Security

The supply and demand for water, energy and mineral resources, and resource security.

Resource Security is a core part of A-Level Geography. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Resource FrontierNewly exploited regions: Arctic oil/gas, deep-sea mining; environmental risks balance against economic potential.
Peak OilHubbert's 1956 theory of maximum extraction point; conventional peak debated; unconventional sources (shale) postponed.
Resource CurseParadox of resource-rich countries underperforming: Dutch Disease, corruption, conflict; e.g. DRC coltan, Niger oil.
Critical Raw MaterialsEU list of 30 materials essential to economy and supply-risky; includes rare earths, lithium, cobalt for green tech.
Circular EconomyReduce-reuse-recycle replacing linear take-make-dispose; design for disassembly; Ellen MacArthur Foundation.
Lithium TriangleChile-Bolivia-Argentina contain 60% of world lithium reserves in salt flats; critical for EV batteries.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

What are the four pillars of food security?✗ Food security just means producing enough food.   ✓ Availability (enough food exists), access (people can obtain it), utilisation (nutritional quality and absorption) and stability (consistent over time) — defined by the FAO.
What is the difference between physical and economic water scarcity?✗ Water scarcity only happens in dry countries.   ✓ Physical scarcity is insufficient water supply for demand (e.g. arid regions); economic scarcity is inability to access available water due to lack of infrastructure or investment, despite water being present.
