Key concepts

Translational kinetic energy (K, unit: J)The energy an object has because its center of mass is moving: K = (1/2)mv², where m is the object's mass and v is its speed.
Work (W)The amount of energy transferred into or out of a system by a force exerted on that system while the point where the force is exerted moves.
SystemThe object or objects chosen for analysis; everything else is the surroundings.
SystemAn object or group of objects chosen for analysis; everything else is its surroundings.
Power (P, unit: W)The rate at which energy changes with respect to time, either by transfer into or out of a system or by conversion from one type of energy to another within a system.
Joule (J)The SI unit of energy.
SystemThe object or collection of objects chosen for analysis.

Single-object systemA system containing one object modeled as having no internal structure.
Watt (W)The SI unit of power: 1 W = 1 J/s = 1 kg·m²/s³.
Dependence of K on mass and speedK is proportional to the mass and to the square of the speed.

Nonconservative forceA force whose work depends on the path taken, such as friction or air resistance.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Does doubling an object's speed double its translational kinetic energy?✗ Translational kinetic energy is proportional to speed, as if K = (1/2)mv, so doubling the speed doubles K and halving it halves K.   ✓ No.
Does a ball held above the ground have potential energy on its own?✗ Potential energy is a property of a single object: a raised ball, a thrown stone or one sphere in a group 'has' potential energy of its own.   ✓ No.
Are power and energy the same thing?✗ Power and energy are the same thing: a process with more power involves more energy, and processes that involve equal amounts of energy have equal power.   ✓ No.
