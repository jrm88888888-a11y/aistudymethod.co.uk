Ratio, Proportion & Rates

Ratio and proportion, sharing in a ratio, direct and inverse proportion and compound measures.

Ratio, Proportion & Rates is a core part of GCSE Maths. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

If 6 workers take 8 hours to paint a wall, how long would 12 workers take (assuming they work at the same rate)?✗ 16 hours, because doubling the workers doubles everything including the time.   ✓ 4 hours — this is inverse proportion: double the workers, halve the time.
In a school the ratio of boys to girls is 3:2. There are 30 boys. How many girls are there?✗ There are 27 girls because the ratio 3:2 means subtracting 3 - 2 = 1 from 30 and adding to make 2 girls' part.   ✓ 20 — each part of the ratio represents 10 pupils, so girls = 2 x 10.
If 5 pens cost £3.50, how much do 7 pens cost?✗ £5.50, by adding £2 to £3.50 because there are 2 more pens.   ✓ £4.90 — find the unit cost (£0.70 per pen), then multiply by 7.
Share 60 in the ratio 2 : 3.✗ 30 and 30, by splitting 60 in half because there are two numbers in the ratio.   ✓ 24 and 36 — 5 parts in total, each part is 12, so 2 x 12 = 24 and 3 x 12 = 36.
What is the difference between simple and compound interest?✗ Simple and compound interest give the same total — compound just uses a different formula.   ✓ Simple interest is calculated only on the original amount; compound interest is calculated on the original amount plus accumulated interest.
