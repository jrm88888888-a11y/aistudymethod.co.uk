Electricity

Current, voltage and resistance; series and parallel circuits; and how mains electricity reaches your home safely.

Electricity is a core part of GCSE Physics (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA and IGCSE). Use the key concepts and common misconceptions below to revise, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

CurrentRate of flow of charge, measured in amperes.
Potential differenceEnergy transferred per unit charge, measured in volts.
ResistanceOpposition to current; V = IR (Ohm's law).
Series circuitsOne path; current is the same throughout.
Parallel circuitsMultiple paths; pd is the same across each branch.
Electrical powerP = VI; the rate of energy transfer.
PowerP = V I = I^2 R = V^2 / R
EnergyE = P t = V I t
Series add RR = R1 + R2
Parallel add 1/R1/R = 1/R1 + 1/R2
ChargeQ = I t
Joule = watt second1 J = 1 W s
Ohmic conductorStraight line through origin
Filament bulbCurve - R rises with T

Common misconceptions to avoid

✗ Adding any resistor always increases the total resistance. ✓ Total resistance decreases — there are more paths for current to flow.
✗ Current gets used up by each component as it flows around the circuit. ✓ It stays the same at every point — current is conserved.
✗ Voltage is the 'amount of electricity' flowing through the wire. ✓ The energy transferred per unit charge passing through that component.
✗ 3 Ω ✓ 12 Ω
✗ 12 V ✓ 6 V
