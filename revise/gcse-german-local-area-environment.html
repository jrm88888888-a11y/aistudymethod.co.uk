Local Area & Environment

Describing your town and local area, the environment and global issues like pollution.

Local Area & Environment is a core part of GCSE German. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Das Dritte ReichNazi Germany 1933–45 under Adolf Hitler and the NSDAP.
Der Holocaust / die SchoahSystematic murder of about six million Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators.
Der Zweite WeltkriegSecond World War; for Germany began 1 September 1939 with the invasion of Poland.
Die KapitulationUnconditional surrender signed on 8 May 1945 (Tag der Befreiung).
Die Nürnberger ProzesseNuremberg Trials 1945–46 of leading Nazi figures by the Allied Tribunal.
Past perfect (Plusquamperfekt)hatte/war + Partizip II: 'er hatte den Krieg überlebt'.
Die TechnologieTechnology in daily life, including computers, smartphones and the internet.
Die sozialen MedienSocial media used to communicate, share content and stay in touch with friends.
Das HandyThe mobile phone, used for messaging, calls and going online.
Die UmweltThe world around us and environmental issues such as pollution and climate change.
Das RecyclingRecycling and reducing waste to protect the environment.
Die MedienThe media, including news, advertising and online information.
TechnikTechnology vocabulary: der Computer, das Handy (mobile), das Tablet, das Internet, die sozialen Medien.
Internet verbssurfen (browse), herunterladen (download), hochladen (upload), chatten, eine E-Mail schicken.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

Do German nouns and articles change in different roles?✗ No, they never change.   ✓ Yes — German uses cases (nominative, accusative, dative, genitive).
Can you always guess a German noun's gender from its meaning?✗ Yes, meaning always tells you the gender.   ✓ No — gender is often grammatical (das Mädchen, 'girl', is neuter).
How many genders do German nouns have?✗ Two, like French and Spanish.   ✓ Three — masculine, feminine and neuter (der, die, das).
Is German word order the same as English?✗ Yes, exactly like English.   ✓ No — the verb is usually second in main clauses and last in subordinate clauses.
