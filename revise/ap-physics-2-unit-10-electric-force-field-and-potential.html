Key concepts

Electric charge, qA fundamental property of the particles that make up all matter, which causes them to exert electrostatic forces on one another.
Net chargeThe sum of all the positive and negative charges in an object or system, each counted with its sign.
Electric fieldA property of the space around charged objects: at every point it has a magnitude and a direction, and a charge placed at the point experiences an electric force.
Electric potential energy of a system, U_EEnergy associated with the arrangement of charged objects that interact electrically.
Electric potential, VThe electric potential energy per unit charge at a point: V = U_E/q, where U_E is the electric potential energy the system would have with a charge q at that point.
Parallel-plate capacitorTwo separated, parallel conducting plates that can hold charges of equal magnitude and opposite sign, +Q and −Q.
Object–field systemThe system made up of a charged object and the electric field it moves in (or the charges that produce that field).
Electrically neutral objectAn object whose net charge is zero.
Charge distributionWhere the charge of an object or system is located within it.
Source chargeA charged object that produces an electric field.
Assembling a configurationBringing charged objects from infinitely far apart to their final positions slowly, so that none of them gains kinetic energy.
Zero of electric potentialBy convention the electric potential is zero infinitely far from an isolated point charge.
Charge of a capacitor, QThe magnitude of the charge on each plate of a capacitor, not the total for both plates.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Does an electrically neutral object contain electric charge?✗ An electrically neutral object contains no electric charge; charged particles are present only in objects that have been charged.   ✓ Yes.
Is the electric potential energy of two charged objects stored in one of them, such as the one that was moved?✗ Electric potential energy is stored in one object, such as the charged object that was moved into place, rather than in the system of interacting objects.   ✓ No.
Does moving to a higher electric potential always increase the electric potential energy?✗ Moving to a higher electric potential always increases the electric potential energy, and moving to a lower potential always decreases it, whatever the sign of the charge.   ✓ Only for a positive charge.
