Key concepts

Linear momentum (unit: kg·m/s)The product of an object's mass and its velocity, p⃗ = mv⃗.
Net external forceThe vector sum of the forces exerted on an object or system by objects outside it, in newtons (N).
System (of objects)A chosen collection of objects that is analyzed together.
Elastic collisionA collision in which the total kinetic energy of the system just after the collision equals the total kinetic energy just before it.
Momentum and kinetic energyTwo different quantities that both depend on mass and speed.
Rate of change of momentumThe change in momentum divided by the time interval over which it happens, Δp⃗/Δt, in kg·m/s² (equivalent to N).
Velocity of the center of mass, v⃗_cmThe velocity of a system treated as a single object: the total momentum divided by the total mass, v⃗_cm = (Σ m_i v⃗_i)/(Σ m_i).
Kinetic energy of a system (unit: J)The sum of the translational kinetic energies, K = (1/2)mv², of all the objects in the system.
Momentum as a vectorMomentum has a magnitude and a direction, and its direction is always the direction of the object's velocity, because mass is positive.
Internal and external forcesInternal forces are exerted by objects inside the chosen system on each other; they come in third-law pairs and do not change the system's total momentum.
Net external forceThe vector sum of the forces exerted on a system by objects outside the system.
Kinetic energy of each object in an elastic collisionIn an elastic collision only the system's total kinetic energy is unchanged.
Collisions and explosionsTwo models for interactions between objects in a system.
ImpulseThe product of the average force exerted on a system and the time interval during which it is exerted, J⃗ = F⃗_avg Δt.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Are momentum and kinetic energy the same quantity, so that doubling an object's speed has the same effect on each?✗ Momentum and kinetic energy are the same thing, the 'amount of motion', so momentum can be calculated as (1/2)mv², ranks objects in the same order as kinetic energy, and adds like kinetic energy.   ✓ No.
Is the velocity of a system's center of mass the average of the velocities of the objects in it?✗ The velocity of a system's center of mass (and the common velocity of objects that stick together) is the simple average of the objects' velocities, whatever their masses.   ✓ Only when the objects have equal masses.
Is every collision in which the objects bounce apart an elastic collision?✗ Collisions are sorted by what the objects do afterward: if the objects bounce or move apart the collision is elastic, and only if they stick together is it inelastic.   ✓ No.
