Key concepts

ElectronegativityA measure of how strongly an atom attracts the shared electrons in a chemical bond.
Potential energy of a pair of atomsThe energy a pair of atoms has because of the Coulombic attractions and repulsions among their nuclei and electrons, which depends on the distance between the nuclei.
Ionic solid (ionic crystal)A solid made of cations and anions held together by Coulombic attractions between oppositely charged ions.
Metallic bondingThe attraction between an array of positive metal ions and the valence electrons that are delocalized among them.
Lewis diagramA representation of a molecule or ion that shows its valence electrons: each line is a bonding (shared) pair of electrons between two atoms, and each pair of dots is a…
ResonanceA refinement of the Lewis structure model that is needed when two or more equivalent Lewis structures can be drawn for the same arrangement of atoms.
VSEPR theoryValence shell electron pair repulsion theory: the electron domains around a central atom repel one another (Coulombic repulsion between electrons) and so are arranged as…
Periodic trends in electronegativityFor the representative elements, electronegativity increases from left to right across a period and decreases down a group.
Internuclear distanceThe distance between the nuclei (centers) of two atoms or ions.
Periodic three-dimensional arrayA regular arrangement of ions in which the same pattern repeats in all three directions throughout the crystal.
Delocalized valence electrons ("sea of electrons")Valence electrons that have left individual metal atoms and are free to move throughout the whole solid.
Total valence electrons of a speciesThe number of electrons a Lewis diagram must show: the sum of the valence electrons of all the atoms, plus one for each unit of negative charge or minus one for each…
Resonance structures (equivalent Lewis structures)Lewis structures of one species that have the atoms in the same positions and the same total number of valence electrons but differ in where the electrons are drawn, for…
Electron domainA region of electron density around a central atom that counts as one unit in VSEPR theory: a lone pair, a single bond, a double bond or a triple bond.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Is the partially negative atom in a polar bond the atom that has more electrons?✗ The atom with more electrons, usually the larger atom, is the negative end of a bond and pulls on the shared electrons more strongly.   ✓ No.
Is each valence electron in a metal held in a bond between one particular pair of atoms?✗ In a metal, each valence electron is held in a bond between one particular pair of atoms, as in a covalent molecule.   ✓ No.
What does VSEPR theory treat as repelling one another?✗ The bonded atoms around a central atom push one another apart, so a molecule's shape is set by repulsion between its outer atoms.   ✓ The electron domains (bonding pairs and lone pairs) around the central atom.
