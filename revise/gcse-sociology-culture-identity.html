GCSE Sociology Revision

Culture, Identity & Socialisation

Culture, norms and values, primary and secondary socialisation, and how identity is formed.

Culture, Identity & Socialisation is a core part of GCSE Sociology. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

IdentitySense of self constructed through interaction with others; both personal (individual) and social (group) dimensions.
SocialisationProcess of learning culture; primary in family, secondary through school, media, peers, religion and work.
Goffman's dramaturgyGoffman (1959): social life is theatre; we present a 'front-stage' self while managing 'back-stage' identities.
GlobalisationIncreased worldwide interconnection through trade, media and migration; produces hybrid identities (Hall 1992).
Postmodern identityBauman (2000) 'liquid modernity': identity is fluid, fragmented and self-chosen through consumption.
HyperrealityBaudrillard (1981): media simulations become more real than reality; identity formed by consumed signs.
CultureThe shared values, norms, beliefs, customs and ways of life that members of a society learn and pass on to the next generation.
NormsSpecific, socially expected rules of behaviour that guide how people should act in particular situations, such as queuing or table manners.
ValuesGeneral principles or beliefs about what a society regards as important or right, such as respect for human life or hard work.
RoleA set of expected behaviours, rights and obligations attached to a particular social position, such as that of teacher, parent or student.
StatusA person's social position within a group or society, which may be ascribed at birth or achieved through their own efforts and actions.
Nuclear familyTwo-generation household of parents and dependent children; Parsons (1955) saw it as the ideal family of industrial society.
Extended familyThree or more generations living together or in close contact; more common in pre-industrial and collectivist societies.
Reconstituted familyStep-family formed when separated adults remarry with children from prior relationships; around 11% of UK families with children (ONS).

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

Do all sociologists agree on how society works?✗ Yes, all sociological theories agree.   ✓ No — perspectives differ (functionalism, Marxism, feminism).
What does the Marxist perspective emphasise?✗ That society runs on shared consensus with no conflict.   ✓ Conflict between social classes over power and resources.
