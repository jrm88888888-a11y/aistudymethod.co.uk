Atmospheric Systems

The atmosphere, air pollution and stratospheric ozone.

Atmospheric Systems is a core part of IB Environmental Systems. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Atmospheric layersTroposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere, exosphere; temperature inversion at stratopause.
Ozone layerO3 in stratosphere 15-35 km altitude; absorbs UV-B 280-315 nm; thinned by CFCs.
Montreal Protocol1987 international treaty phasing out CFCs and halons; ozone recovery on track by ~2070.
Photochemical smogNOx and VOC + sunlight produce ground-level ozone and PAN; respiratory irritant.
Acid depositionWet/dry fallout of H2SO4 and HNO3 from SO2 and NOx emissions; damages forests and lakes.
Greenhouse gasesCO2, CH4, N2O, H2O, fluorinated gases absorb outgoing longwave; quantified by GWP.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

Is energy recycled within an ecosystem?✗ Yes, energy cycles round endlessly.   ✓ No — energy flows through and is lost as heat; nutrients are recycled.
What does biodiversity measure?✗ Only the number of animals.   ✓ The variety of all living things and their genes in an area.
