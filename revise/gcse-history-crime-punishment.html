GCSE History Revision

Crime & Punishment

Changing definitions of crime, methods of policing and punishment through time, and the Whitechapel case study.

Crime & Punishment is a core part of GCSE History. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

WergildAnglo-Saxon system of compensation paid to victim's family for crimes; varied by social status of victim.
Bloody Code 1688-1815Period when over 200 offences carried death penalty, including theft of goods worth more than 5 shillings.
TransportationPunishment of sending convicts to colonies, mainly Australia from 1787; abolished 1868 after 162,000 transported.
Robert Peel 1829Home Secretary who founded the Metropolitan Police; officers nicknamed 'peelers' or 'bobbies' after him.
Pentonville Prison 1842Model prison built on separate system: solitary confinement with hard labour to encourage reflection and reform.
Derek Bentley 1953Hanged for murder despite Craig firing the shot; controversial case that fuelled campaign against capital punishment.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

Has Britain always had a professional police force?✗ Yes, organised police have always existed.   ✓ No — the Metropolitan Police began in 1829; before that, parish constables and watchmen kept order.
Have crimes and punishments stayed the same through history?✗ Yes, they have never changed.   ✓ No — they changed with society, religion and government.
