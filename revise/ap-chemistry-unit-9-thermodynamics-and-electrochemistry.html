Key concepts

Entropy, SA property of a system that increases when its matter becomes more dispersed or when its energy becomes more dispersed among its particles.
Absolute entropy (standard molar entropy), S°The entropy of one mole of a substance in its standard state at a stated temperature, usually 298 K, in J/(mol·K).
Standard stateThe reference conditions to which ΔG° refers: every reactant and product present as a pure substance (solids and liquids), as a solution of concentration 1.0 M…
Thermodynamically favored processA process for which ΔG° < 0.
Thermodynamically favored and KA process is thermodynamically favored when ΔG° < 0, which means that the products are favored at equilibrium (K > 1) under standard conditions.
Free energy of dissolution, ΔG°The standard free energy change when a substance dissolves.
Thermodynamically unfavorable processA process with ΔG° > 0: under standard conditions reactants are favored at equilibrium (K < 1).

Nonstandard conditionsConditions in which the concentrations (or partial pressures) of the species in the cell reaction are not all at their standard values (1 M, 1 atm).
ElectrolysisThe use of an external source of electrical energy to drive a redox reaction that is not thermodynamically favored.
Entropy change of a system, ΔSThe entropy of the system after a process minus its entropy before the process.

Standard Gibbs free energy change, ΔG°The Gibbs free energy change for a process in which all the reactants and products are in their standard states, reported per mole of reaction as written (kJ/mol_rxn).

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Is the sign of ΔS for a reaction decided by the size of the product molecules compared with the reactant molecules?✗ Larger molecules, with more atoms or a greater molar mass, have more entropy, so a reaction that makes larger molecules from smaller ones has a positive ΔS.   ✓ No.
Which end of a water molecule is attracted to a cation?✗ The hydrogen end of a water molecule is the negative end, so the H atoms of water point toward cations and the O atom points toward anions.   ✓ The oxygen end.
Does a given charge produce the same number of moles of any product, whatever the charge of the ion?✗ One mole of electrons produces one mole of product, so the same charge gives the same number of moles of any substance, whatever the charge of the ion.   ✓ No.
