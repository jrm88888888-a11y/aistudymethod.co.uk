Cells & Transport

Cell structure and the cell cycle, transport across membranes, and exchange surfaces and transport systems.

Cells & Transport is a core part of A-Level Biology (AQA, Edexcel, OCR and other boards). Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

CellThe smallest unit of life; carries out the functions needed to live.
Eukaryotic cellA cell with a nucleus enclosed in a membrane; e.g. animal, plant, fungus cells.
Prokaryotic cellA smaller cell with no nucleus; DNA is a loop in the cytoplasm; e.g. bacteria.
Subcellular structuresParts inside a cell with specific jobs: nucleus, mitochondria, ribosomes, chloroplasts.
Specialised cellsCells adapted for a specific role, e.g. red blood cell, sperm, root hair.
Stem cellUndifferentiated cell that can divide to form many cell types.
ProkaryoteCell with no nucleus, 70S ribosomes, circular DNA — e.g. bacteria
EukaryoteCell with nucleus and membrane-bound organelles, 80S ribosomes
Rough ERStudded with ribosomes — synthesises and folds proteins
Smooth ERNo ribosomes — synthesises lipids and steroids
Golgi apparatusModifies, packages and labels proteins into vesicles
LysosomeVesicle of hydrolytic enzymes — breaks down waste
G1 phaseCell growth, organelle replication, protein synthesis
S phaseDNA replication — each chromosome becomes 2 sister chromatids

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

What is the key structural difference between prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells?✗ Prokaryotes have no DNA — that's why they have no nucleus.   ✓ Eukaryotes have a membrane-bound nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; prokaryotes do not.
What is the main role of mitochondria in a cell?✗ Mitochondria produce energy by creating it from nothing.   ✓ To carry out aerobic respiration, producing ATP that cells use as an energy currency.
What is the key difference between mitosis and meiosis?✗ Mitosis and meiosis both produce gametes; they are basically the same.   ✓ Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid cells; meiosis produces four genetically different haploid cells (gametes).
In osmosis, which direction does water move?✗ Water moves from high solute concentration to low solute concentration.   ✓ From a region of higher water potential (less concentrated solution) to lower water potential.
What is a stem cell?✗ Stem cells are taken only from embryos and cannot be found in adults.   ✓ An undifferentiated cell capable of dividing to produce more stem cells or differentiating into specialised cell types.
