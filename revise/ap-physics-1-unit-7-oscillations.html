Key concepts

Periodic motionMotion that repeats itself exactly in equal intervals of time, such as uniform circular motion, a ball bouncing to the same height on every bounce, or an object…
Period of SHMThe time T for an object in SHM to complete one full cycle, for example from one extreme position to the other and back again.
Displacement equations for SHMFor an object in simple harmonic motion, the displacement from the equilibrium position can be written x = A cos(2πft) or x = A sin(2πft), where A is the amplitude, f…
Total mechanical energy of an oscillating system, E_totalThe sum of the kinetic energy and the potential energy of the system, E_total = U + K.
PeriodThe time taken to complete one full cycle of a periodic motion, T.
Frequency of SHMThe number of complete cycles an object in SHM makes per unit time, f.
Amplitude, AThe magnitude of the maximum displacement of an oscillating object from its equilibrium position.
Kinetic energy, KThe energy an object has because of its motion, K = (1/2)mv².
Simple harmonic motion (SHM)A special case of periodic motion: the oscillation of an object about an equilibrium position under a restoring force whose magnitude is proportional to the object's…
Period–frequency relationshipT = 1/f: period and frequency are reciprocals, so when one is multiplied by a factor the other is divided by the same factor.
Turning points (extremes) of SHMThe positions x = +A and x = −A, where the object reverses direction.
Spring potential energy, U_sThe energy stored in a stretched or compressed ideal spring, U_s = (1/2)k(Δx)², where Δx is the stretch or compression from the spring's natural length.
Displacement from equilibriumThe change in position Δx of an object measured from its equilibrium position, not from a wall, the floor or the end of a spring.
Object–ideal-spring oscillatorAn object attached to a spring of negligible mass that obeys Hooke's law, oscillating with no friction or air resistance.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Is every motion that repeats itself at regular time intervals simple harmonic motion?✗ Any periodic motion, anything that repeats at regular time intervals, is simple harmonic motion.   ✓ No.
At t = 0, where is an object whose position is given by x = A cos(2πft)?✗ The cosine and sine forms both start the object at the equilibrium position at t = 0 (cos 0 is taken to be 0), so the choice between them does not matter for where the object starts.   ✓ At x = +A, because cos 0 = 1.
On a graph of potential energy against position, is the height of the curve the kinetic energy at that position?✗ The energy asked about can be read directly as the height of whatever curve is plotted, so the height of a U–x curve at a position is taken to be the kinetic energy there.   ✓ No.
