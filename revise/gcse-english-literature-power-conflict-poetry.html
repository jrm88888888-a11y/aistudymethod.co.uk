Power & Conflict Poetry

The Power and Conflict poetry anthology — themes, comparisons and poetic methods.

Power & Conflict Poetry is a core part of GCSE English Literature. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Iambic pentameterTen-syllable line with alternating stress — heartbeat rhythm
Sonnet14-line poem; Ozymandias is one
QuatrainA four-line stanza, common in Blake's London
PlosiveHard b/p/d sounds suggesting violence or force
Frame narrativeA story told inside another story
In medias resStarting a scene in the middle of action
ComparisonShowing similarities and differences between two texts
ThesisA central argument run through an essay
SynthesisPulling ideas together into a unified point
RomanticismMovement valuing nature, emotion and the sublime
PacifismBelief that war is morally wrong
PatriotismDevotion to one's country, sometimes uncritical
AnthologyA curated collection of poems on linked themes
VoltaA clear turning point in a poem's argument

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

What does analysing a poem actually involve?✗ Just saying what the poem is literally about.   ✓ Exploring how form, language and imagery create meaning and feeling.
Must all poems rhyme and keep a regular rhythm?✗ Yes, a real poem always rhymes with a fixed rhythm.   ✓ No — many use free verse or irregular form deliberately for effect.
Is the 'I' in a poem always the poet?✗ Yes, the speaker is always the poet themselves.   ✓ No — the speaker is a constructed voice that may differ from the poet.
