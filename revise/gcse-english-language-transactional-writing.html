GCSE English Language Revision

Transactional Writing

Writing letters, articles, speeches and essays for a purpose and audience.

Transactional Writing is a core part of GCSE English Language. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

FormLetter (Dear Sir/Madam), article (headline, byline), speech (direct address), report; conventions matter.
PurposeInform, persuade, argue, explain, advise; word choice shifts; persuasive uses emotive language.
AudienceAdjust register: formal for parliament, informal for peers; vocabulary, examples, references.
Headline and SubheadingArticle opens with attention-grabbing headline (often pun); subheadings break up text.
Topic SentenceOpens paragraph stating main idea; rest develops it; clear navigation for reader.
Counter-argumentAcknowledge opposing view ('Some may argue...'); then refute; builds credibility in argument.
Plot StructureOpening hook, rising tension, climax, falling action, resolution; one event focus in short story.
Show Don't TellDescribe action and sensation rather than state emotion; 'tears welled' not 'she was sad'.
Sensory DetailFive senses: sight, sound, smell, taste, touch; immerses reader; not just visual.
DialogueRealistic speech reveals character and advances plot; new line for new speaker; punctuation crucial.
Tone and MoodTone = writer's attitude; mood = reader's feeling; created by word choice, pace, imagery.
ResolutionSatisfying ending: full circle, twist, ambiguity; avoid 'and it was all a dream'.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

Does using longer words always improve writing?✗ Yes, the longer the words the better.   ✓ No — clear, precise words suited to purpose and audience matter more.
What does analysing non-fiction involve?✗ Summarising what the text says.   ✓ Examining how language and structure achieve the writer's purpose.
