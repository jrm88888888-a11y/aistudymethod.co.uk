Key concepts

Rotational kinetic energyThe kinetic energy a rigid system has because it rotates: K = (1/2)Iω², where I is its rotational inertia about the axis of rotation and ω is its angular speed.
TorqueThe rotational effect of a force about an axis: τ = rF sin θ = rF_⊥, where r is the distance from the axis to the point where the force is exerted.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

If the masses on a rotating rod are moved farther from the axis, does the rod's rotational inertia change?✗ Rotational inertia depends only on the amount of mass, so moving the same masses to new positions, or comparing objects of equal mass, leaves the rotational inertia unchanged.   ✓ Yes.
Does an object moving in a straight line have angular momentum about an axis it does not pass through?✗ Only objects that are spinning or moving in a circle have angular momentum; an object moving in a straight line has none.   ✓ Yes.
Does a planet exert a larger gravitational force on a satellite than the satellite exerts on the planet?✗ In a gravitational interaction, the object with more mass exerts the larger force on the other object.   ✓ No.
