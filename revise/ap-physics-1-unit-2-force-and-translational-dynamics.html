Key concepts

SystemAn object or a collection of objects chosen for analysis; everything outside it is the environment.
ForceA push or pull exerted on an object or system by another object or system.
Newton's third lawWhen object A exerts a force on object B, object B exerts a force on object A of equal magnitude and opposite direction: F⃗_A on B = −F⃗_B on A.
Net forceF⃗_net = ΣF⃗: the vector sum of all the forces exerted on a system; unit newton (N).
Unbalanced forcesA set of forces exerted on a system whose vector sum is not zero, so the net force on the system is not zero.
Newton's law of universal gravitation|F⃗_g| = G m_1m_2/r²: any two objects or systems with mass attract each other with a force whose magnitude is proportional to each of their masses and inversely…
Friction force F_fThe component of the contact force exerted on an object by a surface that is parallel to the surface, measured in newtons (N).
Ideal springA model of a spring with negligible mass that exerts a force proportional to the change in its length from its relaxed length.
Uniform circular motionMotion of an object around a circular path at constant speed.
Property of a systemA quantity or behavior that describes the system as a whole, such as its total mass, its shape, or whether it is rigid.
InteractionA mutual influence between two objects or systems.
InteractionA mutual action between two objects or systems in which each exerts a force on the other.
Net force in one dimensionAlong a single axis each force is a signed component, with opposite directions given opposite signs, so the net force is the algebraic sum of the components.
Net force, F⃗_net (N)The vector sum of all the forces exerted on a system, ΣF⃗.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Is a solid made of solid molecules, and a liquid made of liquid molecules?✗ A macroscopic property of a substance, such as being solid, liquid, hard or soft, is possessed by each of its individual particles.   ✓ No.
After a stick hits a puck, does the puck carry a force from the stick with it as it slides?✗ An object that has been hit, thrown or pushed carries a force from that interaction along with it, and this force keeps it moving until it runs out.   ✓ No.
Is the acceleration of a pendulum bob zero as it passes through the lowest point of its swing?✗ A pendulum bob has zero acceleration at the lowest point of its swing, because it is moving fastest there (and that is its resting position).   ✓ No.
