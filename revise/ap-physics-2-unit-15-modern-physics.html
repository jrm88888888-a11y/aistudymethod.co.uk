Key concepts

Classical physicsNewtonian mechanics together with the classical wave model of light and classical electromagnetism.
AtomThe smallest unit of an element.
Photon energy, EThe energy of one photon, E = hf = hc/λ, where h is Planck's constant, f the frequency, and λ the wavelength.
Thermal radiationElectromagnetic radiation that matter emits because of its temperature: the matter spontaneously converts some of its internal (thermal) energy into electromagnetic…
Photoelectric effectThe emission of electrons from a material when electromagnetic radiation, such as ultraviolet light, is incident on it.
Compton scatteringAn interaction in which a photon, typically an X-ray or gamma-ray photon, collides with a free electron.
NucleonA proton or a neutron: the two kinds of particle that make up a nucleus.
Radioactive decayThe spontaneous transformation of an unstable nucleus, in which it emits one or more particles or a photon.
Quantum theoryThe theory developed to explain observations of matter and energy that classical physics could not explain.
NucleusThe small, positively charged center of an atom, made up of protons and neutrons.
Electron volt, eVA unit of energy used for atoms and photons: 1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, the energy gained by a charge e moved through a potential difference of 1 V.
BlackbodyAn idealized model of matter that absorbs all the radiation that falls on it, reflecting none.
Photoactive materialA material, such as a clean metal surface, that emits electrons when electromagnetic radiation of high enough frequency falls on it.
Free electronAn electron that is not bound to an atom, or so loosely bound that the energy binding it can be ignored compared with the photon's energy.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Is the interference pattern of light passing through two slits an effect that classical physics cannot explain?✗ The double-slit interference pattern of light is a quantum effect that classical physics cannot explain, and it is evidence for photons.   ✓ No.
Does light eject electrons from a metal only when a battery applies a potential difference to it?✗ Electrons are ejected only when a battery or other source applies a potential difference to pull them out of the metal; the light merely helps.   ✓ No.
Are all the emissions from radioactive decay forms of electromagnetic radiation?✗ Everything emitted in radioactive decay is a kind of electromagnetic ray, like light or X-rays, with no mass and no charge.   ✓ No.
