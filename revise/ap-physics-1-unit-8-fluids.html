Key concepts

Solid (particle model)A state of matter in which attractive interactions between neighboring atoms or molecules hold each particle near a fixed position, about which it vibrates.
PressureThe magnitude of the component of a force perpendicular to a surface, divided by the area over which it is exerted: P = F_⊥/A.
Fluid parcelA small portion of a fluid, such as a tiny cube of water, treated as a single object.
Pressure difference as the cause of flowA fluid flows from a region of higher pressure toward a region of lower pressure, because the fluid on the high-pressure side pushes a portion of fluid harder than the…
Liquid (particle model)A state of matter in which the particles are about as close together as in the solid, and their interactions keep them together, but they are not held in fixed positions…
Perpendicular force componentThe part of a force that acts at right angles to a surface.
Net force on a fluid parcelThe vector sum of the forces the surrounding fluid exerts on all faces of a parcel, plus the parcel's weight.
Mass flow rateThe mass of fluid passing a cross-section of a pipe per unit time.
Gas (particle model)A state of matter in which the particles are, on average, far apart compared with their size and interact significantly only when they collide.
Pressure as a scalarPressure has a magnitude but no direction.
Conditions for a change in a fluid's velocityA parcel of fluid speeds up, slows down or changes direction only when the net force on it is not zero.
Volume flow rate (V/t = Av)The volume of fluid passing a cross-section per unit time, equal to the cross-sectional area of the flow multiplied by the speed of the fluid there: V/t = Av.
FluidA substance that has no fixed shape, so that a sample takes the shape of the container that holds it.
Incompressible fluid under pressureA given amount of an incompressible fluid keeps the same volume, and so the same density, however great the pressure on it.

Unit 8: Fluids

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Do the individual particles of a substance have the properties of the bulk substance, such as hardness, softness or size that changes with state?✗ The particles of a solid are themselves hard and rigid, the particles of a liquid are soft, and the particles of a substance change their size or mass when it melts, boils, expands or shrinks.   ✓ No.
Can a pressure, or a difference in pressure, be used directly as a force?✗ Pressure and force are the same quantity, so a pressure (or a pressure difference) in pascals can be used as a force in newtons.   ✓ No.
Does water always flow from the container with the higher water surface to the one with the lower surface?✗ Water always flows from the container with the higher water surface toward the lower one, whatever presses on the surfaces.   ✓ No.
