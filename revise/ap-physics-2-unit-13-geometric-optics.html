Key concepts

Light rayA straight line drawn perpendicular to the wavefronts of a light wave and pointing in the direction the wave travels.
Principal axisThe line through the center of a spherical mirror's surface and its center of curvature, perpendicular to the mirror at its center.
RefractionThe change in direction of a light ray at the boundary where it passes from one medium into another.
Converging (convex) lensA lens that is thicker at its center than at its edges.
WavefrontA surface (drawn as a line) joining points of a wave that are in the same phase, such as neighboring crests.
Concave (converging) mirrorA spherical mirror whose reflecting surface curves inward, toward the incident light, like the inside of a spoon.
MediumA material, or a vacuum, through which light travels, such as air, water or glass.
Principal axisThe line through the center of a lens perpendicular to the lens.
Geometric opticsThe study of light using rays that travel in straight lines within one material and change direction only when they interact with matter (for example, at a mirror).
Focal pointFor a concave mirror, the point in front of the mirror where rays arriving parallel to the principal axis meet after reflection.
Speed of light in a medium, vThe speed at which light travels through a material.
Focal pointFor a converging lens, the point on the transmitted side where rays arriving parallel to the principal axis meet.
Point source and shadowA source small enough that all its rays spread out from a single point.
Convex (diverging) mirrorA spherical mirror whose reflecting surface bulges outward, toward the incident light, like the back of a spoon.

Unit 13: Geometric Optics

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Does a ray run along a wavefront?✗ A light ray is drawn along a wavefront, joining points of the wave that are in step.   ✓ No.
Is refraction the same as reflection?✗ Refraction is another name for reflection: light that strikes a surface and bounces off it is being refracted.   ✓ No.
Does a convex lens spread out a parallel beam of light?✗ A convex lens spreads out parallel light, as a convex mirror does.   ✓ No.
