Acidic (ionizable) hydrogenA hydrogen atom that a molecule or ion can transfer to a base as H⁺.

Buffer solutionA solution that contains large concentrations of both members of a conjugate acid-base pair, such as a weak acid HA and its conjugate base A⁻.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer. Make sure you know the right one:

Does pure water contain any ions?✗ Pure water contains only H₂O molecules and no ions; H₃O⁺ and OH⁻ are present only when an acid or a base has been added.   ✓ Yes.
Is every hydrogen atom in the formula of an acid an acidic hydrogen?✗ Every hydrogen atom in the formula of an acid can be donated as H⁺, so the number of acidic hydrogens equals the number of H atoms in the formula.   ✓ No.
Because K_sp is constant, are the solubility of a salt and the concentrations of its ions unaffected by pH?✗ Because K_sp is constant at a given temperature, the solubility of a salt and the concentrations of its ions in a saturated solution cannot change when the pH changes.   ✓ No.
