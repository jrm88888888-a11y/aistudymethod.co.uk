GCSE Geography Revision

Population & Migration

Population growth, distribution and migration, settlement patterns, and globalisation.

Population & Migration is a core part of GCSE Geography (AQA, Edexcel, OCR, CCEA and IGCSE). Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Population PyramidBar graph of age-sex structure; pyramids show youthful populations (Nigeria), inverted shapes show ageing (Japan).
Demographic Transition ModelFive stages tracking birth/death rates with development; UK reached Stage 4 in 20th century.
Dependency RatioRatio of dependents (under 15 and over 65) to working-age population; higher ratio means more economic burden.
Ageing PopulationRising median age and over-65 share; Japan 29% over 65; strains pensions, healthcare; resolved by immigration or pro-natalism.
Voluntary vs Forced MigrationVoluntary for economic/lifestyle reasons (EU workers to UK); forced by conflict, persecution, disaster (Syrian refugees).
Net MigrationDifference between immigration and emigration; positive means population gain; UK net migration around 600,000 (2023).
Hot Desert CharacteristicsLess than 250mm rainfall/year; large diurnal temperature range; sandy or rocky surfaces; e.g. Sahara, Atacama, Sonoran.
DesertificationLand degradation in drylands from climate change and human activity (overgrazing, deforestation, over-cultivation); affects Sahel.
XerophytesPlants adapted to drought: deep tap roots (mesquite), water storage (cacti), waxy leaves; reduce transpiration.
Polar EnvironmentsHigh latitude regions with permanent ice; Arctic (sea ice over ocean) vs Antarctic (ice on continent); minus 60 degrees C possible.
TundraSubarctic biome with permafrost subsoil; short summers allow lichens, mosses, grasses; reindeer, musk ox, lemmings.
PermafrostPermanently frozen ground covering 25% of Northern Hemisphere; thawing releases methane, threatens infrastructure.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

What is the difference between push and pull factors in migration?✗ Push and pull factors are the same thing.   ✓ Push factors drive people away from their origin (war, poverty, persecution, disaster); pull factors attract people to a destination (jobs, safety, family, healthcare, education).
Why do people move from rural to urban areas in LICs?✗ Because cities in poor countries are always better than the countryside.   ✓ A combination of push factors (rural poverty, lack of services, agricultural change) and pull factors (perceived jobs, education, healthcare) — though reality often disappoints.
What happens in Stage 2 of the Demographic Transition Model?✗ Birth rates rise rapidly because people want larger families.   ✓ Death rates fall rapidly (due to better food, sanitation and medicine) while birth rates remain high, causing rapid population growth.
Why is an ageing population a challenge for HICs?✗ Because old people stop contributing anything to society.   ✓ Rising dependency ratio means fewer workers support more pensioners through tax, increasing pressure on pensions, healthcare and social care services.
Why do TNCs locate production in LICs?✗ Because TNCs want to develop poorer countries and lift them out of poverty.   ✓ Lower labour costs, weaker regulation, access to raw materials, large potential markets, and government incentives like tax breaks in special economic zones.
