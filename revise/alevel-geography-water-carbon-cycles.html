Water & Carbon Cycles

The water and carbon cycles as systems, their stores and flows, and water and energy security.

Water & Carbon Cycles is a core part of A-Level Geography. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Positive feedbackInitial change is amplified, e.g. ice-albedo loop accelerating melt.
Negative feedbackInitial change is dampened, e.g. increased CO2 boosting plant growth that absorbs CO2.
Ice-albedo feedbackMelting ice exposes darker surface, absorbs more heat, drives further melt.
Permafrost thawReleases stored methane and CO2, amplifying warming.
DeforestationReduces evapotranspiration and carbon sequestration; Amazon losing ~10000 km^2/yr.
Ocean acidificationRising CO2 lowers ocean pH (from 8.2 pre-industrial to ~8.1 today).
Drainage basinArea of land drained by a river and its tributaries; open subsystem of water cycle.
Water balancePrecipitation = evapotranspiration + runoff + storage change.
Lag timeTime between peak rainfall and peak discharge.
Peak dischargeMaximum river flow following storm event, in m^3/s (cumecs).
ThroughflowLateral movement of water through soil downslope to channel.
BaseflowGroundwater seepage maintaining river flow between storms.
StoreA reservoir holding water or carbon for a measurable time (e.g. oceans, atmosphere, biosphere).
FluxRate of transfer between stores, measured in Gt/yr or km^3/yr.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

What is a positive climate feedback loop?✗ Positive feedback means a good outcome for the climate.   ✓ A process where warming triggers changes that cause more warming — e.g. melting Arctic ice exposes dark ocean, which absorbs more heat, melting more ice (ice-albedo feedback).
How do humans affect the carbon cycle?✗ Humans only affect the carbon cycle by breathing out CO2.   ✓ By burning fossil fuels (releasing stored geological carbon to the atmosphere) and deforestation (reducing the biosphere store and releasing carbon).
What is lag time on a storm hydrograph?✗ Lag time is how long the storm itself lasts.   ✓ The time between peak rainfall and peak river discharge — affected by drainage basin shape, land use, soil saturation and vegetation.
Lag time refers to:✗ Time between storm start and storm end   ✓ Time between peak rainfall and peak discharge
What is the difference between physical and economic water scarcity?✗ Water scarcity only happens in dry countries.   ✓ Physical scarcity is insufficient water supply for demand (e.g. arid regions); economic scarcity is inability to access available water due to lack of infrastructure or investment, despite water being present.
