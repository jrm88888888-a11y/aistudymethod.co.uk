Modern World Studies

Key modern conflicts and societies: Vietnam, the Middle East, China and South Africa.

Modern World Studies is a core part of GCSE History. Revise the key concepts and common mistakes below, then lock them in with the free games.

Key concepts

Dien Bien Phu 1954Decisive Viet Minh victory over French forces under Giap; ended French Indochina, led to Geneva Accords.
Geneva Accords 1954Agreement partitioning Vietnam at 17th parallel pending elections; US blocked elections to prevent Ho Chi Minh victory.
Gulf of Tonkin 1964Alleged North Vietnamese attack on USS Maddox; resolution gave Johnson power for full military involvement.
Tet Offensive 1968Viet Cong surprise attacks on 100 cities during lunar new year; military defeat but US public confidence collapsed.
My Lai Massacre 1968US troops under Lt. Calley killed 504 unarmed civilians; cover-up exposed 1969, devastated US moral authority.

Common mistakes to avoid

Questions where students often pick the tempting wrong answer — make sure you know the right one:

What was the outcome of the Vietnam War?✗ The USA won a clear victory.   ✓ The USA withdrew and the North unified Vietnam in 1975.
Why did the USA struggle in Vietnam?✗ Because its army was tiny.   ✓ Guerrilla tactics, difficult terrain and limited local support made it unwinnable.
What caused the Cold War?✗ Stalin started it by invading Eastern Europe after the war.   ✓ Ideological incompatibility plus mutual suspicion after WWII — disputes over Eastern Europe, the atomic bomb, and broken wartime agreements.
How did the feudal system work in Norman England?✗ Feudalism meant peasants were slaves owned by the king.   ✓ The king granted land to tenants-in-chief in return for knight service; they in turn enfeoffed knights who held land in return for military service to their lord.
What were the Wars of the Roses really about?✗ The whole country was at war for thirty continuous years.   ✓ Dynastic conflict between rival branches of the Plantagenets (Lancaster and York) over the throne, intensified by weak kingship under Henry VI and noble factionalism.
