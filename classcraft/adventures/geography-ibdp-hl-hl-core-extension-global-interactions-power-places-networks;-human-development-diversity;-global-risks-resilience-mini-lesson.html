Global Interactions (HL Core)

The HL-only core extension — Geographic Perspectives: Global Interactions — is an in-depth study of globalisation, examined in Paper 3. It has three units: power, places & networks; human development & diversity; and global risks & resilience.

Expect genuine calculations — globalisation index numbers, the HDI as a geometric mean, and location quotients — plus the concepts of power, flows and resilience. Press Start.

HL core · framing

Globalisation & Paper 3

Globalisation is the growing interconnection of places through flows of capital, goods, people, ideas and data. Paper 3 is a shorter (1 hour) but demanding essay paper that expects a synoptic, evaluative argument.

  • Places are increasingly shaped by their connections, not just their location.
  • Power over these flows is very unevenly held — some places are "switched on" to the network, others "switched off".

Key tension: globalisation both reduces some inequalities (spreading technology and jobs) and widens others — the heart of most Paper 3 questions.

Unit 1 · power, places & networks

Power, places & networks

Global flows move through networks controlled by powerful actors:

  • Transnational corporations (TNCs) organise global production networks, choosing where to locate each stage.
  • Global governance bodies (IMF, World Bank, WTO, UN) set the rules of trade and finance.
  • The KOF Globalisation Index measures how globalised a country is across economic, social and political dimensions.

Networks: a country's role depends on its connectivity — hubs of the network gain most; peripheral places gain least.

Unit 2 · measuring development

Human development & the HDI

Development is more than income. The Human Development Index (HDI) combines three dimensions — a long healthy life, education, and a decent standard of living — into a single 0–1 score. It is the geometric mean of the three dimension indices:

HDI = ( Ihealth × Ieducation × Iincome )1/3the cube root of the product of the three sub-indices (each scaled 0 to 1)
Worked example

If Ihealth = 0.9, Ieducation = 0.6 and Iincome = 0.4:

product = 0.9 × 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.216, and the cube root of 0.216 = 0.60

Unit 2 · diversity & inequality

Diversity, inequality & empowerment

Global interactions reshape culture and inequality:

  • Cultural diffusion & hybridity — cultures spread and blend into new "glocal" forms; some fear a loss of local identity.
  • Income inequality is measured by the Gini coefficient, from 0 (perfect equality) to 1 (one person has everything).
  • Empowerment — access to technology, and gender/indigenous empowerment, can spread the benefits of globalisation more fairly.

Power: whether globalisation narrows or widens the development gap is the classic evaluative debate — use located evidence on both sides.

Unit 3 · global risks

Global risks from interactions

The very networks that connect the world also spread risk quickly across borders:

  • Economic — a financial shock in one market cascades through global finance.
  • Environmental — global supply chains and consumption drive pollution and resource depletion far from where goods are used.
  • Geopolitical & health — the rapid spread of disease, and disputes over data, migration and sovereignty.

Interconnection: these risks are hard for any single government to control — they arise from the global system itself.

Unit 3 · resilience

Resilience & local reactions

Places and communities build resilience to global risks in different ways:

  • Diversifying economies and supply chains so one shock does less damage.
  • Global governance and cooperation to manage shared risks (finance, climate, health).
  • Localism & anti-globalisation — movements that resist global forces, protect local producers, or "re-shore" production.

Possibilities: the debate between deeper integration and greater localism is exactly the kind of judgement Paper 3 rewards.

HL skill · concentration

Location quotient

Global production concentrates activities in particular places. A location quotient (LQ) measures how concentrated an industry is locally compared with the national average:

LQ = local share of the activity ÷ national shareLQ > 1 means the activity is over-represented (concentrated) in that place; LQ < 1 means under-represented

Reading it: an LQ of 3 means the industry is three times as concentrated locally as it is nationally — a sign of a specialised hub in the global network.

Paper 3 · exam craft

Writing a synoptic Paper 3 essay

Paper 3 gives short, high-stakes essays that demand a global, synoptic view:

  • Link the three units — power/networks, development/diversity and risks/resilience — together.
  • Use named, located examples of winners and losers of globalisation.
  • Build a two-sided argument and reach a justified conclusion.

Command terms: "examine", "evaluate", "discuss" and "to what extent" all demand argument and a conclusion — never a list.
