Mission 1

Mission 1: Why does French need two words to say "I played", and how do you build them?

Make a guess: Why does French need two words to say "I played", and how do you build them?

Mission 1

Two parts

  • English has two pasts: "I played" and "I have played".
  • French uses one form for both: j'ai joué.
  • Part 1: avoir in the present (ai). Part 2: the past participle (joué).
  • J'ai joué au foot. – I played football. / I have played football.
  • Elle a fini. – She finished. / She has finished.
  • Your turn: in nous avons joué, which part shows the person?
    Show answer

    avons (avoir). The participle joué stays the same.

Make the participle

  • Tu as choisi ? – Have you chosen?

  • jouer and joué sound the same. finis (present) and fini (participle) sound the same too.

Mission 1

When? And the negative

  • Time phrases:
    • hier – yesterday; hier soir – last night
    • le week-end dernier – last weekend; la semaine dernière – last week
    • l'année dernière – last year; il y a deux jours – two days ago
  • Negative: ne … pas wraps avoir. The participle comes after.
  • Je n'ai pas mangé. – I didn't eat. / I haven't eaten.
  • Il n'a jamais visité Paris. – He has never visited Paris.
  • Nous n'avons rien acheté. – We didn't buy anything.
  • Every form of avoir starts with a vowel, so always n'.
  • Watch out: Je n'ai joué pas → Je n'ai pas joué.
  • Your turn: "They didn't wait." (ils, attendre)
    Show answer

    Ils n'ont pas attendu.

One French form, three English ones

  • J'ai regardé la télé. = I watched TV. / I have watched TV. / I did watch TV.
  • English "did" has no French word. Tu as regardé le match ? – Did you watch the match?

Mission 2

Why some participles break the rule

  • Regular past participles (see Mission 1): jouer → joué, finir → fini, vendre → vendu.
  • English has the same problem: walk → walked, but see → seen, take → taken.
  • The most common verbs are the irregular ones. You use them every day.
  • Good news: the auxiliary is still avoir. Only the participle changes.
  • And most irregular participles end in -u, -is or -it.
  • j'ai vu – I saw / I have seen
  • j'ai pris – I took / I have taken
  • j'ai fait – I did, I made / I have done, I have made

Mission 3

The être verbs

  • Most verbs: avoir + participle (j'ai joué).
  • About 15 verbs use être instead. Most are about movement or change of state.

Mission 3

With être, the participle agrees

  • With être, the participle works like an adjective. It agrees with the subject.
  • Feminine: add -e. Plural: add -s. Feminine plural: add -es.
MasculineFeminine
jeje suis alléje suis allée
tutu es allétu es allée
il / elleil est alléelle est allée
nousnous sommes allésnous sommes allées

  • je and tu: agree with the speaker. A girl writes je suis allée.
  • Mixed group (boys and girls): masculine plural. Paul et Léa sont allés.
  • vous for one person: no -s. Madame, vous êtes allée …
  • Watch out: avoir verbs do not agree with the subject. Elle a mangé, not elle a mangée.
  • Your turn: "My sisters arrived."

Mission 3

Negatives in the perfect

  • The negative wraps round the auxiliary, not the participle.
  • ne + auxiliary + pas + participle.
  • Je n'ai pas mangé. – I didn't eat.
  • Elle n'est pas sortie. – She didn't go out.
  • Reflexive: ne goes before the pronoun. Je ne me suis pas levé(e). – I didn't get up.
  • jamais and rien work the same way:
  • Je n'ai jamais vu la mer. – I have never seen the sea.
  • Il n'a rien fait. – He did nothing.
  • personne is different: it goes after the participle. Je n'ai vu personne. – I didn't see anyone.
  • Watch out: don't use English order. Je n'ai mangé pas. → Je n'ai pas mangé.
  • Your turn: "We (girls) didn't go out."
    Show answer

    Nous ne sommes pas sorties.

Mission 3

One time avoir agrees

les: Higher

  • Normally an avoir participle never changes: J'ai pris la photo. – I took the photo.
  • But put a direct object pronoun (le, la, l', les) before avoir, and the participle agrees with it.
  • La photo ? Je l'ai prise. – The photo? I took it. (l' = la photo, feminine: add -e)
  • Le film ? Je l'ai vu. – The film? I saw it. (masculine singular: no change)
  • Les photos ? Je les ai prises. – The photos? I took them. (feminine plural: add -es)
  • Sometimes you can hear it: pris ("pree") → prise ("preez"). Often you can't (vu / vue), but you must write it.
  • Watch out: the participle agrees with the object in front, never with the subject. Elle a pris la photo. (object after: no agreement)
  • Your turn: La porte ? Il l'a ___ . ouvert or ouverte ?
    Show answer

    Il l'a ouverte. (l' = la porte, feminine)

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Perfect = present of avoir + past participle: j'ai joué = I played / I have played / I did play.
  • -er → -é, -ir → -i, -re → -u.
  • Negative: je n'ai pas joué.

Mission 2

  • Irregular participles still use avoir: j'ai vu, j'ai pris, j'ai fait, j'ai été.
  • Most end in -u (eu, vu, bu, lu, reçu) or -is / -it (pris, mis, dit, fait).
  • Modals: j'ai dû, j'ai pu, j'ai voulu + infinitive.
  • Higher: connu, écrit and the modal participles.

Mission 3

  • About 15 movement and change verbs, and all reflexive verbs, use être.
  • With être, the participle agrees: elle est allée, ils sont partis, elles se sont levées.
  • Negatives wrap the auxiliary: je n'ai pas mangé, elle n'est pas sortie; personne goes after the participle.
  • With avoir, the participle agrees only with a direct object pronoun in front: La photo ? Je l'ai prise.
