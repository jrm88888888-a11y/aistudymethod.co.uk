Mission 1

What earns the marks

Every exam graph needs:

1. Both axes labeled with the variable (Price, Quantity, Price level …), not a curve name.

2. Every curve labeled (D, S, MC, AD …).

3. Equilibrium marked: dotted lines to both axes, labeled (Pe and Qe, or P1 and Q1).

4. Every change shown: an arrow from old curve to new, with numbered labels (S1 → S2).

5. The new equilibrium labeled (P2, Q2), so the direction of each change is clear.

  • AP free-response scoring usually gives a point for a correctly labeled graph and separate points for showing the change and the new equilibrium. An unlabeled graph can lose every graph point.
  • Neat, large and simple beats artistic: straight lines are fine.

Mission 1

Build a demand and supply graph step by step

Example: a new technology cuts the cost of making solar panels.

1. Draw and label axes and curves D and S1. Mark P1 and Q1 with dotted lines.

2. Costs fall → supply increases: draw S2 to the right of S1, with an arrow from S1 to S2.

3. Mark the new equilibrium: P2 (lower) and Q2 (higher), with dotted lines.

  • Say: "an increase in supply" or "supply shifts right" — not: "supply moves down" or "the supply curve goes up".
  • Say: "an increase in quantity demanded" (a movement along D) — not: "demand increases".

Mission 1

Firm curves: the rules examiners mark

  • MC cuts AVC and ATC at their minimum points, from below. Draw AFC only if asked.
  • For a downward-sloping linear D (= AR), MR starts at the same point on the vertical axis and is twice as steep: it hits the horizontal axis halfway to D.
  • Profit-maximizing output: where MR = MC. Then go up to D to read the price.
  • Profit rectangle: (P − ATC) × Q. Its top edge is at P, its bottom at ATC at that output.
  • Perfect competition: the firm's D is horizontal at the market price.
  • AP: label it "P = MR = AR = D".
Mission 1

Worked example: areas on a graph

Illustrative data.

  • Profit rectangle: MR = MC at Q = 100. Price on D = 12. Average total cost at Q = 100 is 9.

1. Profit per unit = 12 − 9 = 3.

2. Total profit = 3 × 100 = 300.

  • Deadweight loss triangle from a tax of 4 per unit: quantity falls from 60 to 40.

1. Base = 60 − 40 = 20; height = the tax = 4.

2. DWL = ½ × 20 × 4 = 40.

  • Shade the area and label it ("profit", "DWL"). The triangle points toward the efficient quantity.

Mission 1

More micro graphs

  • Perfect competition: side by side. Market S and D set P; the firm's P = MR = AR = D is horizontal at that P. Long run: P = minimum ATC, zero economic profit.
  • Competitive labor market: the firm hires where MRP = MRC; for the firm, W = MRC = S is horizontal.
  • Monopsony: MRC lies above S. Hire where MRP = MRC; read the wage down from that quantity to S.
  • Externality (negative): MSC above MPC. Market output where MPC = MPB; socially optimal where MSC = MSB. Shade the DWL triangle pointing to the optimum.
  • Not drawn in AP Micro: quota graphs, Lorenz curves, collusion inefficiency graphs.

Mission 1

Common errors that cost marks

  • Axes labeled with curve names ("Demand", "Supply").
  • A shift drawn, but no arrow and no numbered labels.
  • Equilibrium marked by a dot only, with no dotted lines to the axes.
  • MR drawn above D, or MC not through the minimum of ATC.
  • Price read at the MR = MC height instead of from D.
  • DWL triangle shaded on the wrong side of the market quantity.
  • A graph that contradicts the written answer.
Mission 1

Command words and graphs

  • Draw / show: put it on the graph, with labels and arrows.
  • Explain: give the chain of reasoning in words ("costs fall → supply increases → price falls"). A graph alone is not enough.
  • Calculate: write the formula and each step. Give units.
  • AP: "show on your graph" means label the new curve and the new equilibrium. "Explain" needs the reason.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Label both axes (variables), every curve, the equilibrium (dotted lines) and every change (arrow, S1 → S2, P2, Q2).
  • Use the right axes for each graph.
  • Read monopoly price from D at MR = MC; MC cuts ATC (in your course: ATC) and AVC at their minimum.
  • Draw, then explain the chain in words.
