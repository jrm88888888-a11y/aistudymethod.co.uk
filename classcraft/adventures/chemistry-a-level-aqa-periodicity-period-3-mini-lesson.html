Mission 1

Mission 1: If the oceans are "acidifying", why is seawater still alkaline?

Make a guess: If the oceans are "acidifying", why is seawater still alkaline?

Mission 1

Sodium and magnesium with water

  • Sodium: fast. Floats, melts into a ball, fizzes. 2Na(s) + 2H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H2(g). Solution pH ≈ 13–14.
  • Magnesium with cold water: very slow. Mg(s) + 2H2O(l) → Mg(OH)2(s) + H2(g). Mg(OH)2 is sparingly soluble: pH ≈ 9–10.
  • Magnesium with steam: burns with a bright white light. Mg(s) + H2O(g) → MgO(s) + H2(g).
  • Sodium reacts faster: removing its one outer electron takes far less energy than removing magnesium's two.
Mission 1

Period 3 elements with oxygen

  • 4Na + O2 → 2Na2O: yellow-orange flame, white solid.
  • 2Mg + O2 → 2MgO: brilliant white flame, white solid.
  • 4Al + 3O2 → 2Al2O3: powder burns with a white flame, white solid.
  • Si + O2 → SiO2: when heated strongly, white solid.
  • P4 + 5O2 → P4O10: white phosphorus ignites in air, white flame, white smoke.
  • S + O2 → SO2: blue flame, choking colourless gas. 2SO2 + O2 ⇌ 2SO3 needs a V2O5 catalyst.
Mission 1

Metal oxides are basic

  • Group 1 and Group 2 metal oxides are basic: they react with acids to form a salt and water.
  • They are ionic: the O²⁻ ion takes H⁺. O²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H2O.
  • Soluble ones give alkaline solutions: O²⁻ + H2O → 2OH⁻.
  • Na2O(s) + H2O(l) → 2NaOH(aq). CaO(s) + H2O(l) → Ca(OH)2(aq).
  • MgO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl2(aq) + H2O(l).
Mission 1

Non-metal oxides are acidic

  • Non-metal oxides are covalent. Many react with water to form acids.
  • CO2(g) + H2O(l) ⇌ H2CO3(aq), carbonic acid, a weak acid.
  • SO2(g) + H2O(l) → H2SO3(aq), sulfurous acid.
  • SO3(g) + H2O(l) → H2SO4(aq), sulfuric acid, a strong acid.
  • They react with bases: CO2(g) + 2NaOH(aq) → Na2CO3(aq) + H2O(l).
  • Carbon monoxide is a neutral oxide: no reaction with water, acids or alkalis.

Mission 1

Across Period 3: basic → amphoteric → acidic

  • Na2O, MgO basic → Al2O3 amphoteric → SiO2, P4O10, SO2, SO3 acidic.
  • Amphoteric: reacts with both acids and bases.
  • Oxide character follows metallic character, which falls across a period.
  • pH with water (approximate): Na2O 14 · MgO 9 · Al2O3 7 · SiO2 7 · P4O10 0 · SO2 3 · SO3 0.
  • Ionic Na2O, MgO release O²⁻ → OH⁻. Al2O3 and SiO2 are insoluble: pH stays 7. Simple molecular P4O10, SO2, SO3 react to form acids.
Mission 1

Melting points of the oxides

  • Na2O, MgO: giant ionic. MgO higher: Mg²⁺ is smaller and 2+, so attraction to O²⁻ is stronger.
  • Al2O3: giant ionic with some covalent character (small, highly charged Al³⁺ polarises O²⁻) → high, but well below MgO (about 2070 °C against 2850 °C).
  • SiO2: giant covalent. Many strong covalent bonds to break → high m.p.
  • P4O10, SO3, SO2: simple molecular. Only intermolecular forces to overcome → low m.p.
  • P4O10 > SO3 > SO2: larger molecules → stronger London forces.
Mission 1

Equations with acids and bases

  • Basic: Na2O + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H2O · MgO + H2SO4 → MgSO4 + H2O.
  • Amphoteric: Al2O3 + 3H2SO4 → Al2(SO4)3 + 3H2O · Al2O3 + 2NaOH + 3H2O → 2NaAl(OH)4.
  • Acidic: SiO2 + 2NaOH → Na2SiO3 + H2O (hot, concentrated NaOH).
  • P4O10 + 12NaOH → 4Na3PO4 + 6H2O.
  • SO2 + 2NaOH → Na2SO3 + H2O · SO3 + 2NaOH → Na2SO4 + H2O.
  • SiO2 also reacts with basic CaO: CaO + SiO2 → CaSiO3.
Mission 1

Acids and anions from P4O10, SO2, SO3

  • P4O10 + 6H2O → 4H3PO4. H3PO4: P with one P=O and three P–OH; tetrahedral. Anion PO4³⁻.
  • SO2 + H2O → H2SO3. H2SO3: S with one S=O, two S–OH and a lone pair; pyramidal. Anion SO3²⁻.
  • SO3 + H2O → H2SO4. H2SO4: S with two S=O and two S–OH; tetrahedral. Anion SO4²⁻.
  • The acidic H atoms are those in O–H groups. Losing H⁺ leaves a negative charge delocalised over the O atoms.

Mission 1

Acid rain

  • Background: where acidic oxides in the air come from.
  • Rain is naturally slightly acidic, about pH 5.6, from dissolved CO2.
  • Acid rain, below 5.6, comes from sulfur oxides and nitrogen oxides.
  • SO2: sulfur impurities in coal and oil burn. In air, 2SO2 + O2 → 2SO3.
  • NO: N2 + O2 → 2NO in hot engines; then 2NO + O2 → 2NO2.
  • 2NO2(g) + H2O(l) → HNO3(aq) + HNO2(aq).
Mission 1

Ocean acidification

  • The oceans absorb part of the extra CO2 released by burning fuels.
  • CO2(aq) + H2O(l) ⇌ H2CO3(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + HCO3⁻(aq).
  • More H⁺, lower pH: average surface pH has fallen from about 8.2 to 8.1 since pre-industrial times.
  • Still alkaline: "acidification" means the pH is falling, not that it is below 7.
  • H⁺ also reacts with carbonate ions: H⁺ + CO3²⁻ → HCO3⁻. Corals and shellfish need CO3²⁻ to build CaCO3.
Mission check

?Sulfur dioxide is bubbled slowly into dilute sodium hydroxide solution. The pH falls from 13 to 11. Which statement is correct?

An acidic oxide lowers the pH by removing OH⁻, but the solution can stay alkaline. Seawater is the same: becoming less alkaline, not acidic.

Mission 2

Oxides and hydroxides: salt + water

  • In neutralisation, a base accepts the H⁺ from an acid.
  • Acid + metal hydroxide → salt + water: KOH + HNO3 → KNO3 + H2O.
  • Acid + metal oxide → salt + water: MgO + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2O.
  • Ionic: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H2O(l); for a solid oxide, MgO(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H2O(l).
  • A salt forms when the acid's H is replaced by a metal ion or another cation, such as NH4⁺.
Mission 2

Carbonates and hydrogencarbonates: CO2 too

  • Acid + carbonate or hydrogencarbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide.
  • CO3²⁻ accepts two H⁺; HCO3⁻ accepts one.
  • K2CO3 + 2HNO3 → 2KNO3 + H2O + CO2.
  • Ionic: CO3²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H2O + CO2. HCO3⁻ + H⁺ → H2O + CO2.
  • Observation: fizzing as CO2 escapes; a solid carbonate disappears.
Mission 2

Ammonia, amines; and metals

  • Ammonia or an amine + acid → a salt only; no water forms.
  • NH3 + HNO3 → NH4NO3. CH3CH2NH2 + HCl → CH3CH2NH3Cl.
  • The N lone pair takes the H⁺; there is no OH⁻ or O²⁻ to make water.
  • Metal + acid → salt + hydrogen: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2. That is redox, not neutralisation.
  • Ionic: Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H2(g).
Quick check

Mission 2

Period 3 oxides with acids and bases

  • Basic oxides react with acids: Na2O + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H2O; MgO + 2HCl → MgCl2 + H2O.
  • Amphoteric Al2O3 reacts with both:
  • Al2O3 + 6HCl → 2AlCl3 + 3H2O and Al2O3 + 2NaOH + 3H2O → 2NaAl(OH)4.
  • Acidic oxides react with bases: SiO2 + 2NaOH → Na2SiO3 + H2O.
  • P4O10 + 12NaOH → 4Na3PO4 + 6H2O. SO2 + 2NaOH → Na2SO3 + H2O. SO3 + 2NaOH → Na2SO4 + H2O.
Mission 2

The parent acid and base of a salt

  • A salt's anion comes from its parent acid; its cation from its parent base.
  • KNO3: parent acid HNO3, parent base KOH.
  • (NH4)2SO4: parent acid H2SO4, parent base NH3.
  • HCOOK (potassium methanoate): parent acid HCOOH, parent base KOH.
  • Parents are the neutral acid and base, not the ions in the salt.

Mission 2

The pH curve: strong acid, strong base

  • A preview: pH curves get a full mission later.
  • Flask: 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Burette: 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH.
  • Starts at the flask's pH, 1.00, and rises slowly.
  • Close to 25.0 cm³, pH jumps from about 3 to about 11.
  • Equivalence point: amounts match the equation; here pH 7 (at 298 K, 25 °C), mid-jump.
  • After it, excess NaOH builds up: pH rises slowly towards 13 (about 12.5 at double the volume).
Mission check

?Baking powder contains sodium hydrogencarbonate and a solid acid. When the cake batter gets wet, the two react. Why does the cake rise?

Antacid tablets with CaCO3 do the same: CaCO3 + 2HCl → CaCl2 + H2O + CO2. The CO2 gas is what you burp up.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Metal oxides (ionic) are basic; non-metal oxides (covalent) are acidic; Al2O3 is amphoteric.
  • Across Period 3: pH 14 → 0; m.p. follows structure: giant ionic → giant covalent → simple molecular.
  • Acidification = pH falling. Seawater is still alkaline.

Mission 2

  • Oxides and hydroxides: salt + water. Carbonates: salt + water + CO2. Ammonia and amines: salt only.
  • Metal + acid gives H2, but that is redox.
