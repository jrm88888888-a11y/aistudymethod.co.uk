Mission 1

Rise 1 for every 12 along: which of three ramps is legal?

Make a guess: Rise 1 for every 12 along: which of three ramps is legal?

Algebra · straight-line graphs

Gradient measures steepness

  • Gradient = change in yy ÷ change in xx ("rise over run").
m=x2​−x1​y2​−y1​​m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)
  • Uphill from left to right: positive. Downhill: negative. Horizontal: 0.
  • The bigger the gradient (ignoring its sign), the steeper the line.
  • "Rise 1 for every 12 along" is a gradient of 121​1/12.
  • Core: find the gradient by counting squares on a grid. Extended: also calculate it from two coordinates, as in the worked example below.
Algebra · straight-line graphs

The equation y=mx+cy = mx + c

  • In y=mx+cy = mx + c, mm is the gradient and cc is the yy-intercept: the line crosses the yy-axis at (0,c)(0, c).
  • Rearrange to y=…y = … first: 2y=6x−42y = 6x − 4 gives y=3x−2y = 3x − 2, so m=3m = 3 and c=−2c = −2.
  • To plot: make a table of values (three or more xx values), plot the points, rule the line.
  • y=ky = k is a horizontal line; x=kx = k is a vertical line.
  • Extended also uses forms such as 3x+2y=53x + 2y = 5, which rearranges to y=−1.5x+2.5y = −1.5x + 2.5.

Algebra · straight-line graphs

Parallel and perpendicular lines

  • Parallel lines have equal gradients. The line parallel to y=4x−1y = 4x − 1 through (1,−3)(1, −3): −3=4×1+c−3 = 4 × 1 + c, so y=4x−7y = 4x − 7.
  • Extended: perpendicular lines have gradients that multiply to −1−1. Use the negative reciprocal: gradient 2 becomes −21​−1/2.
  • Extended: 2y=3x+12y = 3x + 1 has gradient 23​3/2, so a perpendicular line has gradient −32​−2/3.

Midpoint and length of a line segment

  • Extended content.
  • The midpoint averages the coordinates:
(2x1​+x2​​,2y1​+y2​​)((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)
  • The length comes from Pythagoras: (x2​−x1​)2+(y2​−y1​)2​√((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²).
  • For A(1,2)A(1, 2) and B(7,10)B(7, 10): the midpoint is (4,6)(4, 6) and the length is 62+82​=10√(6² + 8²) = 10.

Mission check

?The rule: no steeper than a rise of 1 for every 12 along. Ramp P rises 0.6 m over 9 m, ramp Q is the line y=0.1xy = 0.1x, ramp R rises 0.25 m over 3 m. Which ramps are legal?

Steepness is measured by gradient, rise ÷ run. The rule allows a gradient up to 121​≈0.0831/12 ≈ 0.083: ramp P (about 0.067) and ramp R (exactly 121​1/12) pass, but ramp Q, with m=0.1m = 0.1 in y=0.1xy = 0.1x, is too steep.

Mission 2

Where can a mast stand that is equally far from two villages?

Coordinate geometry · lines and bisectors

Drawing a line from its equation

  • Make a table of values: choose three xx values, work out yy, plot the points and rule a line through them. The third point is a check.
  • For y=−3x+2y = −3x + 2: x=−1,0,2x = −1, 0, 2 gives y=5,2,−4y = 5, 2, −4.
  • x=kx = k is a vertical line; y=ky = k is a horizontal line.
  • Extended: for ax+by=cax + by = c, put x=0x = 0, then y=0y = 0, to find where the line crosses the axes. 3x+4y=123x + 4y = 12 crosses at (0,3)(0, 3) and (4,0)(4, 0).

Coordinate geometry · lines and bisectors

Lines in the form ax+by=cax + by = c (Extended)

  • To find the gradient and yy-intercept, make yy the subject: 5x+4y=85x + 4y = 8 gives 4y=−5x+84y = −5x + 8, so y=−1.25x+2y = −1.25x + 2.
  • The gradient is −1.25−1.25 and the yy-intercept is (0,2)(0, 2).
  • To go the other way, clear fractions, then collect xx and yy on one side: y=32​x−4y = 2/3x − 4 gives 3y=2x−123y = 2x − 12, so 2x−3y=122x − 3y = 12.
  • Give equations fully simplified: 4x+6y=104x + 6y = 10 becomes 2x+3y=52x + 3y = 5.
Calculate

Worked example Coordinate geometry · lines and bisectors

Equation in the form ax+by=cax + by = c (Extended)

Question
Find the equation of the line through (−2,5)(−2, 5) and (4,1)(4, 1). Give your answer in the form ax+by=cax + by = c, where aa, bb and cc are integers.
  1. 1
    Find the gradient: m=4−(−2)1−5​=6−4​=−32​m = (1 − 5)/(4 − (−2)) = (−4)/6 = −2/3
  2. 2
    Substitute (4,1)(4, 1) into y=−32​x+cy = −2/3x + c: 1=−38​+c1 = −8/3 + c, so c=311​c = 11/3
  3. 3
    Multiply every term by 3: 3y=−2x+113y = −2x + 11
  4. 4
    Collect xx and yy on one side: 2x+3y=112x + 3y = 11
  5. 5
    Check with (−2,5)(−2, 5): −4+15=11−4 + 15 = 11 ✓
Answer
2x+3y=112x + 3y = 11
⚠️ Watch out
multiply every term by 3, including cc. Writing 3y=−2x+311​3y = −2x + 11/3 is a common slip.
Coordinate geometry · lines and bisectors

The perpendicular bisector (Extended)

  • The perpendicular bisector of ABAB cuts ABAB in half at right angles.
  • Every point on it is the same distance from AA as from BB.
  • To find its equation, first find the midpoint MM of ABAB.
  • Next, find the gradient of ABAB, then the perpendicular gradient (the negative reciprocal).
  • Finally, substitute MM into y=mx+cy = mx + c with that gradient to find cc.

Mission check

?(Extended) Villages stand at A(1,2)A(1, 2) and B(7,6)B(7, 6). Where can a mast go so that it is exactly as far from AA as from BB?

The mast can go anywhere on the perpendicular bisector of ABAB, not just at the halfway point. It passes through the midpoint (4,4)(4, 4) with gradient −23​−3/2, the negative reciprocal of ABAB's gradient 32​2/3, so its equation is 3x+2y=203x + 2y = 20.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Gradient = change in yy ÷ change in xx; in y=mx+cy = mx + c, mm is the gradient and (0,c)(0, c) the yy-intercept.
  • To find a line's equation: gradient first, then substitute a point to find cc.
  • Parallel lines share a gradient; perpendicular gradients multiply to −1−1 (Extended).
  • Midpoint: average the coordinates. Length: Pythagoras on the changes in xx and yy.
  • Draw a line from a table of values (three points); for ax+by=cax + by = c, find where it crosses the axes (Extended).
  • To read the gradient and yy-intercept of ax+by=cax + by = c, make yy the subject first (Extended).
  • Perpendicular bisector of ABAB (Extended): through the midpoint, with the negative reciprocal of ABAB's gradient. Every point on it is equally far from AA and BB.
