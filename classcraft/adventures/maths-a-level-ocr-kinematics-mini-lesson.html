Mechanics · constant acceleration

The language and the graphs

  • Displacement and velocity have a direction (they can be negative). Distance travelled and speed are their sizes, and are never negative.
  • On a displacement–time graph, the gradient is the velocity.
  • On a velocity–time graph, the gradient is the acceleration and the area between the graph and the time axis is the displacement.
  • Area below the time axis counts as negative displacement. For distance travelled, add the sizes of the areas.

Reading a velocity–time graph

Question
A car starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to 12 m/s in 4 s. It travels at 12 m/s for 10 s, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 6 s. Find its acceleration in the first 4 s and the total distance travelled.
  1. 1
    Gradient of the first section: a=412−0​=3a = (12 − 0)/4 = 3 m s−2⁻²
  2. 2
    Split the area into a triangle, a rectangle and a triangle: 21​(4)(12)+10(12)+21​(6)(12)1/2(4)(12) + 10(12) + 1/2(6)(12)
  3. 3
    Add: 24+120+36=18024 + 120 + 36 = 180
Answer
acceleration 3 m s−2⁻²; distance 180 m
⚠️ Watch out
the deceleration is the gradient of the last section, −2−2 m s−2⁻². Do not use 12 m/s for the whole 20 s (that gives 240 m).

Mechanics · constant acceleration

The constant acceleration formulae

  • On a straight-line velocity–time graph from uu to vv in time tt: gradient a=tv−u​a = (v − u)/t gives v=u+atv = u + at, and the trapezium area gives s=21​(u+v)ts = 1/2(u + v)t.
  • Substituting one into another gives the five suvat formulae:
v=u+ats=21​(u+v)ts=ut+21​at2s=vt−21​at2v2=u2+2asv = u + at s = 1/2(u + v)t s = ut + 1/2at² s = vt − 1/2at² v² = u² + 2as
  • Each formula leaves out one of s,u,v,a,ts, u, v, a, t. List the three you know and the one you want; use the formula without the fifth.
  • They are valid only when the acceleration is constant.
  • You may be asked to derive them.

Mechanics · constant acceleration

Vertical motion under gravity

  • Modelled as a particle with no air resistance, a body moving freely up or down has constant acceleration gg downwards.
  • Use g=9.8g = 9.8 m s−2⁻² unless told otherwise. OCR assumes 9.8 unless a question gives another value. gg is not a universal constant: it depends on location.
  • Choose a positive direction and stick to it. With up positive, a=−9.8a = −9.8 and a point below the start has negative ss.
  • At the greatest height, v=0v = 0.

Mechanics · variable acceleration

Differentiate to go from displacement to acceleration

  • When acceleration changes with time, suvat does not apply. Use calculus.
v=dtds​a=dtdv​=dt2d2s​v = ds/dt a = dv/dt = d²s/dt²
  • Instantaneous rest: v=0v = 0. Greatest or least velocity: a=0a = 0, as for any stationary point.

When is the particle at rest?

Question
A particle moves in a straight line. At time tt seconds its displacement from OO is s=t3−6t2+9ts = t³ − 6t² + 9t metres, t≥0t ≥ 0. Find the times when it is at rest and its acceleration at each of those times.

    Differentiate: v=3t2−12t+9v = 3t² − 12t + 9
  2. 2
    Set v=0v = 0 and factorise: 3(t−1)(t−3)=03(t − 1)(t − 3) = 0, so t=1t = 1 or t=3t = 3
  3. 3
    Differentiate again: a=6t−12a = 6t − 12
  4. 4
    Substitute: at t=1t = 1, a=−6a = −6; at t=3t = 3, a=6a = 6

at rest when t=1t = 1 s (a=−6a = −6 m s−2⁻²) and t=3t = 3 s (a=6a = 6 m s−2⁻²)

"at rest" means v=0v = 0, not s=0s = 0. Here s=0s = 0 at t=3t = 3 only by coincidence.

Integrate to go back up

  • Reverse the chain by integrating: v=∫adtv = ∫ a dt and s=∫vdts = ∫ v dt.
  • Each integral gives a constant. Find it from the initial conditions (for example "starts from rest at OO" means v=0v = 0 and s=0s = 0 when t=0t = 0).
  • Displacement between two times is ∫t1​t2​​vdt∫_t₁^t₂ v dt.
  • Distance travelled: find where v=0v = 0, integrate each part separately, then add the sizes. Area below the axis cancels area above if you don't.

Calculus with vectors (A-level only)

  • In two dimensions, position is a vector: r=f(t)i+g(t)jr = f(t)i + g(t)j.
  • Differentiate or integrate each component separately: v=r˙=f′(t)i+g′(t)jv = r = f'(t)i + g'(t)j, a=v˙a = v.
  • When integrating, the constant is a vector, such as c=pi+qjc = pi + qj.
  • Speed is the magnitude of velocity: ∣v∣=v12​+v22​​|v| = √(v₁² + v₂²). Column vectors work the same way.

Constant acceleration with vectors (A-level only)

  • With constant acceleration aa in two dimensions, the suvat formulae hold with vectors:
v=u+atr=ut+21​at2r=21​(u+v)tr=vt−21​at2v = u + at r = ut + 1/2at² r = 1/2(u + v)t r = vt − 1/2at²

Mechanics · projectiles

Time of flight, greatest height and range (A-level only)

  • Launch at speed UU, angle αα above the horizontal: components UcosαUcos α (horizontal, constant) and UsinαUsin α (vertical, acceleration −g−g).
  • Greatest height: vertical velocity is 0, so H=2gU2sin2α​H = U²sin²α/2g. The speed there is UcosαUcos α, not 0.
  • Time of flight on level ground: y=0y = 0 again, so T=g2Usinα​T = (2Usin α)/g.
  • Range: R=Ucosα×T=gU2sin2α​R = Ucos α × T = (U²sin 2α)/g, greatest when sin2α=1sin 2α = 1, so α=45∘α = 45°.
  • You may have to derive these. Otherwise, work from the components.

Mission check

?What angle throws a ball furthest, and why isn't the real answer that?

On level ground the range is gU2sin2α​(U²sin 2α)/g, greatest at α=45∘α = 45°. Real throws start above the ground, and from there a slightly lower angle goes further (10 m/s from 2 m: 12.0 m at 40∘40°, 11.9 m at 45∘45°). Air resistance, which the model ignores, also changes the best angle.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Velocity–time graph: gradient is acceleration, area is displacement.
  • For constant acceleration, list s,u,v,a,ts, u, v, a, t and choose the suvat formula that leaves out the one you don't need.
  • Free fall: a=g=9.8a = g = 9.8 m s−2⁻² downwards; fix a positive direction and give every quantity its sign.
  • Down the chain differentiate: s→v→as → v → a. Up the chain integrate, and find each constant from the initial conditions.
  • At rest: v=0v = 0. Greatest or least velocity: a=0a = 0. Distance travelled: split the integral where vv changes sign.
  • With vectors, work on each component; speed is ∣v∣|v|.
  • Split the motion: horizontal velocity UcosαUcos α stays constant; vertical motion has acceleration gg downwards.
  • Time links the two: find tt from the vertical motion, then use it horizontally.
  • Range gU2sin2α​(U²sin 2α)/g is greatest at 45∘45° only on level ground in the no-air-resistance model.
