Mission 1

Mission 1: Why does a passing siren drop in pitch, and what can that shift tell us about speed?

Make a guess: Why does a passing siren drop in pitch, and what can that shift tell us about speed?

Quick check
?Predict first. Imagine a wavefront diagram of the sound from a source moving at constant speed through still air towards a stationary observer X. Each wavefront is drawn as a circle centred on the point where the source was when that wavefront was emitted. Compared with the waves the same source emits when it is at rest, how do the waves reaching X differ?
Mission 1

A moving source

  • A source emits wavefronts at a steady frequency. Each spreads out from where the source was when it was emitted.
  • Moving source: wavefronts bunched ahead (shorter λ), spread out behind (longer λ).
  • Wave speed through the air is unchanged. So ahead: higher f; behind: lower f.
  • Approaching: higher pitch. Receding: lower pitch. Constant velocity → constant (shifted) pitch.
  • Faster relative motion → bigger shift. Same velocity as the listener → no shift.
Mission 1

A moving observer

  • Source still: wavefronts are evenly spaced circles. λ unchanged.
  • Observer moving towards the source meets wavefronts more often → higher f.
  • Moving away → meets them less often → lower f.
  • What matters is the relative velocity of source and observer.
Quick check
?Predict first. Every absorption line in the spectrum of a star in our galaxy is at a slightly longer wavelength than the same line measured in the laboratory. What does this show about the star?
Mission 1

Doppler shift of light

  • Same effect for light and all EM waves.
  • Receding source: λ longer → redshift. Approaching: λ shorter → blueshift.
  • For v ≪ c:
  • Δf/f = Δλ/λ ≈ v/c
  • v is the radial velocity: the part along the line of sight.
Mission 1

Worked example

  • A hydrogen line: 486.1 nm in the lab, 487.6 nm in a star's spectrum.
  • Δλ = 487.6 − 486.1 = 1.5 nm. Longer → moving away.
  • v = c × Δλ/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 1.5 ÷ 486.1 = 9.3 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹ (about 0.3% of c).
  • v ≪ c, so the approximation is fine.
Quick check
?A spectral line of hydrogen has a wavelength of 656.3 nm when measured in the laboratory. A galaxy is moving directly away from Earth at 3.00 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹. Take c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. At what wavelength is this line observed in the spectrum of the galaxy?
Mission 1

Stars that wobble

  • A star with a companion (another star, or a planet) orbits their common centre of mass.
  • Its line-of-sight velocity swings: towards us, away, towards…
  • Its spectral lines shift blue, red, blue with the orbital period.
  • Plot radial velocity against time: a radial-velocity curve (a sine wave for a circular orbit).
Mission 1

Ultrasound and radar

  • A probe sends ultrasound into an artery. Moving blood cells reflect it back, shifted.
  • Shifted twice: the cells receive it as moving observers, then re-emit it as moving sources.
  • Radar speed guns work the same way with microwaves: a higher reflected frequency means the target approaches.
Mission 1

Sound: how big is the shift?

  • v = wave speed in the air; source moves at us; observer at uo.
  • Moving source: f′ = f v/(v ± us). Use − when approaching, + when receding.
  • Moving observer: f′ = f (v ± uo)/v. Use + when approaching, − when receding.
  • Example: siren 700 Hz, source 25 m s⁻¹, v = 340 m s⁻¹.
  • Approaching: f′ = 700 × 340 ÷ 315 = 756 Hz. Receding: 700 × 340 ÷ 365 = 652 Hz.
Quick check
?A test vehicle moves at constant velocity along a straight track and carries a siren that emits sound of frequency 500 Hz. A stationary microphone on the line of the track, beyond one end of it, detects the sound at 450 Hz. The speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹. Taking velocity away from the microphone as positive, what is the velocity of the vehicle?
Mission check

?A calcium absorption line has a wavelength of 393.4 nm in the laboratory. In the spectrum of a distant galaxy the same line is at 401.3 nm. Take c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. What is the galaxy's velocity relative to Earth?

Approaching: waves bunched, higher f; receding: stretched, lower f. The size of the shift grows with the relative speed, so measuring it measures the speed.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Approaching → higher f, shorter λ; receding → lower f, longer λ; bigger relative speed → bigger shift.
  • Light, v ≪ c: Δλ/λ ≈ v/c; redshift means moving away.
  • Sound: f′ = f v/(v ± us) (moving source); f′ = f (v ± uo)/v (moving observer).
