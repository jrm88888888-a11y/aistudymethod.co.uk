Pronouns in depth

The two object sets

Direct vs indirect objects

A direct object pronoun replaces a noun with no preposition; an indirect object replaces a noun introduced by à (to someone). The two sets overlap but split at the third person:

  • Direct: me / te / le / la / nous / vous / les — Je le vois ("I see him/it").
  • Indirect: me / te / lui / nous / vous / leur — Je lui parle ("I speak to him/her").

The split: third-person direct = le / la / les; third-person indirect = lui / leur. Get that pair right and you're most of the way there.

The two little adverbs

y and en

Two special pronouns replace whole phrases:

  • y replaces à + a thing or a place: Je pense à mon travail → j'y pense; Je vais à Paris → j'y vais ("I go there").
  • en replaces de + a thing, or a quantity: Je parle de mon projet → j'en parle; J'ai des pommes → j'en ai ("I have some").

Memory hook: y ↔ à (to it / there); en ↔ de (of it / some).

Position · Step 1

Where the pronoun goes

Object pronouns sit directly before the verb they belong to — before the auxiliary in a compound tense, and before the infinitive if one governs them:

  • Je le vois. — I see him. (before the verb)
  • Je l'ai vu. — I saw him. (before the auxiliary)
  • Je vais le voir. — I'm going to see him. (before the infinitive)
  • Il m'en a parlé. — He spoke to me about it.

Exception (imperative affirmative): in a positive command the pronouns follow the verb and are joined by hyphens — more on that shortly.

Position · Step 2

The order of multiple pronouns

When two pronouns stack up before the verb, they line up in a fixed order:

me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur → y → enje le lui donne · il m'en a parlé
  • Je le lui donne. — I give it to him. (le before lui)
  • Il m'en a parlé. — He spoke to me about it. (me before en)

Watch the third person: the direct le/la/les comes before the indirect lui/leur — the opposite way round from what English word order suggests.

Position · the imperative

Affirmative commands

In a positive command the pronouns move to after the verb, joined by hyphens, and me / te become moi / toi:

  • Donne-le-moi ! — Give it to me!
  • Dis-le-lui ! — Tell it to him!

But negative commands revert: the pronouns go back before the verb — Ne me le donne pas. ("Don't give it to me.")

Linking clauses

The relative pronouns

Relative pronouns join a second clause onto a noun. The choice depends on the pronoun's job in that clause:

  • qui — subject (who / which): l'homme qui parle.
  • que — object (that / which): le livre que je lis.
  • dont — of which / whose (replaces de): le livre dont j'ai besoin.
  • où — where / when: la ville où je vis.
  • lequel / laquelle — which (after a preposition): la table sur laquelle….
  • ce qui / ce que — what (with no named noun): ce que je veux.

qui vs que: if the relative is doing the verb → qui; if it is receiving it → que. Que elides to qu' before a vowel; qui never does.

The one that hides a preposition

dont — "of which / whose"

Use dont whenever the missing link is de — after verbs like avoir besoin de, parler de, or to mean "whose":

  • Le livre dont j'ai besoin. — The book (that) I need. (avoir besoin de)
  • La femme dont je parle. — The woman I'm talking about. (parler de)
  • L'auteur dont le livre est célèbre. — The author whose book is famous.

The test: if you could rebuild the clause with de (j'ai besoin de ce livre), the relative is dont.

Recap

Your pronoun toolkit

Direct: me/te/le/la/nous/vous/les. Indirect: me/te/lui/nous/vous/leur.

y / en: y = à + thing/place; en = de + thing / a quantity.

Order before the verb: me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur → y → en. Positive imperative: pronouns follow, hyphenated (donne-le-moi).

Relatives: qui (subject), que (object), dont (of which/whose), où (where/when), lequel (after prep), ce qui/ce que (what).
