The le→se rule

When two l-pronouns collide

Spanish avoids two l-pronouns in a row. When le or les is immediately followed by lo / la / los / las, the indirect pronoun changes to se:

would bebecomes
le lo (✗)se lo
le la (✗)se la
les los (✗)se los
les las (✗)se las

Example: Le doy el libro → Se lo doy ("I give it to him"). Never le lo doy.

Double-pronoun order

Indirect before direct — always

When two object pronouns appear together, the order is fixed: indirect first, direct second. A handy mnemonic is "ID" (Indirect, Direct) or "people before things":

  • Te lo doy. = "I give it to you." (te = indirect, lo = direct)
  • Me la da. = "She gives it to me." (me = indirect, la = direct)
  • Nos los muestra. = "He shows them to us."

Reflexive counts too: a reflexive pronoun also comes before the direct object — Se lo pone ("He puts it on").

Position of pronouns

Before the verb — or attached to it

Object pronouns sit before the conjugated verb. But they attach to the end of an infinitive, a gerund, or an affirmative command (adding an accent to keep the stress):

  • Before conjugated verb: Te lo doy. ("I give it to you.")
  • Attached to infinitive: Voy a dártelo. — dármelo = "to give it to me".
  • Attached to gerund: Estoy dándomelo. — dándomelo = "giving it to me".
  • Attached to affirmative command: ¡Dámelo! = "Give it to me!"

The accent is compulsory: once two pronouns attach (dármelo, dándomelo, dámelo), a written accent marks the original stressed syllable.

Relative pronouns

The connectors that build long sentences

Relative pronouns link a clause back to a noun. Getting these right is a fast route to IB-level sentence complexity:

lo que & the personal 'a'

"what", and the little 'a' before a person

Two more essentials. Lo que means "what / that which" (a neuter idea, not a specific noun). And Spanish inserts a personal a before a direct object that is a specific person:

  • Lo que quiero es dormir. = "What I want is to sleep."
  • No entiendo lo que dices. = "I don't understand what you're saying."
  • Veo a María. = "I see María." (personal a before a person)
  • Veo la casa. = "I see the house." (no a — it's a thing).

Contrast: use lo que (not que alone) when "what" refers to a whole idea rather than a named noun.

Recap

Your pronoun toolkit

Direct object: me, te, lo, la, nos, os, los, las. Indirect object: me, te, le, nos, os, les.

le→se rule: before lo/la/los/las, le/les becomes se (se lo di, never le lo di).

Order & position: indirect before direct (te lo doy); before a conjugated verb, but attached to an infinitive / gerund / affirmative command (dármelo, dándomelo, dámelo).

Relative pronouns: que (that/which/who), quien (who), el que / el cual (which), cuyo (whose, agrees), lo que (what), donde (where).

Personal 'a': before a specific person object — Veo a María.
