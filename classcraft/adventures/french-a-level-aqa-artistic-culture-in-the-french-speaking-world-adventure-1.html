Mission 1: France's Constitution calls regional languages heritage. Why can't state schools teach mostly in them?

Make a guess: France's Constitution calls regional languages heritage. Why can't state schools teach mostly in them?

Mission 1

What counts as le patrimoine?

  • Le patrimoine – heritage: what a community receives from the past and wants to pass on.
  • Three levels:
  • national: Versailles, Notre-Dame de Paris, the Mont-Saint-Michel;
  • regional: Breton or Basque culture, Alsatian timber-framed houses, the megaliths of Carnac;
  • local: a village church, a market, a mill, a local festival.
  • Two kinds:
  • material (matériel): buildings, sites, objects;
  • intangible (immatériel): know-how, traditions, languages, cuisine.
  • France has 56 sites on UNESCO's World Heritage List. Newest: Carnac (2025); the Normandy landing beaches and the royal fortresses of Languedoc (July 2026).
  • un monument historique – a listed building · transmettre – to pass on · un savoir-faire – know-how
Mission 1

Heritage reflects a culture

  • Notre-Dame de Paris: fire on 15 April 2019; restored and reopened in December 2024. Its rebuilding showed how much a monument can mean to a nation.
  • Intangible heritage (UNESCO): le repas gastronomique des Français (2010); the craft and culture of the baguette (2022).
  • Carnac (Brittany): thousands of standing stones, listed in 2025.
  • In the French-speaking world:
  • Île de Gorée (Senegal, listed 1978): a memorial to the Atlantic slave trade.
  • Old Québec (listed 1985): the historic French-speaking city centre.
  • Heritage can celebrate a culture, or remind it of a painful past.
  • une cathédrale · la reconstruction · un lieu de mémoire – a place of remembrance
Quick check
?Which of these is on UNESCO's list of France's intangible heritage (le patrimoine immatériel)?
Mission 1

Who protects heritage?

  • The state: the Ministry of Culture lists and protects monuments historiques.
  • Les Journées européennes du patrimoine (since 1984, every September): thousands of buildings, usually closed, open to the public.
  • Le Loto du patrimoine (since 2018): a lottery that funds the restoration of threatened local monuments.
  • Volunteers and associations: restoring churches, castles, mills.
  • Museums: from the Louvre to small local musées, they keep and explain the past.
  • restaurer – to restore · entretenir – to maintain, look after · un(e) bénévole – a volunteer · menacé(e) – under threat
Mission 1

Heritage and tourism

  • France had about 102 million international visitors in 2025, a record. It is the world's most visited country.
  • Benefits: jobs, money for upkeep, pride, a reason to keep traditions alive.
  • Problems: le surtourisme – overtourism: crowds, damage, rising rents, locals feeling pushed out.
  • Some sites now limit visitor numbers or require booking in high season.
  • un(e) touriste · attirer – to attract · rapporter de l'argent – to bring in money · un site classé – a protected site · préserver – to preserve
  • Your turn: "Tourism brings in money but threatens fragile sites."
    Show answer

    Le tourisme rapporte de l'argent mais menace les sites fragiles.

Quick check
?Grâce au tourisme, de nombreux monuments peuvent être entretenus. What does this mean?
Mission 1

Regional languages

  • In mainland France: Breton, Basque, Catalan, Corsican, Occitan, Alsatian, Flemish, Franco-Provençal.
  • Overseas: Creoles (Caribbean, Guiana, Réunion), Kanak languages, Tahitian.
  • 1539: the ordinance of Villers-Cotterêts makes French the language of law and administration.
  • Late 19th century: free compulsory schooling in French only; in many schools pupils were punished for speaking their regional language.
  • 1999: France signed the European charter on regional languages but has never ratified it.
  • 2008: the Constitution (article 75-1): « Les langues régionales appartiennent au patrimoine de la France. »
  • But article 2: « La langue de la République est le français. »
  • un locuteur / une locutrice – a speaker · une langue minoritaire · bilingue · transmettre une langue
Mission 1

The 2021 law and the immersion debate

  • L'enseignement immersif – immersion teaching: most lessons and school life in the regional language.
  • Associative schools such as Diwan (Breton, since 1977) teach this way.
  • 2021, loi Molac: a law to protect and promote regional languages.
  • The Conseil constitutionnel (21 May 2021) struck out two articles, citing article 2:
  • immersion teaching in state schools;
  • regional spelling marks in civil records, e.g. the ñ in the Breton first name Fañch.
  • Still in force: towns must help fund regional-language schools that pupils attend outside their town.
  • So: regional languages can be taught, and bilingual classes exist, but state schools cannot teach mainly in them.
Mission 1

Argue both sides

  • Should regional languages get a bigger place?
  • Yes (arguments supporters make):
  • Une langue qui disparaît, c'est une culture qui disparaît. – When a language dies, a culture dies.
  • L'immersion est le meilleur moyen de former de nouveaux locuteurs. – Immersion is the best way to create new speakers.
  • No, or with limits (arguments supporters make):
  • Le français est le ciment de l'unité nationale. – French holds the nation together.
  • Les élèves doivent d'abord maîtriser le français. – Pupils must master French first.
  • Linkers: Pour les défenseurs des langues régionales, … · Leurs opposants craignent que + subjunctive · Reste à savoir si …
  • Your turn: "Some fear that French may lose its place."
    Show answer

    Certains craignent que le français perde sa place.

Quick check
?Choose the correct French for: "Regional languages are taught in some schools."

  • le patrimoine (matériel / immatériel) · un monument historique · restaurer · entretenir · transmettre · un savoir-faire · le surtourisme · une langue régionale · un locuteur / une locutrice · bilingue · l'enseignement immersif · menacé(e)
  • Le patrimoine ne se limite pas aux monuments : il comprend aussi des savoir-faire et des langues.
  • Chaque année, de nombreux petits monuments sont restaurés grâce au Loto du patrimoine. (present passive)

Mission 2: Why must French radio stations play a set share of songs in French, and does it still matter?

Make a guess: Why must French radio stations play a set share of songs in French, and does it still matter?

Mission 2

One language, many musics

  • La musique francophone: music in French, or by artists from French-speaking countries and communities.
  • France: chanson, rap, electronic music (la French touch).
  • Belgium: Stromae, Angèle. Québec: Céline Dion.
  • Caribbean: zouk (Kassav', from Guadeloupe and Martinique).
  • West and Central Africa: mbalax (Youssou N'Dour, Senegal), zouglou and coupé-décalé (Côte d'Ivoire), Congolese rumba, on UNESCO's intangible heritage list since 2021.
  • Many artists mix French with other languages: Wolof, Lingala, Creole, Arabic, English.
  • un genre musical · un(e) artiste · un tube – a hit · mêler – to mix
Mission 2

Changes and developments

  • 1940s–60s: la chanson à texte: the words matter most (Édith Piaf, Georges Brassens, Jacques Brel).
  • 1960s: les yé-yé: pop for teenagers, inspired by British and American rock (Johnny Hallyday, Françoise Hardy).
  • 1980s–90s: rap arrives and grows fast (MC Solaar, IAM, NTM).
  • Late 1990s: la French touch: French electronic music known worldwide (Daft Punk, Air).
  • 2010s–today: streaming; rap becomes the dominant genre; more women at the top (Aya Nakamura, Angèle).
  • évoluer – to evolve · s'inspirer de – to draw on · un courant – a movement
Quick check
?Which statement about francophone music is accurate?
Mission 2

Who listens, and how?

  • France's recorded music market: €1.07 billion in 2025, growing for ten years (SNEP).
  • Streaming is now the main way to listen: about 165 billion streams in 2025.
  • Vinyl is back: almost 6 million records sold in 2025.
  • French music is strong at home: 16 of the 20 best-selling albums of 2025 were French productions.
  • Young listeners: mainly streaming and short videos. Older listeners: more radio and CDs.
  • écouter en streaming · un abonnement – a subscription · télécharger – to download · un auditeur / une auditrice – a listener · le vinyle
Mission 2

Music's impact on popular culture

  • Language: rap spreads new words and expressions into everyday speech.
  • Fashion and image: streetwear, music videos (les clips), artists as brands.
  • National moments: at the Paris 2024 Olympic opening ceremony, Aya Nakamura (born in Mali) performed with the band of the Garde républicaine, and Céline Dion sang Édith Piaf's L'Hymne à l'amour from the Eiffel Tower.
  • La Fête de la musique (21 June, since 1982): free concerts in the streets, now copied in many countries.
  • Debates: who represents France? Should a national event reflect today's popular music?
  • un chanteur / une chanteuse · se produire – to perform · rassembler – to bring together
Quick check
?Les radios doivent diffuser au moins 40 % de chansons d'expression française. What does this mean?
Mission 2

How to safeguard francophone music

  • Radio quotas (law of 1994, in force 1996): music radio must play at least 40% French-language songs, half of them by new talents or new releases.
  • Quotas apply to radio, not to streaming platforms.
  • Le Centre national de la musique (CNM, 2020): public body that supports artists, venues and producers.
  • Festivals and prizes: Les Francofolies (La Rochelle, since 1985), les Victoires de la musique (since 1985).
  • Schools and local venues: music education, salles de concert, the Fête de la musique.
  • sauvegarder – to safeguard · soutenir – to support · diffuser – to broadcast · un quota
  • Your turn: "Without quotas, radio stations would play fewer French songs."
    Show answer

    Sans quotas, les radios diffuseraient moins de chansons françaises.

Mission 2

Argue both sides

  • Are radio quotas still useful?
  • Yes (arguments supporters make):
  • Les quotas aident les jeunes artistes à se faire connaître. – Quotas help young artists get known.
  • Sans eux, les radios passeraient surtout des tubes anglo-saxons. – Without them, radio would mostly play English-language hits.
  • No (arguments supporters make):
  • À l'ère du streaming, les quotas ont perdu leur sens. – In the streaming age, quotas have lost their point.
  • Imposer une langue limite la liberté artistique. – Imposing a language limits artistic freedom.
  • And English? Some French artists sing in English to reach the world (Daft Punk); others say French is part of their identity.
  • Linkers: Certes …, mais … · Il n'en reste pas moins que … · Selon ses défenseurs, …
Quick check
?Complete: « C'est une chanteuse ___ tout le monde parle. »
Mission 2

Talking about a song without quoting it

  • In exams, describe and analyse in your own words.
  • Dans cette chanson, l'artiste évoque … – In this song, the artist evokes …
  • Les paroles dénoncent / célèbrent … – The lyrics criticise / celebrate …
  • Le refrain répète une idée, ce qui souligne … – The chorus repeats an idea, which highlights …
  • Le rythme est lent / rapide ; la mélodie est … · Le clip montre … – The video shows …
  • les paroles (f pl) – lyrics · un couplet – a verse · le refrain – the chorus · rendre hommage à – to pay tribute to
  • Watch out: les paroles is plural: les paroles sont touchantes. Une parole alone means a word or a promise.
Mission 2

Vocabulary, model sentences and exam tasks

  • un genre musical · un tube · la chanson à texte · le rap · le streaming · un auditeur / une auditrice · diffuser · un quota · sauvegarder · se produire · les paroles · le refrain
  • C'est une artiste dont les chansons mêlent le français, l'argot et des mots d'autres langues. (dont)
  • Bien que le streaming domine, le vinyle connaît un retour en force. (bien que + subjunctive)
  • Si les radios n'étaient pas obligées de diffuser des chansons en français, les jeunes talents auraient plus de mal à percer. (si + imperfect → conditional)
  • Paper 3 card (5 min preparation, 5–6 min): La musique francophone contemporaine; e.g. Comment sauvegarder cette musique ? Use the quota law and one 2025 figure.

Make a guess: The first paid film screening was in Paris in 1895. Why do the French call cinema the seventh art?

Mission 3

Why "the seventh art"?

  • 28 December 1895: the Lumière brothers hold the first public, paid film screening, in a room of the Grand Café in Paris.
  • In the 1910s–20s the critic Ricciotto Canudo (an Italian living in Paris) argued that cinema was a full art form.
  • His list: architecture, sculpture, painting, music, dance, poetry, then cinema, the seventh, because it combines them all (space, image, movement, sound, story).
  • Le septième art has been a common French name for cinema ever since.
  • un film · un long-métrage – a feature film · un court-métrage – a short film · le grand écran – the big screen
  • Watch out: un réalisateur / une réalisatrice = a film director. Un directeur runs a company or a school.
Mission 3

Evolution: the main stages

  • 1902: Georges Méliès, Le Voyage dans la Lune: special effects and fantasy.
  • 1930s: poetic realism and Jean Renoir (La Grande Illusion, 1937).
  • 1946: creation of the CNC (state film body) and the first Cannes festival.
  • 1959–60s: la Nouvelle Vague.
  • 1976: the first César awards, France's film prizes.
  • 2000s–2010s: huge popular comedies: Bienvenue chez les Ch'tis (2008) and Intouchables (2011) each sold close to 20 million tickets.
  • 2023: Justine Triet's Anatomie d'une chute wins the Palme d'or (then an Oscar for its screenplay in 2024).
  • Today: streaming platforms alongside cinemas.
Quick check
?Which of these came first?
Mission 3

La Nouvelle Vague

  • Late 1950s–1960s: young critics from the magazine Les Cahiers du cinéma start making films.
  • Key films: Les Quatre Cents Coups (François Truffaut, 1959) · À bout de souffle (Jean-Luc Godard, 1960) · Cléo de 5 à 7 (Agnès Varda, 1962).
  • What was new:
  • filming outdoors, in real streets, with natural light;
  • light, hand-held cameras and small budgets;
  • young, unknown actors;
  • jump cuts and breaking the rules of continuity;
  • the director as auteur: the film expresses one person's vision.
  • le tournage – filming, the shoot · tourner un film – to shoot a film · un(e) cinéaste – a film-maker · une caméra – a film camera
Mission 3

A national passion?

  • France is Europe's largest cinema market (CNC).
  • 2024: 181.5 million tickets sold; French films took 44.7% of them.
  • 2025: 156.8 million tickets (−13.6%); French films 37.7%, American films 35.8%.
  • Cinemas everywhere: from multiplexes to small cinémas d'art et d'essai (independent cinemas showing arthouse films).
  • Cannes each May: the world's best-known film festival.
  • la fréquentation – attendance · les entrées (f) – tickets sold, admissions · une salle (de cinéma) – a cinema, a screen · un(e) cinéphile – a film lover
  • en version originale (VO) – in the original language · en version française (VF) – dubbed into French (doublé) · sous-titré – subtitled
Quick check
?Hier soir, j'ai assisté à l'avant-première du film. What does this mean?
Mission 3

How France funds its cinema

  • L'exception culturelle: the idea that films are culture, not just goods, so the state may protect them (defended by France in world trade talks in 1993).
  • A tax on every cinema ticket goes to the CNC, which reinvests it in French films and cinemas.
  • L'avance sur recettes (since 1959): CNC loans for films chosen for their quality, often first films.
  • TV channels, and since 2021 streaming platforms, must invest part of their revenue in French and European works.
  • La chronologie des médias: rules on how long a film waits after its cinema release before it can be shown on pay TV, streaming, then free TV.
  • financer – to fund · une subvention – a subsidy · un producteur / une productrice · la sortie en salle – cinema release
Mission 3

Cinema in young people's lives

  • Competition: streaming platforms, series, short videos, games.
  • Why young people still go: a social outing, big releases on the big screen, special events (premieres, festivals).
  • What holds them back: ticket prices, films available soon on platforms.
  • Help: discount prices for young people; the pass Culture (a state credit for young people) can be spent on cinema tickets.
  • School screenings: many pupils discover French films through school programmes.
  • une plateforme de streaming · une série · une sortie – an outing; a release · le prix des places – ticket prices
Mission 3

Argue both sides

  • Do cinemas have a future?
  • Yes (arguments supporters make):
  • Voir un film en salle reste une expérience collective unique. – Seeing a film in a cinema remains a unique shared experience.
  • Les grands films sortent d'abord au cinéma. – Major films are released in cinemas first.
  • No, or not as before (arguments supporters make):
  • Les plateformes offrent un choix immense pour le prix d'une place. – Platforms offer a huge choice for the price of one ticket.
  • Le prix des places décourage les familles et les jeunes. – Ticket prices put off families and young people.
  • And state funding? Supporters: it keeps French and arthouse films alive. Critics: it funds films few people see.
  • Your turn: "Although streaming is convenient, nothing replaces the big screen."
    Show answer

    Bien que le streaming soit pratique, rien ne remplace le grand écran.

Quick check
?En 2025, la fréquentation des salles a baissé de 13,6 %. What does this mean?
Mission 3

Vocabulary, model sentences and exam tasks

  • le septième art · un réalisateur / une réalisatrice · un(e) cinéaste · le tournage · un long-métrage · la fréquentation · les entrées · une avant-première · en VO / doublé / sous-titré · l'exception culturelle · une subvention · la sortie en salle
  • Ce qui distingue la Nouvelle Vague, c'est sa liberté : tournage en extérieur, caméra légère et jeunes acteurs.
  • Bien que la fréquentation ait baissé en 2025, la France reste le premier marché du cinéma en Europe. (bien que + perfect subjunctive)
  • Si le CNC ne finançait pas les premiers films, beaucoup de jeunes réalisateurs ne pourraient pas tourner. (si + imperfect → conditional)

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Le patrimoine: national / regional / local; material / intangible (baguette 2022, repas gastronomique 2010); 56 UNESCO sites.
  • Protection: the state, Journées du patrimoine (1984), Loto du patrimoine (2018), volunteers; tourism (102 million visitors in 2025) helps and harms.
  • Regional languages: heritage (art. 75-1) but French is the language of the Republic (art. 2); 2021 immersion ruling.

Mission 2

  • Diversity: France, Belgium, Québec, the Caribbean, West and Central Africa; many languages mixed with French.
  • Change: chanson → yé-yé → rap → French touch → streaming era (rap dominant; 16 of the top 20 albums of 2025 French).
  • Safeguarding: radio quotas (1994: 40% in French), CNM (2020), festivals, Fête de la musique (1982).

Mission 3

  • Le septième art: Canudo's name for cinema, which combines the other six arts; first paid screening, Paris, 1895.
  • Stages: Méliès (1902) → Renoir → CNC and Cannes (1946) → Nouvelle Vague (1959–60s) → Césars (1976) → popular comedies → streaming.
  • Passion and protection: 181.5 m tickets (2024), 156.8 m (2025); exception culturelle, ticket tax, CNC.
