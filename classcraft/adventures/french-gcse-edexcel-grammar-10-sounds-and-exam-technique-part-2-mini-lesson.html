Mission 1

The translation task

  • You read French sentences and write them in English.
  • Reading paper, Section B: 10 marks. Foundation: five sentences. Higher: one short passage.
  • No dictionary.
  • The goal: an English reader gets the same meaning, in natural English.
  • Every idea must be there. Nothing added, nothing left out.
Mission 1

Meaning, not words

  • French says things its own way. Translate the meaning.

Mission 1

Get the time right

future, conditional, venir de, depuis: Higher

  • The tense of the verb is part of the meaning. Check every verb.
FrenchTenseEnglish

  • Watch out: Je viens de manger = I have just eaten (not "I come from eating").
  • Watch out: J'habite ici depuis cinq ans = I have lived here for five years (still true now).
  • Your turn: Nous allons manger au restaurant.

Mission 1

Worked example: five steps

  • Le week-end dernier, je suis allée au cinéma avec mes amis, mais je n'ai pas aimé le film parce qu'il était trop long.

1. Read it all. Past tense; a trip to the cinema; an opinion.

2. Chunk it: [Le week-end dernier] [je suis allée au cinéma] [avec mes amis] [mais je n'ai pas aimé le film] [parce qu'il était trop long].

3. Verbs and tenses: suis allée (perfect) → went · n'ai pas aimé (perfect, negative) → didn't like · était (imperfect) → was.

4. Draft: "The weekend last, I am gone to the cinema with my friends, but I have not liked the film because it was too long."

5. Polish: "Last weekend, I went to the cinema with my friends, but I didn't like the film because it was too long."

  • Note: allée tells you the writer is female. English can't show that, and doesn't need to.
  • Check: every chunk is there, and nothing extra (trop → too).

Mission 1

Sound natural

  • le, la, les often disappear in English:
    • J'adore le sport. → I love sport.
    • Les légumes sont bons pour la santé. → Vegetables are good for your health.
  • Jobs: English adds "a": Mon frère est étudiant. → My brother is a student.
  • le + a day = every week: le samedi → on Saturdays.
  • chez → chez moi = at my house / at home · chez le médecin = at the doctor's.
  • beaucoup de devoirs → a lot of homework (not "a lot of the homework").
  • Your turn: Mon père est médecin.
    Show answer

    My father is a doctor.

Mission 2

The translation task

  • You read English sentences and write them in French.
  • Writing paper, 10 marks. Foundation: five sentences. Higher: a short paragraph. Both get harder as they go.
  • The sentences use words and grammar from your course lists. You have learned everything you need.
Mission 2

Chunk it

  • Don't translate word by word. Split the sentence into chunks of meaning.
  • "I like going to the cinema with my friends."
ChunkFrenchWhy

  • Each chunk is a piece of meaning. Lose a chunk and you lose its mark.
  • Your turn: "I like playing football."

Mission 2

The little words

  • à + le = au, à + les = aux · de + le = du, de + les = des: au parc, du pain.
  • English often hides "some": "I eat bread" → Je mange du pain.
  • After a negative, un / une / du / de la / des → de: Je n'ai pas de chien.
  • Watch out: le, la, les never change: Je n'aime pas le poisson. (keep le)
  • Jobs: no un/une: "He is a teacher" → Il est professeur.
  • Age uses avoir: "I am fifteen" → J'ai quinze ans.
  • Possessives agree with the thing, not the owner: "his mother" → sa mère; "her brother" → son frère.
  • Your turn: "I don't have a cat."
    Show answer

    Je n'ai pas de chat.

Quick check
?Choose the correct French for: "He doesn't have any brothers."
Mission 2

Verbs: who, when, agree?

  • For every verb, ask three questions:

1. Who? → the ending: nous jouons, ils jouent.

2. When? → the tense: present, perfect, imperfect, future.

3. Être? → in the perfect, does the participle agree?

  • "We went to the beach." → Nous sommes allés à la plage.
  • "She was happy." → Elle était contente.
  • "I'm going to play tennis." → Je vais jouer au tennis.
  • Watch out: English "-ing" is usually the plain present: "I am watching TV" → Je regarde la télé (not je suis regardant).
  • Higher tier (AQA, Edexcel): "I have been learning French for three years" → J'apprends le français depuis trois ans (present + depuis).
  • Your turn: "They (girls) arrived at eight o'clock."
    Show answer

    Elles sont arrivées à huit heures.

Quick check
?Choose the correct French for: "Last year, my sister went to Spain."

Mission 2

Worked example: a full set

  • Five sentences, 37 words of English. The trap in each is in brackets.

1. "I have a brother and a sister." → J'ai un frère et une sœur. (gender: une sœur)

2. "My brother is very funny and quite tall." → Mon frère est très amusant et assez grand. (adjectives agree: for ma sœur, write amusante, grande)

3. "We often play football in the park." → Nous jouons souvent au foot dans le parc. (nous → -ons; jouer au + sport; souvent after the verb)

4. "Yesterday, I watched a film at home." → Hier, j'ai regardé un film à la maison. (perfect tense: ai + participle)

5. "Next week, we are going to visit Paris." → La semaine prochaine, nous allons visiter Paris. (near future: aller + infinitive)

  • Check each sentence: who, when, agreement, little words.
Mission 2

Don't know a word? Then proofread

  • Never leave a gap. A gap is a section of meaning lost.
  • Say the same meaning with words you know:
    • "enormous" → très grand
    • "my parents' car" → la voiture de mes parents
  • Keep the tense and the person right, even if one word is weak.
  • Final check, sentence by sentence:
    • verb: right person, tense, agreement?
    • nouns: right gender? adjectives agree?
    • little words: au / du / de, en / à?
    • accents and apostrophes: é, è, à, ç, j', l', n'?
Quick check
?Choose the correct French for: "I'm fifteen and I live in a small town."

Chunk the meaning, then check every verb (who, when, agree?) and every little word.

Mission 3

You will meet words you don't know

  • Every reading and listening task has a few. That's normal, and you can work them out.
  • Reading paper: two words that are not on the vocabulary list. You work out what each means (1 mark each).
  • Three tools: cognates, word parts, context.
Mission 3

Mission 3

Context: be a detective

1. What kind of word? After un / le / des → a noun. After a subject → a verb. Next to a noun → maybe an adjective.

2. Look around: the rest of the sentence, the title, the topic.

3. Guess a meaning that fits.

4. Test it: put your guess in. Does the sentence make sense?

  • Example: Au marché, j'ai acheté du pain, du fromage et des cerises.
    • des cerises → a noun, plural.
    • Around it: a market, bread, cheese: things to buy and eat.
    • Guess: a food, maybe a fruit. Cognate? "cerise" is a red colour in English → cherries.
  • Get as close as you can. "Some kind of fruit" already shows you understood.
  • Watch out: don't stop at the first idea. Test it in the sentence.
Quick check
?Mon chien aboie toute la nuit, alors les voisins ne peuvent pas dormir. What does aboie most likely mean?

Mission 4

Mission 4: What does a full-marks answer to a French role-play card actually sound like?

Make a guess: What does a full-marks answer to a French role-play card actually sound like?

Mission 4

Your role-play

  • A short scene: the teacher plays a waiter, a receptionist, a ticket seller… You play yourself.
  • Speaking test, Task 2: about 1–1.5 minutes, 10 marks. Five bullet points in English.
  • Foundation: ask one question; speak in the present (or je voudrais). Higher: ask two questions and answer one question about the future.
  • Marks go to getting the message across clearly.
Mission 4

Decode the card

  • Example card: You are in a café in France. The teacher is the waiter.

1. Say how many people are in your group.

2. Order a drink and something to eat.

3. Give your opinion of the café and a reason.

4. Ask how much it costs.

5. Say what you are going to do this afternoon.

  • For each bullet, find three things:
    • What to do: say, order, ask, give an opinion…
    • How many details: "a drink and something to eat" = two.
    • Which time: "going to do" = future → Je vais…
  • "Ask…" means you ask: it must be a real question.
  • Your turn: "Say where you are going and who with." How many details?
    Show answer

    Two: the place and the person.

Mission 4

Worked example: the café card

  • Teacher: Bonjour ! Vous êtes combien ?
  • You: Nous sommes trois. — bullet 1, one detail ✓
  • Teacher: Qu'est-ce que vous voulez ?
  • You: Je voudrais un thé et une glace, s'il vous plaît. — bullet 2, two details ✓✓
  • Teacher: Comment trouvez-vous le café ?
  • You: Je trouve que c'est très sympa, parce que c'est calme. — bullet 3, opinion + reason ✓
  • You: C'est combien, s'il vous plaît ? — bullet 4, a real question ✓
  • Teacher: Douze euros. Qu'est-ce que vous allez faire cet après-midi ?
  • You: Je vais visiter le musée avec mes amis. — bullet 5, future ✓
  • Short, clear, complete. Nothing extra that could go wrong.
Mission 4

Every detail, the right time

  • Do exactly what each bullet says. Two details asked? Give both.
  • A clear, complete answer to every prompt is what scores.
  • Match the time of the question:
    • Qu'est-ce que vous allez faire ? → Je vais…
    • Qu'est-ce que vous avez fait ? → J'ai…
  • Watch out: a single word (Trois.) can be unclear. A short sentence (Nous sommes trois.) is safer.
Quick check
?At a station. The card says: Buy a ticket to Lyon and say when you want to leave. What do you say?
Mission 4

Problems and complaints

  • Some cards ask you to report a problem or complain. Two steps: say there's a problem, then say what it is.
    • Il y a un problème avec ma chambre. – There's a problem with my room.
    • La télé ne marche pas. – The TV doesn't work.
    • Il y a trop de bruit. – There's too much noise.
  • Other useful lines:
    • Station: Le train part à quelle heure ? – What time does the train leave?
    • Doctor's: J'ai mal à la tête. – I've got a headache.
    • Shop: Ce pantalon est trop petit. – These trousers are too small.
  • Your turn: "Tell the receptionist the TV in your room doesn't work."
    Show answer

    Dans ma chambre, la télé ne marche pas.

Mission 4

Use your preparation time

  • Read every bullet. Mark the number of details and the time frame.
  • Write a short sentence for each bullet.
  • Write your question(s) out in full.
  • Predict the teacher's question for each bullet.
  • Don't know a word? Plan a way round it: "a croissant" → quelque chose à manger (something to eat).

  • In the test: listen to the question, answer it, and move on.

Mission 5

Say more than you see

  • After who, where and what, add one more idea:
    • weather: Il fait chaud. – It's hot.
    • clothes: Ils portent des tee-shirts. – They are wearing T-shirts.
    • mood (a guess): Ils sont contents, je pense. – They are happy, I think.

    • opinion: J'aime cette photo parce que… – I like this photo because…
    • before or after: Après, ils vont rentrer à la maison. – Afterwards, they are going to go home.
  • Higher: the second question is in the past. Answer in the perfect: Le week-end dernier, j'ai joué au tennis.

  • Foundation paper only.
  • Edexcel: four short sentences, marked for communication.
  • One clear idea per sentence, about what is in the photo.
  • Use the present tense. Short and correct beats long and unclear.

    • Le garçon porte un short. – The boy is wearing shorts.
  • Watch out: don't write about your own family or holidays here. Stick to the photo.

Mission 5

Your exam-day list

  • Qui ? Où ? Quoi ? +1 for every photo.
  • Use position words: à gauche, à droite, au premier plan.
  • Present tense for what you see; add one past or future idea.
  • Give an opinion with a reason: J'aime cette photo parce que…
  • Answer follow-up questions about you, with a reason and an example.
  • Stuck on a word? Describe around it: un animal instead of the breed of dog.
  • Describe people, location and activity.

Mission 6

Your conversation task

  • After the read aloud: a short unprepared interaction about the text.
  • After the picture: two questions about your own life, then a broader conversation on the same theme: 3–3.5 minutes (Foundation), 5–5.5 minutes (Higher). Expect present, past and future questions.
  • Same skill for everyone: develop every answer and cope with the unexpected.

  • Watch out: parce que + vowel → parce qu': parce qu'il fait beau.
  • Your turn: add a reason. J'aime ma ville…
    Show answer

    J'aime ma ville parce qu'il y a beaucoup de magasins.

Mission 6

When you're stuck

  • Buy time: Alors… – So… · Bon… – Well… · C'est une bonne question. – That's a good question.
  • Ask: Pouvez-vous répéter, s'il vous plaît ? – Can you repeat that, please? · Je n'ai pas compris. – I didn't understand.
  • Rescue: say what you can say. No word for "skateboarding"? J'aime faire du sport dans le parc avec mes amis.
  • A calm way to ask for help.
  • Watch out: never switch to English, and never stop at one word.
Mission 6

Show initiative

  • Add something nobody asked: J'adore la musique. En plus, je joue de la guitare dans un groupe. – I love music. What's more, I play the guitar in a band.
  • Compare: Le foot est plus amusant que le tennis. – Football is more fun than tennis.
  • Vary opinions: Je pense que… – I think that… · À mon avis,… – In my opinion,… · Je trouve que… – I find that…
  • Give both sides: J'aime mon collège, mais les journées sont longues. – I like my school, but the days are long.
  • If it is natural, ask back: Et vous, vous aimez la musique ? – And you, do you like music?
  • Your turn: "Maths is more interesting than history."
    Show answer

    Les maths sont plus intéressantes que l'histoire. (les maths is feminine plural: intéressantes)

Mission 7

Grow a sentence into a paragraph

  • Each bullet: fact + opinion/reason + extra detail.
  • Simple: Je suis allé(e) en France.
  • + when, who: L'année dernière, je suis allé(e) en France avec ma famille.
  • + opinion, reason: C'était super parce qu'il faisait beau.
  • + detail: Nous avons visité Paris et nous avons mangé des croissants.
  • Link with: et, mais, aussi, parce que, car, donc, par contre – on the other hand.

Mission 7

Sweep like an examiner

  • Leave five minutes. Sweep your text four times:

1. Bullets: every one covered, in the right tense?

2. Verbs: right ending for the person; être or avoir; agreement with être.

3. Agreements and accents: contente, intéressantes; été, très, à.

4. Register: tu or vous, all the way through.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Translate the meaning, not the words: nothing added, nothing left out.
  • Check every tense, every negative and every small word (souvent, déjà, trop).
  • Watch for false friends (actuellement, librairie, rester) and polish into natural English.

Mission 2

  • Split the English into chunks of meaning; each chunk carries marks.
  • For every verb: who, when, agree?
  • Little words matter: au/du, de after a negative, no article for jobs, avoir for age.

Mission 3

  • Cognates: look for English inside (ê → es, é- → s-, -eux → -ous).
  • False friends: if the look-alike doesn't fit the sentence, think again.
  • Context: what kind of word, what's around it, guess, then test.

Mission 4

  • Each bullet: what to do, how many details, which time.
  • Give every detail, in short complete sentences; "Ask…" means a real question.
  • Prepare a sentence for every bullet, and your question(s), in full.

Mission 5

  • Answer Qui ? Où ? Quoi ? and add one more idea (weather, clothes, mood, opinion).
  • "They are playing" = ils jouent: one verb, no "are"; use il y a for "there is / there are".
  • Develop follow-up answers: answer, reason, past or future example.

Mission 6

  • A-R-T: answer, reason, time-shift. Never stop at one word.
  • The question's verb shows the tense: tu as fait → j'ai fait; tu vas faire → je vais faire.
  • Stuck? Buy time, ask for a repeat, or say it more simply, always in French.

Mission 7

  • Every bullet: the right time frame (past, present, future) and its own paragraph.
  • Register: tu / Salut / À bientôt for friends; vous / Madame, Monsieur / Cordialement for formal.
  • Develop (fact + opinion + detail), then sweep for verbs, agreements, accents and register.
