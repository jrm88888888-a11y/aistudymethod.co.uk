Formation

The perfect subjunctive

The perfect subjunctive is built like the passé composé, but the auxiliary goes into the present subjunctive:

present subjunctive of avoir / être+ past participle
  • que j'aie fait — that I have done
  • qu'il soit parti — that he has left
  • que tu aies fini — that you have finished
  • qu'elle soit partie — that she has left

Agreement: être verbs and reflexives make the past participle agree with the subject — qu'elle soit partie, qu'ils soient partis.

Two auxiliaries

avoir-verbs vs être-verbs

Which auxiliary you use is exactly the same choice as in the passé composé — most verbs take avoir, but the movement/change-of-state verbs and all reflexives take être:

The key choice

Present vs perfect subjunctive

After a trigger + que, pick the subjunctive by timing:

  • Ongoing or still-to-happen → present subjunctive: Il faut que tu finisses (finish now / soon).
  • Already completed → perfect subjunctive: Je suis content que tu aies fini (you have finished).

Test: could you swap in "have done" / "has happened" in English? Then use the perfect subjunctive. If it's "do" / "will do", use the present.

A-Level key point

French has no strict sequence of tenses

In older or literary French, a past main verb pulled the subjunctive back into the imperfect subjunctive. Modern French does not do this. Even after a past main verb you keep the present subjunctive (for ongoing) or the perfect subjunctive (for completed):

  • Je veux qu'il vienne. — I want him to come.
  • Je voulais qu'il vienne. — I wanted him to come. (still present subjunctive!)

Rule of thumb: in speech and normal writing, choose only between the present and perfect subjunctive — never the imperfect subjunctive, whatever the tense of the main verb.

Receptive only

The imperfect & pluperfect subjunctive

You will meet the imperfect subjunctive (qu'il fût, qu'il eût) and the pluperfect subjunctive (qu'il eût fait) in set texts and literature. They are receptive only: recognise them, but do not produce them in your own writing.

  • qu'il fût — literary imperfect subjunctive of être
  • qu'il eût fait — literary pluperfect subjunctive

In your own French: replace these with the ordinary present or perfect subjunctive — qu'il soit, qu'il ait fait.

Recap

Your subjunctive-II toolkit

Perfect subjunctive: present subjunctive of avoir/être + past participle — que j'aie fait, qu'il soit parti. Être-verbs and reflexives agree.

Choosing: ongoing/future → present subjunctive; completed → perfect subjunctive.

Sequence: modern French keeps the present/perfect subjunctive even after a past main verb — je voulais qu'il vienne.

Literary only: imperfect & pluperfect subjunctive (qu'il fût, qu'il eût fait) — recognise, never produce.
