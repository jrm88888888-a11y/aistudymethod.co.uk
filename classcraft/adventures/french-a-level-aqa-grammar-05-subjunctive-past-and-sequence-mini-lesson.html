Mission 1

Present or perfect subjunctive?

  • Compare the time of the que-clause with the main verb.
  • Same time or later → present subjunctive:
    • Je suis content que tu viennes. – I'm glad you're coming.
  • Earlier, completed → perfect subjunctive:
    • Je suis content que tu sois venu. – I'm glad you came.
  • Je doute qu'il comprenne. – I doubt he understands. / Je doute qu'il ait compris. – I doubt he understood.
  • Completed by a deadline: Il faut que tu aies fini avant lundi. – You must have finished by Monday.
  • Past main verb? Modern French still uses the present or perfect subjunctive:
    • Il fallait que je parte. – I had to leave.
    • J'étais ravie qu'il soit venu. – I was delighted he had come.
  • Your turn: "It's a pity they lost the match."
    Show answer

    C'est dommage qu'ils aient perdu le match.

Mission 1

After superlatives, seul, premier, dernier

  • le plus / le moins / le meilleur / le pire … qui / que → subjunctive when it is your judgement.
  • Also after le seul, l'unique, le premier, le dernier.
  • C'est le meilleur film que j'aie jamais vu. – It's the best film I've ever seen.
  • C'est la seule personne qui puisse m'aider. – She's the only person who can help me.
  • C'est le livre le plus émouvant que je connaisse. – It's the most moving book I know.
  • A plain, recorded fact often takes the indicative: Neil Armstrong est le premier homme qui a marché sur la Lune. – Neil Armstrong is the first man who walked on the Moon.
  • Watch out: here jamais = ever, not "never": le meilleur … que j'aie jamais vu.
  • Your turn: "It's the most beautiful town I know."
    Show answer

    C'est la plus belle ville que je connaisse.

Mission 1

Relative clauses: wanted, not found

  • The thing described is sought, hypothetical or denied → subjunctive.
  • It is real and identified → indicative.
Sought or non-existent: subjunctiveReal: indicative
Je cherche un logement qui soit proche de la fac. – I'm looking for a place (any) that's near the university.J'ai trouvé un logement qui est proche de la fac. – I've found a place that's near the university.
Il n'y a personne qui puisse répondre. – There's nobody who can answer.Il y a quelqu'un qui peut répondre. – There's someone who can answer.
Je ne connais aucun pays qui ait résolu ce problème. – I don't know any country that has solved this problem.Je connais un pays qui a résolu ce problème. – I know a country that has solved this problem.

Mission 1

Put it to work

  • C'est l'une des pires catastrophes écologiques que la région ait connues. – It's one of the worst environmental disasters the region has known. (que = catastrophes: feminine plural → connues)
  • Je ne connais personne qui n'ait jamais utilisé les réseaux sociaux. – I don't know anyone who has never used social media.
  • Bien que la loi ait changé, les inégalités persistent. – Although the law has changed, inequalities persist.
  • Je suis fier que ma famille m'ait toujours soutenu. – I'm proud that my family has always supported me. (speaker male: fier, soutenu; female: fière, soutenue)
  • Checklist before you write it:

1. Is there a trigger (superlative, sought or denied antecedent)?

2. Earlier and completed → perfect subjunctive; same time or later → present.

3. avoir or être? Then check agreement (subject with être; preceding direct object with avoir).

  • Essays on a film or text: C'est la scène la plus forte que le réalisateur ait tournée. – It's the most powerful scene the director shot.

Mission 2

The storytelling past

Recognise only

  • Speaking and everyday writing: completed events in the perfect tense (passé composé).
  • Novels, tales, history books, some journalism: the past historic (passé simple).
  • Same meaning: il a parlé = il parla – he spoke.
  • The imperfect still sets the scene: Il pleuvait. Soudain, elle entra. – It was raining. Suddenly she came in.
  • You'll meet it mostly in the third person: il, elle, on, ils, elles.
  • Albert Camus tells L'Étranger (1942) in the perfect tense, not the past historic: a deliberate choice that critics often discuss.
  • Your turn: what is Elle entra in the perfect tense?
    Show answer

    Elle est entrée. – She came in.

Mission 2

Translating literary French

Recognise only

  • Past historic → English simple past: elle partit – she left (not "she did leave").
  • Imperfect → "was …ing", "used to …", or simple past for states: il était fatigué – he was tired.
  • fut + past participle is a past historic passive: Il fut arrêté le lendemain. – He was arrested the next day.
  • fut + adjective often marks a sudden change: Elle fut surprise. – She was surprised (at that moment).
  • Worked example: Le soir, il rentra chez lui. Sa femme l'attendait dans la cuisine.

1. rentra = past historic of rentrer: an event → "went home".

2. attendait = imperfect: background → "was waiting".

  • → In the evening he went home. His wife was waiting for him in the kitchen.
Mission 2

The imperfect subjunctive: recognise it

Recognise only, A-level not AS

  • In literary texts, after a past main verb, the subjunctive can appear in the imperfect.
    • Modern: Il fallait qu'elle parte. Literary: Il fallait qu'elle partît. – She had to leave.
  • Spot it from the past historic il form + a circumflex (and a t for -er verbs):
Past historicImperfect subjunctive
il parlaqu'il parlât
il finitqu'il finît
il futqu'il fût
il eutqu'il eût
il fitqu'il fît
il vintqu'il vînt
  • Plurals are rare: qu'ils parlassent, qu'ils fussent, qu'ils eussent.
  • Watch out: one accent changes the form: il fut – he was / qu'il fût – that he were.
  • Also in literature: il eût fallu = il aurait fallu – it would have been necessary.
  • To translate it, read it as a present subjunctive with a past main verb.
  • Your turn: modern French for Bien qu'il fût malade, il travaillait.
    Show answer

    Bien qu'il soit malade, il travaillait. – Although he was ill, he was working.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Perfect subjunctive = aie / sois … + past participle; use it for an action earlier than the main verb.
  • After a superlative of judgement, le seul, le premier, le dernier → subjunctive.
  • After a sought, hypothetical or denied antecedent (je cherche un…, personne qui, rien qui) → subjunctive.

Mission 2

  • Past historic = the written narrative past; translate it as an English simple past. Learn the il and ils forms.
  • Watch the traps: fut / fit, vit (voir / vivre), dit (present / past).
  • Imperfect subjunctive = past historic il form + circumflex (qu'il fût, qu'il eût, qu'il parlât): recognise it, write the present subjunctive instead.
