Mission 1

Mission 1: With air resistance ignored, which forces still act on a ball once it has left your foot?

Make a guess: With air resistance ignored, which forces still act on a ball once it has left your foot?

Mission 1

A force is an interaction

  • A force is a push or pull by one object on another. Unit: the newton, N.
  • Every force has a source: name it as "force on X by Y".
  • Force is a vector: size and direction.
  • No object exerts a net force on itself. No source, no force.
Mission 1

Weight

  • Weight: the gravitational force on an object by Earth (or another planet or moon).
  • W = mg
  • Mass (kg): how much matter. Same everywhere. Weight (N): changes with g.
  • g = F/m: gravitational field strength, the force per kilogram, in N kg⁻¹. Same value as the free-fall acceleration in m s⁻².
  • Weight acts at the centre of gravity. It needs no contact: a field force.
Quick check
?A space probe weighs 4905 N on Earth, where g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹. It lands on Mars, where g = 3.7 N kg⁻¹. What is the probe's mass on Mars?
Mission 1

Normal force, friction, tension

  • Normal contact force: perpendicular to the surface.
  • Friction: parallel to the surface. Opposes sliding, or the tendency to slide, between the surfaces.
  • Tension: the pull of a string, rope or cable, along it, away from the object.
  • Ideal string (light, does not stretch): the same tension all along it, and on both sides of an ideal pulley.
Mission 1

Forces from fluids

  • Drag (air resistance): opposite to the velocity; grows with speed.
  • Upthrust: upward force from a fluid a body is in.
  • Thrust: the driving force of an engine.
Quick check
?A ball has been thrown straight up and is still rising. Air resistance is not negligible; the upthrust of the air is negligible. Which list gives all the forces acting on the ball, and no others?
Mission 1

Drawing a free-body diagram

  • One object only. Show every force on it, by something else.
  • Label each arrow with what exerts it. Arrow length shows size.
  • Never draw a "force of motion" or the object's own push on something else.
  • For a particle, start each arrow on a dot.
  • For an extended body, start each arrow where the force acts (weight at the centre of gravity). This matters for moments.
Mission 1

From diagram to resultant

  • Add all the forces as vectors: the resultant (net) force.
  • Resolve each force along two perpendicular axes, then add components.
  • A sledge is pulled by a rope: tension 50 N at 30° above the horizontal; friction 30 N.
  • Horizontal: 50 cos 30° − 30 = 43.3 − 30 = 13.3 N forwards.
  • Vertical: the rope's 50 sin 30° = 25 N upwards, plus the normal force, balances the weight.
Quick check
?A crate is given a push and then slides down a rough ramp on its own. Which list gives all the forces acting on the crate while it slides, and no others?
Mission check

?A hockey puck is hit by a stick and then slides across level ice. Friction and air resistance are negligible. Which forces are exerted on the puck while it slides, after it has left the stick?

Same for the kicked ball: once the foot has gone, only Earth pulls on it (its weight). Nothing pushes it along its path.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Can something be moving fast when all the forces on it cancel out?

Make a guess: Can something be moving fast when all the forces on it cancel out?

Mission 2

Newton's first law

  • A body stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it.
  • Moving things do not stop "naturally". Friction and drag stop them.
  • No resultant force needed to keep moving; one is needed to change velocity.
Quick check
?A space probe is so far from any star or planet that the forces exerted on it are negligible. Its engines fire briefly and then shut off. Which statement describes the probe's motion after the engines have shut off?
Mission 2

Equilibrium

  • Resultant force: the vector sum of all the forces on a body.
  • Translational equilibrium: resultant force zero → at rest or at constant velocity.
  • A skydiver whose drag equals her weight falls at constant velocity: her terminal velocity. She is in equilibrium.
  • A rigid body also needs zero resultant moment to stay unturned (moments: later in this topic).
Quick check
?A puck slides east at constant velocity across level ice with negligible friction. Starting at time t, a constant force directed north is exerted on the puck, and no other horizontal force acts. Which statement describes the puck's motion after time t?
Mission 2

Balanced one way, not the other

  • Resolve forces along two perpendicular axes. Each axis on its own.
  • Equilibrium needs both sums zero.
  • Forces can balance along one axis and not the other: the object accelerates only along the unbalanced axis.
  • A constant force perpendicular to the initial velocity changes only that perpendicular component. The original component stays constant: projectile-like motion.
Mission 2

On a slope

  • Take axes along the slope and perpendicular to it.
  • Weight resolves into mg sin θ down the slope and mg cos θ into the slope.
  • A 5.00 kg block rests on a rough slope at 30.0°. Equilibrium: friction = mg sin θ, normal force = mg cos θ.
  • g = 9.81 m s⁻²: friction = 24.5 N up the slope · normal force = 42.5 N
Quick check
?A block is held at rest on a ramp by a light string that is parallel to the ramp and tied to a post at the top, as shown in the diagram. The ramp is inclined at 37° above the horizontal, friction between the block and the ramp is negligible, and the gravitational force on the block is 50 N. Use sin 37° = 0.60 and cos 37° = 0.80. What is the tension in the string?
Mission 2

Three forces: the closed triangle

  • Three coplanar forces in equilibrium, drawn tip to tail, form a closed triangle.
  • A 50 N lamp is pulled sideways by a horizontal 20 N force; the cord holds it still.
  • Triangle: 50 N down, 20 N sideways, cord tension closes it.
  • Tension = √(50² + 20²) = 53.9 N, at tan⁻¹(20/50) = 21.8° to the vertical.
  • Use a scale drawing, or Pythagoras and trigonometry for a right-angled triangle.
Quick check
?Three forces act at a point. Which set of magnitudes could keep the point in equilibrium?
Quick check
?A hanging basket of mass 3.0 kg is held at rest by two ropes: one horizontal, and one at 55° above the horizontal. Take g = 9.81 m s⁻². What is the tension in the rope at 55°?
Mission check

?Which of the following objects is in translational equilibrium?

Yes. Moving fast or slow, an object with constant velocity has zero net force on it.

A skydiver at terminal velocity falls very fast, typically around 50 m s⁻¹, with zero resultant force: drag balances weight. Zero resultant force means constant velocity, not zero velocity.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Push a car and a wheelbarrow equally hard. How much more slowly does the car speed up?

Make a guess: Push a car and a wheelbarrow equally hard. How much more slowly does the car speed up?

Mission 3

Newton's second law

  • F = ma: resultant force = mass × acceleration (constant mass).
  • The acceleration is in the direction of the resultant force, not necessarily the velocity.
  • 1 newton: the resultant force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m s⁻². 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻².
  • Same force: a ∝ 1/m. Same mass: a ∝ F.
Quick check
?A crate slides to the left across a level floor with negligible friction. At one instant, two students push on it horizontally: one pushes to the left with a force of 20 N, and the other pushes to the right with a force of 30 N. Which statement about the crate at this instant is correct?
Mission 3

Worked example

  • A 1200 kg car: driving force 3000 N forwards, resistive forces 600 N backwards.
  • Resultant force = 3000 − 600 = 2400 N forwards.
  • a = F/m = 2400 ÷ 1200 = 2.0 m s⁻² forwards.
  • From rest, after 5.0 s: v = 0 + 2.0 × 5.0 = 10 m s⁻¹.
  • Always use the resultant force, never just the driving force.
Quick check
?A loaded trolley of mass 8.0 kg moves along a straight, level track. The graph shows the trolley's velocity v as a function of time t. What is the magnitude of the resultant force exerted on the trolley between t = 0 and t = 2.0 s?
Mission 3

Connected bodies

  • Whole system first: only external forces count. Internal pulls and pushes cancel in pairs.
  • Then one object on its own: this gives the internal force (tension, or a contact force).
  • A 0.80 kg block on a smooth table is pulled by a string over an ideal pulley to a 0.20 kg hanging mass.
  • System: resultant = 0.20 × 9.81 = 1.962 N; total mass 1.00 kg → a = 1.962 ÷ 1.00 = 1.96 m s⁻².
  • Block alone: T = 0.80 × 1.96 = 1.6 N: less than the hanging weight, because the hanging mass accelerates.
Quick check
?Block X (2.0 kg) and block Y (4.0 kg) rest on a frictionless horizontal surface, joined by a light string. A horizontal force of 24 N pulls X away from Y, and both accelerate together. What is the tension in the string?
Mission check

?A student exerts different constant resultant forces on a trolley on a level track and measures the trolley's acceleration each time. The graph shows the results. A block is then fixed to the trolley so that the mass of the trolley–block system is twice the mass of the trolley, and the experiment is repeated with the same resultant forces. Which describes the new graph of acceleration against resultant force?

Same force, a ∝ 1/m. A 1200 kg car has 40 times the mass of a 30 kg wheelbarrow, so the same push (ignoring friction) gives it 1/40 of the acceleration.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Raindrops fall from high clouds. Why don't they hit the ground like bullets?

Make a guess: Raindrops fall from high clouds. Why don't they hit the ground like bullets?

Mission 4

Drag

  • Drag: the resistive force on an object moving through a fluid (a liquid or a gas). Air resistance is drag in air.
  • It acts opposite to the velocity relative to the fluid.
  • It increases with speed.
  • It also grows with cross-sectional area, a less streamlined shape, and the fluid's density.
Mission 4

Falling with drag

  • At release: v = 0, so drag = 0.
  • In air (upthrust negligible), the only force is the weight.
  • Speed rises → drag rises → resultant force (weight − drag) falls.
  • When drag = weight: resultant force zero.
  • The object then falls at a constant terminal velocity.
  • v–t graph: starts as steeply as the no-drag line, then curves and levels off at vT.
Quick check
?A small steel ball is released from rest in air and falls until it reaches terminal velocity. How does its acceleration change during this time?
Mission 4

Worked example: a falling ball

  • A 0.20 kg ball falls. At one instant the drag on it is 0.80 N.
  • Weight = 0.20 × 9.81 = 1.96 N. Resultant = 1.96 − 0.80 = 1.16 N downwards.
  • a = 1.16 ÷ 0.20 = 5.8 m s⁻²: less than g.
  • It keeps speeding up until drag = weight, about 2.0 N. Then a = 0: terminal velocity.
Mission 4

Measuring terminal velocity (PAG1)

  • A tall tube of viscous liquid (glycerol or oil), with rubber bands at equal spacings, for example every 10 cm.
  • Drop a ball-bearing in on the axis. Time it between successive bands.
  • Equal times between bands → it has reached terminal velocity: v = spacing ÷ time.
  • Start the bands well below the surface, so the ball is already at terminal velocity.
  • In air: paper cones, filmed against a scale, or timed with light gates. Add mass or change size to investigate factors.
Mission check

?Two paper cones have the same size and shape. A second cone is pushed inside one of them, doubling its mass without changing its outside shape. Both are dropped from a high balcony. Which statement about their terminal velocities is correct?

Drag grows with speed until it balances the weight. Falling raindrops reach this balance at under 10 m s⁻¹, not the 140 m s⁻¹ they would reach falling 1 km without air.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Every force is exerted on one object by another; a free-body diagram shows only the forces on the chosen object, each with its source.
  • Weight = mg (Earth's pull); normal force ⟂ surface; friction ∥ surface; tension along the string.

Mission 2

  • Zero resultant force ⇔ constant velocity (including at rest): translational equilibrium.
  • On a slope, resolve along and perpendicular to the surface: mg sin θ and mg cos θ.
  • Three forces in equilibrium form a closed triangle.

Mission 3

  • F = ma (constant mass): use the resultant force; the acceleration is in its direction.

Mission 4

  • Drag grows with speed. Falling, the resultant force and acceleration fall to zero when drag = weight: terminal velocity.
