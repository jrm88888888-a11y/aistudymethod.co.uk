Mechanics · quantities, units and modelling

SI units: base and derived

  • The SI base quantities are length (metre, m), mass (kilogram, kg) and time (second, s). They are independent of each other.
  • Derived units combine them: velocity m s−1⁻¹, acceleration m s−2⁻², force and weight in newtons (N).
  • From F=maF = ma, 1 N=1 kg m s−21 N = 1 kg m s⁻²: the force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m s−2⁻².
  • Moment (turning effect) is measured in N m (A-level only).

Converting to SI units

Question
A car speeds up from 36 km h−1⁻¹ to 90 km h−1⁻¹ in 12 s. Find its average acceleration in m s−2⁻².
  1. 1
    Convert km h−1⁻¹ to m s−1⁻¹: multiply by 1,000 and divide by 3,600, which is dividing by 3.6
  2. 2
    Apply it: 36÷3.6=1036 ÷ 3.6 = 10 m s−1⁻¹ and 90÷3.6=2590 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m s−1⁻¹
  3. 3
    Acceleration is change in velocity over time: a=1225−10​a = (25 − 10)/12
Answer
a=1.25a = 1.25 m s−2⁻²

working in km h−1⁻¹ gives 1254​=4.554/12 = 4.5, which is not in m s−2⁻². Convert first.

Mechanics · quantities, units and modelling

Scalars, vectors and weight

  • A scalar has size only: distance, speed, mass, time. A vector has size and direction: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force.
  • Weight is the force of gravity on a mass, acting vertically downwards:
W=mgW = mg
  • Mass is in kg and never changes; weight is in N and depends on gg.
  • Use g=9.8g = 9.8 m s−2⁻² unless told otherwise (sometimes 9.81 or 10). gg is not a universal constant: it depends on location.

Mission check

?Why can a 70 kg person on a zip wire be modelled as a dot?

A particle model treats all of the mass as if it were concentrated at one point. That is reasonable when the object's size, shape and spinning do not noticeably change the motion you are studying, such as a person sliding along a zip wire. The person keeps their 70 kg of mass and their weight of 70×9.8=68670 × 9.8 = 686 N.

Recap

Big ideas

  • SI base units: m, kg, s. Derived: m s−1⁻¹, m s−2⁻², N == kg m s−2⁻². Convert km h−1⁻¹ to m s−1⁻¹ by dividing by 3.6.
  • Weight W=mgW = mg is a force (a vector, in N); mass is a scalar in kg. Use g=9.8g = 9.8 m s−2⁻² unless told otherwise.
  • Modelling words (particle, light, inextensible, smooth, rigid, uniform) each tell you what to ignore and what you may assume.
