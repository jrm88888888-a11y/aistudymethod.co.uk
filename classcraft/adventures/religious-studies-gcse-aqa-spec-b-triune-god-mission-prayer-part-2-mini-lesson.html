Mission 1

Mission 1: Is a Christian who never preaches still spreading her faith?

Make a guess: Is a Christian who never preaches still spreading her faith?

Mission 1

Mission and evangelism

  • Mission (Latin mittere, "to send"): the whole task the Church is sent to do: sharing God's love in word and action.
  • Evangelism (Greek euangelion, "good news"): sharing the Gospel so that others may come to faith in Jesus.
  • So all evangelism is mission, but not all mission is evangelism: a food bank is mission; explaining the Gospel is evangelism.
  • Church growth: more people joining churches, and new churches being started.
  • Catholics often say evangelisation: bringing the Gospel into every part of life and society.
  • Catholic teaching counts the witness of a Christian life as the first step of evangelisation, completed by words.
Mission 1Read the source

The Great Commission

After his resurrection, Jesus tells the eleven disciples:

> "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, teaching them to observe all things that I commanded you. Behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." (Matthew 28:19–20)

Q1 Name the three things Jesus tells the disciples to do.

Show answer

Make disciples; baptise; teach.

Q2 What promise comes with the command?

Show answer

Jesus will be with them always.

Mission 1Diversity

Salvation and the urgency of mission

  • How Christians understand salvation shapes how they see mission.
  • Exclusivist (many evangelical Christians): salvation only through conscious faith in Jesus (John 14:6; Acts 4:12). So evangelism is urgent.
  • Catholic teaching: salvation comes through Christ; the Church is his instrument; God's grace can reach people who, through no fault of their own, do not know Christ. But the Church is still sent to proclaim him to all.
  • Liberal or pluralist Christians: God is at work in many faiths; mission stresses dialogue and justice more than conversion.
  • Most Christians agree that mission includes both words and loving action; they differ on the balance.
Mission 1

The Spirit sends

  • Mission is Trinitarian: the Father sends the Son; the Father and Son send the Spirit; the Spirit sends the Church (John 20:21–22).
  • Deus Caritas Est 19 (Benedict XVI, 2005, paraphrase): the Spirit is the inner power that brings believers' hearts into harmony with Christ's heart and moves them to love as he loved. The Church's mission, including its charity, flows from this love.
  • Drama can express mission: Passion plays in town squares and nativity plays retell the Gospel for people who would not enter a church.
Mission 1

Locally, nationally, globally

  • Locally: welcoming newcomers; courses for adults exploring faith or wanting to be baptised or received; youth work; food banks and street pastors; inviting friends.
  • Nationally: Christian radio and online media; national youth festivals and pilgrimages; church leaders speaking on public issues; joint campaigns by Churches Together.
  • Globally: missionary societies and orders (e.g. the Church Mission Society; for Catholics, Missio, the Pope's mission charity) support churches, schools and clinics worldwide.
  • Reverse mission: many churches in Britain are now led or planted by Christians from Africa, Asia, Latin America and Eastern Europe.

Mission 1Evaluate

"Showing love in action is a better way to spread Christianity than preaching."

  • For: Jesus served the sick and poor; "faith without works is dead" (James 2:17, paraphrase); actions build trust where words may be resented.
  • For: Benedict XVI: Christian charity must not be used as a way of pressing people to convert (Deus Caritas Est 31, paraphrase).
  • Against: the Great Commission commands teaching (Matthew 28:19–20); "how will they believe in him whom they have not heard?" (Romans 10:14, paraphrase).
  • Against: good works alone may be seen as simple kindness; people need to hear why Christians act.
  • Judge it by: What does "spread" mean: more believers, or more love? Can word and action be separated? Which reaches people in Britain today?
Mission check

?Dr Grace, a Christian, works for two years at a rural clinic in Malawi run by a church agency. She treats everyone free, whatever their religion, and never preaches; when patients ask why she came, she tells them about her faith in Jesus. Which description is most accurate?

Mission is everything the Church is sent to do; many Christians keep "evangelism" for putting the good news into words, and Catholic teaching sees loving witness as its first step. Most Christians hold the two together.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Who decides what Catholics believe, and why are they trusted to do it?

Make a guess: Who decides what Catholics believe, and why are they trusted to do it?

Mission 2

Scripture, Tradition, Magisterium

  • Scripture: the Bible, the written Word of God.
  • Tradition: the faith handed on by the apostles in the Church's life, creeds, worship and teaching: "hold the traditions which you were taught by us, whether by word or by letter" (2 Thessalonians 2:15).
  • Magisterium (Latin magister, teacher): the Church's living teaching office.
  • Catholic teaching: the three are so joined that none stands without the others (Dei Verbum 10, paraphrase).
  • The Magisterium is not above the Word of God; it serves it.
Mission 2

The living teaching office

  • Who: the pope and the bishops in communion with him (CCC 100, paraphrase).
  • Function: to interpret Scripture and Tradition authentically, and apply them to new questions.
  • Why "living": new questions (medicine, technology, the economy) need answers faithful to the old truths.
  • Importance: it keeps Catholics united in one faith across the world and the centuries.
  • Catholics believe the Holy Spirit guides it: "he will guide you into all truth" (John 16:13).
Mission 2

Apostolic succession

  • Jesus to Peter: "you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my assembly" (Matthew 16:18). "Feed my sheep." (John 21:17)
  • To the apostles: "Whoever listens to you listens to me" (Luke 10:16).
  • Catholics believe the apostles appointed successors by the laying on of hands: "commit the same things to faithful men who will be able to teach others also" (2 Timothy 2:2).
  • Catholics believe bishops are the successors of the apostles, and the pope, Bishop of Rome, is the successor of Peter.
  • So, Catholics believe, their teaching authority comes from Christ, through an unbroken line.
  • This is part of the mark apostolic: one of the four marks of the Church.
Mission 2

The hierarchy and how it consults

  • Pope: Bishop of Rome; head of the College of Bishops; chief teacher and pastor.
  • Cardinals: senior clergy who advise the pope; those under 80 elect a new pope in a conclave.
  • Bishops lead dioceses; archbishops lead groups of dioceses (in Ireland, the Archbishop of Armagh is Primate of All Ireland).
  • Priests lead parishes, helped by deacons; the laity are all the baptised who are not ordained.
  • Consultative: bishops share responsibility with the pope (collegiality, Vatican II); the Synod of Bishops (since 1965); national bishops' conferences; diocesan and parish councils with lay members.
  • The Synod on Synodality (2021–2024) consulted parishes worldwide, and lay men and women voted in its assemblies.

Mission 2

Conciliar and pontifical

  • Conciliar: teaching given by the bishops of the world gathered in an ecumenical council with the pope.
  • Model: the Council of Jerusalem: "It seemed good to the Holy Spirit, and to us" (Acts 15:28).
  • The Catholic Church counts 21 ecumenical councils, from Nicaea (325) to the Second Vatican Council (1962–65).
  • Pontifical (from pontifex, a title of the pope): teaching given by the pope himself.
  • Forms: encyclicals (letters to the whole Church, e.g. Laudato Si', 2015, on care for creation), apostolic exhortations (e.g. Evangelii Gaudium, 2013).
  • Catholics believe both carry authority because both are the voice of the successors of the apostles.
Mission 2Read the source

Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381)

  • The crisis: Arius, a priest in Alexandria, taught that the Son was created: "there was when he was not".
  • Nicaea (325): about 300 bishops, called by Emperor Constantine, declared the Son "of one substance" (homoousios) with the Father, and issued a creed.
  • Constantinople (381): called by Emperor Theodosius I; expanded the creed on the Holy Spirit: the Lord, the giver of life, worshipped and glorified with the Father and the Son.
  • This Nicene Creed is said at Sunday Mass today.
  • Conciliar authority: bishops together, guided by the Spirit, settled the faith for the whole Church; Catholic teaching holds these councils' decisions binding (they were received by the Church and the Bishop of Rome).

Q1 What did the word homoousios rule out?

Show answer

That the Son is a creature, less than the Father.

Q2 Why does a council carry more weight than one bishop?

Show answer

It is the voice of the whole college of bishops, successors of the apostles, with the pope.

Mission 2

The Magisterium and social teaching

  • Catholic social teaching: the Church's teaching on justice, poverty, work and the common good.
  • Conciliar example: Gaudium et Spes (Vatican II, 1965), paragraph 1: "The joys and the hopes, the griefs and the anxieties" of people today, especially the poor and afflicted, are those of Christ's followers too (paraphrase after the opening words).
  • So the Church cannot ignore the world's suffering.
  • Pontifical example: Evangelii Gaudium (Pope Francis, 2013), 53–54: just as "Thou shalt not kill" protects life, Christians must say "thou shalt not" to an economy of exclusion and inequality: "Such an economy kills."
  • EG 54 criticises trickle-down theories and a "globalisation of indifference" to the poor (paraphrase).
  • Some Catholics and economists argue that free markets have lifted millions out of poverty; the Church does not reject markets as such, but insists they serve people.
Quick check
?In 1965, bishops from around the world, meeting with the pope at the Second Vatican Council, approved a document on the Church in the modern world. Which kind of magisterium is this?
Mission 2Evaluate

"The Magisterium is the most important source of authority for Catholics."

  • For: CCC 100 gives it alone the task of authentic interpretation. Without it, people read the Bible in conflicting ways. Its authority comes from Christ through the apostles (Luke 10:16). It answers new questions Scripture does not address directly.
  • Against: it is not above the Word of God but serves it (Dei Verbum 10): Scripture is primary. Conscience is where God's voice echoes within each person (Gaudium et Spes 16, paraphrase). The three sources depend on each other, so none is "most" important.
  • Other Christians: most Protestants hold Scripture alone as the final authority (Luther, 1521: unless convinced by Scripture and plain reason). The Orthodox accept councils and Tradition but not the pope's universal authority.
  • Judge it by: Can authority be ranked if the sources depend on each other? What prevents division? What role is left for conscience?
Mission check

?A new technology raises a moral question that the Bible never mentions. According to Catholic teaching, how would the Church give authoritative guidance on it?

For Catholics, authority is not one person's opinion: the pope and bishops, as successors of the apostles, serve and interpret the Word handed on to them.

Mission 3: Why do some Christians baptise babies, while others wait until people can choose for themselves?

Make a guess: Why do some Christians baptise babies, while others wait until people can choose for themselves?

Mission 3

What baptism is

  • Baptism is the ceremony of initiation: Christians believe it makes a person a member of the Church, the Body of Christ.
  • It uses water, with the words "in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit".
  • Jesus was baptised by John in the River Jordan (Matthew 3:13–17), and he sent his followers out "baptising them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19).
  • Most churches baptise babies (infant baptism). Some baptise only people old enough to say they believe (believers' baptism).
Mission 3

Into the life of the Trinity

  • Baptism is "in the name of" Father, Son and Holy Spirit: Christians believe the person is joined to God's own life.
  • Father: the baptised become God's adopted children.
  • Son: they share in Jesus' death and resurrection (Romans 6:4).
  • Holy Spirit: they receive the Spirit, who makes them holy.
  • Catholic teaching: baptism washes away original sin and all sin, gives grace, and marks the person for ever, so it is given only once.
  • It is the "door" to the other sacraments: no one receives Confirmation or the Eucharist without being baptised.
Mission 3

Infant baptism: the rite

  • Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, Methodist and Presbyterian churches baptise babies; usually at a parish service.
  • Welcome: the priest or minister traces the sign of the cross on the baby's forehead.
  • Promises: parents and godparents reject evil and profess the faith for the child; the congregation promises support.
  • Water: poured three times over the head (or the baby is dipped), with the Trinity words: a sign of washing from sin and new birth.
  • Catholic rite: anointing with chrism (perfumed oil: sharing in Christ's role as priest, prophet and king), a white garment (clothed with Christ, cleansed from sin), and a candle lit from the Easter (Paschal) candle (the light of the risen Christ).

Mission 4: If God already knows what you need, why do Christians pray at all?

Make a guess: If God already knows what you need, why do Christians pray at all?

Mission 4

Raising the mind and heart

  • A classic Christian definition: prayer is the raising of the mind and heart to God (St John of Damascus, 8th century).
  • AQA's wording for this course: "raising of the heart and mind to God".
  • For Christians, prayer is communication with God: speaking and listening.
  • Jesus prayed often, alone and with others (e.g. Luke 5:16; Mark 14:32–36).
  • Why pray, if God knows? "your Father knows what things you need before you ask him" (Matthew 6:8). Christians say prayer is about relationship and trust, not giving God information.
Mission 4

Types of prayer

  • Adoration: praising God for who God is.
  • Confession: admitting sin and asking forgiveness.
  • Thanksgiving: thanking God for gifts and blessings.
  • Supplication: asking. Petition = asking for yourself; intercession = praying for others.
  • Memory aid: ACTS (adoration, confession, thanksgiving, supplication).

Mission 4Read the source

The Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9–13)

"Our Father in heaven, may your name be kept holy.

Let your Kingdom come. Let your will be done on earth as it is in heaven.

Give us today our daily bread.

Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.

Bring us not into temptation, but deliver us from the evil one."

  • Many churches add: "For yours is the Kingdom, the power, and the glory forever. Amen." (not in the earliest manuscripts).
  • In worship most Christians say a traditional wording ("Our Father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name…").

Q1 Which lines are about God, and which are requests for people?

Show answer

The first three are about God's name, Kingdom and will; the rest ask for bread, forgiveness and protection.

Q2 What does "daily bread" suggest about how Christians should depend on God?

Show answer

Day by day, for what is needed.

Mission 4

What the Lord's Prayer means

  • "Our Father": God is close and loving, like a parent; "our" makes it a prayer of the whole community.
  • "May your name be kept holy": adoration; God is honoured above all.
  • "Your Kingdom come": asks for God's rule of justice and peace on earth.
  • The Kingdom is already present in Jesus and the Church, but not yet complete: Christians pray for it and work for it.
  • "Your will be done": trust and obedience, as Jesus prayed in Gethsemane that his Father's will, not his own, be done (Luke 22:42, paraphrase).
  • "Daily bread": needs, physical and spiritual (Catholics also think of the Eucharist).
  • "Forgive us… as we also forgive": forgiveness received is to be passed on.
  • "Deliver us": protection from evil.
  • Significance: Jesus gave it as a model for all prayer; said at every Mass and most services, and by Christians of almost every denomination.
Quick check
?Which line of the Lord's Prayer shows most clearly that Christians link God's forgiveness to the way they treat other people?
Mission 4

Set and informal prayer

  • Set prayers: written words, said alone or together: the Lord's Prayer, the Hail Mary, the Book of Common Prayer collects, the Jesus Prayer.
  • Informal prayer: in your own words, as you feel led.
  • AQA's terms for this course: traditional and spontaneous prayer.
  • Why set prayers: shared and unifying; rich in belief; help when words fail; connect with Christians through history.
  • Why informal prayer: personal and sincere; fits the moment; open to the Spirit's leading.
  • When each is used: set prayers in liturgical services (Mass, Holy Communion, baptisms, funerals) and in private (e.g. the Lord's Prayer, the Jesus Prayer); informal prayer in private, at prayer meetings and in non-liturgical services.
  • Different views: Catholics, Orthodox and Anglicans value set prayer highly; evangelicals and Pentecostals stress extempore prayer; most Christians use both.
Mission 4

Prayer and posture

  • Catholics believe the whole person, body and soul, prays.
  • Kneeling: humility and sorrow for sin; often during the Eucharistic Prayer.
  • Standing: respect and joy (the Gospel, the Lord's Prayer at Mass); Orthodox mostly stand.
  • Sitting: listening and reflecting.
  • Bowing and genuflecting (one knee): reverence, e.g. before the tabernacle.
  • Prostration (lying face down): total self-giving, at ordination and on Good Friday.
  • Hands: joined in prayer; raised (the orans posture) by the priest at Mass and by many charismatic Christians.
  • The Sign of the Cross: begins and ends prayer, naming the Trinity.
Quick check
?Many evangelical Christians prefer extempore prayer in their own words. Which reason best explains this?
Mission 4Evaluate

"Set prayers are more valuable than informal prayer."

  • Reasons for: Jesus gave a set prayer (Matthew 6:9–13); set prayers unite Christians across the world and through history; their words are tested and full of belief; they help when people cannot find words (grief, illness).
  • Reasons against: Jesus warned against empty repeated phrases (Matthew 6:7); informal prayer is personal and sincere; the Spirit helps believers pray (Romans 8:26); Quakers find God in silent, wordless prayer.
  • Another view: both are needed: set prayer shapes informal prayer, and informal prayer brings set words to life; the Lord's Prayer can be a pattern as well as a script.
  • Judge it by: What makes prayer "valuable": sincerity, unity, or faithfulness to Jesus' words? Can set prayers be sincere? Does it depend on the situation?
Mission 4Worked answer

Two different ways to pray

Question: Explain two different ways in which Christians pray.

  • Way 1: set prayers, such as the Lord's Prayer said together at Mass. Explained: Jesus taught these words (Matthew 6:9–13), so saying them unites Christians everywhere.
  • Way 2: informal prayer in their own words, such as at an evangelical prayer meeting. Explained: it is personal and fits the need of the moment, like speaking to a friend.
  • 4-mark tip: two contrasting ways, each explained; a source is not required but strengthens the answer.
Mission check

?A Catholic family sits at a grandparent's hospital bed. They say the Our Father together, then each prays a few words of their own for her, then sit in quiet prayer. Which statement best describes how they are praying?

Christians pray to be close to God, not to inform God: in set words, in their own words, and in silence.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Mission is the Church being sent to share God's love in word and action; evangelism is sharing the Gospel (Matthew 28:19–20).
  • Mission happens locally, nationally and globally, including new forms of church; Christians differ on how urgent conversion is.
  • Catholic sources (Evangelii Gaudium; Deus Caritas Est 19) call every Catholic to go out, moved by the Spirit and the love of Christ.

Mission 2

  • Scripture, Tradition and the Magisterium (pope and bishops in communion with him) belong together; the Magisterium serves the Word of God (CCC 100).
  • Apostolic succession: Catholics believe bishops succeed the apostles and the pope succeeds Peter.
  • Magisterium is conciliar (councils, e.g. Nicaea 325, Vatican II) or pontifical (popes, e.g. encyclicals); it can be ordinary (everyday) or extraordinary (solemn definitions).

Mission 3

  • Baptism is the sacrament (or ordinance) of initiation, with water and the name of the Trinity.
  • Godparents promise to support a baptised child.

Mission 4

  • In Christian teaching, prayer is the raising of the mind and heart to God; types: adoration, confession, thanksgiving, supplication (petition and intercession).
  • The Lord's Prayer (Matthew 6:9–13) is Jesus' model prayer: God's name, Kingdom and will first; then bread, forgiveness and protection.
  • Set (formulaic, traditional) and informal (extempore, spontaneous) prayer are valued differently across denominations.
