Internal Assessment: Historical Investigation

Every IB History candidate — SL and HL — completes the Internal Assessment (IA): a historical investigation of your own choosing, worth 20% of the final grade.

This mini-lesson walks through the three sections, the 2,200-word limit, and the skills of OPVL and historiographical reflection. Work through the checks to collect ⭐ stars, then press Start.

IA · overview

What the historical investigation is

The IA is an independent inquiry into a focused historical question you choose (it must not overlap with the syllabus you are examined on). It has a firm limit of 2,200 words and is marked out of 25, structured in three sections.

Golden rule: a sharp, answerable question is half the battle. "To what extent...", "How far...", and "What were the causes of..." questions invite analysis rather than description.

IA · Section 1

Section 1 — Identification and evaluation of sources

Around 500 words, worth 6 marks. You state your question, explain the nature and relevance of two chosen sources, and evaluate each using OPVL.

  • Origin — who, when, where, what form.
  • Purpose — why it was made, and for whom.
  • Value — why it helps answer your question.
  • Limitations — what it cannot tell you.

IA · Section 2

Section 2 — The investigation

The largest part: about 1,300 words, worth 15 marks. Here you build a critical, well-organised argument that answers your question, supported by evidence and referencing a range of sources and different historians’ interpretations (historiography). It must reach a clear, evidence-based conclusion.

Marker’s eye: analysis over narrative. Every paragraph should advance an argument, not just tell the story.

IA · Section 3

Section 3 — Reflection

About 400 words, worth 4 marks. You reflect on what doing the investigation taught you about the methods historians use and the challenges they face — for example the problems of reliability, bias, selection of evidence, or gaps in the record.

This is not a summary of your findings. It is about the nature of historical knowledge itself.

IA · sources

Choosing and citing sources

Use a range of sources — primary and secondary — and reference them properly with a consistent citation style and a bibliography (the bibliography is not counted in the word limit). Choosing two contrasting sources for Section 1 (e.g. a participant’s memoir versus a modern historian) lets you show real evaluative range.

IA · marks

How the marks add up

The three sections total 25 marks: Section 1 (6), Section 2 (15), Section 3 (4). The IA counts for 20% of the overall grade at both SL and HL, and is marked by your teacher and moderated by the IB.

Watch the count: examiners stop reading at 2,200 words, so front-load your best analysis.

IA · common pitfalls

Common pitfalls to avoid

Weak IAs usually share the same faults:

  • A question too broad to answer in 2,200 words.
  • Narrative that describes rather than argues.
  • OPVL that lists features instead of judging value and limitations.
  • A reflection that summarises findings rather than discussing method.

IA · good practice

What a strong investigation looks like

Strong IAs pose a tight question, evaluate two well-chosen sources with pointed OPVL, and then argue a case that engages competing interpretations before reaching a judgement the evidence actually supports. The reflection connects that experience to the wider craft of the historian — how we know what we claim to know.

Recap

The big ideas to take away

The IA: a 2,200-word historical investigation; 20% of the grade; marked out of 25

Section 1: identify the question and evaluate two sources with OPVL (6 marks)

Section 2: a critical, referenced investigation with a conclusion (15 marks)

Section 3: reflect on the methods and challenges of the historian (4 marks)

Key skill: argue and evaluate — do not merely narrate
