Relative pronouns

Gender from the noun, case from the role

A relative pronoun takes its gender and number from the noun it refers back to, but its case from the job it does inside the relative clause. The verb goes to the end, and the clause is fenced off with commas:

  • Der Mann, der dort steht … — subject → nominative.
  • Der Mann, den ich sehe … — object → accusative.
  • Der Mann, dem ich helfe … — helfen + dative.
  • Der Mann, dessen Auto rot ist … — possession → genitive.

Two steps every time: (1) which noun? → fixes gender/number; (2) what does the pronoun do in the clause? → fixes the case.

was, wo & prepositions

Beyond der/die/das

Three refinements complete the system:

  • was — after alles, etwas, nichts, das, vieles or referring to a whole clause: Alles, was er sagt, ist wahr.
  • wo — for places: Die Stadt, wo ich wohne (or in der ich wohne).
  • Preposition + pronoun: the preposition sets the case — Der Freund, mit dem ich spreche; Das Haus, in dem ich wohne.

Word order stays firm: whatever opens the relative clause, the finite verb still lands at the very end.

Essay register

Connectors & nominalisation

To lift an essay into the top band, deploy discourse markers and noun-heavy phrasing:

  • Connectors: jedoch (however), dennoch / trotzdem (nevertheless), außerdem (moreover), einerseits … andererseits (on the one hand … on the other), zusammenfassend (in summary).
  • Stance: meiner Meinung nach, es lässt sich argumentieren, dass …, es ist unbestreitbar, dass …
  • Nominalisation: prefer die Entwicklung, die Auswirkung, die Zunahme over long verb strings — it reads as more academic.

Remember the syntax: jedoch and trotzdem take normal V2/inversion, while obwohl is subordinating (verb to the end).

Translation traps

English → German pitfalls

The translation questions reward accuracy on a handful of recurring traps:

  • Word order: keep the finite verb in position 2 in main clauses and at the end after weil/dass/obwohl.
  • Separable verbs: split them in main clauses (Ich rufe dich an).
  • Tense mapping: English present perfect with "since/for" → German present + seit (Ich wohne seit drei Jahren hier).
  • False friends: see the next screen — aktuell, bekommen, also, Gift.

Method: translate the meaning, not word-for-word; identify the verb and its position first, then slot the rest around it.

Your relative-clause & technique toolkit

Relative pronouns: gender/number from the noun, case from the role; verb to the end.

Special forms: dative plural denen; genitives dessen / deren.

was / wo / prep + pronoun: alles, was …; die Stadt, wo …; mit dem, in dem.
