Mission 1

"Immediately they left their nets"

  • By the Sea of Galilee Jesus sees Simon (Peter) and Andrew fishing.
  • "Come after me, and I will make you into fishers for men." (Mark 1:17)
  • "Immediately they left their nets, and followed him." (Mark 1:18)
  • James and John leave their father Zebedee in the boat with the hired men (1:19–20).
  • Matthew 4:18–22 tells the same call; James and John "immediately left the boat and their father" (Matthew 4:22).
  • Normally a student chose a rabbi. Here Jesus chooses them.
Mission 1

The call of Levi

  • Jesus sees Levi, a tax collector, at his booth: "Follow me." Levi gets up and follows (Mark 2:14).
  • Tax collectors worked for Rome or Herod Antipas, often overcharged, and were seen as traitors and unclean.
  • Jesus eats at Levi's house with "tax collectors and sinners". Scribes and Pharisees object.
  • "Those who are healthy have no need for a physician, but those who are sick. I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance." (Mark 2:17)
  • In Matthew 9:9–13 he is named Matthew. Jesus adds Hosea 6:6: "I desire mercy, and not sacrifice" (Matthew 9:13).
  • Discipleship is open to outsiders: no one is too far gone to be called.

Mission 1

Jesus chooses the Twelve

  • On a mountain Jesus "appointed twelve, that they might be with him, and that he might send them out to preach" (Mark 3:14).
  • They receive authority to heal and to cast out demons (3:15).
  • Twelve recalls the twelve tribes: a renewed Israel.
  • The list (3:16–19): Simon, given the name Peter ("rock"); James and John, "Sons of Thunder"; Andrew; Philip; Bartholomew; Matthew; Thomas; James son of Alphaeus; Thaddaeus; Simon the Zealot; Judas Iscariot, "who also betrayed him".
  • Two purposes: to be with him (relationship) and to be sent (mission). "Apostle" means "one who is sent".
Mission 1

Rejected at Nazareth

  • Jesus teaches in his home town's synagogue. People are amazed, then offended.
  • "Isn't this the carpenter, the son of Mary…?" (Mark 6:3): they think they know him too well.
  • "A prophet is not without honour, except in his own country, and amongst his own relatives, and in his own house." (Mark 6:4)
  • "He could do no mighty work there", except heal a few sick people (6:5).
  • He "marvelled because of their unbelief" (6:6): in Mark, miracles are often linked to faith.
  • The challenge of witnessing: family, friends and neighbours can be the hardest people to tell about faith.

Mission 1Read the source

Sent out two by two

"He called to himself the twelve, and began to send them out two by two; and he gave them authority over the unclean spirits. He commanded them that they should take nothing for their journey, except a staff only: no bread, no wallet, no money in their purse." (Mark 6:7–8)

  • They may wear sandals but not two tunics; they stay in one house; where rejected, they "shake off the dust" from their feet (6:9–11).
  • They preach repentance, cast out demons and anoint the sick with oil (6:12–13).

Q1 What are the Twelve allowed to take?

Show answer

A staff and sandals, and one tunic.

Q2 What three things do they do?

Show answer

Preach repentance, cast out demons, heal the sick.

Mission 1

What the mission shows

  • Shared authority: Jesus' power to teach and heal is passed to his followers.
  • Dependence: taking nothing means trusting God and the hospitality of others.
  • Urgency: travel light, move on; there is no time to waste.
  • Two by two: support and a reliable witness (two witnesses in Jewish law, Deuteronomy 19:15).
  • Preach and serve: words (repentance) and actions (healing) go together.
  • Rejection is expected: shaking off dust is a sign, not revenge: the message was offered.

Mission 1Diversity

Following the call today

  • Religious orders take the mission of the Twelve literally: Franciscans and others vow poverty and depend on others.
  • Many Protestants stress that every Christian is called where they are (the "priesthood of all believers"): at work, at school, at home.
  • Evangelical and Pentecostal Christians stress telling others about Jesus; Pentecostals also pray for healing.
  • In today's pluralist society, many Christians witness through service and example more than street preaching.
  • Humanists admire commitment and service but question leaving family and work for a belief they see as unproven.
Mission 1Evaluate

"Following Jesus today demands as much as it did of the first disciples."

  • Reasons for: Jesus' call is still to put him first (Mark 1:18); some Christians today face persecution or prison; religious orders still give up possessions and family life.
  • Reasons against: most Christians do not leave job or family; in Western countries, faith rarely costs freedom; Jesus was physically present then.
  • Another view: the demand is the same (whole-hearted commitment), but the form differs for each person.
  • Judge it by: Is the "cost" outward (things given up) or inward (priorities)? Where in the world are you looking?

Mission 2

Salt, light and the cross

  • The Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5–7) gathers Jesus' teaching for disciples.
  • "You are the salt of the earth" (Matthew 5:13): salt preserves and flavours; disciples keep the world from going bad.
  • "You are the light of the world." (Matthew 5:14) A lamp goes on a stand, not under a basket.
  • "Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven." (Matthew 5:16)
  • The demand: "If anyone desires to come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me." (Matthew 16:24)
  • Discipleship is visible (good works) and costly (the cross).
Mission 2

Anger (5:21–26)

  • "You have heard that it was said… 'You shall not murder'". Jesus goes deeper: anger and insult are already in danger of judgement (5:21–22).
  • Raca ("empty-head") and "fool": contempt for another person.
  • If you remember at the altar that someone has something against you: "First be reconciled to your brother, and then come and offer your gift." (Matthew 5:24)
  • Settle disputes quickly, before they reach court (5:25).
  • Point: God looks at the heart, not only actions. Reconciliation comes before worship.
Mission 2

Revenge (5:38–42)

  • "You have heard that it was said, 'An eye for an eye, and a tooth for a tooth.'" (Matthew 5:38; Exodus 21:24)
  • In the Law this limited revenge: no more than the harm done. Later Jewish courts applied it as payment of compensation.
  • Jesus: "whoever strikes you on your right cheek, turn to him the other also." (Matthew 5:39)
  • Give your cloak as well as your coat; go the second mile (a Roman soldier could force a civilian to carry his pack for one mile).
  • Point: break the cycle of revenge; do not answer evil with evil.
Mission 2Read the source

Love your enemies

"But I tell you, love your enemies, bless those who curse you, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who mistreat you and persecute you, that you may be children of your Father who is in heaven. For he makes his sun to rise on the evil and the good, and sends rain on the just and the unjust." (Matthew 5:44–45)

  • "Love your neighbour" is in the Law (Leviticus 19:18). "Hate your enemy" is not found in the Hebrew Bible: many scholars think it reflects a popular attitude Jesus challenges.
  • Even tax collectors love those who love them (5:46).

Show answer

Mission 2

Religious observance (6:1–18)

  • Three Jewish practices: giving to the poor, prayer, fasting.
  • The warning: "don't do your charitable giving before men, to be seen by them" (Matthew 6:1).
  • Hypocrites do them for show: they "have received their reward" (praise from people).
  • Giving: in secret: "don't let your left hand know what your right hand does" (6:3).
  • Prayer: in your inner room, without empty, showy words; Jesus teaches the Lord's Prayer (6:9–13).
  • Fasting: wash your face; let God, not people, see it (6:17–18).
  • Point: the motive matters. God "sees in secret".
Mission 2

Forgiveness (18:21–35)

  • Peter: should I forgive "until seven times?" Jesus: "until seventy times seven" (Matthew 18:21–22): without limit.
  • The Unforgiving Servant: a king cancels a servant's debt of ten thousand talents (more than a lifetime of wages, many times over).
  • The same servant throttles a fellow servant over one hundred denarii (about a hundred days' wages) and has him jailed.
  • The king: "Shouldn't you also have had mercy on your fellow servant, even as I had mercy on you?" (Matthew 18:33)
  • Point: those forgiven by God must forgive others "from your hearts" (18:35); compare "Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors" (6:12).
Quick check

Mission 2

Caesar's coin (22:15–22)

  • Pharisees and Herodians set a trap: "Is it lawful to pay taxes to Caesar, or not?" (Matthew 22:17)
  • Say yes: he seems to support hated Roman rule. Say no: he can be reported to Rome as a rebel.
  • Jesus asks for a denarius (the coin for the tax), stamped with Caesar's image.
  • "Give therefore to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's." (Matthew 22:21)
  • Point: Christians have duties to the state (taxes, laws) and to God; many Christians add that humans bear God's image, so they belong to God first.
Mission 2

The rich young man (19:16–26)

  • A young man asks what good thing to do to gain eternal life. Jesus: keep the commandments. He has.
  • "If you want to be perfect, go, sell what you have, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me." (Matthew 19:21)
  • He goes away sad: he had great possessions.
  • "It is easier for a camel to go through a needle's eye than for a rich man to enter into God's Kingdom." (Matthew 19:24)
  • The disciples: "Who then can be saved?" Jesus: "with God all things are possible" (19:25–26).
  • Point: wealth can come between a person and God; salvation is God's gift.
Mission 2Diversity

How Christians live it

  • Revenge and enemies: Quakers and Mennonites are pacifist, taking 5:39–44 literally. Catholic, Anglican and many other Christians accept a just war as a last resort, but still forbid personal revenge.
  • Turn the other cheek: some read it as passive acceptance; others as non-violent resistance (as Martin Luther King Jr, 1929–1968, taught).
  • Forgiveness: after the Enniskillen bomb (1987), Gordon Wilson, whose daughter Marie was killed, said he bore the bombers no ill will and would pray for them. Some Christians say forgiving does not mean giving up justice or staying in danger.
  • Wealth: monks, nuns and friars give up possessions; many Christians see 19:21 as one man's call and stress generous stewardship.
  • Observance: set prayers and fasts (Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican) or free prayer (many Protestants): both aim at sincerity, not show.
Mission 2Evaluate

"The Sermon on the Mount is too demanding to live by today."

  • Reasons for: "be perfect" (5:48) is impossible; non-retaliation could leave victims unprotected; anger cannot simply be switched off.
  • Reasons against: people do forgive, give in secret and love enemies; it is an aim to grow towards; God's grace helps ("with God all things are possible", 19:26).
  • Another view: it describes the Kingdom's values, showing how far people fall short and why they need God's forgiveness.
  • Judge it by: Is it a rule book or an ideal? Is it for individuals only, or also for governments? Does failing to reach it make it worthless?

Mission 3

Who gets in?

  • On the road to Jerusalem (Mark 10) and in the Temple (Mark 12), Jesus teaches what it takes to enter God's Kingdom.
  • Many assumed the Kingdom belonged to the important: the respected, the rich, the religious experts.
  • Jesus turns this upside down: "many who are first will be last, and the last first" (10:31).
  • Entry is God's gift, received with trust; it also asks a response: following Jesus and loving God and neighbour.
  • Three scenes: children, a rich man, and a scribe who asks about the greatest commandment.
Mission 3

Jesus and the children

  • People bring little children for Jesus to touch; the disciples rebuke them (10:13).
  • Children then had little status: not yet able to keep the Law or contribute.
  • Jesus is indignant: "Allow the little children to come to me! Don't forbid them, for God's Kingdom belongs to such as these." (Mark 10:14)
  • "Whoever will not receive God's Kingdom like a little child, he will in no way enter into it." (Mark 10:15)
  • He takes them in his arms and blesses them (10:16).
  • Like a child: trusting, dependent, receiving a gift without claiming to have earned it.

Mission 3

The rich man

  • A man runs, kneels: "Good Teacher, what shall I do that I may inherit eternal life?" (Mark 10:17)
  • Jesus lists commandments about others: do not murder, commit adultery, steal, lie, defraud; honour your parents (10:19).
  • "Teacher, I have observed all these things from my youth." (Mark 10:20)
  • "Jesus looking at him loved him" (10:21).
  • "One thing you lack. Go, sell whatever you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me" (10:21).
  • "His face fell at that saying, and he went away sorrowful, for he was one who had great possessions." (Mark 10:22)
  • In Matthew he is a "young man", and Jesus says: "If you want to be perfect, go, sell what you have, and give to the poor" (Matthew 19:21).

The camel and the needle

"It is easier for a camel to go through a needle's eye than for a rich man to enter into God's Kingdom." (Mark 10:25)

"Jesus, looking at them, said, 'With men it is impossible, but not with God, for all things are possible with God.'" (Mark 10:27)

  • The largest animal they knew, the smallest opening: a deliberate exaggeration.

Mission 3

Leaving everything, gaining more

  • Peter: "Behold, we have left all and have followed you." (Mark 10:28)
  • Jesus: anyone who leaves home, family or land for him and the Good News will receive "one hundred times more now in this time … with persecutions; and in the age to come eternal life" (10:29–30).
  • Now: a new family in the community of believers, though with suffering.
  • Future: eternal life in the age to come.
  • "Many who are first will be last, and the last first." (Mark 10:31)

Mission 3Diversity

Must Christians sell everything?

  • Literal, for some: monks, nuns and friars take a vow of poverty. St Antony of Egypt and St Francis of Assisi gave away everything after hearing Jesus' words to the rich man (Matthew 19:21).
  • Personal command: many Christians say this was what this man needed: his wealth held him back; each person must let go of whatever comes first.
  • Stewardship: wealth is held on trust from God, to be used generously for others (tithing, charity, fair business).
  • Prosperity teaching: some preachers say God rewards faith with wealth; most churches reject this as contrary to this passage.
  • The "needle gate" in Jerusalem, sometimes used to soften the saying, is a later legend.
Mission 3

The greatest commandment

  • A scribe asks: "Which commandment is the greatest of all?" (12:28)
  • Jesus quotes the Shema, said daily by devout Jews (Deuteronomy 6:4–5):
  • "Hear, Israel, the Lord our God, the Lord is one. You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, with all your mind, and with all your strength." (Mark 12:29–30)
  • And adds Leviticus 19:18: "You shall love your neighbour as yourself." (Mark 12:31)
  • The scribe agrees: love matters "more … than all whole burnt offerings and sacrifices" (12:33).
  • Jesus: "You are not far from God's Kingdom." (Mark 12:34) A friendly scribe: not every religious leader opposed Jesus.

Mission 3

Love in practice

  • Agape: self-giving love that seeks the good of others, not a feeling only.
  • Who is my neighbour? In Luke's Good Samaritan (Luke 10:25–37), anyone in need, even an enemy.
  • Love of God and love of neighbour cannot be separated (1 John 4:20).
  • Pluralist society: Christians work with people of all faiths and none in care, justice and peace.
  • Non-religious view: Humanists hold the Golden Rule (treat others as you would want to be treated) without belief in God.
Mission 3Evaluate

"It is impossible to be rich and a true follower of Jesus."

  • Reasons for: "Go, sell whatever you have" (Mark 10:21); the camel and the needle (10:25); "You can't serve both God and Mammon" (Matthew 6:24); wealth breeds trust in riches (10:24).
  • Reasons against: "all things are possible with God" (10:27); Jesus' command was to one man whose wealth held him back; Joseph of Arimathea was "a rich man" and "also Jesus' disciple" (Matthew 27:57); wealth can do great good if shared.
  • Another view: the issue is not owning wealth but trusting in it (10:24).
  • Judge it by: Is the command for everyone or for this man? How rich is "rich"? What do Christians do with wealth, not just how much they have?

Mission 4Read the source

Take up your cross

"Whoever wants to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me. For whoever wants to save his life will lose it; and whoever will lose his life for my sake and the sake of the Good News will save it." (Mark 8:34–35)

  • Spoken just after Peter calls Jesus the Christ and is rebuked for rejecting the idea of a suffering Messiah (8:29–33).
  • Deny himself: put God's will before one's own wants.
  • Take up his cross: a condemned man carried his own cross: be ready for shame, suffering, even death.

Mission 4

Cost and rewards

  • "For what does it profit a man to gain the whole world and forfeit his life?" (Mark 8:36)
  • Whoever is ashamed of Jesus, the Son of Man will be ashamed of when he comes in glory (8:38).
  • "Many who are first will be last, and the last first." (10:31)
Mission 4

Coping with temptation

  • Causing "one of these little ones who believe" to stumble: better to be thrown into the sea with a millstone round your neck (Mark 9:42).
  • If your hand, foot or eye causes you to sin, cut it off or throw it out (9:43–47).
  • This is hyperbole (deliberate exaggeration): almost no Christian reads it as a command to harm the body.
  • Point: remove whatever leads you into sin (habits, places, company, apps), however precious.
  • Gehenna: a valley outside Jerusalem, once linked to child sacrifice and later pictured as the place of punishment.
  • "Have salt in yourselves, and be at peace with one another." (Mark 9:50)

Mission 4

Three predictions, three misunderstandings

  • On the journey to Jerusalem (8:27–10:52), Jesus predicts his death three times. Each time the disciples misunderstand, and he teaches about discipleship:
  • 8:31 → Peter rebukes him → "take up his cross" (8:34).
  • 9:31 → they argue who is greatest → "servant of all" (9:35).
  • 10:33–34 → James and John ask for top seats → "came not to be served but to serve" (10:45).
  • The third is the fullest: handed to the chief priests, condemned, given to the Gentiles, mocked, spat on, flogged, killed; "On the third day he will rise again." (Mark 10:34)
  • The disciples "were amazed; and those who followed were afraid" (10:32).
Mission 4

James and John's request

  • "Grant to us that we may sit, one at your right hand and one at your left hand, in your glory." (Mark 10:37)
  • They expect a glorious kingdom now; they want status.
  • Jesus: "Are you able to drink the cup that I drink, and to be baptised with the baptism that I am baptised with?" (10:38)
  • Cup and baptism are images of the suffering ahead.
  • They say "We are able". Jesus says they will share his suffering, but the seats are not his to give (10:39–40).
  • Irony: the places at his right and left at the cross are taken by two criminals (15:27).
Mission 4

Greatness is service

  • The other ten are angry with James and John (10:41).
  • Gentile rulers "lord it over" people. "But it shall not be so amongst you, but whoever wants to become great amongst you shall be your servant." (Mark 10:43)
  • "For the Son of Man also came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." (Mark 10:45)
  • Ransom: the price paid to free a slave or prisoner. Christians believe Jesus' death frees people from sin.
  • Today: leaders as servants (the Pope's title "Servant of the servants of God"); foot-washing on Maundy Thursday; service in food banks, hospitals, care work.
Mission 4

Bartimaeus: the model disciple

  • Straight after, at Jericho, the blind beggar Bartimaeus calls out: "Jesus, you son of David, have mercy on me!" (Mark 10:47)
  • Jesus asks him the same question he asked James and John: "What do you want me to do for you?" (10:36, 10:51)
  • They asked for glory; he asks to see.
  • He throws off his cloak (perhaps all he owned), is healed, and "followed Jesus on the way" (10:52).
  • Mark sets the blind man who sees beside the disciples who do not yet "see".

Mission 4

Peter's denials

  • After the Last Supper, on the way to the Mount of Olives, Jesus predicts that all will fall away. Peter: "Although all will be offended, yet I will not." (Mark 14:29)
  • Jesus: before the rooster crows twice, you will deny me three times (14:30). Peter: "If I must die with you, I will not deny you." (14:31)
  • In the high priest's courtyard, a servant girl and bystanders challenge Peter three times; his Galilean accent gives him away (14:66–70).
  • Third time he curses and swears: "I don't know this man of whom you speak!" (Mark 14:71)
  • The rooster crows a second time. Peter remembers, and weeps (14:72).
  • Matthew 26:69–75: the rooster crows once; Peter "went out and wept bitterly" (Matthew 26:75).
  • Jesus, meanwhile, inside, openly says "I am" (14:62). The contrast is deliberate.
Mission 4Diversity

Example and warning

  • Warning: even the leading disciple failed under pressure. Fear of others can silence any Christian.
  • Example: Peter repents (he weeps) and is restored: the angel says "tell his disciples and Peter" (Mark 16:7). He becomes a leader of the Church.
  • Many Protestants stress that every believer, like Peter, is saved by grace, not by never failing.
  • Then and now: the first disciples gave up home and work and faced death; most 21st-century Christians face pressure at school or work; some face prison or death today.
Quick check

Mission 4Evaluate

"In Mark, the disciples are more a warning than an example for Christians today."

  • Reasons for: they misunderstand all three predictions; James and John seek status; all desert Jesus at his arrest (14:50); Peter denies him with an oath.
  • Reasons against: they leave everything at once (1:18; 10:28); they preach and heal (6:12–13); Peter repents and is restored (16:7); tradition says Peter, James and others died as martyrs.
  • Another view: Mark shows both on purpose: failure is real, but so is forgiveness, so ordinary Christians can identify with them.
  • Judge it by: Does Mark's story end with failure or restoration? Is a disciple defined by mistakes or by what follows them?

Mission 5

The Great Commission (Matthew 28:16–20)

  • The eleven meet Jesus on a mountain in Galilee. "They bowed down to him; but some doubted." (28:17)
  • "All authority has been given to me in heaven and on earth." (28:18)
  • "Go and make disciples of all nations, baptising them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19).
  • Teach them to obey all Jesus commanded. "Behold, I am with you always, even to the end of the age." (Matthew 28:20)
  • Today: mission, baptism in the name of the Trinity, teaching; in a pluralist society many Christians share faith by example, dialogue and service as well as words.

Mission 5Diversity

Many ways to be a disciple

  • Heroic witness: a few Christians are called to risk their lives, like Bonhoeffer (killed by the Nazis, 1945) and Romero (shot in El Salvador, 1980).
  • Everyday discipleship: most follow Jesus through family, work, honesty, prayer and service in their own community.
  • Vocations: priests, ministers, monks and nuns, missionaries.
  • Evangelical Christians stress telling others about Jesus; Catholic social teaching and many other Christians stress action for justice; most hold both.
  • Persecuted Christians today: in some countries, being baptised can cost family, work or freedom.
  • Non-religious view: Humanists can admire these lives of courage and service, but say they do not need religious faith.
Mission 5Evaluate

"To be a true disciple today, a Christian must be ready to die for their faith."

  • Reasons for: Jesus says "take up his cross" (Mark 8:34; Matthew 16:24); Bonhoeffer and Romero did die; some Christians face that choice today.
  • Reasons against: most disciples serve in ordinary ways; Jesus calls people to serve (Mark 10:45) and to make disciples (Matthew 28:19), not to seek death; Mother Teresa's witness was a long life of service.
  • Another view: "ready" is the key word: every disciple must put Christ first, even if few are ever asked to die.
  • Judge it by: Is readiness the same as action? Where in the world, and in what kind of society, is the Christian living?
Mission check

Discipleship today takes many forms, but the core demand is the same: put Christ first, whatever it costs.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Fishermen (Mark 1:16–20) and a tax collector (2:13–17) respond at once: Jesus calls ordinary people and outsiders.
  • The Twelve are sent two by two with nothing, to preach repentance and heal (6:7–13).
  • At Nazareth, familiarity and unbelief block Jesus' work (6:1–6).

Mission 2

  • Disciples are salt and light, and take up the cross (5:13–16; 16:24).
  • Jesus deepens the Law: no anger or contempt, no revenge, love enemies (5:21–48).
  • Give, pray and fast in secret; forgive without limit; give to God what is God's; wealth can block the way to the Kingdom.

Mission 3

  • The Kingdom belongs to those who receive it like children: with trust, as a gift.
  • The rich man kept the commandments but could not let go of his wealth; "all things are possible with God".
  • The greatest commandments are to love God with everything and your neighbour as yourself.

Mission 4

  • Cost: deny yourself, take up your cross (8:34); rewards: a new family "with persecutions" and eternal life (10:28–31).
  • Three predictions, three misunderstandings; the answer to the third is service: "not to be served but to serve" (10:45).
  • Peter's three denials (14:66–72) are a warning and, with his restoration (16:7), an example.

Mission 5

  • Matthew 28:16–20: make disciples of all nations, baptise, teach; "I am with you always".
