Mission 1

Mission 1: France tried to keep under-15s off social media in 2026. What stopped it, and who is right?

Make a guess: France tried to keep under-15s off social media in 2026. What stopped it, and who is right?

Mission 1

Who are the cybernauts?

  • Un(e) cybernaute / un(e) internaute – an internet user.
  • Baromètre du numérique 2026 (people aged 12+, surveyed in 2025):
  • 91% own a smartphone.
  • 86% have a home internet subscription; 86% use instant messaging.
  • 48% use generative AI, up 15 points in one year.
  • But not everyone is connected: l'illectronisme – lacking the skills or the access to use the internet. INSEE: 15.4% of people aged 15+ in 2021, mostly older people and people on low incomes.
  • la fracture numérique – the digital divide
  • être connecté(e) – to be online · un écran – a screen · une appli(cation) – an app
Mission 1

How technology makes daily life easier

  • Public services online (les démarches en ligne): tax returns, ID applications, benefits.
  • Health: booking a doctor online; video consultations (la téléconsultation).
  • Work and study: le télétravail; online school platforms (e.g. Pronote) for marks, homework and messages to parents.
  • Money: mobile banking, contactless payment.
  • Staying in touch: messaging and video calls with family far away.
  • Grâce à Internet, on gagne du temps. – Thanks to the internet, we save time.
  • Tout est à portée de main. – Everything is within reach.
  • Watch out: grâce à (thanks to, positive) vs à cause de (because of, negative).
Quick check
?Ma grand-mère est en situation d'illectronisme. What does this mean?
Mission 1

The dangers

  • Le cyberharcèlement – online harassment. A criminal offense in France. Young victims can call 3018, a free national helpline.
  • Les infox / les fausses informations – fake news; la désinformation.
  • La dépendance aux écrans – screen addiction; effects on sleep, attention and mood are widely debated.
  • Les données personnelles – personal data. The EU's GDPR (le RGPD) sets rules; the CNIL enforces them in France.
  • Les arnaques en ligne – online scams, e.g. l'hameçonnage (phishing); l'usurpation d'identité – identity theft.
  • Les discours de haine – hate speech; illegal content can be reported on the state platform Pharos.
  • signaler un contenu – to report content · protéger sa vie privée – to protect one's privacy
Mission 1

Protecting young people: the 2026 law

  • 2018: phones banned in écoles and collèges.
  • 2023: a law sets a "digital majority" at 15 (parental consent for younger users). It never came into force.
  • 2026: Parliament votes to ban social media for under-15s, with age checks for all users.
  • 14 August 2026: the Conseil constitutionnel strikes the ban down. It finds it a disproportionate limit on freedom of expression and communication, and says the compulsory age checks lacked safeguards for privacy.
  • 24 August 2026: the rest of the law is promulgated. Phones are now banned in lycées too (from September 2026), and schools must teach about screen risks.
  • The government says it is aiming for a new framework by spring 2027.
  • le Conseil constitutionnel censure la loi – strikes down the law · promulguer – to sign into law
Quick check
?Which statement about the 2026 law is accurate?
Mission 1

Vocabulary bank

  • la cyber-société – the cyber-society
  • les réseaux sociaux – social media
  • naviguer sur Internet – to browse the internet
  • télécharger – to download
  • un mot de passe – a password
  • la vie privée – privacy
  • une infox – a piece of fake news
  • l'intelligence artificielle (IA) – artificial intelligence
  • un algorithme – an algorithm
  • la vérification de l'âge – age verification
  • la liberté d'expression – freedom of expression
  • l'éducation aux médias – media literacy
Mission 1

Faut-il interdire les réseaux sociaux aux moins de 15 ans ? Argue both sides

  • Arguments made by supporters of a ban:
  • Les réseaux sociaux nuisent au sommeil et à la santé mentale des adolescents. – Social media harm teenagers' sleep and mental health.
  • Les plateformes sont conçues pour capter l'attention. – Platforms are designed to capture attention.
  • Les parents ne peuvent pas tout contrôler seuls. – Parents cannot control everything on their own.
  • Arguments made by opponents:
  • Une interdiction générale porte atteinte à la liberté d'expression. – A general ban harms freedom of expression.
  • Vérifier l'âge de tous les utilisateurs menace la vie privée. – Checking every user's age threatens privacy.
  • Mieux vaut éduquer qu'interdire. – Better to educate than to ban.
  • Balance: La question n'est pas seulement de savoir s'il faut interdire, mais comment protéger. – The question is not only whether to ban, but how to protect.
Quick check
?Choose the best French for: "If social media were banned, young people would find other ways."
Mission 1

Model sentences and exam tasks

  • Il faut que les plateformes protègent davantage les mineurs. (il faut que + subjunctive)
  • Bien qu'Internet nous facilite la vie, il nous rend parfois dépendants. (bien que + subjunctive; rendre + adjective)
  • Ce que les jeunes craignent le plus, c'est d'être exclus du groupe. (cleft for emphasis)
  • Project Presentation angle: how France tried to regulate teenagers' social media use and the cultural perspectives on childhood and freedom behind the debate. Argumentative Essay prompt: Faut-il interdire les réseaux sociaux aux moins de 15 ans ?

Mission 2

Mission 2: From Marie Curie to AI: who decides what science should be allowed to do?

Make a guess: From Marie Curie to AI: who decides what science should be allowed to do?

Mission 2

French science and innovation

  • 1885: Louis Pasteur gives the first rabies vaccine to a human.
  • 1903 and 1911: Marie Curie wins the Nobel Prize in Physics (shared), then in Chemistry: the first person to win two.
  • 1954: CERN, the European particle physics laboratory, is founded; it straddles the French–Swiss border near Geneva.
  • 1979–1982: first Ariane rocket launch (1979); TGV high-speed train (1981); Minitel (1982), an online service before the internet.
  • 1983: Françoise Barré-Sinoussi and Luc Montagnier identify HIV (Nobel Prize 2008).
  • 2020: Emmanuelle Charpentier shares the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the CRISPR gene-editing tool.
  • une découverte · une invention · un chercheur / une chercheuse · la recherche
Mission 2

Vocabulary bank

  • l'intelligence artificielle (l'IA) · un algorithme · les données (f) – data
  • la vie privée – privacy · la protection des données · la surveillance
  • une avancée médicale · un traitement · la greffe – transplant
  • l'éthique (f) · la bioéthique · le clonage · la modification génétique
  • la fracture numérique – the digital divide
  • un enjeu – an issue, what is at stake
  • Watch out: une expérience = an experiment or an experience; context decides.
  • Watch out: English "research" is uncountable; French often uses the plural: faire des recherches – to do research.
Quick check
?Les chercheurs ont mené une expérience sur des cellules. What did the researchers do?
Mission 2

Medical advances and bioethics

  • France updates its bioethics laws regularly (1994, 2004, 2011, 2021).
  • 2021 law:
  • medically assisted reproduction (la PMA) opened to couples of women and single women;
  • people conceived with sperm or eggs donated under the new rules (from September 2022) can learn the donor's identity at 18.
  • Banned in France: reproductive cloning; surrogacy, paid or unpaid (la gestation pour autrui, GPA).
  • End of life: after more than a year of debate, Parliament adopted a law creating a right to l'aide à mourir (assisted dying) in July 2026 (291 votes to 241): adults with a serious, incurable illness at an advanced stage, under strict conditions.
  • Medical advances: gene therapy, transplants, mRNA vaccines; questions of cost, access and consent.
  • le consentement · la dignité · un don d'organes – organ donation
Mission 2

Artificial intelligence

  • French players: Mistral AI (Paris, founded 2023); research labs in Paris and Grenoble.
  • February 2025: Paris hosts the AI Action Summit.
  • EU AI Act (in force from 2024): bans some uses judged unacceptable (e.g. social scoring); strict rules for high-risk uses (health, recruitment, justice).
  • Uses: medical diagnosis, translation, education, help for people with disabilities.
  • Risks raised: jobs, bias in data, false images and videos (les hypertrucages / deepfakes), energy use, cheating.
  • automatiser · remplacer · un biais · la désinformation
Quick check
?Complete: C'est un outil ___ les médecins détectent certains cancers plus tôt. (a tool thanks to which …)
Mission 2

Smart technology, privacy and security

  • 1978: the loi Informatique et libertés creates the CNIL, France's data-protection authority.
  • 2018: the EU's GDPR (le RGPD) applies: consent, right to access and delete your data, heavy fines.
  • Smart objects: speakers, watches, connected cars collect data on daily life.
  • Cameras: algorithmic video surveillance was trialled for the 2024 Paris Olympics; supporters cite security, critics cite privacy.
  • les objets connectés · le consentement · pirater – to hack · une cyberattaque
Mission 2

Access: technology in school and at work

  • La fracture numérique: not everyone has a device, a connection or the skills; older people and rural areas are most affected.
  • L'illectronisme: difficulty using digital tools.
  • School: pupils' mobile phones have been banned in French écoles and collèges (ages about 3–15) since 2018, and in lycées since September 2026.
  • Work: remote working (le télétravail) grew sharply from 2020; France also has a right to disconnect (le droit à la déconnexion, 2017).
  • un écran · se connecter · être joignable – to be reachable
Mission 2

Argue both sides

  • Should scientific progress have limits?
  • Sans limites éthiques, la science risque de menacer la dignité humaine. – Without ethical limits, science risks threatening human dignity.
  • Trop de règles freinent l'innovation et poussent les chercheurs à partir à l'étranger. – Too many rules slow innovation and push researchers abroad.
  • AI: threat or opportunity?
  • Supporters say: L'IA pourrait détecter des maladies plus tôt que les médecins. – AI could detect diseases earlier than doctors.
  • Critics say: Les algorithmes reproduisent les préjugés contenus dans les données. – Algorithms reproduce the prejudices in the data.
  • Linkers: Il serait naïf de croire que … · Encore faut-il que … (+ subjunctive) – But only if … · À condition que … (+ subjunctive)
Quick check
?Choose the best French for: "Provided that the data are protected, AI can help doctors."
Mission 2

Model sentences and exam tasks

  • Si les chercheurs n'avaient pas découvert le virus, on n'aurait pas pu développer de traitements. (si + pluperfect → conditional perfect; pas de after a negative)
  • Il est à craindre que l'IA ne remplace certains emplois. (il est à craindre que + subjunctive; optional ne explétif in formal style)
  • Ce n'est pas la technologie elle-même qui pose problème, mais l'usage qu'on en fait. (cleft; en = of it)
  • Argumentative Essay prompt: Faut-il limiter l'usage de l'intelligence artificielle à l'école ? Project Presentation angle: how a French-speaking country uses technology in everyday life (e.g. mobile money in West Africa).
Mission check

?Which statement is accurate?

Name the discovery, law or regulation with its date, and set benefits against ethical limits.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Cybernauts: 91% own a smartphone; 48% use generative AI; illectronisme 15.4% (2021).
  • Benefits: online public services, health, work, staying in touch. Dangers: cyberharcèlement (3018), infox, addiction, data, scams.
  • 2026: under-15 social-media ban struck down (14 August: freedom of expression, privacy); phones banned in lycées from September 2026.

Mission 2

  • French science: Pasteur 1885, Curie 1903/1911, CERN 1954, TGV 1981, HIV 1983, CRISPR Nobel 2020.
  • Rules: bioethics laws (2021: PMA for all women; GPA still banned); assisted-dying law 2026; CNIL 1978, GDPR 2018; EU AI Act 2024.
  • Debates: limits on science; AI threat or opportunity; digital divide; phones banned in collèges since 2018 and lycées since 2026.
