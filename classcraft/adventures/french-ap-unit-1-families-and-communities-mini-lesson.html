Mission 1

Mission 1: Six babies in ten in France are born to unmarried parents. What has happened to the family?

Make a guess: Six babies in ten in France are born to unmarried parents. What has happened to the family?

Mission 1

Three ways to be a couple

  • Le mariage: a civil ceremony at the town hall (la mairie). Only the civil marriage is legally valid; a religious wedding may follow it, never replace it.
  • Le PACS (pacte civil de solidarité, since 1999): a contract between two adults, of the same or different sex. Simpler to sign and to end than a marriage.
  • L'union libre (also le concubinage): living together with no legal tie.
  • 2025: 251,000 marriages, of which about 7,000 between two people of the same sex.
  • 2024: 197,200 PACS (about 10,400 same-sex; latest year available). Marriage is still more common.
  • New trends: living together before marrying is now the norm; some couples stay together without living together (les couples non cohabitants).
  • se marier (avec) – to get married (to) · se pacser – to sign a PACS · vivre en couple – to live as a couple
Mission 1

Laws that changed the family

  • 1975: divorce by mutual consent (le divorce par consentement mutuel) becomes possible.
  • 1999: the PACS is created.
  • 2013: le mariage pour tous – same-sex couples can marry and adopt (law of 17 May 2013). The bill was fiercely debated, with large demonstrations both for and against.
  • 2017: a divorce by mutual consent normally no longer needs a judge: the spouses sign an agreement drawn up by their lawyers.
  • 2021: medically assisted reproduction (la PMA) is opened to female couples and single women (bioethics law of 2 August 2021).
  • Pattern: the law has followed changes in how people live, and each change was debated.

Mission 1

New family shapes

  • INSEE (France's statistics office), 2020, families with children under 18:
  • One in four is a single-parent family (une famille monoparentale): 25%, double the 1990 share (12%). About four in five are headed by the mother.
  • 9% are blended families (des familles recomposées): a couple living with at least one child from a previous relationship.
  • Children under 18: 67% live with both parents, 22% in a single-parent family, 11% in a blended family.
  • Une famille homoparentale: a family with two parents of the same sex.
  • un beau-père / une belle-mère – a stepfather / stepmother (also father-/mother-in-law)
  • un demi-frère / une demi-sœur – a half-brother / half-sister (everyday French also uses it for a stepbrother or stepsister)
  • Watch out: the adjectives agree with famille: une famille monoparentale / recomposée, des familles monoparentales / recomposées. Une famille nombreuse = a large family (3+ children), not a numerous one.
Mission 1

Fewer babies, born later

  • 2025: 645,000 births and 651,000 deaths. For the first time since the Second World War, France had more deaths than births.
  • Fertility: 1.56 children per woman (1.61 in 2024). The generations are no longer fully replaced (about 2.1 needed).
  • Women have children later: average age at childbirth 31.2.
  • Marriage and children are no longer linked: about six births in ten (59.7% in 2024) are to unmarried parents.
  • Across the French-speaking world: in Quebec, over four couples in ten (42.7% in 2021) live in union libre, against 16.9% in the rest of Canada.

Mission 1

Grandparents, parents and children

  • People live longer: life expectancy in 2025, 85.9 for women and 80.3 for men. Many children know their great-grandparents.
  • Grandparents often look after grandchildren (garder les petits-enfants) and help adult children with money or housing.
  • La génération pivot (sandwich generation): adults in their 50s and 60s who support their children and also care for aging parents. Un(e) aidant(e) – an unpaid carer.
  • Un EHPAD – a care home for dependent older people. Whether to move a parent there is a common family dilemma.
  • Concerns: le fossé des générations (the generation gap) over screens, values and work; young adults who stay longer at home because of the cost of housing; conflict after separation (custody, la garde alternée – shared custody).
  • s'entendre bien avec – to get on well with · se disputer – to argue · soutenir – to support

Mission 1

La famille traditionnelle est-elle en crize ? Argue both sides

  • Arguments made by those who see a crisis or a loss:
  • Les séparations fragilisent les enfants. – Separations make children more vulnerable.
  • Le mariage protégeait mieux les conjoints et les enfants. – Marriage protected spouses and children better.
  • La baisse de la natalité fait craindre pour l'avenir des retraites. – The falling birth rate raises fears about future pensions.
  • Arguments made by those who see change, not crisis:
  • La famille n'a pas disparu, elle s'est diversifiée. – The family has not disappeared; it has diversified.
  • Ce qui compte, c'est l'amour et la stabilité, pas la forme de la famille. – What matters is love and stability, not the shape of the family.
  • Les liens entre générations restent très forts. – Ties between generations remain very strong.
  • Balance: Plutôt qu'une crise, on assiste à une transformation. – Rather than a crisis, we are seeing a transformation.

Mission 1

Model sentences and exam tasks

  • Bien que le nombre de mariages ait baissé, la plupart des Français restent attachés à la vie de couple. (bien que + perfect subjunctive)
  • Si le PACS n'avait pas été créé, beaucoup de couples ne se seraient jamais engagés officiellement. (si + pluperfect → conditional perfect)
  • Ce sont souvent les grands-parents qui gardent les enfants pendant les vacances. (c'est … qui for emphasis)
  • Project Presentation angle: how families in France and in Quebec define a couple (marriage, PACS, union libre) and the perspectives behind those choices. Argumentative Essay prompt: Le mariage est-il encore nécessaire pour fonder une famille ?

Mission 2

Mission 2: One person in four in France gives time for free to an association. Who gains the most?

Make a guess: One person in four in France gives time for free to an association. Who gains the most?

Mission 2

Bénévole, volontaire, salarié

  • Le bénévolat: giving your time freely, unpaid, usually to an association. Un(e) bénévole – a volunteer.
  • Le volontariat: a formal commitment for a set period, with an allowance, e.g. le service civique.
  • Un(e) salarié(e): a paid employee. Many associations have both employees and volunteers.
  • Une association (under the law of 1 July 1901): a non-profit group of people with a shared aim, e.g. a sports club, a food bank, a festival committee.
  • Un don – a donation of money or goods (not time).
  • Watch out: volontaire also means willing or strong-willed (un élève volontaire). Bénévole is the word for unpaid work.
  • faire du bénévolat · s'engager dans une association · donner de son temps
Mission 2

Who volunteers?

  • La France bénévole 2026 (survey of 3,155 people aged 15+, January 2026):
  • About one person in four (25%) volunteers in an association, slightly up since 2010 (23%).
  • That is 13 to 13.5 million people; about 10 million help at least once a month.
  • Weekly volunteers: 10% of the population (5.2 million), down from 11% in 2025.
  • Main fields: sport, culture and leisure, social and humanitarian action, education, environment.
  • A new generation: 24% of 15–24s volunteer (16% in 2010), but only 28% of over-65s (38% in 2010).

?Which person is doing du volontariat, not du bénévolat?
Mission 2

What do volunteers do?

  • Les Restos du Cœur: founded in 1985 by the comedian Coluche to give free meals. In 2024–25:
  • about 1.3 million people helped;
  • over 161 million meals;
  • about 108,000 volunteers (78,000 regular).
  • Others: le Secours populaire, la Croix-Rouge française, les Petits Frères des Pauvres (against loneliness in old age).
  • Activities: food aid, night rounds to meet homeless people (les maraudes), tutoring (le soutien scolaire), visiting isolated older people, coaching sport, cleaning up beaches and rivers, running festivals.
  • Most French firefighters are volunteers (les sapeurs-pompiers volontaires).
Mission 2

Le service civique

  • Created in 2010 for young people aged 16–25 (up to 30 for young people with disabilities).
  • A mission of 6 to 12 months in an association or public body: education, health, environment, sport, culture, solidarity.
  • An allowance of about €620 a month (2026). No qualifications needed.
  • More than 900,000 young people since 2010; the 2026 target is up to 135,000 volunteers.
  • Many say it helps them choose a career and gain confidence.
  • effectuer une mission – to carry out a placement · un(e) volontaire – a service civique volunteer

Mission 2

What value for those who are helped?

  • Basic needs: food, clothes, a warm place, help with forms.
  • Human contact: a visit or a phone call breaks loneliness (rompre l'isolement).
  • Dignity and a fresh start: tutoring, help to find work or housing.
  • Limits (as critics and some volunteers say):
  • volunteers are not always trained professionals;
  • help can be irregular when volunteers leave;
  • associations sometimes fill gaps that some say the State should fill (l'État se désengage).
  • venir en aide à – to help · les plus démunis – the most deprived · un bénéficiaire – a recipient
Mission 2

What value for those who help?

  • Skills: teamwork, responsibility, organization (les compétences); useful on a CV and in a Parcoursup application.
  • Meaning: the feeling of being useful (se sentir utile), acting on one's values.
  • Social links: meeting people of other ages and backgrounds.
  • Wellbeing: volunteers often say they get as much as they give.
  • Recognition: some volunteering can earn training credits (le compte d'engagement citoyen).
  • Debate: if volunteers gain so much, is it still pure altruism (l'altruisme)? Many argue the two can go together.

Mission 2

Argue both sides

  • Le bénévolat devrait-il être obligatoire pour les jeunes ?
  • Supporters: Cela développerait le sens civique et la cohésion sociale. – It would build civic spirit and social cohesion.
  • Supporters: Beaucoup de jeunes découvriraient un métier ou une vocation. – Many young people would discover a job or a calling.
  • Opponents: Un bénévolat imposé n'est plus du bénévolat. – Compulsory volunteering is no longer volunteering.
  • Opponents: Les jeunes ont déjà leurs études et souvent un petit boulot. – Young people already have their studies and often a part-time job.
  • Les associations remplacent-elles l'État ?
  • Some say: Sans les bénévoles, beaucoup de personnes n'auraient rien à manger. – Without volunteers, many people would have nothing to eat.
  • Others reply: La solidarité ne devrait pas dépendre de la bonne volonté. – Solidarity should not depend on goodwill.
Mission 2

Model sentences and exam tasks

  • Il est essentiel que chacun puisse s'engager selon ses moyens. (il est essentiel que + subjunctive)
  • Si je faisais un service civique, je choisirais une mission dans l'environnement. (si + imperfect → conditional)
  • Non seulement le bénévolat aide les autres, mais il enrichit aussi ceux qui le pratiquent. (non seulement … mais aussi)
  • Project Presentation angle: the Restos du Cœur as a community practice in France, and the perspectives on solidarity behind it. Argumentative Essay prompt: Le bénévolat devrait-il être obligatoire au lycée ?

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Couples: mariage (251,000 in 2025), PACS (since 1999; 197,200 in 2024), union libre. Key laws: 1975, 1999, 2013, 2017, 2021.
  • Family shapes (INSEE 2020): 25% single-parent, 9% blended; six births in ten outside marriage; fertility 1.56 in 2025.
  • The family has diversified: argue "crisis" vs "transformation" with evidence.

Mission 2

  • Bénévolat (unpaid) ≠ volontariat (service civique: 16–25, 6–12 months, about €620 a month) ≠ salariat.
  • About 25% volunteer in an association (13–13.5 million); 10% every week. Restos du Cœur (Coluche, 1985): 1.3 million people helped in 2024–25.
  • Value for the helped (needs, contact, dignity) and for helpers (skills, meaning, links); debate on compulsory volunteering and the role of the State.
