Mission 1: Eight young people in ten in France get the bac. So why do so many students feel under pressure?

Make a guess: Eight young people in ten in France get the bac. So why do so many students feel under pressure?

Mission 1

From 3 to 18

  • L'école maternelle (3–6): compulsory from age 3 since 2019.
  • L'école élémentaire (6–11): from CP to CM2.
  • Le collège (11–15): from la sixième to la troisième, ending with the diplôme national du brevet.
  • Le lycée (15–18): la seconde, la première, la terminale. Three tracks (voies): general, technological, vocational (professionnelle).
  • Since 2020, young people aged 16–18 must be in education, training or work.
  • Watch out: le collège is a secondary school for 11–15-year-olds, not a college. Year groups count down: la sixième is the first year of collège.
  • un(e) collégien(ne) · un(e) lycéen(ne) · un(e) étudiant(e) – a student in higher education only
Mission 1

The bac

  • Le baccalauréat ends lycée and opens the door to higher education.
  • Since the 2021 reform: 40% continuous assessment (le contrôle continu), 60% final exams.
  • Final exams: French (in première), philosophy, two specialist subjects (les spécialités), and le grand oral, a 20-minute oral.
  • New: since June 2026, pupils in the general and technological tracks also sit a two-hour maths exam in première (it counts from the 2027 bac).
  • 2025 results: 91.6% passed (general track 96.2%, technological 90.9%, vocational 83.9%).
  • 80.1% of a generation now hold the bac.
  • Une mention – a distinction (assez bien, bien, très bien). Marks are out of 20; 10 is a pass.
  • Watch out: passer le bac = to sit the bac. To pass it: réussir le bac or avoir le bac.

Mission 1

After the bac

  • Students apply on Parcoursup (since 2018), a national platform: up to 10 wishes (vœux); each course ranks applicants.
  • L'université: licence (3 years), master (+2), doctorat (+3). Open to bac holders; selection for some courses.
  • 2026–27 fees for French and EU students: €178 a year for a licence, €255 for a master. Non-EU students can be charged €2,902 / €3,950; universities may exempt some of them (a May 2026 decree caps these exemptions).
  • Les grandes écoles (engineering, business, Sciences Po …): selective, often after two years of classes préparatoires and a competitive exam (un concours).
  • Shorter, job-focused courses: BTS (2 years), BUT (3 years).
  • L'alternance: study combined with paid work in a company (l'apprentissage).
Mission 1

Student issues

  • La précarité étudiante: many students struggle with rent, food and transport.
  • Housing: student halls run by the CROUS are cheap but too few; private rents in big cities are high.
  • Les bourses sur critères sociaux: means-tested grants.
  • Since 4 May 2026, a meal in a CROUS university restaurant costs €1 for every student (before, only grant holders and students in difficulty).
  • Many students work alongside their studies (un job étudiant), which can hurt results.
  • Other worries: stress over Parcoursup, choosing a path at 15–17, dropping out in the first year (le décrochage).
  • joindre les deux bouts – to make ends meet · avoir recours à l'aide alimentaire – to turn to food aid

Mission 1

Life at school: today's debates

  • Phones: banned in écoles and collèges (2018 law), and in lycées since September 2026 (law of 24 August 2026).
  • Bullying (le harcèlement scolaire) has been a criminal offence since 2022. Schools run the pHARe anti-bullying programme.
  • Workload: long school days and heavy terminale timetables; debate about homework and stress.
  • Equality of opportunity: OECD PISA studies regularly find that in France pupils' results depend more on their social background than in the average OECD country.
  • Repeating a year (le redoublement) is now rare.
  • Religion at school and laïcité: see another lesson.

Mission 1

Faut-il sélectionner à l'entrée de l'université ? Argue both sides

  • Arguments made by supporters of selection:
  • La sélection oriente les étudiants vers des filières où ils ont des chances de réussir. – It steers students to courses where they can succeed.
  • Elle réduit l'échec en première année. – It reduces first-year failure.
  • Arguments made by opponents:
  • Le bac doit rester la clé d'entrée à l'université. – The bac should remain the key to university.
  • La sélection renforce les inégalités sociales. – Selection reinforces social inequality.
  • Les algorithmes de Parcoursup manquent de transparence. – Parcoursup's algorithms lack transparency.
  • Balance: Tout dépend de la manière dont on accompagne les étudiants. – It all depends on how students are supported.

Make a guess: Why does France have a 35-hour week, and what happens when its workers go on strike?

Mission 2

Working life in France

  • Les 35 heures: the legal working week since the Aubry laws (1998 and 2000). Hours beyond it are overtime (les heures supplémentaires), paid extra.
  • Les congés payés: five weeks of paid holiday (the first two weeks were won in 1936, the fifth in 1982).
  • Le SMIC: the national minimum wage, raised every year in line with prices.
  • Retirement: the 2023 reform began raising the minimum age from 62 to 64. In December 2025 Parliament suspended it: the legal age stays at 62 years and 9 months until January 2028.
  • Pôle emploi became France Travail in 2024: the public job centre.
  • travailler à temps plein / à temps partiel – to work full-time / part-time
Mission 2

Contracts and the changing work scene

  • Un CDI (contrat à durée indéterminée) – a permanent contract. Un CDD (à durée déterminée) – a fixed-term contract. L'intérim – temping.
  • Young people often start with a CDD, an internship (un stage) or work-study (l'alternance).
  • Part-time jobs for students: un job d'été, du baby-sitting, la restauration rapide.
  • Remote work (le télétravail) spread after 2020, mainly in office jobs.
  • Platform work (l'ubérisation): delivery riders and drivers, often self-employed (auto-entrepreneurs), with fewer protections.
  • Attitudes: many workers say they look for meaning (le sens du travail) and a better work–life balance (l'équilibre vie professionnelle – vie privée).
  • Watch out: un stage = an internship or work placement (it can also be a short training course), never a theatre stage.

Mission 2

The right to strike

  • A constitutional right: the 1946 Preamble, still in force, says « Le droit de grève s'exerce dans le cadre des lois qui le réglementent. »
  • Strikers are not paid for strike days. In public services, unions must give 5 days' notice (un préavis).
  • Minimum service: since 2007, transport staff must say 48 hours ahead whether they will strike, so users can be told which services will run; since 2008, primary schools must look after pupils during a teachers' strike.
  • Key movements:
  • May 1968: a general strike by millions; the accords de Grenelle raise the minimum wage by 35%.
  • 1995: weeks of transport strikes against a social-security and pensions plan.
  • 2019–2020: a long rail and metro strike against a pensions reform.
  • 2023: 14 national days of strikes and marches against raising the pension age to 64.
  • faire grève (no article) · être en grève · un(e) gréviste · un syndicat – a trade union · une manifestation – a protest march (not a "manifestation"). Protest in depth.

Mission 2

Gender equality at work

  • INSEE, private sector, 2024:
  • On average, women earn 21.8% less than men over the year.
  • At the same working time, the gap is about 14%.
  • In the same job with the same employer, it is 3.6%.
  • Why the gap: more women work part-time; jobs and sectors differ (care, education); careers slow after children; few women at the top (le plafond de verre – the glass ceiling).
  • Laws: equal pay for equal work (1972); 40% women on the boards of large companies (Copé-Zimmermann law, 2011); a gender-equality index that companies with 50+ staff must publish (phased in 2019–2020); 30% women among senior executives of large firms by 2026, 40% by 2029 (Rixain law, 2021).

Mission 2

Argue both sides

  • La grève est-elle encore utile ?
  • Supporters: La grève a permis de grandes avancées sociales, comme les congés payés. – Strikes won major social progress, such as paid holidays.
  • Supporters: C'est souvent le seul moyen de se faire entendre. – It is often the only way to be heard.
  • Critics: Les usagers sont les premières victimes des grèves. – Users are the first victims of strikes.
  • Critics: Le dialogue social devrait passer avant le conflit. – Negotiation should come before conflict.
  • Les quotas de femmes sont-ils justes ?
  • Supporters: Sans quotas, la parité ne serait jamais atteinte. – Without quotas, parity would never be reached.
  • Critics: Il faut recruter selon les compétences, pas selon le sexe. – Recruitment should be by skills, not sex.
  • Balance: Certes …, mais … · D'un côté …, de l'autre …

Mission 3

Bénévole, volontaire, salarié

  • Le bénévolat: giving your time freely, unpaid, usually to an association. Un(e) bénévole – a volunteer.
  • Le volontariat: a formal commitment for a set period, with an allowance, e.g. le service civique.
  • Un(e) salarié(e): a paid employee. Many associations have both employees and volunteers.
  • Une association (under the law of 1 July 1901): a non-profit group of people with a shared aim, e.g. a sports club, a food bank, a festival committee.
  • Un don – a donation of money or goods (not time).
  • Watch out: volontaire also means willing or strong-willed (un élève volontaire). Bénévole is the word for unpaid work.
  • faire du bénévolat · s'engager dans une association · donner de son temps
Mission 3

Who volunteers?

  • La France bénévole 2026 (survey of 3,155 people aged 15+, January 2026):
  • About one person in four (25%) volunteers in an association, slightly up since 2010 (23%).
  • That is 13 to 13.5 million people; about 10 million help at least once a month.
  • Weekly volunteers: 10% of the population (5.2 million), down from 11% in 2025.
  • Main fields: sport, culture and leisure, social and humanitarian action, education, environment.
  • A new generation: 24% of 15–24s volunteer (16% in 2010), but only 28% of over-65s (38% in 2010).

Mission 3

What do volunteers do?

  • Les Restos du Cœur: founded in 1985 by the comedian Coluche to give free meals. In 2024–25:
  • about 1.3 million people helped;
  • over 161 million meals;
  • about 108,000 volunteers (78,000 regular).
  • Others: le Secours populaire, la Croix-Rouge française, les Petits Frères des Pauvres (against loneliness in old age).
  • Activities: food aid, night rounds to meet homeless people (les maraudes), tutoring (le soutien scolaire), visiting isolated older people, coaching sport, cleaning up beaches and rivers, running festivals.
  • Most French firefighters are volunteers (les sapeurs-pompiers volontaires).
Mission 3

Le service civique

  • Created in 2010 for young people aged 16–25 (up to 30 for young people with disabilities).
  • A mission of 6 to 12 months in an association or public body: education, health, environment, sport, culture, solidarity.
  • An allowance of about €620 a month (2026). No qualifications needed.
  • More than 900,000 young people since 2010; the 2026 target is up to 135,000 volunteers.
  • Many say it helps them choose a career and gain confidence.
  • effectuer une mission – to carry out a placement · un(e) volontaire – a service civique volunteer

Mission 3

What value for those who are helped?

  • Basic needs: food, clothes, a warm place, help with forms.
  • Human contact: a visit or a phone call breaks loneliness (rompre l'isolement).
  • Dignity and a fresh start: tutoring, help to find work or housing.
  • Limits (as critics and some volunteers say):
  • volunteers are not always trained professionals;
  • help can be irregular when volunteers leave;
  • associations sometimes fill gaps that some say the State should fill (l'État se désengage).
  • venir en aide à – to help · les plus démunis – the most deprived · un bénéficiaire – a recipient
Mission 3

What value for those who help?

  • Skills: teamwork, responsibility, organisation (les compétences); useful on a CV and in a Parcoursup application.
  • Meaning: the feeling of being useful (se sentir utile), acting on one's values.
  • Social links: meeting people of other ages and backgrounds.
  • Wellbeing: volunteers often say they get as much as they give.
  • Recognition: some volunteering can earn training credits (le compte d'engagement citoyen).
  • Debate: if volunteers gain so much, is it still pure altruism (l'altruisme)? Many argue the two can go together.

Mission 3

Argue both sides

  • Le bénévolat devrait-il être obligatoire pour les jeunes ?
  • Supporters: Cela développerait le sens civique et la cohésion sociale. – It would build civic spirit and social cohesion.
  • Supporters: Beaucoup de jeunes découvriraient un métier ou une vocation. – Many young people would discover a job or a calling.
  • Opponents: Un bénévolat imposé n'est plus du bénévolat. – Compulsory volunteering is no longer volunteering.
  • Opponents: Les jeunes ont déjà leurs études et souvent un petit boulot. – Young people already have their studies and often a part-time job.
  • Les associations remplacent-elles l'État ?
  • Some say: Sans les bénévoles, beaucoup de personnes n'auraient rien à manger. – Without volunteers, many people would have nothing to eat.
  • Others reply: La solidarité ne devrait pas dépendre de la bonne volonté. – Solidarity should not depend on goodwill.

Mission 4

The right to vote in France

  • 1974: the voting age falls from 21 to 18 (law of 5 July 1974).
  • Since 1997, young French citizens have been registered automatically at 18.
  • Voting is not compulsory in France. In Belgium it is.
  • Blank votes (le vote blanc) have been counted separately since 2014, but they do not count towards the result.
  • Vocabulary:
  • le droit de vote – the right to vote
  • voter / aller aux urnes – to vote / go to the polls
  • s'inscrire sur les listes électorales – to register to vote
  • s'abstenir / l'abstention – to abstain / abstention
  • un électeur, une électrice – a voter
  • le scrutin – the ballot, the vote
  • Watch out: un homme / une femme politique = a politician (neutral). Un politicien often sounds negative.
Mission 4

Pour ou contre le vote à 16 ans ?

  • Comparisons: Austria lets 16-year-olds vote in national elections (since 2007).
  • Belgium let 16- and 17-year-olds vote in the 2024 European elections.
  • Pour (arguments supporters make):
  • À 16 ans, on peut travailler et payer des impôts : on devrait pouvoir voter. – At 16 you can work and pay tax; you should be able to vote.
  • Voter tôt crée une habitude de participation. – Voting early builds a habit.
  • Les décisions sur le climat engagent l'avenir des jeunes. – Climate decisions shape young people's future.
  • Contre (arguments opponents make):
  • À 16 ans, on manque encore de maturité et d'expérience. – At 16, people still lack maturity.
  • Les jeunes risquent d'être influencés par leurs parents ou les réseaux sociaux. – Young people may be influenced by parents or social media.
  • La majorité civile est à 18 ans : il faut rester cohérent. – Legal adulthood is 18; the rules should be consistent.

Mission 4

The numbers: who stays home?

  • 2022 presidential election, second round, abstention by age (Ipsos poll):
  • 18–24: 41% · 25–34: 38% · 60–69: 20% · 70+: 15%
  • So young people abstained almost three times as much as over-70s.
  • Among 18–24s who cast a valid vote, 61% chose Emmanuel Macron (so about 39% Marine Le Pen).
  • Reasons young non-voters give in surveys: « Ça ne changera rien », distrust of politicians, not feeling represented, being registered far from where they study.
  • Many young people vote intermittently: they skip some elections and turn out for those they see as decisive.
  • Phrase: Les jeunes s'abstiennent nettement plus que leurs aînés. – Young people abstain much more than their elders.

Mission 4

Motivés ou démotivés ? Other ways to take part

  • Many researchers say young people are not apathetic: they engage differently.
  • signer une pétition (en ligne) – sign a (online) petition
  • manifester / participer à une marche pour le climat – demonstrate / join a climate march (e.g. school strikes of 2019)
  • faire du bénévolat dans une association – volunteer
  • boycotter une marque – boycott a brand
  • faire un service civique – a paid civic service placement for 16–25s (since 2010), 6–12 months
  • être élu(e) au conseil municipal des jeunes / au conseil de la vie lycéenne – be elected to a youth council / school council
  • s'exprimer sur les réseaux sociaux – speak out on social media
  • Critics reply: a "like" or a petition is not the same as voting; protest alone does not make laws.
  • Your turn: "I prefer to sign a petition rather than vote." → Je préfère signer une pétition plutôt que de voter.
Mission 4

Quel avenir pour la politique ?

  • Ideas debated to bring young people (and others) back to the polls:
  • le vote obligatoire – compulsory voting, as in Belgium
  • la reconnaissance du vote blanc – counting blank votes in the result
  • le vote électronique – online voting (security concerns)
  • les conventions citoyennes – citizens' assemblies drawn by lot, e.g. la Convention citoyenne pour le climat (2019–2020, 150 citizens)
  • le référendum d'initiative citoyenne (RIC) – citizens' referendums, a demand of the gilets jaunes (2018–2019)
  • Supporters: more legitimacy, more voices heard.
  • Critics: compulsory voting forces uninformed votes; referendums can oversimplify complex issues.
Mission 4

Model opinions to reuse

  • Il faut que les partis s'adressent davantage aux jeunes. – Parties need to speak to young people more. (il faut que + subjunctive)
  • Si le vote était obligatoire, l'abstention disparaîtrait, mais le désintérêt resterait. – If voting were compulsory, abstention would disappear, but lack of interest would remain.
  • Loin d'être indifférents, les jeunes s'engagent autrement. – Far from being indifferent, young people get involved in other ways.
  • Ce n'est pas la politique qui ennuie les jeunes, c'est la façon dont on la fait. – It is not politics that bores young people, it is the way it is done.
  • Watch out: la politique = politics or a policy; le politique (rare) = the political sphere.

Mission 5: Every French citizen is also a citizen of the European Union. What does that actually give them?

Make a guess: Every French citizen is also a citizen of the European Union. What does that actually give them?

Mission 5

From war to union

  • 9 May 1950: French foreign minister Robert Schuman, with Jean Monnet, proposes pooling French and German coal and steel. 9 May is now Europe Day.
  • 1951: six countries found the European Coal and Steel Community (la CECA); 1957: the Treaty of Rome creates the European Economic Community.
  • 1963: the Élysée Treaty of Franco-German friendship (de Gaulle and Adenauer).
  • 1979: first direct elections to the European Parliament (seat: Strasbourg).
  • 1992: the Maastricht Treaty creates the European Union and European citizenship; French voters approve it by referendum with 51%.
  • 2002: euro notes and coins replace the franc.
  • 2005: French voters reject the European Constitutional Treaty (55% no).
  • 2020: the United Kingdom leaves the EU; 27 member states remain.
Mission 5

What European citizenship gives

  • Every national of an EU country is automatically an EU citizen. It adds to national citizenship; it does not replace it.
  • Rights:
  • to move, live, study and work in any member state;
  • to vote and stand in municipal and European elections where you live, even if you are not a national;
  • to protection from any EU country's embassy outside the EU if your own has none there;
  • to petition the European Parliament and complain to the European Ombudsman;
  • a European Citizens' Initiative: 1 million signatures from at least 7 countries can ask the Commission to propose a law.
  • Watch out: la citoyenneté – citizenship; un citoyen / une citoyenne – a citizen. La nationalité is not the same as la citoyenneté européenne.

Mission 5

Opportunities for young people

  • Erasmus (1987; now Erasmus+): millions of students, apprentices and young workers have studied or trained in another country.
  • DiscoverEU: free rail passes for some 18-year-olds to travel around Europe.
  • No visa or work permit to study or work in another member state; no roaming charges on phones since 2017.
  • The UK after Brexit:
  • the UK left Erasmus+ and set up its own Turing scheme (2021);
  • the Irish government funded Erasmus+ places for students at Northern Ireland universities;
  • in December 2025 the UK announced it would rejoin Erasmus+ from 2027.
  • Northern Ireland: people born there are entitled to Irish citizenship, and so to EU citizenship (Good Friday Agreement, 1998).
  • un séjour à l'étranger · un échange · un stage – internship · la mobilité
Mission 5

France in Europe today

  • France is a founding member; Franco-German cooperation (le couple franco-allemand) is often seen as the EU's engine.
  • Strasbourg hosts the European Parliament and the Council of Europe (a separate organisation, which oversees the European Court of Human Rights).
  • 2024 European elections in France: turnout just over 50%; the list led by Jordan Bardella (Rassemblement National) came first.
  • French parties' positions, by policy:
  • those wanting closer integration (e.g. Renaissance and allies);
  • those wanting powers returned to nations (e.g. the Rassemblement National, which now proposes a "Europe of nations");
  • those wanting to change EU treaties on economic rules (e.g. La France insoumise).
  • l'abstention · un(e) député(e) européen(ne) – MEP · la souveraineté – sovereignty

Mission 5

Advantages and disadvantages

  • Advantages often cited:
  • peace between former enemies;
  • the single market and the euro (no exchange costs, easier trade);
  • freedom to travel, study and work;
  • common action on climate, health (e.g. joint vaccine purchases in 2021) and trade.
  • Disadvantages often cited:
  • a democratic deficit: decisions seen as distant from voters;
  • loss of national sovereignty;
  • bureaucracy and rules seen as costly for farmers and small firms;
  • France pays more into the EU budget than it receives directly.
  • 2024–2026: French farmers protested against EU rules and the EU–Mercosur trade agreement (signed in January 2026).
Mission 5

Argue both sides

  • Is European citizenship a real advantage for young people?
  • Grâce à l'Union européenne, les jeunes peuvent étudier et travailler dans 27 pays sans visa. – Thanks to the EU, young people can study and work in 27 countries without a visa.
  • Beaucoup de jeunes ne connaissent pas leurs droits et ne votent pas aux élections européennes. – Many young people don't know their rights and don't vote in European elections.
  • More Europe or less?
  • Supporters of closer union say: Face à la Chine et aux États-Unis, les pays européens sont plus forts ensemble. – Facing China and the US, European countries are stronger together.
  • Supporters of a "Europe of nations" say: Les décisions importantes doivent être prises au niveau national, au plus près des citoyens. – Important decisions should be taken nationally, closer to citizens.
  • Linkers: Si l'on considère … · Il est vrai que …, néanmoins … · Somme toute – All things considered

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Maternelle (compulsory from 3) → élémentaire → collège (brevet) → lycée (bac). Passer = sit; réussir / avoir = pass.
  • Bac 2025: 91.6% pass; 80.1% of a generation. Parcoursup since 2018; licence €178 a year.
  • Student issues: précarité, housing, grants, €1 CROUS meals for all since May 2026; phones banned up to lycée since September 2026.

Mission 2

  • 35-hour week (1998/2000), five weeks' paid holiday, SMIC; pension age held at 62 years 9 months until January 2028.
  • Right to strike in the 1946 Preamble; key movements 1968, 1995, 2019–20, 2023. Faire grève, être en grève depuis + present.
  • Pay gap (INSEE 2024): 21.8% overall, about 14% at equal hours, 3.6% in the same job; laws 1972, 2011, 2019 index, 2021.

Mission 3

  • Bénévolat (unpaid) ≠ volontariat (service civique: 16–25, 6–12 months, about €620 a month) ≠ salariat.
  • About 25% volunteer in an association (13–13.5 million); 10% every week. Restos du Cœur (Coluche, 1985): 1.3 million people helped in 2024–25.
  • Value for the helped (needs, contact, dignity) and for helpers (skills, meaning, links); debate on compulsory volunteering and the role of the State.

Mission 4

  • Vote at 18 since 1974; automatic registration; not compulsory; blank votes counted separately since 2014.
  • 2022 run-off abstention: 41% (18–24) vs 15% (70+). Engagement takes other forms: petitions, marches, volunteering, service civique.
  • Debates: vote at 16, compulsory voting, blank votes, citizens' assemblies.

Mission 5

  • 1950 Schuman → 1957 Rome → 1992 Maastricht (EU citizenship) → 2002 euro → 2005 French non → 2020 Brexit.
  • EU citizens: free movement; vote in municipal and European elections where they live; consular protection; petitions and citizens' initiatives.
  • Young people: Erasmus+, DiscoverEU, no roaming; UK rejoining Erasmus+ in 2027.
