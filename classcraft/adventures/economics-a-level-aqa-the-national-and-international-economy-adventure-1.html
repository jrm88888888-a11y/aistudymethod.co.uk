AQA A-level Economics · The National and International Economy

The National and International Economy
Now in 4 mini-lessons.

This topic is taught in the mini-lessons below. Each mission starts from a question: make a guess, learn the idea, then check it. About 20 minutes each. No login needed.

4.2.1 The measurement of macroeconomic performance (part 1 of 2)

  1. Why can't a government get fast growth, plenty of jobs and steady prices all at once?
  2. If a country's output is worth more money this year, has it really produced more?
  3. Can an economy keep growing just by people spending more?
  4. How can one number tell you how much prices have risen for a whole country of shoppers?

Start this mini-lesson →

4.2.2 How the macroeconomy works: the circular flow of income, AD/AS analysis and related concepts (part 1 of 2)

  1. Why must a country's total spending, total income and total output always be equal?
  2. If every price in a country goes up at once, why would people buy less of the country's output?
  3. What sets the most a country can produce, and can extra spending push past it?
  4. Why can prices and unemployment rise at the same time?
  5. What makes a whole country suddenly stop spending, even when pay has not changed?

Start this mini-lesson →

4.2.4 Financial markets and monetary policy

  1. Why will a shop swap a meal for a printed slip of paper but not for a bag of apples?
  2. Why would anyone sell a safe government bond for less than they paid for it?
  3. When a bank lends you money, whose money are you actually borrowing?
  4. Why can't you open an account at a central bank, and what does it do instead?
  5. Why do governments let most firms fail but write strict rules to stop banks failing?
  6. How can one interest rate, set by a handful of people, slow down inflation across a whole country?
  7. When interest rates can go no lower, how can a central bank still get people spending?

Start this mini-lesson →

4.2.6 The international economy (part 1 of 3)

  1. Why do countries buy from abroad things they could make perfectly well at home?
  2. Why did the world start trading so much more, and does everyone come out ahead?
  3. When a global company builds a factory in a poorer country, who really gains?
  4. When a country's export prices go up, is that good news or bad news?
  5. When a country taxes goods from abroad, who ends up paying, and who comes out ahead?
  6. Why would a country give up control over its own trade rules to join a club of its neighbours?
