Infinitive & participle constructions

Once you leave single verbs behind, Spanish strings verbs together in fixed patterns. Some verbs take an infinitive directly; others demand a preposition first. Then there is al + infinitive, the gerund (-ando/-iendo), and the rule that trips up every English speaker: Spanish uses the infinitive, not the gerund, as a noun. In IB Diploma Spanish B HL, this grammar powers your work across the five prescribed themes — Identities, Experiences, Human ingenuity, Social organization and Sharing the planet — and the two literary works you study, especially when you recount your Experiences.

Pattern 1

Verb + infinitive (no preposition)

A core group of verbs is followed by the infinitive directly — nothing in between. These are your everyday modal-style verbs:

  • quiero ir — I want to go
  • puedo hacer — I can do
  • debo estudiar — I must study
  • prefiero salir — I prefer to go out

The tell: verbs of wanting, being able, having to and liking (querer, poder, deber, preferir) glue straight onto the infinitive — no a, de or que.

Pattern 2 · takes "a"

Verb + a + infinitive

A big family of verbs needs a before the infinitive. Many involve starting, learning or movement toward an action:

  • empezar a — to begin to (empiezo a trabajar)
  • aprender a — to learn to (aprendo a nadar)
  • ir a — to be going to (voy a comer)
  • ayudar a — to help to (ayudo a cocinar)
  • volver a — to do again (vuelvo a intentar)

Memory hook: verbs of beginning and motion point toward the action — so they take a ("to / towards").

Pattern 2 · takes "de"

Verb + de + infinitive

Another family needs de. Several signal finishing or stopping:

  • acabar de — to have just (acabo de comer = I have just eaten)
  • dejar de — to stop (dejo de fumar)
  • tratar de — to try to (trato de ayudar)
  • terminar de — to finish (termino de leer)

Watch the meaning of acabar de: it does NOT mean "to finish". Acabo de comer = "I have just eaten" — the recent past.

Pattern 2 · takes "que"

Verb + que + infinitive (obligation)

Two key expressions of obligation take que before the infinitive:

  • tener que — to have to (tengo que estudiar = I have to study)
  • hay que — one must / it is necessary to (hay que trabajar = one must work)

The difference: tener que is personal ("I have to"), while hay que is impersonal ("one must", no named subject).

al + infinitive

"On / upon doing"

Spanish expresses "on doing" or "upon doing" with al + infinitive — a neat, high-frequency construction:

  • Al llegar, me senté. — On arriving, I sat down.
  • Al ver la carta, sonrió. — On seeing the letter, she smiled.
  • Al salir de casa, empezó a llover. — On leaving home, it began to rain.

English trap: English uses "-ing" here ("on arriving"), but Spanish uses the infinitive — al llegar, never al llegando.

The gerund · formation

The gerundio (-ando / -iendo)

Form the gerund by dropping the ending and adding -ando (for -ar verbs) or -iendo (for -er/-ir verbs):

hablar → habl-
hablando
comer → com-
comiendo
vivir → viv-
viviendo

Two uses: (1) with estar for the continuous — estoy hablando = "I am speaking"; (2) for "by / while doing" — aprendo escuchando música = "I learn by listening to music".

Infinitive as a noun

The infinitive is the noun form

Here is the rule that separates advanced Spanish from GCSE. Where English uses "-ing" as a noun or subject, Spanish uses the infinitive:

  • Ver es creer. — Seeing is believing. (both are subjects → infinitives)
  • Fumar es malo. — Smoking is bad.
  • After a preposition, always the infinitive: antes de salir (before leaving), después de comer (after eating).

Never use the gerund as a subject: Ver es creer, never Viendo es creyendo.

Infinitive vs gerund

The English-speaker's decision

English uses "-ing" for almost everything, so you must decide each time which Spanish form fits:

  • After a preposition → infinitive: después de comer (after eating).
  • As a subject / noun → infinitive: Nadar es sano (Swimming is healthy).
  • "By / while doing" → gerund: Aprendo practicando (I learn by practising).
  • With estar (continuous) → gerund: Estoy estudiando (I am studying).

Exam gold: using al + infinitive and acabar de + infinitive in your writing shows immediate IB-level range.

Recap

Your infinitive-construction toolkit

Direct: querer, poder, deber, preferir + infinitive (quiero ir).

+ a: empezar, aprender, ir, ayudar, volver a + inf.

+ de: acabar (to have just), dejar, tratar, terminar de + inf.

+ que: tener que (personal), hay que (impersonal).

al + inf = "on doing"; gerund = continuous / "by doing"; infinitive as noun/subject (Ver es creer) and after prepositions (antes de salir).
