Mission 1: Why can a cloud of flour dust explode, when a heap of flour only smoulders?

Make a guess: Why can a cloud of flour dust explode, when a heap of flour only smoulders?

Mission 1

Collision theory

  • Particles must collide to react. The collision starts the breaking and making of bonds.
  • The collision needs energy at least equal to the activation energy, Ea.
  • Activation energy: the minimum energy that colliding particles need for a reaction to take place.
  • Most collisions do not react: their energy is less than Ea, so the particles just bounce apart.
Quick check
?In a mixture of two gases at room temperature, each molecule collides with others billions of times every second, yet the reaction between them is slow. Why do most of these collisions not lead to a reaction?
Mission 1

Concentration, pressure, surface area

  • Higher concentration: more particles per unit volume → more frequent collisions.
  • Higher gas pressure: the same particles are squeezed closer → more frequent collisions.
  • Larger surface area of a solid: more particles exposed → more frequent collisions.
  • These change how often particles collide, not the energy of each collision.
  • So more successful collisions per second: a faster rate.
Quick check
?Hydrogen iodide decomposes: 2HI(g) → H2(g) + I2(g). A sample of hydrogen iodide is compressed to half its volume at constant temperature. What is the effect on the rate of reaction, and why?
Mission 1

Temperature

  • Higher temperature: particles move faster, with more kinetic energy.
  • Main effect: a much larger fraction of collisions have energy ≥ Ea.
  • Smaller effect: collisions are also a little more frequent.
  • So a small rise in temperature can give a large rise in rate.
  • A later mission shows this on the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution.
Mission 1

Catalysts

  • A catalyst increases the rate of a reaction without being used up by the overall reaction.
  • It provides an alternative route with a lower activation energy.
  • So, at the same temperature, more collisions have E ≥ Ea. ΔH is unchanged.

Mission check

?An iron nail held in a Bunsen flame only glows. The same mass of fine iron filings sprinkled into the flame burns in bright sparks. Why do the filings react so much faster with oxygen?

Same as the flour dust. A dust cloud surrounds every tiny grain with air, so collisions with oxygen are so frequent that the energy is released in an instant.

Mission 2

Energy profiles

  • Energy (y) against progress of reaction (x).
  • Activation energy, Ea: from the reactants up to the top of the barrier.
  • ΔH: from reactants to products. Products lower: exothermic. Products higher: endothermic.
  • Ea is always positive, even for an exothermic reaction.
  • This is a reaction profile.
Quick check
?Which energy profile correctly shows an endothermic reaction?
Mission 2

The Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution

  • The molecules in a gas have a range of energies at any temperature.
  • y: number of molecules with a given energy. x: energy.
  • Starts at the origin: no molecule has zero energy. Rises to a peak, the most likely energy.
  • Long tail that never reaches the energy axis.
  • Area under the curve = total number of molecules. Area beyond Ea = molecules that can react.
Mission 2

Two temperatures

  • Hotter sample: peak lower and further right; the curve is broader.
  • Total area unchanged: same number of molecules.
  • Area beyond Ea is much larger: a much bigger fraction of collisions can succeed.
  • Ea does not change with temperature.
  • So a small temperature rise gives a large rate rise: near room temperature, +10 °C often roughly doubles the rate.
Quick check
?A Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution is plotted for a sample of gas at 300 K, with number of particles on the y-axis and kinetic energy on the x-axis. The curve starts at the origin, rises to a peak and falls to a long tail that approaches the energy axis. Which describes the curve for the same sample at 400 K?
Mission 2

Catalysts on an energy profile

  • A catalyst provides an alternative route with a lower Ea. It is not used up.
  • Reactant and product energies are unchanged, so ΔH is unchanged.
  • Draw both profiles: same start and end levels; the catalysed peak is lower.
  • Enzymes are biological catalysts. Raw potato contains catalase, which catalyses 2H2O2 → 2H2O + O2.
Mission 2

The reverse reaction's barrier

  • The reverse reaction starts from the products: its Ea runs from the products up to the peak.
  • Read it off the profile. As a check: Ea(reverse) = Ea(forward) − ΔH.
  • Example: Ea(forward) = 60 kJ mol⁻¹, ΔH = −90 kJ mol⁻¹, so Ea(reverse) = 60 + 90 = 150 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • A catalyst lowers Ea(forward) and Ea(reverse) by the same amount.
Mission 2

A catalyst on the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve

  • Same temperature, so the same curve.
  • Mark a second, lower Ea(catalysed), to the left of Ea(uncatalysed).
  • The area beyond Ea(catalysed) is larger: more molecules have enough energy.
  • The molecules have no extra energy; the barrier is lower.
Quick check
?Which sketch correctly shows how a catalyst affects a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution at constant temperature?
Mission 2

Solid catalysts in industry

  • Heterogeneous catalyst: a different phase from the reactants, e.g. a solid with gases.
  • It provides a surface: gas molecules adsorb, bonds weaken, they react, products desorb.
  • More surface, more sites: the catalyst is finely divided or spread thinly on a support.
  • Example: iron in the Haber process, N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3.
  • Economic benefits: faster at lower temperatures, so less fuel is burned, costs fall and less CO2 is released.
Mission check

?A mixture of hydrogen and oxygen can stay unchanged at room temperature for years. In Döbereiner's lamp (1823), a jet of hydrogen in air strikes a piece of platinum and reacts at once, with no flame or spark to start it. Which explanation is correct?

The potato works the same way. Its enzyme, catalase, gives the decomposition a much lower Ea, so far more collisions succeed. In the bottle there is no catalyst.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • A reaction needs collisions with energy ≥ Ea; most collisions fail.
  • Concentration, pressure and surface area raise collision frequency; temperature mainly raises the fraction with E ≥ Ea; a catalyst lowers Ea.

Mission 2

  • Maxwell–Boltzmann: heating spreads the curve (same area, lower peak, further right), so far more molecules have E ≥ Ea.
  • A catalyst lowers Ea by an alternative route: same curve, Ea line moves left; ΔH unchanged.
  • Ea(reverse) = Ea(forward) − ΔH.
