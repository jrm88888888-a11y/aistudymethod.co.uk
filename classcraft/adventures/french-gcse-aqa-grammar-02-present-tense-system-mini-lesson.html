The present-tense system

The present tense is the engine of French — you will use it in every theme, every paper. This lesson builds it from the ground up: the three regular families (-er / -ir / -re), the spelling-change verbs, the four key irregulars (être, avoir, aller, faire), the avoir idioms, and être en train de.

What the present tense does

One tense, three English ideas

A single French present-tense verb can be translated three ways in English. There is no separate word for "am/do":

  • J'étudie le français. → I study French.
  • J'étudie le français. → I am studying French.
  • Oui, j'étudie le français. → I do study French.

Why this matters: beginners often try to translate "am" with a word (suis) and write je suis étudie. Don't — the ending already carries "I". The verb does the whole job.

The six persons

Every verb has six "persons". Unlike Spanish, French keeps the subject pronoun — you write it every time. You still need to know them to pick the right ending:

je (j' before a vowel)I
tuyou (one friend)
il / elle / onhe / she / we-or-one
nouswe
vousyou (polite, or plural)
ils / ellesthey (m / f)

Two French quirks: on literally means "one", but in speech it is the everyday word for "we" and takes the il/elle ending — on va = "we go". And je becomes j' before a vowel or silent h: j'aime, j'habite.

Regular verbs · Step 1

-ER verbs: the biggest family

Around 90% of French verbs are -er verbs. Take parler (to speak), chop off -er, and add the endings:

parler — to speak
personform
jeparle
tuparles
il / elle / onparle
nousparlons
vousparlez
ils / ellesparlent

Ear-trap: parle, parles, parle and parlent all sound identical — the endings are silent. Only nous (-ons) and vous (-ez) are heard. Same pattern for every regular -er verb: travailler, jouer, aimer, habiter.

Spelling-change (boot) verbs

The "boot" verbs

Some -er verbs keep regular endings but tweak their spelling — in every form except nous and vous. Shade the changed forms and you get a boot shape:

e → è · acheter
j'achète
tu achètes
il achète
nous achetons
vous achetez
ils achètent
l → ll · appeler
j'appelle
tu appelles
il appelle
nous appelons
vous appelez
ils appellent
é → è · préférer
je préfère
tu préfères
il préfère
nous préférons
vous préférez
ils préfèrent

Two more you must know: -ger verbs keep the e in the nous form (nous mangeons), and -cer verbs take a cedilla (nous commençons) — both just to keep the soft sound. The nous/vous forms are the top of the boot and never take the accent change.

The must-know irregulars

The high-frequency irregular verbs

A handful of verbs are so common — and so irregular — that you must know them by heart. Beyond the "big four", these come up constantly:

infinitiveje form
prendre (to take)je prends
venir (to come)je viens
pouvoir (to be able)je peux
vouloir (to want)je veux
devoir (to have to)je dois
mettre (to put)je mets

Good news: pouvoir, vouloir, devoir are the "modal" verbs — they are usually followed straight by an infinitive (je peux aller = I can go). You'll drill those fully in cluster 4.

The core irregulars

The "big four" to memorise whole

Four verbs are so common — and so irregular — that AQA expects the full present tense of each. Learn them cold:

Notice the traps: vous êtes and vous faites break the usual -ez pattern, and ils sont / ils ont / ils vont / ils font all end in -ont. These four power almost every sentence you'll write.

The big split · avoir vs être

Where French uses "have" for "be"

English uses to be for age and many feelings ("I am 15", "I am hungry"). French uses avoir ("to have") for these — a classic trap:

avoir — "have"

Age and bodily feelings: hunger, thirst, cold, heat, fear, age. J'ai faim / j'ai 15 ans.

être — "be"

Identity, nationality, description, mood and the time: who/what you are, how you feel as an adjective. Je suis fatigué.

  • J'ai faim. — I am hungry. (feeling → avoir)
  • Il a 14 ans. — He is 14. (age → avoir)
  • Je suis anglais. — I am English. (nationality → être)
  • Il est deux heures. — It is two o'clock. (time → être)

Never translate "I am hungry" word-for-word as je suis faim. Hunger, thirst, fear, cold, heat and age all take avoir.

Right now · être en train de

Stressing "in the middle of"

French normally uses the plain present for "I am doing" — there is no separate continuous tense. But to stress that something is happening at this very moment, use être en train de + the infinitive:

être + en train de + infinitive= to be in the middle of (do)ing
  • travailler → je suis en train de travailler — I'm (busy) working.
  • manger → il est en train de manger — he's (right now) eating.
  • écrire → nous sommes en train d'écrire — we're in the middle of writing.

Don't overuse it. For habits and general truths, the plain present is better: j'étudie le français ("I study / am studying French" in general), not je suis en train d'étudier unless you mean at this exact second.

Everyday present-tense phrases

avoir, il y a and il fait

Three high-frequency patterns use the present tense where English does something different:

  • avoir for feelings & age. French "has" the feeling: j'ai faim (I'm hungry), j'ai soif (thirsty), j'ai froid/chaud (cold/hot), j'ai peur (afraid), j'ai 15 ans (I'm 15).
  • il y a = "there is / there are". One phrase for both: il y a un problème, il y a beaucoup de touristes. And il faut + infinitive = "one must" (il faut étudier).
  • il fait for weather. il fait chaud / froid / beau / mauvais; also il pleut (it's raining), il neige (it's snowing).

Watch out: never translate "I am hungry" word-for-word as je suis faim. Feelings like hunger, thirst, fear, cold and age use avoir.

Recap

Your present-tense toolkit

Regular: chop -er/-ir/-re, add endings — -er: e, es, e, ons, ez, ent · -ir: is, is, it, issons, issez, issent · -re: s, s, —, ons, ez, ent.

Boot verbs: spelling shifts (e→è, é→è, l→ll) in all forms except nous/vous.

Big four: être, avoir, aller, faire — learn whole (watch vous êtes / vous faites).

Other key irregulars: prendre, venir, pouvoir, vouloir, devoir, mettre.

avoir vs être: age & feelings take avoir; identity, description & time take être.

être en train de + infinitive for "right now".

Idioms: avoir for feelings/age, il y a = there is/are, il fait for weather.
