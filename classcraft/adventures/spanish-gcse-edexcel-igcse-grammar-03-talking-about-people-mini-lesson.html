Talking about people

To describe yourself, your family and your daily routine you need pronouns — the little words that stand in for people and things. This lesson builds them cleanly: subject pronouns, direct and indirect object pronouns, reflexive verbs, plus how to make sentences negative and how to ask questions.

Work through each screen and answer the questions as you go — some ask you to recognise the right form, some ask you to produce it by typing. Collect ⭐ stars and press Start.

Who is doing it?

Subject pronouns — a quick recap

These name who does the action. You'll rarely write them — the verb ending already shows the person — but you must know them:

yoI
túyou (one friend)
él / ella / ustedhe / she / you (polite)
nosotros / nosotraswe
vosotros / vosotrasyou all (Spain, informal)
ellos / ellas / ustedesthey / you all (polite)

Key: Spanish normally drops the pronoun — just hablo, not yo hablo — because the ending shows the person. Use one only for emphasis, contrast or clarity (Yo estudio, tú no). usted / ustedes = the polite "you" and take the él / ellos endings.

Direct object pronouns

DOPs — the "what / whom"

A direct object pronoun replaces the thing or person the action is done to, so you don't repeat it:

singularplural
me (me)nos (us)
te (you)os (you all)
lo / la (it, him / it, her)los / las (them)

Placement — three cases:

  • Before a conjugated verb: Lo veo — I see it / him.
  • Attached to an infinitive or gerund: Voy a verlo = Lo voy a ver; Estoy leyéndolo (I'm reading it).
  • Attached to an affirmative command: ¡Míralo! (Look at it!).

Meaning: lo = it / him, la = it / her. They must match the gender of the noun they replace: la película → la veo.

Indirect object pronouns

IOPs — "to / for whom"

An indirect object pronoun shows to or for whom something happens:

singularplural
me (to/for me)nos (to/for us)
te (to/for you)os (to/for you all)
le (to/for him/her)les (to/for them)

Le doy el libro — I give the book to him / her. The placement rules are exactly the same as for DOPs: before a conjugated verb, or attached to an infinitive, gerund or command.

Good to know: when a sentence has two pronouns together, le / les changes to se before lo / la / los / las — Se lo doy (I give it to him). Most of the time you can get by with just one pronoun at a time.

Reflexive verbs · daily routine

Doing it to yourself

Reflexive verbs describe an action done to oneself — the pronoun "bounces back" to the subject. They power your daily routine: levantarse (to get up), ducharse (to shower), lavarse (to wash), llamarse (to be called), acostarse (to go to bed).

levantarse — to get up
personform
yome levanto
túte levantas
él / ella / ustedse levanta
nosotrosnos levantamos
vosotrosos levantáis
ellos / ustedesse levantan

Core pronouns: me, te, se · nos, os, se. Me llamo… = "I'm called…". Watch acostarse — it's also a boot verb: me acuesto (o→ue).

Saying "no"

Negation — put no before the verb

To make any sentence negative, drop no straight before the verb: No hablo francés (I don't speak French).

Spanish uses double negatives — and they are correct. Keep no before the verb and add the negative word after:

  • no… nada — nothing: No veo nada (I see nothing).
  • no… nunca — never: No como carne nunca.
  • no… nadie — nobody: No viene nadie (Nobody's coming).
  • no… ninguno / ningún — none / not any.

Shortcut: nunca and nadie can go before the verb on their own — then you drop no: Nunca como carne, Nadie viene.

Useful extras: ya no (no longer), tampoco (neither / not either), ni… ni… (neither… nor).

Asking questions

Questions and question words

Wrap every question in ¿ … ? — note the upside-down opener. You can ask by intonation alone (¿Hablas español?) or use a question word. Every question word carries a written accent:

SpanishEnglish
quéwhat
quién(es)who
cuándowhen
dóndewhere
cómohow
cuánto / a / os / ashow much / many
por quéwhy
cuál(es)which

Word order: question word + verb + subject — ¿Dónde vive tu hermano? (Where does your brother live?).

qué vs cuál: use qué for "what" (a definition) and cuál for "which one" (a choice): ¿Qué es esto? vs ¿Cuál prefieres?

Recap

Your "talking about people" toolkit

Subject pronouns: yo, tú, él/ella/usted, nosotros, vosotros, ellos/ustedes — usually dropped; use for emphasis.

DOPs (what/whom): me, te, lo, la (+ nos, os, los, las) — before the verb or attached to infinitive/gerund/command.

IOPs (to/for whom): me, te, le (+ nos, os, les). Le doy…

Reflexive: me, te, se — daily routine (me levanto, me ducho, me llamo).

Negation: no before the verb; double negatives (no… nada / nunca / nadie).

Questions: ¿ … ? with accented question words: qué, quién, cuándo, dónde, cómo, cuánto, por qué, cuál.
