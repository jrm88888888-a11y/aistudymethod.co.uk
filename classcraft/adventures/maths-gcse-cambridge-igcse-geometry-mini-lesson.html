Geometry · angles and polygons

Triangles and quadrilaterals

  • Angles in a triangle sum to 180°. Proof: draw a line through one corner parallel to the opposite side; the two alternate angles and the corner angle make a straight line.
  • An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
  • Isosceles: two equal sides and two equal base angles. Equilateral: three equal sides, every angle 60°.
  • Angles in a quadrilateral sum to 360°: a diagonal splits it into two triangles.

Geometry · angles and polygons

Angles in polygons

  • From one corner, an nn-sided polygon splits into n−2n − 2 triangles, so the interior angle sum is
(n−2)×180°(n−2) × 180°
  • The exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360° (walk round it and you turn once).
  • In a regular polygon all angles are equal: exterior angle =360°÷n= 360° ÷ n, and interior angle =180°−= 180° − exterior angle (they sit on a straight line).
  • Cambridge: angle sums of irregular polygons are Extended; regular polygons are Core.
Worked example Geometry · angles and polygons

Number of sides from an interior angle

Question
Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 156°. How many sides does it have?
  1. 1
    Interior and exterior angles sit on a straight line: exterior angle =180°−156°=24°= 180° − 156° = 24°
  2. 2
    The exterior angles sum to 360°: n=360°÷24°=15n = 360° ÷ 24° = 15
  3. 3
    Check: (15−2)×180°=2340°(15 − 2) × 180° = 2340° and 2340°÷15=156°2340° ÷ 15 = 156°
Answer
15 sides
⚠️ Watch out
dividing 360 by the interior angle (360÷156≈2.31360 ÷ 156 ≈ 2.31) gives nonsense. Use the exterior angle.

Mission check

?Why can identical regular pentagons never tile a floor?

Tiles meeting at a point must have angles adding to exactly 360°. A regular pentagon's interior angle is 180°−360°÷5=108°180° − 360° ÷ 5 = 108°, and 360÷108=3.33…360 ÷ 108 = 3.33…, so they never fit. Only equilateral triangles (60°), squares (90°) and regular hexagons (120°) tile on their own.

Geometry · bearings and constructions

Three-figure bearings

  • A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from north, written with three figures: 065°, 008°, 300°.
  • "The bearing of BB from AA": stand at AA, face north, turn clockwise until you face BB.
  • Compass points as bearings: N 000°, NE 045°, E 090°, SE 135°, S 180°, SW 225°, W 270°, NW 315°.
  • The back bearing (the bearing of AA from BB) differs by exactly 180°, because the north lines at AA and BB are parallel.
Worked example Geometry · bearings and constructions

Finding a back bearing

Question
The bearing of BB from AA is 065°. The bearing of QQ from PP is 300°. Find the bearing of AA from BB, and the bearing of PP from QQ.
  1. 1
    065° is less than 180°, so add 180°: 65°+180°=245°65° + 180° = 245°
  2. 2
    300° is more than 180°, so subtract 180°: 300°−180°=120°300° − 180° = 120°
  3. 3
    Write each answer with three figures
Answer
AA from BB is 245°; PP from QQ is 120°
⚠️ Watch out
"the bearing of AA from BB" means you stand at BB. Reading the question the wrong way round gives the original bearing back.

Geometry · bearings and constructions

Scale drawings: fixing a point from two bearings

  • A scale: "1 cm represents 1 km", or 1 : 100,000.
  • Two bearings fix a point: draw a north line at each known point, measure each bearing clockwise, draw the lines. The point is where they cross.
  • Measure the drawn distance; convert with the scale.
  • Triangle from three sides: draw one side; from its ends draw arcs of the other two lengths; join to the crossing. Leave the arcs.
  • Extended: know the term perpendicular bisector (at 90° through a segment's midpoint).
Quick check

Mission check

?Tower BB is 8 km due east of tower AA. A fire is on a bearing of 045° from AA and 315° from BB. Where is the fire?

Each bearing gives a line from its tower, and the fire must lie on both lines, so it is where they cross. Here the lines meet symmetrically, 4 km north of the midpoint of ABAB. On a real map you draw both lines to scale and measure.

Geometry · congruence and similarity

Congruent or similar?

  • Congruent shapes are the same shape and size: one fits exactly on the other (after turning or flipping).
  • Similar shapes are the same shape: corresponding angles are equal and every length is multiplied by the same scale factor kk.
  • k=corresponding length on the originallength on the new shape​k = (length on the new shape)/(corresponding length on the original)
  • A 4 by 6 rectangle and a 6 by 9 rectangle are similar (k=1.5k = 1.5); a 4 by 6 and a 6 by 8 are not.
  • Triangles with equal angles are similar. Give a reason for each equal angle.
Worked example Geometry · congruence and similarity

A missing length in similar triangles

Question
In triangle ABC, D is on AB and E is on AC with DE parallel to BC. AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm and DE = 6 cm. Find BC.
  1. 1
    Show similarity: angle A is shared; ∠ADE=∠ABC∠ ADE = ∠ ABC (corresponding angles), so the triangles are similar.
  2. 2
    Use whole sides from A: AB=4+6=10AB = 4 + 6 = 10 cm
  3. 3
    Scale factor from ADE to ABC: k=ADAB​=410​=2.5k = AB/AD = 10/4 = 2.5
  4. 4
    Multiply: BC=6×2.5=15BC = 6 × 2.5 = 15
Answer
BC = 15 cm
⚠️ Watch out
using ADDB​=1.5DB/AD = 1.5 gives 9 cm. DB is not a side of either triangle.

Geometry · congruence and similarity

Area and volume scale factors (Extended)

  • If lengths are multiplied by kk, then:
  • areas (including surface areas) are multiplied by k2k²
  • volumes (and so masses of the same material) are multiplied by k3k³
  • Double every length of a cube: area of each face ×4× 4, volume ×8× 8.
  • Given an area or volume factor, work back to kk: area factor 9 means k=3k = 3; volume factor 8 means k=2k = 2.

Question

  2. 2

    Multiply: 400×3.375=1350400 × 3.375 = 1350

Mission check

?A giant ape is similar to a gorilla but 10 times as tall. Why would its legs snap?

With scale factor 10, the giant's volume, and so its weight, is 103=1,00010³ = 1,000 times the gorilla's, but the cross-section of its leg bones is only 102=10010² = 100 times as big. Each square centimetre of bone carries 10 times the load, so the legs would fail. Weight grows faster than strength.

Mission 4

Geometry · circle theorems

The parts of a circle

  • Radius: centre to the circle. Diameter: a chord through the centre, twice the radius.
  • Chord: a straight line joining two points on the circle. Tangent: a straight line touching the circle at one point.
  • Arc: part of the circumference. Sector: the slice between two radii and an arc. Segment: the region between a chord and an arc.
  • Extended: a quadrilateral with all four corners on a circle is a cyclic quadrilateral.
Geometry · circle theorems

Angle in a semicircle (Core and Extended)

  • AB is a diameter and C is any point on the circle. Then angle ACB=90∘ACB = 90°: the angle in a semicircle is a right angle.
  • Why: join C to the centre O. OA=OB=OCOA = OB = OC (radii), so triangles AOCAOC and BOCBOC are isosceles.
  • Call the base angles a,aa, a and b,bb, b. Triangle ABCABC gives 2a+2b=180∘2a + 2b = 180°, so a+b=90∘a + b = 90°.
  • Exam reason: "angle in a semicircle is 90°".
Quick check
?AB is a diameter of a circle and C is a point on the circle. Angle CAB=35∘CAB = 35°. Find angle ABCABC.
Geometry · circle theorems

Tangents and chords (tangent–radius: Core; the rest: Extended)

  • A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
  • Two tangents from the same outside point are equal in length (by RHS congruence), so they make a kite with the two radii.
  • The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord. The converse holds too: the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it.
  • Also: a chord's perpendicular bisector passes through the centre; equal chords are equidistant from it.
Calculate
?Exam-style (Extended). PA and PB are tangents from a point P to a circle, centre O, touching it at A and B. Angle APB=50∘APB = 50°. Find angle OABOAB.
degrees
Geometry · circle theorems

Four angle theorems (Extended)

  • The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal (they stand on the same chord, on the same side).
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.
  • Alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
  • In an exam, quote the theorem in words for every step. "Circle theorem" alone earns no reason mark.
Worked example Geometry · circle theorems

Chasing angles with reasons (Extended)

Question
A, B, C and D lie on a circle, centre O. A is on the major arc BD and C is on the minor arc BD. Angle BOD=140∘BOD = 140°. Find angle BADBAD and angle BCDBCD, giving reasons.
  1. 1
    Angle BADBAD stands on arc BD, like BODBOD: BAD=140÷2=70∘BAD = 140 ÷ 2 = 70° (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
  2. 2
    ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral: BCD=180−70=110∘BCD = 180 − 70 = 110° (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°)
Answer
Angle BAD=70∘BAD = 70° and angle BCD=110∘BCD = 110°.
⚠️ Watch out
halving 140° for angle BCDBCD too. C is on the other side of BD, so it does not stand on the same arc as BODBOD.
Calculate
?Exam-style (Extended). The tangent to a circle at A makes an angle of 64° with the chord AB. C is a point on the circle in the alternate segment, and angle ABC=71∘ABC = 71°. Find angle BACBAC.

Mission check

?AB is a diameter and C is any point on the circle. Why is angle ACBACB always 90°?

Join C to the centre. The three radii are equal, so the two small triangles are isosceles, and their four base angles fill triangle ABC: 2a+2b=180∘2a + 2b = 180°, so a+b=90∘a + b = 90°. Equally, the diameter makes a straight angle of 180° at the centre, and the angle at the circumference is half of it.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Every angle needs a reason: angles at a point (360°), on a straight line (180°), vertically opposite, alternate, corresponding and co-interior angles.
  • Triangle 180°, quadrilateral 360°, any polygon (n−2)×180°(n−2) × 180°; exterior angles of a polygon always sum to 360°.
  • For a regular polygon, find the exterior angle 360°÷n360° ÷ n first, then interior =180°−= 180° − exterior.
  • Bearings are measured clockwise from north and written with three figures; a back bearing differs by 180°.
  • Two bearing lines from two known points cross at exactly one place: that is how you fix a position on a scale drawing.
  • Similar: equal angles, every length ×k× k. Use whole corresponding sides to find kk.
  • Lengths ×k× k, areas ×k2× k², volumes ×k3× k³.
  • Tangent ⟂ radius; two tangents from a point are equal; the perpendicular from the centre bisects a chord.
  • Angle in a semicircle is 90°; angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°; alternate segment theorem.
