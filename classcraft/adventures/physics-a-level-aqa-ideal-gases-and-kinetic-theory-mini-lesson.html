Mission 1

Mission 1: Keep cooling a gas in a sealed box. At what temperature would its pressure reach zero?

Make a guess: Keep cooling a gas in a sealed box. At what temperature would its pressure reach zero?

Mission 1

Pressure and the mole

  • p = F/A, F the force perpendicular to the surface. 1 Pa = 1 N m⁻².
  • A gas pushes perpendicular to every surface it touches.
  • 1 mole: NA = 6.02 × 10²³ particles (atoms or molecules).
  • Amount of substance n = N/NA.
  • Molar mass M: the mass of one mole. n = total mass ÷ M.
  • Molecular mass = M/NA: the mass of one molecule.
Quick check
?A cylinder contains 0.64 kg of oxygen gas, which consists of O2 molecules. The molar mass of oxygen atoms is 16 g mol⁻¹, and NA = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. How many oxygen molecules are in the cylinder?
Mission 1

Boyle's law and its experiment

  • Fixed mass of gas at constant temperature: pV = constant. Halve the volume, double the pressure.
  • Apparatus: air trapped above oil in a sealed tube; a pump pushes the oil up; Bourdon gauge reads p; scale reads V. (Or: syringe sealed to a pressure sensor.)
  • Change V slowly, then wait: compressing warms the gas, and T must return to room temperature.
  • Plot pressure against 1/V: a straight line through the origin.
  • Errors to name: leaks (mass not fixed); temperature not constant; volume of tubing not counted.
Mission 1

Charles's law and the pressure law

  • Charles's law: constant pressure, V/T = constant.
  • Apparatus: air trapped in a capillary tube by a bead of liquid, tube in a water bath. Length of air column ∝ V.
  • Pressure law: constant volume, p/T = constant.
  • T in kelvin, always.
  • Combine the three: pV/T = constant for a fixed mass of gas.
  • These laws are empirical: found by experiment, not derived.
Quick check
?Predict first. Students measure the pressure of three samples of gas, each sealed in a rigid container, at several temperatures between 0 °C and 100 °C. The samples are of different gases and different amounts, and each behaves as an ideal gas. For each sample they plot pressure against temperature in °C and get a straight line. They then extend each best-fit line to zero pressure. What do they find?
Mission 1

Absolute zero by extrapolation

  • Fixed volume of gas: measure pressure at several temperatures, 0–100 °C.
  • Plot pressure against θ in °C: a straight line.
  • Extend the line to zero pressure: it meets the axis at about −273 °C.
  • Every gas, any amount: the same intercept.
  • This is absolute zero: 0 K = −273.15 °C.
  • T(K) = θ(°C) + 273.15 (often + 273).
Mission 1

The ideal gas equation

  • pV = nRT (n moles) and pV = NkT (N molecules)
  • R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ (molar gas constant).
  • k = R/NA = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹ (Boltzmann constant).
  • SI units only: pressure in Pa, V in m³, T in K.
Mission 1

Worked example: a gas cylinder

  • Rigid cylinder of helium: V = 0.020 m³, pressure 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa, θ = 20 °C → T = 293 K.
  • Moles: n = pV/RT = (2.0 × 10⁵ × 0.020)/(8.31 × 293) = 1.6 mol.
  • Molecules: N = pV/kT = (2.0 × 10⁵ × 0.020)/(1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 293) = 9.9 × 10²³.
  • Warmed to 60 °C (333 K), V fixed: pressure ∝ T, so the pressure = 2.0 × 10⁵ × 333/293 = 2.3 × 10⁵ Pa.
Quick check
?A bubble of gas of volume 2.0 cm³ is released at the bottom of a lake, where the pressure is 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa and the temperature is 7 °C. It rises to the surface, where the pressure is 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and the temperature is 17 °C. Treat the gas as ideal, with no gas gained or lost. What is its volume at the surface?
Mission 1

Reading gas graphs

  • p against V, constant T: a curve (pV = constant). Hotter gas: curve further from the origin. Each curve is an isotherm.
  • pV against p, constant T: horizontal line.
  • V against T (constant p) and p against T (constant V): straight lines through 0 K.
  • On p–V axes, the product pV at a point is proportional to T (fixed amount of gas).
  • A gas expanding at constant pressure does work W = pΔV: the area under the p–V line.
Quick check
?The pressure–volume diagram shows three states, A, B and C, of a fixed amount of ideal gas. Which ranks the temperatures of the gas in the three states, from highest to lowest?
Mission check

?A sealed, rigid container holds an ideal gas at 7 °C. The gas is heated to 287 °C. How does its new pressure compare with its starting pressure?

Pressure ∝ T in kelvin. Every gas line points to the same zero: 0 K = −273 °C, absolute zero.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Molecules hit the walls at random. How can that add up to a pressure you can calculate exactly?

Make a guess: Molecules hit the walls at random. How can that add up to a pressure you can calculate exactly?

Mission 2

Brownian motion

  • Smoke cell: smoke in a small glass cell, lit from the side, viewed through a microscope.
  • Smoke particles appear as bright specks moving in jerky, random paths.
  • Cause: invisible air molecules hit each speck unevenly, more on one side than the other at any instant.
  • The specks are large and slow; the molecules are tiny and fast.
  • Evidence that molecules exist and move randomly. Einstein's analysis (1905), confirmed by Perrin's measurements (1908), convinced most remaining sceptics.
Quick check
?Which change would make each smoke speck in the cell jiggle faster, on average?
Mission 2

Derivation 1: one molecule

  • Cube of side L, volume V = L³. One molecule, mass m, velocity component u along x.
  • Hits the end wall and rebounds elastically: momentum change = −mu − (+mu) = −2mu.
  • Time to cross and return to the same wall: Δt = 2L/u.
  • Force on the wall = rate of change of momentum = 2mu ÷ (2L/u) = mu²/L.
  • Pressure on that wall = force ÷ area = mu²/L ÷ L² = mu²/V.
Mission 2

Derivation 2: all N molecules

  • Add all N molecules: p = m(u1² + u2² + … + uN²)/V = Nm⟨u²⟩/V.
  • For each molecule c² = u² + v² + w².
  • Random motion, large N: no direction is special, so ⟨u²⟩ = ⟨v²⟩ = ⟨w²⟩ = ⅓(crms)².
  • pV = ⅓Nm(crms)², where (crms)² is the mean square speed.
  • Assumptions used: elastic collisions (2mu); negligible molecule size (each crosses distance L); no forces between collisions (constant speed across the box); negligible collision time; large N in random motion (averaging).
Mission 2

Using the kinetic theory equation

  • pV = ⅓Nm(crms)²
  • Nm is the total mass of gas, so Nm/V = ρ and p = ⅓ρ × (mean square speed).
  • Air: p = 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa, ρ = 1.2 kg m⁻³.
  • Mean square speed = 3p/ρ = 2.5 × 10⁵ m² s⁻². rms speed = 500 m s⁻¹.
Quick check
?Nitrogen in a cylinder has a density of 3.40 kg m⁻³. The root-mean-square speed of its molecules is 517 m s⁻¹. Treat it as an ideal gas. What is its pressure?
Mission 2

Linking speed to temperature

  • Theory: pV = ⅓Nm(crms)². Experiment: pV = NkT.
  • Equate: ⅓Nm(crms)² = NkT, so m(crms)² = 3kT.
  • Halve both sides: ½m(crms)² = (3/2)kT. Mean translational KE ∝ T.
  • With k = R/NA: ½m(crms)² = (3/2)kT = 3RT/(2NA).
Quick check
?An ideal gas is heated from 27 °C to 927 °C. By what factor does the rms speed of its molecules increase?
Mission 2

Empirical laws, theoretical model

  • Empirical: Boyle (1662) and the other gas laws came from measurements. They describe; they do not explain.
  • Theoretical: kinetic theory starts from assumptions + Newton's laws and predicts pV = constant at fixed T.
  • Bernoulli (1738) first explained pressure by moving particles. Clausius (1857) and Maxwell (1860) built the full theory, including the speed distribution.
  • Some leading scientists, notably Mach, doubted that atoms were real. Einstein's analysis of Brownian motion (1905) and Perrin's measurements (1908) persuaded most of them.
  • Ideas changed as evidence accumulated: the model was accepted because its predictions matched experiment.
Quick check
?Which of these statements about gases is theoretical rather than empirical?
Mission check

?Two sealed containers of equal volume are at the same temperature. Each holds the same number N of molecules of an ideal gas. Each molecule of gas Y has 4 times the mass of a molecule of gas X. What is (pressure of Y)/(pressure of X)?

About 10²⁷ collisions on each square metre every second average out. Momentum change per hit × hits per second gives a steady force: pV = ⅓Nm × (mean square speed), which agrees with pV = NkT.

Mission 3

The ideal gas model

  • A very large number of particles (atoms or molecules) in rapid, random motion.
  • Particle volume is negligible compared with the volume of the container.
  • Collisions with each other and the walls are perfectly elastic: no kinetic energy lost.
  • Collision time is negligible compared with the time between collisions.
  • No forces between particles except during collisions.
Quick check
?Careful measurements show that at very high pressures a real gas no longer obeys the ideal gas equation closely. Which reasoning, based on the assumptions of the ideal-gas model, best supports this observation?
Mission 3

Pressure from collisions

  • A molecule that rebounds from a wall changes momentum, so the wall pushes on it, and it pushes back on the wall (Newton's third law).
  • Many hits every second → an average force on the wall. Pressure = total perpendicular force ÷ area.
  • Smaller volume (T fixed): same speeds, but hits on each square metre are more frequent → pressure rises.
Quick check
?A sealed, rigid container of ideal gas is heated. What happens to the pressure of the gas, and why?
Mission 3

Temperature measures mean kinetic energy

  • ½m(crms)² = (3/2)kT = 3RT/(2NA)
  • Mean translational kinetic energy per molecule ∝ absolute temperature.
  • Translational: moving from place to place. Molecules such as N2 also spin; that energy is extra.
  • Same temperature → same mean translational kinetic energy, whatever the gas.
  • In this model, as T → 0 K this mean kinetic energy → 0: absolute zero.
  • k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹, the Boltzmann constant.
Mission 3

Worked example: root-mean-square speed

  • rms speed: square every speed, take the mean, then the square root.
  • crms = √(3kT/m)
  • Speeds 300, 400, 500 m s⁻¹: mean = 400; rms = √((300² + 400² + 500²)/3) = 408 m s⁻¹. Not the same.
  • Nitrogen, N2, at 300 K.
  • m = 0.028 kg mol⁻¹ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 4.65 × 10⁻²⁶ kg.
  • Mean translational kinetic energy = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300 = 6.21 × 10⁻²¹ J.
  • rms speed = √(3kT/m) = √(3 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300 ÷ 4.65 × 10⁻²⁶) = 517 m s⁻¹.
Quick check
?A sealed container of gas is heated from 27 °C to 177 °C. By what factor does the mean kinetic energy of the gas molecules increase?
Mission 3

Internal energy of an ideal gas

  • Internal energy: total random kinetic energy of the particles + total potential energy from the forces between them.
  • Ideal gas: no forces between particles → no potential energy. U is all kinetic.
  • Monatomic: U = (3/2)NkT = (3/2)nRT
  • U depends only on T (for a fixed amount). Same T → same U, whatever the volume.
Mission check

?A container holds a mixture of hydrogen (H2) and oxygen (O2) at one temperature. An oxygen molecule has 16 times the mass of a hydrogen molecule. What is (rms speed of the hydrogen molecules)/(rms speed of the oxygen molecules)?

Temperature fixes the mean translational kinetic energy. For air (mostly N2) at room temperature that means rms speeds near 500 m s⁻¹, faster than a passenger jet.

Mission 4

The first law

  • Conservation of energy for a gas: change in U = energy in by heating + work done on it.
  • Heating a gas, or doing work on it, increases U. If the gas does work (expands), U falls unless it is heated.
Quick check
?The outlet of a bicycle pump is blocked, and the frictionless piston is pushed in quickly, compressing the air inside. The piston, the cylinder and the air start at the same temperature. Model the air as an ideal gas. The temperature of the air rises. Which explanation is correct?
Mission 4

Work done by a gas

  • Gas at constant pressure pushes a piston of area A a distance Δx.
  • Work = force × distance = pA × Δx = pΔV (p = pressure).
  • Work done by the gas = pΔV. Expansion: positive. Compression: negative (work is done on the gas).
  • Units: Pa × m³ = N m⁻² × m³ = J.
Mission 4

Worked example: heating at constant pressure

  • A monatomic ideal gas at 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa expands from 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 2.5 × 10⁻³ m³. Pressure stays constant.
  • pΔV = 2.0 × 10⁵ × 1.5 × 10⁻³ = 300 J of work done by the gas.
  • The temperature rises, so U rises too. The heat supplied must be more than 300 J.
Quick check
?Helium, a monatomic ideal gas, is kept at a constant pressure of 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa while it expands from 2.0 dm³ to 5.0 dm³. What is the work done by the helium?
Mission 4

Reading the signs

  • Energy can leave by heating the surroundings: the gas loses energy.
  • If the internal energy falls, the temperature of an ideal gas falls.
  • Work done by the gas is negative when the gas is compressed.
  • Constant volume: no work, so all the heat supplied goes into U.
Mission check

?An ideal gas is in a cylinder whose walls and piston are perfectly insulated. The gas expands, pushing the piston outwards. What happens to the temperature of the gas?

Energy in = energy out + change stored. Push a piston in and you do work on the gas: its internal energy, and so its temperature, rises. Let it push out with no heating, and it cools.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Fixed mass: pV = constant (constant T); V/T and p/T constant; combined pV/T = constant. T in kelvin.
  • Extrapolating pressure against θ (°C) to zero pressure gives absolute zero, −273 °C.
  • pV = nRT = NkT; k = R/NA.

Mission 2

  • Brownian motion: uneven bombardment by unseen molecules; evidence for molecules.
  • Derivation: Δp = 2mu, Δt = 2L/u, sum over N, ⟨u²⟩ = ⅓(crms)².
  • pV = ⅓Nm(crms)².
  • Combine with pV = NkT: ½m(crms)² = (3/2)kT = 3RT/2NA.
  • Gas laws are empirical; kinetic theory is a theoretical model that predicts them.

Mission 3

  • Ideal gas: many particles in random motion, negligible volume, elastic collisions, no forces except in collisions.
  • Pressure comes from momentum changes as particles hit a surface.
  • Mean translational KE per particle ∝ absolute temperature: (3/2)kT. Same T, same mean translational KE; lighter particles move faster.
  • Ideal gas internal energy is all kinetic: U = (3/2)nRT for a monatomic gas.

Mission 4

  • Heat and work are energy in transit; the first law is conservation of energy.
  • Heating a gas or doing work on it raises its internal energy; work done by a gas = pΔV.
