Syria

The Syrian Refugee Crisis

Since 2011, civil war has displaced ~13.5 million Syrians: ~6.8 million abroad, ~6.7 million IDPs (UNHCR 2023). Major host states: Turkey (3.6m), Lebanon (~800,000, 1 in 6 residents), Jordan (650,000), Germany (~850,000). The 2015-16 European migration crisis saw over 1 million arrivals via the eastern Mediterranean. The EU-Turkey Statement (March 2016) traded 6bn euros for stricter Turkish border control. The Syrian crisis remains a protracted displacement.

Rohingya

Rohingya Persecution in Myanmar

The Rohingya are a Muslim minority in Rakhine State, Myanmar, denied citizenship under the 1982 Citizenship Law - effectively stateless. In August 2017, military clearance operations forced ~745,000 to flee to Cox's Bazar, Bangladesh, joining earlier arrivals to form the world's largest refugee camp (~960,000 by 2023). The UN labelled the action textbook ethnic cleansing. ICJ proceedings (Gambia v Myanmar, 2019) test the Genocide Convention.

Law

International Refugee Architecture

The 1951 Refugee Convention and 1967 Protocol define refugees and codify non-refoulement (Article 33). UNHCR oversees ~108 million displaced people globally (2023), the highest on record. Major gaps: climate refugees not recognised; the Global Compact on Refugees (2018) is non-binding. The UK Illegal Migration Act 2023 and Rwanda asylum plan illustrate increasingly restrictionist policies in the Global North.
