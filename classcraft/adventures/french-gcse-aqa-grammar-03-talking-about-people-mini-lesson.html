Talking about people

To talk naturally about people you need the little words that replace and describe them. This lesson covers object pronouns (me, te, le, la, lui, leur), the handy y and en, reflexive verbs (je me lave), all the ways to say no (ne…pas, ne…jamais, ne…rien) and the three ways to ask a question.

Who does the action?

Subject pronouns — a quick recap

The subject pronoun tells you who is doing the verb. In French you write it every time:

je / tuI / you (one friend)
il / elle / onhe / she / we-or-one
nous / vouswe / you (polite or plural)
ils / ellesthey (m / f)

Now the twist. When the person is the target of the action ("I see him", "I speak to her"), you need a different set — the object pronouns. That is what this lesson builds.

Direct object pronouns

me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les

A direct object pronoun replaces the person or thing the verb acts on directly. In French it goes before the verb — the opposite of English:

pronounmeaning
me / teme / you
le / lahim-it / her-it
nous / vousus / you
lesthem

Position rule: je le vois = "I see him/it" — the pronoun sits just before the verb, never after. Do not say je vois le.

Indirect object pronouns

me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur = "to …"

When the meaning is "to someone" (speak to, give to, say to), you use the indirect object pronouns. Two forms differ from the direct set: lui (to him/her) and leur (to them):

pronounmeaning
me / teto me / to you
luito him / to her
nous / vousto us / to you
leurto them

Same position, before the verb: je lui parle = "I speak to him/her"; je leur donne = "I give to them". English hides the "to", but French makes you choose the indirect pronoun.

Two special pronouns

y = "there" · en = "some / of it"

French has two tiny pronouns with no neat English match. Both go before the verb like the others:

  • y = "there / to it". Je vais à Paris → j'y vais ("I'm going there").
  • en = "some / of it". J'ai du pain → j'en ai ("I have some").

Handy shortcut: y replaces a place or an à + thing, and en replaces a de + thing or a quantity. Both attach to the front of the verb: j'y vais, j'en ai.

Reflexive verbs

Doing it to yourself

Reflexive verbs describe an action you do to yourself — washing, getting up, being called. Each person has a matching reflexive pronoun before the verb:

se laver — to wash (oneself)
jeje me lave
tutu te laves
il / elleil se lave
nousnous nous lavons
vousvous vous lavez
ils / ellesils se lavent

Same shape for the family: se lever (to get up) and s'appeler (to be called) work the same way — je me lève, je m'appelle. The reflexive pronoun always matches the subject and sits before the verb.

Saying no · negation

ne … pas and its cousins

French negation wraps the verb: ne goes before, and a second word goes after. Swap that second word to change the meaning:

negativemeaning
ne … pasnot
ne … jamaisnever
ne … riennothing
ne … plusno longer
ne … personnenobody
ne … queonly

Wrap the verb: je ne fume jamais = "I never smoke". The ne hugs the front of the verb, the second word follows it.

Asking questions

Three ways to ask

French gives you three ways to turn a statement into a question — from casual to formal:

  • Intonation: keep the word order, raise your voice. Tu aimes ? ("You like it?")
  • est-ce que: add it to the front. Est-ce que tu aimes ?
  • Inversion: swap verb and pronoun, add a hyphen. Aimes-tu ?

Add a question word for detail: qui (who), que (what), quand (when), où (where), pourquoi (why), comment (how), combien (how many), quel (which). E.g. Pourquoi tu es en retard ?

Right now · y and en in use

Making sentences shorter with y and en

Once you can drop y and en in front of the verb, your French sounds much more natural:

  • Tu vas à l'école ? — Oui, j'y vais. "Are you going to school? — Yes, I'm going (there)."
  • Tu veux du gâteau ? — Oui, j'en veux. "Do you want some cake? — Yes, I want some."

Don't leave them out. English can say just "Yes, I am", but French needs the pronoun: j'y vais / j'en ai, never je vais on its own as the answer.

Putting people-talk together

Pronouns, reflexives, negation, questions

You now have the toolkit for talking about people naturally. Keep the moving parts in order:

  • Object pronouns before the verb: direct le/la/les, indirect lui/leur — je le vois, je lui parle.
  • y / en also before the verb — j'y vais, j'en ai.
  • Reflexive pronoun matches the subject: je me lave, nous nous levons.
  • Negation wraps the verb: ne … pas / jamais / rien / plus / personne / que.

Golden habit: whenever a person is the target of the verb, ask "direct or to-someone?" — that one question picks le vs lui every time.

Recap

Your talking-about-people toolkit

Direct objects: me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les — before the verb (je le vois).

Indirect objects: me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur = "to …" (je lui parle, je leur donne).

y / en: y = there/to it (j'y vais); en = some/of it (j'en ai).

Reflexives: je me lave, tu te laves, il se lave, nous nous lavons, vous vous lavez, ils se lavent.

Negation: ne … pas / jamais / rien / plus / personne / que — wraps the verb.

Questions: intonation, est-ce que, or inversion; words qui, que, quand, où, pourquoi, comment, combien, quel.
