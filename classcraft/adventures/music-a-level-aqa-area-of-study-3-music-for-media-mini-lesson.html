Music for Media

This mini-lesson covers AQA Area of Study 3 — Music for Media: how music for film, TV and games works with the image — its functions, the leitmotif, tension devices, orchestration and synchronisation.

Function

What media music does

Music for film, TV and games is functional — it works with the images. Its jobs include:

  • Setting mood and emotion; establishing place and time (period, geography).
  • Revealing character and inner life; building tension and release.
  • Giving structure and continuity across a scene or whole film.

Diegetic music exists in the story world (a radio on screen); non-diegetic underscore is heard by the audience only.

Thematic devices

The leitmotif

A leitmotif is a short recurring theme tied to a character, place or idea. Composers transform it — changing key, tempo, orchestration or mode — to mirror the drama.

  • Turning a major-key motif to the minor can signal danger or loss.
  • Fragmenting or slowing a motif builds suspense; a triumphant statement signals victory.

Tension & mood

Tension and mood devices

Composers have a toolkit of tension devices:

  • Dissonance, clusters and chromaticism; unresolved harmony.
  • Tremolo, ostinato and pedal points that refuse to move.
  • Rising dynamics and pitch, accelerating tempo, and sudden silence or a stinger chord for shock.

Sound & sync

Orchestration, synths and synchronisation

Media scores mix the symphony orchestra with electronics:

  • Rich orchestration and leitmotif in the symphonic tradition; synths, samples and hybrid textures in modern scoring.
  • Synchronisation to picture uses a click track and hit points so accents land on the action.
  • Games add adaptive or looping music that responds to the player.

Underscore styles

Minimalism and underscore

Much modern screen music borrows from minimalism:

  • Repeating ostinati and additive patterns create momentum and drive.
  • Slow-changing pads and drones underscore dialogue without distracting.
  • Ambient textures and processed timbres suit sci-fi and thrillers.

Appraising

Appraising media music

When appraising screen music, link sound to screen:

  • Name the function (mood, place, character, tension, structure).
  • Identify the device (leitmotif, tremolo, ostinato, stinger) as evidence.
  • Comment on orchestration and whether music is diegetic or non-diegetic.
