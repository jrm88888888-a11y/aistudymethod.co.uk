Probability · notation

Set notation & the basic rules

  • A ∩ B — "A and B": both events happen (the intersection).
  • A ∪ B — "A or B or both": at least one happens (the union).
  • A′ — "not A": the complement, so P(A′) = 1 − P(A).
  • A | B — "A given B": B is known to have happened.
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)the addition formula — subtract the overlap so it is not counted twice

Why subtract? Adding P(A) and P(B) counts everything in the overlap A ∩ B twice. Subtracting P(A ∩ B) once puts it right. This formula is true for any two events.

Probability · mutually exclusive & independent

Two ideas that are often confused

These two words sound similar but mean completely different things.

  • Mutually exclusive — the events cannot both happen. P(A ∩ B) = 0, so P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B). On a Venn diagram the circles do not overlap.
  • Independent — one event happening does not change the probability of the other. P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B), and equivalently P(A | B) = P(A) and P(B | A) = P(B).
Worked example — is it independent?

P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4, P(A ∩ B) = 0.2.

Test: P(A) × P(B) = 0.5 × 0.4 = 0.2 = P(A ∩ B) ✔ — so A and B are independent.

They are not mutually exclusive, because P(A ∩ B) = 0.2 ≠ 0.

Key insight: two events with non-zero probabilities can never be both mutually exclusive and independent — if they cannot occur together, then knowing B happened tells you A definitely did not, which is a huge change in probability.

Probability · conditional probability

Conditional probability

P(A | B) is the probability of A given that B has happened. Knowing B has occurred shrinks the sample space down to B alone, so we scale by P(B):

P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) ÷ P(B)rearranged: P(A ∩ B) = P(B) × P(A | B) = P(A) × P(B | A)
Worked example

P(A ∩ B) = 0.18 and P(B) = 0.45.

P(A | B) = 0.18 ÷ 0.45 = 0.4.

Interpretation: among the outcomes where B happens, A happens 40% of the time.

Warning: P(A | B) and P(B | A) are not the same. Nearly all people who have a rare disease test positive; that does not mean nearly all people who test positive have the disease. Divide by the probability of the event you are given.

Probability · tree diagrams

Tree diagrams & sampling without replacement

A tree diagram is the fastest way to handle two-stage experiments — especially without replacement, where the second set of branches uses conditional probabilities.

  • Multiply along the branches of a path (this is P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B | A)).
  • Add the paths that give the outcome you want.
  • All the final probabilities must sum to 1 — always check.

With replacement the counter goes back, so the second draw has the same probabilities: the draws are independent. Without replacement they are not — there is one fewer counter and possibly one fewer red.

Probability · modelling

Modelling — and criticising the assumptions

Spec point 3.3 asks you to model with probability and to critique the assumptions made.

  • "The die is fair" — is it? A biased die would make every calculated probability wrong.
  • "The events are independent" — often false. Two children in the same family catching a cold are not independent.
  • "Each person is equally likely to be chosen" — only true if the sampling really is random.

Continuous variables: for a continuous distribution, probability is the area under the curve, so P(X = a) = 0 for any single value. That is why P(X < a) and P(X ≤ a) are equal for a continuous random variable — but they are different for a discrete one such as the binomial.

Probability · two-way tables

Two-way tables

A two-way table is just a Venn diagram in a grid. Conditional probabilities become "divide by the row or column total".

PassedFailedTotal
Boys351550
Girls401050
Total7525100
  • P(Girl and Passed) = 40 ÷ 100 = 0.4 — divide by the grand total.
  • P(Passed | Girl) = 40 ÷ 50 = 0.8 — given a girl, divide by the Girls total.
  • P(Girl | Passed) = 40 ÷ 75 = 0.533 (3 s.f.) — given a pass, divide by the Passed total.

Notice P(Passed | Girl) = 0.8 but P(Girl | Passed) = 0.533. Conditioning is not symmetric — the two are completely different questions.

Recap

The big ideas to know

Notation: A ∩ B (both) · A ∪ B (at least one) · A′ (not A) · A | B (A given B)

Any events: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B) and P(A′) = 1 − P(A)

Mutually exclusive: P(A ∩ B) = 0 ⇒ P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)

Independent: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B) ⇔ P(A | B) = P(A)

Conditional: P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B) ÷ P(B) — and P(A | B) ≠ P(B | A)

Trees: multiply along branches, add the paths, check they total 1

Modelling: state and question the assumptions (fairness, independence)
