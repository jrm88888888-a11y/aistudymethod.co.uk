Konjunktiv I & reported speech

German has a dedicated mood for reporting what someone else said — Konjunktiv I. You meet it constantly in newspapers and formal writing: it lets the writer relay a claim without vouching for it. The signature forms are er sei, er habe, er komme.

What it does

Reporting at arm's length

Konjunktiv I (indirect speech / indirekte Rede) signals "these are someone else's words, not necessarily true":

  • Direct: Der Minister sagt: "Ich habe keine Zeit."
  • Indirect: Der Minister sagt, er habe keine Zeit.
  • Direct: Sie sagt: "Ich bin krank." → Indirect: Sie sagt, sie sei krank.

No English-style backshift: German does not move the tense back one step. The KI form reflects the tense of the original utterance, not the reporting verb.

Formation

Stem + KI endings

Take the infinitive stem and add -e, -est, -e, -en, -et, -en. The 3rd person singular is the workhorse (-e). sein is the one irregular:

infinitiveer/sie/es (KI)
habener habe
kommener komme
werdener werde
könnener könne
wissener wisse
sein (irregular)er sei (no -e)

sein in full: ich sei, du sei(e)st, er sei, wir seien, ihr seiet, sie seien — note there is no -e on ich sei / er sei.

The fallback rule

When KI looks like the indicative → use KII

In the plural and the 1st person, the KI form is identical to the ordinary present — so it would not be recognisable as reported speech. There, German switches to Konjunktiv II:

  • sie haben (KI = indicative) → use sie hätten.
  • sie kommen (KI = indicative) → use sie kämen (or würden kommen).
  • But 3rd person singular stays KI: er habe, er komme — these are distinct, so no switch.

Practical rule: use KI wherever it is visibly different from the indicative (mostly the 3rd person singular); otherwise fall back to KII.

Tenses in reported speech

Present, past & future

The KI form mirrors the tense of the original words:

  • Original present → KI present: "Ich arbeite" → er arbeite.
  • Original past (any of the three) → KI perfect: "Ich habe/hatte gearbeitet / arbeitete" → er habe gearbeitet.
  • Original future → werde + infinitive: "Ich werde kommen" → er werde kommen.

So the three German past tenses collapse into one reported form: the KI perfect (er sei gekommen, er habe gesehen).

Structure & journalism

dass, word order & reporting verbs

The reported clause can use dass (verb to the end) or drop it (verb second):

  • With dass: Er sagt, dass er keine Zeit habe.
  • Without: Er sagt, er habe keine Zeit. (verb-second, no comma-verb clash).
  • Common reporting verbs: sagen, behaupten, erklären, betonen, meinen, berichten — Der Sprecher betonte, die Firma werde mehr investieren.

Questions & commands: yes/no questions use ob (Er fragte, ob sie komme); commands use sollen (Sie sagte, er solle warten).

Recap

Your Konjunktiv I toolkit

Use: indirect speech — relaying a claim without vouching for it (journalism, formal writing).

Formation: stem + -e, -est, -e, -en, -et, -en; sein irregular (sei, seien).

Signature forms: er sei, er habe, er komme, er werde, er könne.

Fallback: where KI = indicative (plural / 1st person), use KII (hätten, kämen).

Tenses: past original → KI perfect; future → werde + infinitive; no English-style backshift.
