Music technology in the electronic and digital age

This area of inquiry explores how electronic and digital tools have transformed music-making — from synthesisers and samplers to the DAW on a laptop — and how technology shapes creating, performing and listening.

What this area explores

Technology as a creative instrument

  • How electronic and digital technology creates, records, manipulates and distributes music.
  • Genres shaped by it: musique concrete, electronic and dance music (EDM), hip-hop, ambient and pop production.
  • Technology as a creative instrument, not just a recording tool.
The studio in a computer

The DAW

A DAW (Digital Audio Workstation) is the digital studio: record, sequence, edit, mix and arrange on multiple tracks.

  • Key features: multitrack recording, MIDI sequencing, quantise (snapping notes to the grid), looping, automation and audio effects.
  • It puts a whole studio inside a computer.

Making sound

Synthesis

A synthesiser generates sound electronically. Common methods:

  • Subtractive synthesis: start with a rich waveform (saw or square) and filter frequencies away.
  • FM synthesis: one oscillator modulates another to create complex timbres.
  • Additive synthesis: build a sound by adding simple sine waves.

Shape the sound with an envelope (ADSR: attack, decay, sustain, release) and with filters.

Reusing sound

Sampling

  • Sampling is taking a segment of existing recorded sound and reusing it musically (a drum break, a vocal, a horn stab).
  • It is central to hip-hop and much electronic music; samples can be looped, chopped, pitched and layered.
  • It raises creative and copyright questions — a real-world issue for the digital music-maker.

Performance data

MIDI and sequencing

  • MIDI carries performance data (which note, how hard, how long) — not the audio itself.
  • A sequencer records and edits MIDI so you can change the sound, fix timing (quantise) or edit notes after playing.
  • This separation of performance data from sound is a foundation of modern production.
Shaping the mix

Effects and mixing

  • Effects shape and enhance sound: reverb (space), delay (echo), EQ (balancing frequencies), compression (evening dynamics), distortion (grit).
  • Mixing balances all tracks — levels, panning (left and right placement) and effects — into a finished stereo whole.
  • Automation lets these change over time (a filter sweep into a drop).

Genres

Electronic aesthetics

From musique concrete (music built from manipulated recorded sounds) to ambient, techno, house and hip-hop, technology has created whole genres and new aesthetics — the loop, the drop, the sampled collage. The producer becomes a composer-performer at the console.
