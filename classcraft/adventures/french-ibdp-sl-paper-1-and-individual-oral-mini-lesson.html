Mission 1

Three families of text

  • IB groups text types by who the text is for.
PersonalProfessionalMass media
blog, diary, email, personal letter, social media postblog, email, essay, formal letter, proposal, questionnaire, report, set of instructions, surveyadvertisement, article, blog, brochure, interview, leaflet, news report, opinion column, podcast, poster, review, speech, travel guide, web page…
  • Personal: you write to people you know, or to yourself.
  • Professional: to an organisation, an official, a colleague.
  • Mass media: to the public.
  • A blog appears in all three. The context decides which one.
Mission 1

Conventions: what the reader expects

  • Conventions = the features a reader expects: layout, greeting, register, structure.
  • Get them right and the reader recognises the text type at a glance.
  • In Paper 1, Criterion C (conceptual understanding) is worth 6 of 30 marks. It rewards:
    • the right text type for the task
    • register and tone that fit the audience and purpose
    • the conventions of that text type
  • Watch out: a perfect message in the wrong format still loses Criterion C marks.
Quick check
?You are writing an entry in your diary (journal intime). Which opening fits best?
Mission 1

Personal texts

  • Audience: friends, family, yourself. Register: informal (tu), but still accurate.
  • Email / personal letter: Cher Paul, / Chère Léa, (note Chère for a girl) · Salut ! · Comment ça va ?
    • Close: Grosses bises, · À bientôt ! · Amitiés,
  • Diary: date at the top · je · feelings and reflection · no greeting needed.
  • Personal blog: a title · speaks to readers (vous to all readers, or tu to one) · ends by inviting replies: Et vous, qu'en pensez-vous ?
  • Your turn: open an email to your cousin Sophie.
    Show answer

    Chère Sophie, comment ça va ?

Mission 1

Professional texts

  • Audience: an official, a company, a head teacher. Register: formal (vous).
  • Formal letter:
    • your details top left; the recipient's below on the right
    • place and date: Lyon, le 12 mars 2027
    • Objet : demande de stage – Re: work placement request
    • Madame, Monsieur, (or Monsieur le Directeur,)
    • close: Je vous prie d'agréer, Madame, Monsieur, l'expression de mes salutations distinguées.
  • Formal email: Objet : · Madame, · close Cordialement,
  • Proposal / report: a title · headings (Introduction, Constat, Recommandations, Conclusion) · impersonal phrases: Il est recommandé de… – it is recommended to…
  • Set of instructions: numbered steps; imperative (Mélangez) or infinitive (Mélanger).
Quick check
?Which line correctly ends a formal letter to a woman you have never met?
Mission 1

Mass media texts

  • Audience: the public. Register: usually formal or neutral; tone depends on purpose.
  • Article: titre (headline) · chapeau (a bold lead sentence that sums up the story) · intertitres (subheadings) · journalist's name.
  • Speech: Mesdames et Messieurs, · nous to include the audience · rhetorical questions · close Merci de votre attention.
  • Review (critique): title and key details (author, director) · short summary, no spoilers · evaluation · recommendation.
  • Brochure / leaflet: headings · bullet points · imperatives (Venez découvrir…) · practical details (horaires, tarifs).
  • Interview: a short introduction, then questions and answers with names; vous.
  • Watch out: a speech written like an essay has no listener. Speak to the audience.

Mission 2

Paper 1 at a glance

  • One piece of writing. Choose one task from three.
  • Each task offers three text types. You choose the one that fits.
  • 1 hour 15 minutes · 250–400 words.
  • 30 marks, 25% of the grade:
    • A Language (12): range and accuracy
    • B Message (12): relevant, developed, well organised ideas
    • C Conceptual understanding (6): text type, register, tone, conventions
  • No dictionary. Your knowledge of facts is not tested: plausible invented details (a date, a place) are fine.
Mission 2

Decode the prompt: C, A, P

  • Example prompt (original):
    • Votre ville a décidé de fermer la piscine municipale pour construire un parking. Vous voulez convaincre les jeunes de la ville de participer à un rassemblement contre ce projet.
    • Article de blog · Lettre formelle · Page de journal intime
  • Context (situation): the town pool will close; a car park will replace it.
  • Audience (reader): young people of the town. Not the mayor, not you.
  • Purpose (aim): persuade them and get them to act (come to the rally).
  • Text type: a blog post. It speaks to many young readers and can call them to act.
  • The formal letter fits the mayor; the diary has no reader at all.

Mission 2

Context: answer this task, not another

  • Criterion B (Message) rewards ideas that are relevant to the task, developed and clearly organised.
  • Use the context: name the pool, the car park, the date of the rally.
  • Develop each idea: point → reason → example.
  • Watch out: a learnt essay on "sport and health" ignores the situation and the reader. It loses Message marks, however good the French.
  • Your turn: one sentence of context for the blog.
    Show answer

    La mairie veut fermer la piscine des Tilleuls en septembre pour construire un parking.

Mission 2

Worked example: plan and opening

  • Plan (5 minutes):

1. Hook: a question to the readers.

2. The problem: the car-park project.

3. Three reasons: health, a place to meet, learning to swim.

4. Call to action: the rally (day, time, place).

5. Close: invite comments.

  • Opening (example text):
    • Sauvons la piscine des Tilleuls !
    • Publié le 3 juin par Inès
    • Vous avez appris à nager à la piscine des Tilleuls ? Moi aussi. Pourtant, la mairie veut la fermer en septembre pour construire… un parking ! Avant qu'il ne soit trop tard, nous devons réagir.
  • Close (example text): Rendez-vous samedi à 10 heures devant la mairie. Venez nombreux, et partagez cet article ! Et vous, qu'en pensez-vous ? Laissez un commentaire.
  • What scores: title, date and author (blog conventions) · vous to readers (audience) · the pool and the car park named (context) · the call to act (purpose).
Mission 2

SL and HL; your time

  • 250–400 words: three well-developed ideas are enough.
  • Time: about 10 minutes to choose and plan · write · keep 10 minutes to check.
  • Checklist:
    • Text type chosen from the three, and its conventions shown.
    • tu or vous consistent all the way through.
    • Purpose achieved (did you persuade? recommend? inform?).
    • Context details used.
    • Paragraphs; connectors (de plus, cependant, par conséquent).
    • Accuracy: agreements, verb endings, accents.

Mission 3

The oral at a glance

  • You see two visual stimuli, each on a different theme. You choose one.
  • Each is labelled in French with its theme. Any words in the picture itself are in French, and there are only a few.
  • 15 minutes to prepare. Brief notes only (at most 10 bullet points), no script.
  • Part 1: presentation, 3–4 min. You talk; the teacher listens.
  • Part 2: follow-up discussion, 4–5 min, on the stimulus and its theme.
  • Part 3: general conversation, 5–6 min, on at least one other theme.
  • 30 marks, 25% of the grade: A Language 12 · B1 Message, visual stimulus 6 · B2 Message, conversation 6 · C Interactive skills 6.
Mission 3

Four moves for the presentation

  • 1. Décrire: what you see, briefly.
  • 2. Interpréter: the message. What do the theme label and any words in the picture add?
  • 3. Relier: link to the theme and to the French-speaking world.
  • 4. Réagir: your opinion, an example from your own experience.
  • B1 rewards a talk that stays on the stimulus and makes clear links to francophone culture.
  • Watch out: four minutes of description is not a presentation. Keep décrire to about 45 seconds.
  • Watch out: a learnt speech on the theme that ignores the picture scores low on B1.
Quick check
?Stimulus: a Repair Café, people fixing a bike and sewing a jacket. A sign on the wall reads « Ne jetez plus, réparez ! » Theme label: Partage de la planète. Which plan best meets B1?
Mission 3

Language for a picture

  • Il s'agit de… – it shows / it is about…
  • Au premier plan – in the foreground · à l'arrière-plan – in the background · au centre, à gauche, à droite
  • On voit… · On aperçoit… – you can make out…
  • On dirait que… – it looks as if… · Peut-être que… + indicative
  • Le slogan signifie que… – the slogan means that… · Sur le panneau, on peut lire… – on the sign you can read…
  • L'image veut montrer que… – the picture aims to show that…
  • Watch out: dans le premier plan → au premier plan.
  • Watch out: French has no "is repairing" tense: il répare, or il est en train de réparer.
  • Your turn: "In the background, a woman is sewing a jacket."
    Show answer

    À l'arrière-plan, une femme coud une veste.

Mission 3

Worked example: the presentation

  • Stimulus from the earlier screen. Theme: Partage de la planète.
  • Décrire: Sur cette image, on voit un Repair Café. Au premier plan, un homme répare un vélo ; à l'arrière-plan, une femme coud une veste.
  • Interpréter + thème: Le slogan, « Ne jetez plus, réparez ! », résume le message : réparer, c'est jeter moins. L'image illustre donc le thème du partage de la planète.
  • Relier + réagir: En France, depuis 2021, beaucoup d'appareils électroniques affichent un indice de réparabilité. Personnellement, je trouve cette idée excellente, parce que…
  • Then develop: why it matters, a personal example, one problem (Cependant, réparer coûte parfois plus cher que racheter.).
  • Notice: present tense for the image; depuis + present for "since 2021".
Quick check
?Choose the best French for: "In the foreground, a man is repairing a bike."
Mission 3

The follow-up discussion

  • The teacher asks about your stimulus and its theme, to help you go further.
  • Extend every answer: answer → reason → example → go further (a comparison or a hypothesis).
  • si + imperfect → conditional: Si tout le monde réparait, on jetterait moins.
  • Example question: Avez-vous déjà fait réparer quelque chose ?
  • Example answer: Oui, l'an dernier, j'ai fait réparer mon téléphone au lieu d'en acheter un neuf. Si tout le monde faisait pareil, on jetterait moins.
  • Your turn: extend Oui, je recycle.
    Show answer

    Oui, je recycle le papier et le verre, parce que je veux réduire nos déchets.

Mission 3

The general conversation

  • 5–6 minutes on at least one other theme. Revise all five: Identités, Expériences, Ingéniosité humaine, Organisation sociale, Partage de la planète.
  • B2 rewards relevant, developed answers. C rewards real interaction: you understand, respond and keep it going.
  • If you don't understand, ask, in French:
    • Pourriez-vous répéter la question, s'il vous plaît ?
    • Si je comprends bien, vous voulez savoir si…
  • To gain thinking time: C'est une bonne question. Je dirais que… · Comment dire… ?
  • Watch out: a learnt monologue that does not answer the question shows no interaction.
Quick check
?Teacher: Pensez-vous que les jeunes réparent moins que leurs grands-parents ? Which reply scores best for interaction (C)?
Mission 3

Your 15 minutes

  • 2 min: look at both stimuli. Choose the one you can link to the francophone world best.
  • 8 min: brief notes for the four moves: key words, not sentences, and no more than 10 bullet points. One French fact or example you know.
  • 3 min: plan your first sentence and your last.
  • 2 min: predict three follow-up questions and how you'd extend each.
  • Watch out: writing a full script wastes time, and reading it aloud sounds unnatural.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Text types come in three families: personal, professional, mass media. The reader decides.
  • Conventions (layout, greeting, register, structure) earn Criterion C marks.
  • Pick tu or vous once, and keep it all the way through.

Mission 2

  • Decode every prompt: Context, Audience, Purpose, then choose the text type.
  • Audience sets register; purpose sets the language moves; context keeps the message relevant.
  • 250–400 words in 1 h 15.

Mission 3

  • Choose the stimulus you can link to the French-speaking world.
  • Presentation: décrire, interpréter, relier, réagir in 3–4 minutes.
  • In the discussion and conversation: answer, reason, example, go further, and ask if you don't understand.
