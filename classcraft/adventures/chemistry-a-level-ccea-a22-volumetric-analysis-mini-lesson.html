Mission 1

Redox titrations

  • Like an acid–base titration, but the reaction is redox.
  • An oxidising agent of known concentration reacts with a reducing agent (or the reverse).
  • You need the balanced equation to get the mole ratio.
  • Build it from the half-equations: electrons lost = electrons gained.
Mission 1

The manganate(VII) titration

  • Potassium manganate(VII), KMnO4(aq), goes in the burette. It is deep purple.
  • The flask holds the Fe²⁺(aq) plus excess dilute sulfuric acid.
  • Each drop turns colourless as MnO4⁻ is reduced to almost colourless Mn²⁺.
  • End-point: the first permanent pale pink. One drop of MnO4⁻ is now in excess.
  • Self-indicating: no indicator needed.
  • The solution is too dark to see the bottom of the meniscus: read the top.
Mission 1

The equation

  • Reduction: MnO4⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H2O
  • Oxidation: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻ (× 5)
  • MnO4⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H2O + 5Fe³⁺
  • Ratio: 1 mol MnO4⁻ : 5 mol Fe²⁺.
  • The H⁺ must be in excess; too little acid gives brown MnO2 instead of Mn²⁺.
Quick check
?Why is the iron(II) solution acidified with dilute sulfuric acid, not dilute hydrochloric acid?
Mission 1

Worked example: iron in a tablet

One tablet is dissolved in dilute H2SO4 and made up to 100.0 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions need a mean titre of 23.45 cm³ of 0.00200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO4 (concordant titres, within 0.10 cm³). Find the mass of iron per tablet.

  • n(MnO4⁻) = 0.00200 × 23.45/1000 = 4.69 × 10⁻⁵ mol
  • n(Fe²⁺) in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 4.69 × 10⁻⁵ = 2.345 × 10⁻⁴ mol
  • In 100.0 cm³: × 4 = 9.38 × 10⁻⁴ mol
  • Mass of Fe = 9.38 × 10⁻⁴ × 55.8 = 0.0523 g = 52.3 mg
  • Each burette reading is ±0.05 cm³, so a titre is ±0.10 cm³.
Quick check
?25.0 cm³ of an iron(II) solution needs 24.00 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO4 for complete reaction in acid. What is the concentration of Fe²⁺?
Mission check

?Hair bleach contains hydrogen peroxide. A diluted sample (25.0 cm³) is acidified and titrated with 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO4; the mean titre is 18.60 cm³. Half-equations: MnO4⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H2O and H2O2 → O2 + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻. What is the concentration of H2O2 in the diluted sample?

No indicator needed: the first lasting pink shows MnO4⁻ in excess. The ratio always comes from balancing the electrons.

Mission 2

Two steps: free iodine, then titrate it

  • Step 1: the oxidising agent reacts with excess iodide ions. Iodine is freed: 2I⁻ → I2 + 2e⁻.
  • Step 2: titrate the iodine with sodium thiosulfate, Na2S2O3(aq), from the burette.
  • I2 + 2S2O3²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S4O6²⁻
  • Thiosulfate half-equation: 2S2O3²⁻ → S4O6²⁻ + 2e⁻ (tetrathionate forms).
  • So: oxidising agent → I2 → 2S2O3²⁻ per I2.
Mission 2

The end-point: starch

  • Iodine solution is brown. As thiosulfate is added it fades to pale yellow ("straw").
  • Now add starch: a blue-black colour forms with the remaining iodine.
  • Add thiosulfate dropwise: the end-point is when the blue-black disappears.
  • Add starch near the end-point. With a lot of iodine, the starch complex releases it slowly, so the end-point would be late.
Quick check
?In an iodine–thiosulfate titration, why is starch added only when the solution is pale yellow?
Mission 2

Worked example: copper in a copper(II) salt

  • Copper(II) ions oxidise iodide: 2Cu²⁺ + 4I⁻ → 2CuI + I2 (off-white CuI precipitate).
  • Chain: 2Cu²⁺ → I2 → 2S2O3²⁻, so Cu²⁺ : S2O3²⁻ = 1 : 1.
  • 2.50 g of a copper(II) salt is made up to 250.0 cm³. 25.0 cm³ + excess KI needs 19.80 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ Na2S2O3.
  • n(S2O3²⁻) = 0.0500 × 19.80/1000 = 9.90 × 10⁻⁴ mol = n(Cu²⁺) in 25.0 cm³.
  • In 250.0 cm³: × 10 = 9.90 × 10⁻³ mol. Mass Cu = × 63.5 = 0.629 g.
  • % Cu = 0.6287/2.50 × 100 = 25.1%.
  • End-point: blue-black → off-white (the CuI precipitate stays).
Quick check
?Bleach contains chlorate(I) ions. In acid with excess iodide: ClO⁻ + 2I⁻ + 2H⁺ → Cl⁻ + I2 + H2O. The iodine is then titrated: I2 + 2S2O3²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S4O6²⁻. How many moles of thiosulfate react for each mole of ClO⁻?
Mission 2

Other oxidising agents: iodate(V)

  • The same method measures many oxidising agents: add excess acidified KI, titrate the I2.
  • Iodate(V): IO3⁻ + 5I⁻ + 6H⁺ → 3I2 + 3H2O.
  • Chain: IO3⁻ → 3I2 → 6S2O3²⁻.
  • KIO3 can be weighed out pure, so it is used to standardise thiosulfate.
  • Example: 25.0 cm³ of 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ KIO3 needs 29.40 cm³ of thiosulfate: c = (2.50 × 10⁻⁴ × 6)/0.02940 = 0.0510 mol dm⁻³.
Mission 2

Method

  • Pipette the sample into a conical flask; add excess KI (and acid if the equation needs H⁺).
  • Titrate with thiosulfate until pale yellow; add starch; continue dropwise to the end-point.
  • Repeat to concordant titres (within 0.10 cm³); use their mean.
  • Excess KI makes sure all the oxidising agent reacts; its amount need not be known.
Mission check

?25.0 cm³ of diluted hydrogen peroxide is added to excess acidified KI: H2O2 + 2I⁻ + 2H⁺ → I2 + 2H2O. The iodine needs 22.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ Na2S2O3. What is the concentration of H2O2?

Copper, bleach or peroxide: the oxidising agent frees iodine, and the vanishing blue-black shows when thiosulfate has used it all.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Manganate(VII) titrations: excess dilute H2SO4; self-indicating; end-point first permanent pale pink.
  • MnO4⁻ : Fe²⁺ = 1 : 5; build any other ratio from the half-equations.

Mission 2

  • Oxidising agent + excess I⁻ → I2; I2 + 2S2O3²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S4O6²⁻.
  • Starch near the end-point; end-point: blue-black disappears.
  • Cu²⁺ : S2O3²⁻ = 1 : 1; ClO⁻ and H2O2 : S2O3²⁻ = 1 : 2.
  • IO3⁻ : S2O3²⁻ = 1 : 6.
