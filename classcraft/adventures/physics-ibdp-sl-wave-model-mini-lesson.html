Mission 1: Sing a higher note. Does the sound reach the far side of the room any sooner?

Make a guess: Sing a higher note. Does the sound reach the far side of the room any sooner?

Quick check
?A loudspeaker plays a steady note to a listener 5 m away in a room with still air. Which of the following travels from the loudspeaker to the listener?
Mission 1

What a wave carries

  • A progressive (travelling) wave transfers energy from place to place.
  • It does not transfer matter. Each particle oscillates about a fixed position.
Mission 1

Transverse and longitudinal

  • Transverse: oscillations perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer. Waves on a string; all EM waves.
  • Longitudinal: oscillations parallel to the direction of energy transfer. Sound; a slinky pushed along its length.
  • Longitudinal waves have compressions (particles close, pressure higher) and rarefactions (spread out, pressure lower).
Mission 1

Describing a wave

  • Displacement: distance and direction of a particle from its rest position.
  • Amplitude A: maximum displacement.
  • Wavelength λ: distance between neighbouring points that move in step, e.g. crest to crest.
  • Period T: time for one complete oscillation. Frequency f: oscillations per second, in Hz.
  • f = 1/T.
Mission 1

The wave equation

  • In one period the wave moves on one wavelength: v = λ/T, so v = fλ.
  • For sound in air and waves on a string, the medium sets v and the source sets f.
  • Change f in the same medium: λ changes, v stays the same.
  • Example: sound at 340 m s⁻¹, f = 680 Hz → λ = 340/680 = 0.50 m.
Mission 1

Two graphs, two meanings

  • Displacement–position graph: a snapshot of the whole wave. Read λ and A.
  • Displacement–time graph: one particle over time. Read T and A.
  • Same shape, different axis: always check the x-axis label.
  • Particle direction: for a wave moving right, a point copies what the point just to its left did a moment ago.
Quick check
?A transverse wave travels along a stretched string. Adjacent crests are 1.2 m apart. A graph of displacement y against time t for one point on the string is a sine curve: y = 0 at t = 0, y is a maximum at t = 5 ms, y = 0 at t = 10 ms, y is a minimum at t = 15 ms and y = 0 at t = 20 ms, after which the pattern repeats. What is the wave speed?
Mission 1

Graphs of a longitudinal wave

  • Plot displacement along the direction of travel (+ = forwards) against rest position.
  • The graph looks like a transverse wave, but the particles move along the axis.
  • Compression centre: displacement zero, with particles on both sides displaced towards it.
  • Rarefaction centre: displacement zero, with neighbours displaced away from it.
Quick check
?A sound wave travels in the +x direction through air. Displacements of air layers in the +x direction are positive. At one instant the graph of displacement s against rest position x is a sine curve: s = 0 at x = 0, s = −2.0 μm at x = 15 cm, s = 0 at x = 30 cm, s = +2.0 μm at x = 45 cm and s = 0 at x = 60 cm. Where, between x = 0 and x = 60 cm, is the centre of a rarefaction?
Mission check

?A vibrator sends waves along a stretched string at constant tension. At 25 Hz the wavelength is 0.80 m. The frequency is raised to 100 Hz. What is the new wavelength, and how long does a wave now take to travel 4.0 m along the string?

The medium sets the speed. A higher note has a higher frequency and a shorter wavelength, but the same speed: it arrives no sooner.

Quick check
?A candle flame is held just in front of a loudspeaker playing a loud, low note. The flame flickers rapidly towards and away from the speaker, but it is not blown away from it. What does this show about the air at the flame?
Quick check
?A student at one end of a long steel railway track puts an ear to the rail. At the other end, 1.0 km away, the rail is struck once with a hammer. The student hears two separate sounds, 0.20 s and 2.9 s after the blow. Which conclusion do these observations support?
Mission 2

Speed of sound depends on the medium

  • Rough speeds: air ≈ 340 m s⁻¹, water ≈ 1500 m s⁻¹, steel ≈ 5000–6000 m s⁻¹.
  • The source sets f; the medium sets v. Into a new medium, f stays the same and v changes, so λ = v/f changes.
Mission check

?A singer on a stage is 17 m from a listener. Her sound reaches the listener 0.050 s later. Which observation is the best evidence that the air itself does not make the trip from the singer to the listener?

Same for air: each layer only oscillates about its own position. Energy reaches your ear; the air that was at the speaker stays there.

Quick check
?Predict first. Which statement describes the nature of electromagnetic waves?
Mission 3

What an EM wave is

  • An electromagnetic (EM) wave is an oscillating electric field and an oscillating magnetic field, travelling together.
  • The two fields are perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel: EM waves are transverse.
  • Energy travels along the direction of travel; the fields oscillate across it.
  • EM waves need no medium: they cross a vacuum.
  • In a vacuum all EM waves travel at the same speed, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, whatever their wavelength.
  • Examples: the radio waves from an FM radio station, visible light, the X-rays used in medical imaging.
Quick check
?Predict first. Which statement describes a difference between mechanical waves and electromagnetic waves?
Mission 3

Mechanical versus EM

  • Mechanical waves (sound, water waves, waves on a string) are oscillations of a material medium. No medium, no wave.
  • EM waves are oscillating fields. They need no medium.
  • Mechanical wave speed depends on the medium: sound is faster in water than in air, and faster in warm air than in cold.
  • EM waves travel at c only in a vacuum. In glass or water they are slower.
  • Mechanical waves can be transverse or longitudinal. EM waves are always transverse.
  • Both transfer energy, not matter.
Quick check
?Predict first. The diagram shows the regions of the electromagnetic spectrum on a logarithmic scale of wavelength λ (wavelength increases to the right). A wave has a wavelength of 2 × 10⁻⁵ m. In which region of the spectrum shown does it lie?
Mission 3

The spectrum, in order

  • Seven regions, by decreasing wavelength: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
  • Same order: increasing frequency (f = c/λ) and increasing photon energy.
  • Visible light, longest to shortest: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, violet.
  • Visible runs from about 700 nm (red) to 400 nm (violet).
  • The regions differ only in wavelength (and so frequency). Same nature, same c.
  • Boundaries are not sharp. X-rays and gamma rays overlap: they are named by origin (X-rays from electrons, gamma rays from nuclei).
Mission 3

Orders of magnitude

  • Typical wavelengths (learn the power of ten):
  • Radio: 1 m (from about 0.1 m up to kilometres)
  • Microwave: 10⁻² m (1 mm to 0.1 m)
  • Infrared: 10⁻⁵ m (700 nm to 1 mm)
  • Visible: 5 × 10⁻⁷ m (400–700 nm)
  • Ultraviolet: 10⁻⁷ m (10–400 nm)
  • X-ray: 10⁻¹⁰ m (about 10⁻⁸ to 10⁻¹² m)
  • Gamma: 10⁻¹² m (below about 10⁻¹¹ m)
Mission check

?In a vacuum, the microwaves in a speed camera have a wavelength of 3.0 cm, and the light from a yellow lamp has a wavelength of 600 nm. How do their frequencies compare?

Microwaves and light, like radio waves and X-rays, are the same kind of wave, with the same speed c in a vacuum. Only the wavelength, and so the frequency, tells them apart.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • A progressive wave transfers energy, not matter. Transverse: oscillation ⟂ travel; longitudinal: oscillation ∥ travel.
  • f = 1/T and v = fλ. For sound and waves on strings, the medium sets v and the source sets f.

Mission 2

  • Sound: a longitudinal wave of compressions and rarefactions; the medium oscillates about fixed positions and carries energy, not itself. It needs a medium; v = fλ, set by the medium.

Mission 3

  • EM waves: oscillating E and B fields, perpendicular to each other and to the direction of travel (transverse); no medium; all travel at c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ in a vacuum.
  • Radio → microwave → infrared → visible → UV → X-ray → gamma: wavelength falls, frequency rises.
