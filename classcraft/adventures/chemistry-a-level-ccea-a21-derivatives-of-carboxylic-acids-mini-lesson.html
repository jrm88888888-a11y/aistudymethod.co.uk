Mission 1

Meet the acid derivatives

  • Swap the –OH of a carboxylic acid for another group: an acid derivative.
  • Acyl chloride: –COCl. Ethanoyl chloride, CH3COCl. Name ends -oyl chloride.
  • Acid anhydride: two acyl groups joined by O. Ethanoic anhydride, (CH3CO)2O.
  • Amide: –CONH2. Ethanamide, CH3CONH2 (a primary amide).
  • Secondary (N-substituted) amide: –CONHR, e.g. N-methylethanamide, CH3CONHCH3.
  • Esters and acyl chlorides are both derivatives of carboxylic acids.
Mission 1

Making an acyl chloride

  • Start from the carboxylic acid. Dry conditions: water destroys the product.
  • PCl5: CH3COOH + PCl5 → CH3COCl + POCl3 + HCl
  • Steamy fumes of HCl. The acyl chloride is separated by distillation.
Mission 1

Why so reactive?

  • The carbonyl carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms: O and Cl.
  • So it is strongly δ+: a target for any nucleophile with a lone pair.
  • Chlorine then leaves easily as Cl⁻: a good leaving group.
  • So acyl chlorides react at room temperature, often violently. No catalyst.
  • In a carboxylic acid the C is less δ+, and –OH is a poor leaving group.
  • So, the opening question: water vapour in the air hydrolyses it. The "fumes" are HCl.
Quick check
?Ethanoyl chloride reacts with cold water within seconds. Ethanoic acid can stay in water for years without changing. Which explains the difference?
Mission 1

Four nucleophiles, one pattern

  • The nucleophile swaps in for Cl. HCl is the other product.
  • Water → carboxylic acid: CH3COCl + H2O → CH3COOH + HCl
  • Alcohol → ester: CH3COCl + CH3CH2OH → CH3COOCH2CH3 + HCl. Fast, and not reversible.
  • Ammonia → primary amide:
  • CH3COCl + 2NH3 → CH3CONH2 + NH4Cl. The second NH3 takes up the HCl.
  • Primary amine → N-substituted amide: CH3COCl + 2CH3NH2 → CH3CONHCH3 + CH3NH3⁺Cl⁻
Quick check
?Ethanoyl chloride is added dropwise to an excess of ethylamine, CH3CH2NH2. Which two products form?
Mission 1

Acid anhydrides: the gentler cousin

  • Same reactions, but the leaving group is ethanoate, not Cl⁻.
  • By-product: ethanoic acid, not HCl. Slower: warming is usually needed.
  • You meet it in one key reaction: making aspirin.
Mission 1

Making aspirin

  • Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) has a phenolic –OH and a –COOH.
  • Ethanoic anhydride ethanoylates the –OH, making an ester: aspirin.
  • C7H6O3 + (CH3CO)2O → C9H8O4 + CH3COOH
  • Method: warm with a few drops of concentrated acid catalyst (H3PO4 or H2SO4).
  • Add cold water; collect crystals by filtration under reduced pressure.
  • Recrystallise from the minimum of hot solvent; dry; check the melting point (pure: about 136 °C, sharp).
Mission 1

Aspirin in the body and in a burette

  • Aspirin is a weak acid, only slightly soluble in water.
  • Its sodium salt (–COO⁻Na⁺, "soluble aspirin") dissolves far better: absorbed faster, with less irritation of the stomach lining.
  • Titration with NaOH: one –COOH, so 1 mol aspirin : 1 mol NaOH.
  • Worked example: 25.0 cm³ of aspirin solution needs 15.60 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ NaOH.
  • n(NaOH) = 0.01560 × 0.0200 = 3.12 × 10⁻⁴ mol = n(aspirin).
  • c = 3.12 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0125 mol dm⁻³. × Mr 180.0 = 2.25 g dm⁻³.
  • Often done as a back titration: boil with excess NaOH (the ester is hydrolysed too, so 1 : 2), then titrate the leftover NaOH with HCl.
Quick check
?Salicylic acid (2-hydroxybenzoic acid) is warmed with ethanoic anhydride to make aspirin. What happens?
Mission check

?Chloroethane, CH3CH2Cl, and ethanoyl chloride, CH3COCl, are each added to cold water. What do you see, and why?

Two electronegative atoms on one carbon make acyl chlorides the most reactive acid derivatives. Even water, a weak nucleophile, attacks at once.

Mission 2

Heating under reflux

  • Use it for slow reactions with volatile liquids: heat for a long time, lose nothing.
  • Quickfit glassware: ground-glass joints fit together without bungs.
  • Round-bottomed flask + anti-bumping granules (smooth boiling).
  • Condenser vertical: vapour condenses and drips back into the flask.
  • Water in at the bottom, out at the top: the jacket stays full.
  • Top left open. Never heat a sealed apparatus.
  • Heat with an electric heater, water bath or sand bath: organic liquids are flammable.
Mission 2

Distillation

  • Separates liquids by boiling point.
  • Flask + still head + thermometer bulb level with the side arm: it reads the vapour that is distilling.
  • Condenser sloping down, water in at the lower end.
  • Collect the fraction that distils within a degree or two of the product's boiling point.
  • Cool the receiver in ice for a very volatile product.
Mission 2

Reflux or distil? Oxidising ethanol

  • Ethanol + acidified potassium dichromate(VI). Boiling points: ethanal 20 °C, ethanol 78 °C, ethanoic acid 118 °C.
  • Want the aldehyde? Add the oxidant slowly to warm ethanol and distil off ethanal as it forms.
  • It leaves before it can be oxidised further.
  • Want the carboxylic acid? Heat under reflux with excess oxidant. Ethanal can't escape, so it is oxidised to the acid.
  • Then distil to collect the acid.
Quick check
?A student wants to make propanal from propan-1-ol using acidified potassium dichromate(VI). Which method gives the most propanal?
Mission 2

Separating funnel and washing

  • Two liquids that don't mix form layers. The denser layer is at the bottom.
  • Not sure which is which? Add a little water and see which layer grows.
  • Washing: shake the crude product with an aqueous solution, then separate.
  • Water removes water-soluble impurities. Sodium hydrogencarbonate solution removes acid.
  • Stopper, invert, open the tap often to release pressure (NaHCO3 + acid gives CO2).
  • Run off the lower layer through the tap. Keep the layer that holds your product.
Mission 2

Drying and redistillation

  • The washed organic layer is cloudy: it holds tiny drops of water.
  • Add an anhydrous salt: MgSO4, CaCl2 or Na2SO4. Swirl.
  • Keep adding until the salt no longer clumps and the liquid is clear.
  • Decant or filter off the drying agent.
  • Redistil, collecting only near the product's boiling point.
Mission 2

Worked example: 2-chloro-2-methylpropane

  • Shake 2-methylpropan-2-ol with concentrated HCl in a separating funnel (fast at room temperature: no reflux).
  • Two layers form. The product (density 0.84 g cm⁻³) is the upper layer. Discard the lower aqueous layer.
  • Wash with NaHCO3(aq), venting often, to remove acid. Then wash with water.
  • Dry with anhydrous CaCl2 or Na2SO4 until clear.
  • Distil, collecting at about 50–52 °C (bp 51 °C).
Quick check
?A student shakes a crude organic product with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution in a separating funnel. Why must the tap be opened often?
Mission 2

Purifying a solid: recrystallisation

  • Choose a solvent the product dissolves in well when hot, poorly when cold.
  • Dissolve the crude solid in the minimum volume of hot solvent.
  • Filter while hot to remove insoluble impurities.
  • Cool slowly: the product crystallises; soluble impurities, present in small amounts, stay in solution.
  • Filter under reduced pressure (Büchner funnel), wash with a little cold solvent, dry.
  • Methyl 3-nitrobenzoate is recrystallised this way, from ethanol.
Mission 2

Melting point: is it pure?

  • Melting point apparatus: a little solid in a capillary tube, heated slowly near the melting point.
  • Pure: melts sharply (within about 1 °C), at the data-book value. Aspirin: 136 °C.
  • Impure: melts lower and over a wider range.
  • Identify a carbonyl: make its 2,4-DNP derivative (orange crystals), recrystallise, measure its melting point, match it to a table.
Quick check
?Pure aspirin melts at 136 °C. A student's recrystallised aspirin melts over the range 126–131 °C. What does this show?
Mission check

?A student makes 1-bromobutane (bp 102 °C, density 1.27 g cm⁻³) from butan-1-ol (bp 118 °C). The crude product contains water, acid and some unreacted alcohol. Which purification is best?

Each step uses a different property: density (separate), reactivity (wash), water uptake (dry), boiling point (distil). Then a melting or boiling point proves purity.

Mission 3: How can boiling cooking fat with an alkali turn it into soap?

Make a guess: How can boiling cooking fat with an alkali turn it into soap?

Mission 3

Fats and oils are triesters

  • Glycerol: propane-1,2,3-triol, CH2(OH)CH(OH)CH2OH. Three –OH groups.
  • Fatty acids: long-chain carboxylic acids, e.g. octadecanoic (stearic) acid, C17H35COOH.
  • Each –OH of glycerol forms an ester with one fatty acid.
  • The result is a triester (a triglyceride): three ester groups in one molecule.
  • Animal fats (mostly solid) and vegetable oils (liquid) are both triesters of glycerol.
Mission 3

Soap: hydrolysis in alkali

  • Boil (reflux) the triester with NaOH(aq).
  • All three ester groups are hydrolysed.
  • Triester + 3NaOH → glycerol + 3RCOO⁻Na⁺
  • The sodium salts of long-chain acids are soap.
  • As with any ester in alkali, the alkali makes the salt, and the reaction goes to completion.
Quick check
?Glyceryl trioctadecanoate, C57H110O6 (Mr 890.0), is a fat. What mass of NaOH (Mr 40.0) is needed to hydrolyse 8.90 g of it completely?
Mission 3

Biodiesel

  • Heat vegetable oil with methanol and a catalyst (e.g. KOH).
  • Triester + 3CH3OH → 3RCOOCH3 + glycerol
  • One ester swapped for another: transesterification.
  • Biodiesel: a mixture of methyl esters of long-chain carboxylic acids.
Mission 3

Transesterification

  • Ester + alcohol ⇌ new ester + new alcohol
  • Ester + carboxylic acid ⇌ new ester + new acid
  • Biodiesel uses the alcohol route (methanol).
  • Margarines and spreads: chains are swapped between triesters to change how the fat melts.
Mission 3

Margarine: hardening oils

  • Oils have many C=C bonds: kinked chains, low melting point.
  • Hydrogen + finely divided nickel catalyst, heated.
  • Some C=C become C–C: straighter chains pack better.
  • Melting point rises: liquid oil → soft solid spread.
  • The ester groups are not changed.
Quick check
?Rapeseed oil is heated with methanol and a potassium hydroxide catalyst. What are the products?
Quick check
?Sunflower oil is hardened with hydrogen and a nickel catalyst to make a spread. What changes?
Quick check
?Which of these is a soap?
Mission check

?Triacetin, a food additive, is the triester of propane-1,2,3-triol and ethanoic acid, (CH3COO)3C3H5. It is boiled with excess sodium hydroxide solution. What are the products?

Hot alkali hydrolyses every ester group in a fat: glycerol plus the sodium salts of the fatty acids. Those salts are soap.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Acyl chlorides: δ+ carbonyl C and Cl⁻ leaving group. Water → acid, alcohol → ester, NH3 → amide, amine → N-substituted amide; HCl formed.
  • Ethanoic anhydride reacts the same way, more gently, giving ethanoic acid: used to make aspirin.

Mission 2

  • Reflux: heat without loss. Distil: separate by boiling point. Separating funnel: by density. Recrystallise: by solubility, hot v cold.
  • Pure solid: sharp melting point at the data value. Impure: lower and broader.
  • Your practicals: halogenoalkane, oxidation, ester, aspirin, 2,4-DNP derivatives, methyl 3-nitrobenzoate.

Mission 3

  • Fats and oils are triesters of glycerol. Hot NaOH hydrolyses all three ester groups: glycerol + soap (3 RCOO⁻Na⁺).
  • Methanol + catalyst instead: transesterification → methyl esters (biodiesel) + glycerol.
