IB World Religions SL · Religious Practices & Rituals

Religious Practices & Rituals
Now in 7 mini-lessons.

This topic is taught in the mini-lessons below. Each mission starts from a question: make a guess, learn the idea, then check it. About 20 minutes each. No login needed.

Part 3: Internal assessment – investigative study

  1. You have 1,800 words to study one living religious practice. How do you choose a question worth asking?
  2. Your interviews are done. How do you turn what people told you into real analysis?

Part 2: In-depth study – Hinduism (part 1 of 3)

  1. In Hindu worship, why does seeing the deity matter, and being seen by it matter just as much?
  2. Why do Hindus light bonfires and throw colours at Holi, and welcome a warrior goddess home each autumn?
  3. Can a river wash away wrongdoing, and can a holy place exist outside India?
  4. Hindus say some scriptures were "heard" and others "remembered". Why does the difference matter?
  5. A boy asks Death what happens after we die. What does Death teach him about the self?
  6. A warrior refuses to fight his own family. Why does his charioteer Krishna tell him to stand up?

Start this mini-lesson →

Part 2: In-depth study – Buddhism (part 1 of 2)

  1. Buddhists "take refuge" every day. What are they sheltering from, and where do they go?
  2. Why would Buddhists bow to a statue and give gifts, if the Buddha cannot receive them?
  3. The Buddha wrote nothing down. How do Buddhists know what he taught?
  4. If a father lies to get his children out of a burning house, has he done wrong?
  5. Why did the Buddha's first sermon sound like a doctor's diagnosis?
  6. If a chariot is only its parts, is there anyone inside you doing the steering?
  7. If the Buddhist goal is beyond words, how can anyone aim for it?

Start this mini-lesson →

Part 2: In-depth study – Sikhism (part 1 of 2)

  1. Any building can become a gurdwara. What turns it into one, and why does everyone eat there?
  2. If two people are both Sikhs, why might only one of them wear a steel bangle, comb and sword?
  3. Why do Sikhs light up the Golden Temple for a prisoner's release, and mark New Year with a call for a head?
  4. Who wrote the Sikh scripture, and why do scholars argue about how it was put together?
  5. Sikhs treat a book as their living Guru. What does that mean, and is it worship?
  6. Why do Sikhs start each day with a poem that says God cannot be grasped by thinking?
  7. Can a God "without qualities" also be a friend, a mother and a king?

Start this mini-lesson →

Part 2: In-depth study – Judaism (part 1 of 3)

  1. Why would someone choose not to switch on a light for 25 hours every week?
  2. Why does a Jewish family retell the same escape story at the same meal every year?
  3. Why do Jews blow a ram's horn, then fast for 25 hours ten days later?
  4. Is the Torah God's own words, or human words inspired by God?
  5. If the Torah was written down, why do Jews say Moses also received a Torah by word of mouth?
  6. Jews believe in one God who has no body. So how can anyone say what God is like?

Start this mini-lesson →

Part 2: In-depth study – Christianity (part 1 of 3)

  1. When Christians share bread and wine, what do they believe they are really receiving?
  2. Why do some churches share bread every day and others only once a month?
  3. Should a baby be baptised before it can choose, or should baptism wait until a person can say yes?
  4. Why do some Christians celebrate Christmas on 7 January, and Easter weeks after their neighbours?
  5. Christians call the Bible God's word, yet human hands wrote and chose every book. How can both be true?
  6. A book finished nearly 2,000 years ago: how do Christians get advice from it for life today?

Start this mini-lesson →

Part 2: In-depth study – Islam (part 1 of 3)

  1. Muslims pray at set times, in set words, facing one place. Is there room left for the heart?
  2. Why do pilgrims at Makkah dress so simply and alike, and what changed when pilgrims began to fly?
  3. What can an empty stomach teach that a full one cannot?
  4. If the Qur'an came in pieces over 23 years, how did it become one book?
  5. Why do Muslims who don't speak Arabic still learn to recite the Qur'an in Arabic?
