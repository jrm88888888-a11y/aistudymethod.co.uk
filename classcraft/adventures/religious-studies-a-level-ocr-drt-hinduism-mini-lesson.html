Sources of authority

Sources of authority

Hindu thought draws on a large body of sacred texts and living tradition:

  • The Vedas — the most ancient scriptures, regarded as shruti ('that which is heard', revealed).
  • The Upanishads — philosophical texts exploring ultimate reality and the self.
  • Smriti texts such as the Bhagavad Gita and the great epics, together with the guidance of gurus.

Ultimate reality

Brahman and atman

The central concept is Brahman — the ultimate reality, the ground of all being.

  • The atman is the true self or soul. In Advaita (non-dualist) Vedanta, taught by Shankara, atman is Brahman — expressed in the Upanishadic saying 'that thou art' (tat tvam asi).
  • Other schools (such as Ramanuja) understand the relationship more as loving devotion between the self and a personal God.
  • The many deities (such as Vishnu, Shiva and Devi) are understood as expressions of the one ultimate reality.

Self and afterlife

The self and rebirth

Hindu thought teaches that the atman passes through many lives:

  • Samsara — the cycle of birth, death and reincarnation.
  • Karma — actions have consequences that shape future lives.
  • Moksha — liberation, release from the cycle of rebirth and union with, or nearness to, the ultimate reality.

Moral principles

Moral principles

Hindu ethics centres on living rightly according to one place and stage in life:

  • Dharma — duty, righteousness and moral order, appropriate to one role and stage of life.
  • The four aims of life (purusharthas): dharma, artha (prosperity), kama (pleasure) and moksha (liberation).
  • Ahimsa — non-violence — is a key virtue. Different paths (yogas) lead toward liberation: action, devotion and knowledge.

Identity and challenges

Identity and modern challenges

Expression of identity: Hindu life is expressed through puja (worship), temples, pilgrimage and festivals such as Diwali and Holi, within a very diverse tradition.

Challenges to Hindu thought today:

  • Secularism and science, and reform movements within Hinduism.
  • Debates over caste (varna) and social justice.
  • Sustaining identity in the diaspora, and Western appropriation of practices such as yoga.

The big ideas to take into the exam

Sources of authority: the Vedas (shruti), the Upanishads, and smriti texts such as the Bhagavad Gita
