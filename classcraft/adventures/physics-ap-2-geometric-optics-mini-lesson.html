Mission 1Topics 13.1, 13.3

Mission 1: Why does light bend as it enters water, but not when it hits the surface head-on?

Make a guess: Why does light bend as it enters water, but not when it hits the surface head-on?

Quick check
?In geometric optics, light is represented by rays. Which of the following best describes a light ray?
Mission 1

Wavefronts and rays

  • Wavefront: a line (or surface) joining points that are in phase, e.g. every crest.
  • Neighboring wavefronts are one wavelength apart.
  • Ray: a line perpendicular to the wavefronts, showing the direction the wave's energy travels.
  • Point source: circular wavefronts, rays spreading out.
  • Distant source or laser: straight (plane) wavefronts, parallel rays.
Mission 1

Reflection

  • The normal: a line at 90° to the surface where the ray hits it.
  • Measure every angle from the normal, not from the surface.
  • Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
  • θi = θr.
  • Incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.
  • Reflection changes direction only: speed, frequency and wavelength stay the same.
Mission 1

Why refraction happens

  • At a boundary, part of a wave is reflected and part is transmitted.
  • The transmitted wave changes speed. Its frequency stays the same (set by the source), so its wavelength changes too: wavelength = speed ÷ frequency.
  • Hitting at an angle, one end of each wavefront reaches the boundary first and slows first. The wavefront turns, so the ray changes direction: refraction.
  • Into a slower medium: wavefronts closer together, ray bends toward the normal.
  • Into a faster medium: ray bends away from the normal.
  • Along the normal: the whole wavefront slows at once. Speed and wavelength change; the direction does not.
Mission 1

Refractive index

  • n = c/v: speed of light in a vacuum ÷ speed in the material.
  • No unit. Vacuum: n = 1 exactly. Air: n ≈ 1.00 (1.0003).
  • Water 1.33 · typical glass about 1.5 · diamond 2.42.
  • Larger n, slower light.
Quick check
?The index of refraction of a certain plastic is 1.5. Which statement correctly describes what this value means?
Mission 1

Snell's law

  • n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2, with both angles measured from the normal.
  • Into larger n: smaller angle (toward the normal). Into smaller n: larger angle (away).
  • θ1 = 0 gives θ2 = 0: no bending along the normal.
Mission 1

Worked example

  • Light of wavelength 589 nm in air enters water (n = 1.33) at 60.0° to the normal.
  • 1.00 × sin 60.0° = 1.33 × sin θ2 → sin θ2 = 0.651 → θ2 = 40.6°.
  • Speed in water = c/n = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.33 = 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s.
  • Same frequency, so the wavelength falls by the same factor: 589 ÷ 1.33 = 443 nm.
  • Check: 40.6° < 60.0°, so the ray bent toward the normal, as it should entering a slower medium.
Quick check
?A ray of light in air strikes the flat surface of a liquid at an angle of incidence of 50.0°, and the angle of refraction is 35.0°. What is the speed of light in the liquid? Use c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s and nair = 1.00.
Mission check

?A ray of light in air strikes a flat block of plastic P at an angle of incidence of 40°. The same ray then strikes a flat block of a different plastic, Q, at the same angle of incidence. The ray changes direction by a larger angle as it enters P than as it enters Q. Which conclusion can be drawn from this observation?

Refraction happens because the speed changes at the boundary. Head-on, the whole wavefront slows at once, so the direction does not change.

Mission 2Topics 13.1, 13.3

Mission 2: Why can everyone see a sheet of paper, but only one spot sees a laser's reflection in a mirror?

Make a guess: Why can everyone see a sheet of paper, but only one spot sees a laser's reflection in a mirror?

Quick check
?A narrow laser beam passes through a very narrow slit and then spreads out, making a band of light on a distant screen far wider than the slit. Which explanation of this observation is correct?
Mission 2

When rays work, and when they fail

  • Recall: a ray is ⟂ to the wavefronts.
  • In a uniform medium light travels in straight lines. That is why a small lamp casts a sharp shadow.
  • Geometric optics uses rays. It works when the wave nature of light can be neglected: objects and openings much wider than λ.
  • Rays fail when light passes through very narrow openings and spreads out. Interference and diffraction need the wave model (Unit 14).
  • A laser gives a single, coherent, single-color beam. Model it as one ray.
Mission 2

Drawing ray diagrams

  • A ray diagram shows the path of light before and after it meets matter.
  • Arrows run from the source, to the surface, to the eye. Never from the eye.
  • You see a book because light reflected from it enters your eye.
  • At each surface draw the normal and measure angles from it.
  • Mirror: θi = θr, so a narrow beam leaves in one direction only. Only an eye on that line receives it.
Quick check
?A laser beam strikes a smooth, flat mirror at point O, as shown. The small circles P, Q and R mark the positions of three observers' eyes. Which observer or observers receive light from the laser after it reflects?
Mission 2

Specular and diffuse reflection

  • Every ray obeys θi = θr, on every surface.
  • Specular reflection (smooth surface: mirror, still water): the normal points the same way everywhere. Parallel rays in, parallel rays out. You see a clear image.
  • Diffuse reflection (rough surface: paper, cloth, dry road): the normal changes direction from spot to spot. Parallel rays in, rays out in many directions.
  • Diffuse reflection is why the lit patch of paper can be seen from anywhere in the room.
Quick check
?Frosted glass is ordinary glass with one face roughened. Light still passes through it, but you cannot see a clear image through it. Which explanation is correct?
Mission 2

Reflected, transmitted, absorbed

  • When light meets a new medium, some is reflected, some is transmitted and some is absorbed.
  • Energy is conserved: the three parts add up to the incident light.
  • Even clear glass reflects a little: about 4% at each surface, for light hitting it head-on.
  • At night a window works as a dim mirror: the small reflection from inside is no longer swamped by light from outside.
  • The shares depend on the materials and on the angle of incidence.
Mission 2

Time to cross a medium

  • In a vacuum all light travels at c, whatever its wavelength. In a medium its speed is inversely proportional to the index of refraction: v = c/n.
  • Time to cross a slab of thickness x: t = x/v = nx/c.
  • Example: a glass window 3.0 mm thick (n = 1.50): t = 1.50 × 3.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ (3.00 × 10⁸) = 1.5 × 10⁻¹¹ s.
Quick check
?A short pulse of light crosses a glass plate (n = 1.50) along the normal to its faces in a time t. The pulse then crosses a diamond plate (n = 2.42) that is twice as thick as the glass plate, also along the normal. How long does the pulse take to cross the diamond plate?
Mission check

?At night, a dry road lit by a car's headlights looks evenly gray from every direction. After rain, a smooth film of water covers the road. Now the road looks dark from most directions, with a bright glare from one direction only. Which explanation is correct?

The law of reflection always holds. On rough paper the normal changes from spot to spot, so light leaves in all directions; on a mirror it leaves in one.

Mission 3Topic 13.2

Mission 3: Why can a dish-shaped mirror focus sunlight to a burning spot when a flat mirror never can?

Make a guess: Why can a dish-shaped mirror focus sunlight to a burning spot when a flat mirror never can?

Mission 3

Parts of a curved mirror

  • A spherical mirror is a slice of a sphere of radius R.
  • Center of curvature C: the center of that sphere.
  • Principal axis: the line through C and the center of the mirror.
  • Concave: reflecting surface curves inward (like the inside of a spoon).
  • Convex: reflecting surface bulges outward (like the back of a spoon).
  • Every ray obeys the law of reflection, measured from the normal. On a spherical mirror, the normal at any point passes through C.
Mission 3

Concave mirror: the focal point

  • Rays parallel to the principal axis reflect and meet at one point: the focal point F.
  • F lies on the axis, halfway between the mirror and C: f ≈ R/2.
  • "≈": exact only for rays close to the axis. Rays far from the axis cross slightly nearer the mirror.
  • Sunlight arrives as parallel rays, so a concave mirror pointed at the Sun concentrates it at F.
  • A concave mirror is a converging mirror.
Quick check
?The diagram shows three rays of light traveling parallel to the principal axis toward a concave mirror whose center of curvature is C. Which statement describes the rays after they reflect from the mirror?
Quick check
?A convex mirror bulges toward the light. Predict before the next screen. A beam of light rays traveling parallel to the principal axis of a convex mirror strikes the mirror. Which statement describes the reflected rays?
Mission 3

Convex mirror: a focal point behind

  • Parallel rays reflect and spread out.
  • Extend the reflected rays backward: they all appear to come from one point behind the mirror. That point is F.
  • Again |f| ≈ R/2.
  • No light actually reaches F: it is behind the reflecting surface.
  • A convex mirror is a diverging mirror. Wide view: shop and road-safety mirrors.
Mission 3

Plane mirror: focal point at infinity

  • A flat mirror is part of a sphere of infinite radius.
  • Parallel rays reflect and stay parallel: they never meet, in front or behind.
  • So the focal point is infinitely far away (f → ∞), not on the surface.
  • Image of an object: as far behind the mirror as the object is in front, same size, upright.
  • This is why a flat mirror can never concentrate sunlight to a spot.
Mission 3

Real and virtual images

  • Rays from one point of an object reflect from the mirror.
  • Real image: reflected rays actually meet at a point. Light passes through it, so a screen placed there shows it.
  • Virtual image: reflected rays diverge but appear to come from a point. No light is there; a screen shows nothing.
  • Both can be seen by an eye in the reflected light.
  • Plane and convex mirrors give virtual images. A concave mirror can give either, depending on where the object is.
Quick check
?The diagram shows two rays from the top of an object O that reflect from a convex mirror, and the backward extensions of the reflected rays. Which claim about the image of O at I, with its reasoning, is correct?
Quick check
?A student walks toward a tall plane mirror, from 3.0 m away to 1.0 m away. How does the height of the student's image at 1.0 m compare with its height at 3.0 m?
Mission check

?A flashlight has a concave spherical mirror, with a radius of curvature of 6.0 cm, behind a small bulb on its principal axis. Where should the bulb be placed so that the light leaves the mirror as a beam of rays parallel to the axis?

Parallel rays meet at F only if the mirror curves inward. A flat mirror's focal point is infinitely far away, so its reflected sunlight stays as spread out as it arrived.

Mission 4: A make-up mirror shows your face upright and enlarged, but a far wall upside down. What decides which?

Make a guess: A make-up mirror shows your face upright and enlarged, but a far wall upside down. What decides which?

Quick check
?Rays are reversible: a ray can retrace its path. Use that to predict before the next screen. The diagram shows one ray from the top of an object traveling toward a concave mirror whose focal point is F and center of curvature is C. Which describes the path of this ray after it reflects from the mirror?
Mission 4

The three principal rays

From the top of the object, draw any two of:

  • Ray 1: parallel to the principal axis → reflects through F (concave) or as if from F (convex).
  • Ray 2: hits the center of the mirror, where the axis meets it → reflects at an equal angle on the other side of the axis.
  • Ray 3: through F (concave), or heading toward F (convex) → reflects parallel to the axis.
  • Where the reflected rays meet (or appear to come from) is the top of the image.
  • A third ray is a check: all three meet at one point.
Mission 4

Concave mirror: it depends where the object is

ObjectImage
Beyond CReal, inverted, reduced, between F and C
At CReal, inverted, same size, at C
Between C and FReal, inverted, enlarged, beyond C
At FReflected rays parallel: no image forms
Between F and mirrorVirtual, upright, enlarged, behind the mirror
  • The switch happens at F: outside F, real and inverted; inside F, virtual and upright.
Quick check
?A convex mirror in a store shows images of customers standing at various distances in front of it. How does the height of each customer's image compare with the customer's height?
Mission 4

Convex mirror: always the same kind

  • Reflected rays always diverge: the image is always virtual.
  • Always upright and reduced, behind the mirror, between the mirror and F.
  • The farther the object, the smaller the image and the closer it is to F.
Mission 4

The mirror equation

  • 1/si + 1/so = 1/f
  • Sign convention:
    • so: positive (object in front of the mirror).
    • si: positive in front (real); negative behind (virtual).
    • f: positive for concave; negative for convex.
  • Magnification: |M| = |hi/ho| = |si/so|. |M| > 1 enlarged; |M| < 1 reduced.
  • One mirror: si positive → real and inverted; si negative → virtual and upright.
  • Plane mirror: 1/f = 0, so si = −so. Same distance behind, |M| = 1.
Mission 4

Worked example

Concave mirror, f = +10.0 cm. Object 3.0 cm tall.

  • Object at 30.0 cm: 1/si = 1/10.0 − 1/30.0 = 2/30.0 → si = +15.0 cm.
    • |M| = 15.0/30.0 = 0.50 → hi = 1.5 cm. Real, inverted, reduced.
  • Object at 5.0 cm: 1/si = 1/10.0 − 1/5.0 = −1/10.0 → si = −10.0 cm.
    • |M| = 10.0/5.0 = 2.0 → hi = 6.0 cm. Virtual (behind), upright, enlarged.
  • Check each answer against the concave-mirror table earlier in this mission.
Quick check
?An object is placed 30.0 cm in front of a convex mirror whose focal length has a magnitude of 20.0 cm. Use the convention that so is positive, si is positive for an image in front of the mirror and negative for an image behind it, and f is positive for a concave mirror and negative for a convex mirror. What is the image distance si?
Quick check
?An object 2.0 cm tall is placed 15.0 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 10.0 cm. What is the height of the image?
Mission check

?A small object is placed on the principal axis of a concave mirror whose radius of curvature is R. The mirror forms an upright image five times as tall as the object. Use the convention that so is positive, si is positive for an image in front of the mirror and negative for an image behind it, and f is positive for a concave mirror and negative for a convex mirror. Which expression gives the distance of the object from the mirror?

Upright and enlarged means virtual, so the object is inside F (0.40R < R/2). A make-up mirror is concave and your face is inside F; the far wall is beyond C, so its image is real, inverted and reduced.

Mission 5Topic 13.3

Mission 5: How can light stay trapped inside a thin glass strand, even when the strand bends?

Make a guess: How can light stay trapped inside a thin glass strand, even when the strand bends?

Mission 5

Heading into a lower n

  • Light going from glass into air (larger n to smaller n) bends away from the normal.
  • Increase the angle of incidence: the refracted ray swings toward the surface.
  • At the critical angle, the refracted ray runs along the boundary (angle of refraction 90°).
  • Beyond the critical angle no light leaves: all of it reflects back. This is total internal reflection (TIR).
  • Below the critical angle there is also a partial reflection, growing stronger as the angle nears the critical angle.
  • TIR obeys the law of reflection.
Mission 5

The critical angle

  • Put θ2 = 90° (sin 90° = 1) into Snell's law.
  • θc = sin⁻¹(n2/n1).
  • Glass (1.50) to air: 41.8°. Water (1.33) to air: 48.8°.
  • TIR needs both: light heading into a medium of lower n, and angle of incidence greater than the critical angle.
Quick check
?Light can cross a flat boundary between water (n = 1.33) and glass (n = 1.50) in either direction. Which statement about total internal reflection at this boundary is correct?
Mission 5

Worked example: will it reflect?

  • A ray in water (n = 1.33) meets the water surface. Critical angle: sin⁻¹(1/1.33) = 48.8°.
  • At 50.0° to the normal: 50.0° > 48.8°, so TIR. No light leaves.
  • At 45.0°: 45.0° < 48.8°, so it refracts out. 1.33 × sin 45.0° = 1.00 × sin θ2 → sin θ2 = 0.940 → θ2 = 70.1°.
  • Always compare the angle from the normal with the critical angle.
  • An optical fiber uses this: light meets the wall at more than the critical angle at every bounce, even around gentle bends.
Quick check
?For light inside a block of glass striking a glass–air boundary, the critical angle is 41.8°. The block is then placed in water (n = 1.33). What is the critical angle for light inside the glass striking the glass–water boundary? Take nair = 1.00.
Mission check

?The diagram shows a ray of light entering a glass prism (n = 1.50) along the normal to one face. The prism is in air (n = 1.00). The dashed line is the normal to the longest face at the point where the ray reaches it. What happens to the light at the longest face?

In a fiber, light heads toward lower n and meets the wall at more than the critical angle, so it is totally internally reflected at every bounce, around gentle bends too.

Mission 6Topic 13.4

Mission 6: A magnifying glass can show a scene upside down or upright. What decides which?

Make a guess: A magnifying glass can show a scene upside down or upright. What decides which?

Quick check
?This lens is thinner in the middle than at its edges. Predict before the next screen. The diagram shows a thin lens, its two focal points F, and two rays of a beam of light traveling parallel to the principal axis toward the lens from the left. Which statement describes the light after it passes through the lens?
Mission 6

Two kinds of lens

  • Convex (converging) lens: rays parallel to the principal axis meet at the focal point F.
  • Concave (diverging) lens: parallel rays spread out as if from F on the incident side.
  • Focal length f: lens center to F.
  • A thin lens has a focal point on each side, the same distance from the lens.
Mission 6

Ray diagrams

  • Draw two (or three) rays from the top of the object:
  • 1. Parallel to the axis → refracted through F (converging) or as if from F (diverging).
  • 2. Through the center of the lens → undeviated.
  • 3. Through F (or heading for F) → leaves parallel to the axis.
  • Rays actually meet: a real image. It can be caught on a screen.
  • Rays only appear to come from a point: a virtual image. No screen can show it.
Mission 6

Where the object is decides

  • Converging lens, object beyond 2F: real, inverted, smaller.
  • At 2F: real, inverted, same size (image at 2F).
  • Between F and 2F: real, inverted, larger.
  • At F: rays leave parallel: no image forms.
  • Inside F: virtual, upright, larger. A magnifying glass.
  • Diverging lens, anywhere: virtual, upright, smaller.
Quick check
?The ray diagram shows an object O near a thin converging lens with focal points F, two rays from the tip of O, and the image I that an observer on the right sees by looking through the lens. Which claim about the image I is supported by the diagram?
Mission 6

The lens equation

  • 1/so + 1/si = 1/f: so = object distance, si = image distance.
  • si + on the far side (real), − on the object's side (virtual). f + converging, − diverging.
  • |M| = |hi/ho| = |si/so|.
  • A negative image distance means a virtual image, on the same side as the object.
Mission 6

Worked example

  • Converging lens, f = 10.0 cm.
  • Object at so = 14.0 cm: 1/si = 1/10.0 − 1/14.0 → si = +35.0 cm. Real, inverted. |M| = 35.0/14.0 = 2.5.
  • Move it to so = 6.0 cm (inside F): 1/si = 1/10.0 − 1/6.0 → si = −15.0 cm. Virtual, upright. |M| = 15.0/6.0 = 2.5.
  • Same lens, both images 2.5 times larger, but different object distances: the image flips from inverted to upright.
Quick check
?An object is placed 24.0 cm from a thin diverging lens whose focal length has a magnitude of 12.0 cm. Use the convention that so is positive, si is positive for an image on the side of the lens opposite the object and negative for an image on the same side as the object, and f is positive for a converging lens and negative for a diverging lens. What is the image distance si?
Quick check
?An object 3.0 cm tall is placed 30.0 cm from a thin converging lens of focal length 20.0 cm. What is the height of the image?
Mission check

?A projector has a thin converging lens of focal length 8.0 cm. A slide is placed 9.0 cm from the lens, close to it. Which describes the image?

Object outside F → rays really meet → real, inverted image. Object inside F → rays only appear to meet → virtual, upright image.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Rays are perpendicular to wavefronts. Reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection, measured from the normal.
  • n = c/v. Refraction is caused by a change of speed; frequency is unchanged, wavelength changes.
  • n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2: toward the normal into larger n.

Mission 2

  • Rays work when openings are much wider than λ; spreading through narrow openings needs the wave model. A laser beam can be modeled as one ray.
  • Specular (smooth) and diffuse (rough) reflection both obey θi = θr; diffuse reflection happens because the normal varies.
  • At a boundary light is partly reflected, transmitted and absorbed. In a medium v = c/n; frequency unchanged, λ = λvacuum/n.

Mission 3

  • Parallel rays: concave mirror, they meet at F in front (f ≈ R/2); convex mirror, they appear to come from F behind; plane mirror, they stay parallel (F at infinity).
  • Real image: reflected rays really meet. Virtual image: they only appear to come from a point. Plane mirror image: same distance behind, same size.

Mission 4

  • Principal rays: parallel → through F; through F → parallel; at the center → equal angle back. Concave: object outside F gives real, inverted; inside F gives virtual, upright, enlarged. Convex: always virtual, upright, reduced.
  • 1/si + 1/so = 1/f (f + concave, − convex; si − means virtual, behind); |M| = |hi/ho| = |si/so|.

Mission 5

  • TIR: light heading into lower n at more than the critical angle is all reflected.
  • θc = sin⁻¹(n2/n1).

Mission 6

  • Converging lens: object outside F → real, inverted image; inside F → virtual, upright, magnified. Diverging lens → always virtual, upright, smaller.
  • 1/so + 1/si = 1/f; negative si → virtual image; |M| = |si/so|.
