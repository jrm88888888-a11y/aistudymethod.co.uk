HL statistics · estimators and confidence intervals

Transforming and combining random variables

  • One variable: E(aX+b)=aE(X)+bE(aX+b) = aE(X)+b and Var(aX+b)=a2Var(X)Var(aX+b) = a²Var(X). Adding bb shifts; it never changes the spread.
  • Any XX and YY: E(aX±bY)=aE(X)±bE(Y)E(aX ± bY) = aE(X) ± bE(Y).
  • Independent XX and YY: Var(aX±bY)=a2Var(X)+b2Var(Y)Var(aX ± bY) = a²Var(X) + b²Var(Y). Variances always add, even for a difference.
  • Two separate observations are not the same as doubling one: Var(X1​+X2​)=2Var(X)Var(X₁ + X₂) = 2Var(X) but Var(2X)=4Var(X)Var(2X) = 4Var(X).
  • A linear combination of independent normal variables is normal.

Packing a box of flour

Question
Bags of flour have mass X∼N(1010,62)X ~ N(1010, 6²) grams. An empty box has mass Y∼N(250,42)Y ~ N(250, 4²) grams. A full box holds 5 bags, all masses independent. Find the probability that a full box weighs more than 5,320 g.
  1. 1
    Write the total as separate bags: T=X1​+X2​+X3​+X4​+X5​+YT = X₁ + X₂ + X₃ + X₄ + X₅ + Y
  2. 2
    Expected value: E(T)=5×1010+250=5300E(T) = 5 × 1010 + 250 = 5300
  3. 3
    Variance, adding each part: Var(T)=5×36+16=196Var(T) = 5 × 36 + 16 = 196, so the standard deviation is 14
  4. 4
    TT is normal, so use your GDC: P(T>5320)P(T > 5320) with T∼N(5300,142)T ~ N(5300, 14²) gives 0.0766
Answer
0.0766 (3 s.f.)
⚠️ Watch out
writing 5X5X gives Var(T)=25×36+16=916Var(T) = 25 × 36 + 16 = 916 and a probability of 0.254. Five different bags are X1​+⋯+X5​X₁ + ⋯ + X₅, not one bag counted five times.

HL statistics · estimators and confidence intervals

Estimators and the central limit theorem

  • xˉ=n∑xi​​x̄ = (Σ xᵢ)/n is an unbiased estimate of the population mean μμ.
  • sn−12​=n−1n​sn2​sₙ₋₁² = n/(n−1)sₙ² is an unbiased estimate of σ2σ². Dividing by nn tends to underestimate it.
  • If X∼N(μ,σ2)X ~ N(μ, σ²), then Xˉ∼N(μ,nσ2​)X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n) for any nn.
  • Central limit theorem: whatever the distribution of XX, XˉX̄ is approximately N(μ,nσ2​)N(μ, σ²/n) for large nn. In IB exams, n>30n > 30 counts as large.

HL statistics · estimators and confidence intervals

Confidence intervals for a mean

  • A confidence interval is estimate ± margin: xˉ±z∗n​σ​x̄ ± z^(*)σ/(√n) when σσ is known.
  • When σσ is unknown, use sn−1​sₙ₋₁ and the tt-distribution with n−1n−1 degrees of freedom, whatever the sample size. Your GDC does both.
  • Meaning: if you took many samples and built a 95% interval from each, about 95% of the intervals would contain μμ. The 95% belongs to the method, not to one interval.
  • Higher confidence or a smaller sample gives a wider interval.

Question

HL statistics · Poisson and hypothesis tests

The Poisson model

  • X∼Po(m)X ~ Po(m) counts events in a fixed interval: P(X=x)=x!mxe−m​P(X = x) = (m^x e^(−m))/(x!), for x=0,1,2,…x = 0, 1, 2, …
  • Mean = variance = mm.
  • Use it when events are independent and occur at a uniform average rate. Scale mm with the interval: 3 a week is 6 a fortnight.
  • If X∼Po(m1​)X ~ Po(m₁) and Y∼Po(m2​)Y ~ Po(m₂) are independent, then X+Y∼Po(m1​+m2​)X + Y ~ Po(m₁ + m₂).
Worked example HL statistics · Poisson and hypothesis tests

Two help desks

Question
Calls reach help desk A at an average rate of 2.5 per hour and desk B at 1.5 per hour, independently and at random. Find the probability that the two desks receive at most 2 calls in total in one hour.
  1. 1
    Add the independent Poisson means: T∼Po(2.5+1.5)=Po(4)T ~ Po(2.5 + 1.5) = Po(4)
  2. 2
    Write the event: P(T≤2)=P(T=0)+P(T=1)+P(T=2)P(T ≤ 2) = P(T = 0) + P(T = 1) + P(T = 2)
  3. 3
    Substitute: e−4(1+4+242​)=13e−4e⁻⁴(1 + 4 + 4²/2) = 13e⁻⁴
  4. 4
    Evaluate, or use the Poisson cdf on your GDC with m=4m = 4, upper bound 2
Answer
13e−4≈0.23813e⁻⁴ ≈ 0.238 (3 s.f.)
⚠️ Watch out
"at most 2" includes 2. Using P(T<2)P(T < 2) leaves out P(T=2)P(T = 2) and gives 0.0916.

HL statistics · Poisson and hypothesis tests

Testing a parameter; critical regions and errors

  • Binomial and Poisson tests are one-tailed, e.g. H0​:m=3H₀: m = 3, H1​:m>3H₁: m > 3.
  • pp-value: probability, under H0​H₀, of a result at least as extreme as the one seen. Reject H0​H₀ if p<p < the significance level.
  • Critical region: the values that lead to rejecting H0​H₀. For a discrete variable, make P(X∈CR)P(X ∈ CR) as large as possible while staying below the significance level.
  • Type I error: rejecting a true H0​H₀; its probability is P(X∈CR∣H0​)P(X ∈ CR | H₀). Type II error: accepting a false H0​H₀.

A binomial test for a proportion

Question
A seed company claims 80% of its seeds germinate. A gardener suspects the rate is lower. She plants 20 seeds and 12 germinate. Test the claim at the 5% significance level.
  1. 1
    Hypotheses: H0​:p=0.8H₀: p = 0.8, H1​:p<0.8H₁: p < 0.8, where pp is the proportion that germinate
  2. 2
    Under H0​H₀, X∼B(20,0.8)X ~ B(20, 0.8)
  3. 3
    Use your GDC: pp-value =P(X≤12)≈0.0321= P(X ≤ 12) ≈ 0.0321
  4. 4
    Compare: 0.0321<0.050.0321 < 0.05, so reject H0​H₀
Answer
there is significant evidence at the 5% level that fewer than 80% of the seeds germinate.
⚠️ Watch out
the pp-value is P(X≤12)P(X ≤ 12), not P(X=12)≈0.0222P(X = 12) ≈ 0.0222. "At least as extreme" means 12 or fewer here.

HL statistics · Poisson and hypothesis tests

Tests for a mean and for correlation

  • σσ known: zz-test on xˉx̄, with Xˉ∼N(μ0​,nσ2​)X̄ ~ N(μ₀, σ²/n) under H0​H₀. Critical values and error probabilities can be found.
  • σσ unknown: tt-test using sn−1​sₙ₋₁, whatever the sample size. Use the pp-value from your GDC.
  • Paired samples (before and after, on the same people): find each difference dd, then do a one-sample test on the differences, H0​:μd​=0H₀: μ_d = 0.
  • Correlation: for bivariate normal data, your GDC tests H0​:ρ=0H₀: ρ = 0 and gives a pp-value.
Calculate
?Bags of rice have masses that are normal with known standard deviation 4 g. The label says the mean is 500 g. An inspector weighs a random sample of 16 bags and tests H0​:μ=500H₀: μ = 500 against H1​:μ<500H₁: μ < 500 at the 5% significance level. The true mean is in fact 497 g. Find the probability of a Type II error. Do not round the critical value, and give your answer to 3 significant figures.
Mission check

?Usually 3 complaints a week. This week, 8. Bad luck or a real problem?

Model the complaints as X∼Po(3)X ~ Po(3) and test H0​:m=3H₀: m = 3 against H1​:m>3H₁: m > 3. The pp-value is P(X≥8)≈0.0119P(X ≥ 8) ≈ 0.0119, below 5%, so 8 complaints is significant evidence that the rate has risen. There is still a small chance (a Type I error) that it was bad luck.

HL statistics · data collection and non-linear regression

Designing valid data collection

  • Questions must be unbiased (not leading), precise and, where possible, structured with consistent answer choices. Open, unstructured questions are hard to analyse.
  • Avoid needlessly personal questions: people skip them or answer untruthfully.
  • Choose only the relevant variables and data for your question.
  • For a χ2chi² test, choose categories so every expected frequency is greater than 5, merging neighbouring classes if needed.
  • In a goodness of fit test, each parameter estimated from the data removes one more degree of freedom.

HL statistics · data collection and non-linear regression

Reliability and validity

  • Reliable: gives consistent results when repeated. Valid: measures what it claims to measure. A test can be reliable but not valid.
  • Test-retest (reliability): the same people take the same test again later; the scores should correlate strongly.
  • Parallel forms (reliability): the same people take two equivalent versions; the scores should correlate strongly.
  • Content validity: the test covers the full range of what it should measure.
  • Criterion-related validity: results agree with an established measure (the criterion).

HL statistics · data collection and non-linear regression

Residuals, SSres​SS_res and R2R²

  • Your GDC fits least squares models: linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, power and sine.
  • A residual is y−y^​y − ŷ: observed minus predicted. The sum of square residuals SSres​=∑(y−y^​)2SS_res = Σ (y − ŷ)². Smaller means a closer fit.
  • R2R² is the proportion of the variability in yy explained by the model. For a linear model, R2=r2R² = r².
  • R2R² alone can't choose a model: extra terms never lower it. Check the model makes sense in context.

Recap

Big ideas

  • E(aX±bY)=aE(X)±bE(Y)E(aX ± bY) = aE(X) ± bE(Y); for independent variables Var(aX±bY)=a2Var(X)+b2Var(Y)Var(aX ± bY) = a²Var(X) + b²Var(Y).
  • xˉx̄ and sn−12​sₙ₋₁² are unbiased estimates; XˉX̄ has standard deviation n​σ​σ/(√n) and is approximately normal for n>30n > 30.
  • Confidence interval for μμ: normal if σσ is known, tt with n−1n − 1 degrees of freedom if not. 95% describes the method.
  • X∼Po(m)X ~ Po(m): independent events at a uniform average rate; mean = variance = mm; independent Poisson variables add.
  • Binomial and Poisson tests are one-tailed. The critical region is as large as possible with P(Type I error)P(Type I error) below the significance level.
  • σσ known: zz-test; σσ unknown: tt-test; paired data: one-sample test on the differences.
  • Good questions are unbiased, precise and structured; a reliable test is consistent (test-retest, parallel forms); a valid test measures the right thing (content, criterion-related).
  • χ2chi² goodness of fit: merge classes so expected frequencies exceed 5; df = categories − 1 − parameters estimated.
  • SSres​=∑(y−y^​)2SS_res = Σ (y − ŷ)² measures fit; R2R² is the proportion of variability explained; neither alone decides between models.
