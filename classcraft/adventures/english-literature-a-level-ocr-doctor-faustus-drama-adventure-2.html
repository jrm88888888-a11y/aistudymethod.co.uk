Variants

Three Major Differences

First: the B-text adds 676 lines, mostly comic scenes at the papal court and the imperial court. Second: the B-text gives Faustus's final monologue minor verbal changes ('Ugly hell, gape not!' becomes 'Ugly hell, gape not, come not, Lucifer!'). Third: only the B-text contains the discovery scene where the scholars find Faustus's dismembered body. Editorial choice shapes interpretation: A presents a more austere, tragic Faustus; B emphasises grotesque physical damnation.

Documents

Henslowe's Payment

Philip Henslowe's diary records, on 22 November 1602, payment of £4 to William Birde and Samuel Rowley 'for ther adicyones in docter fostes'. This is concrete documentary evidence that the B-text's additions postdate Marlowe (d. 1593) by nearly a decade. Editors disagree about how much of the additional material is theirs. Knowing this provenance lets AO3 responses discuss authorship and collaboration with precision.

Editorial

Which Faustus on the Examiner's Desk?

specifies which edition is set; most contemporary teaching uses the A-text via the Bevington & Rasmussen edition or the New Mermaids/Norton. Critics like Leah Marcus argue the two texts represent different theological dramas: the A-text is more sceptical, the B-text more orthodox. AO5 responses gain credit for noting which text they are reading and what interpretive consequences follow.
