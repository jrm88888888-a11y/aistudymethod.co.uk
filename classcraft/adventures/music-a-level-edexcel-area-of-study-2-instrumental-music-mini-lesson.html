Genres

Instrumental genres

Instrumental music is written for players without voices, in genres that developed from the Baroque onward:

  • Solo and trio sonata; concerto (soloist versus orchestra).
  • Symphony, suite and the Romantic character piece.
  • Each exploits idiomatic writing suited to its instruments.

Sonata form

Sonata form

Sonata form is the central instrumental structure:

  • Exposition — first subject in the tonic, transition modulating to a related key, second subject there.
  • Development — themes fragmented and modulated; harmonically unstable.
  • Recapitulation — both subjects return, now in the tonic; often a coda to close.

Other forms

Other instrumental forms

Beyond sonata form, instrumental movements use:

  • Ternary (ABA) — often the slow movement or minuet and trio.
  • Rondo (ABACA) — a lively recurring refrain, common as a finale.
  • Theme and variations — a theme restated with changing decoration, harmony or texture.

Style & idiom

Style across periods

Instrumental style evolves and exploits each instrument:

  • Baroque ritornello concertos and continuo; Classical clarity and sonata form.
  • Romantic virtuosity, expanded range, expressive rubato and rich harmony.
  • Idiomatic writing — double stops on strings, arpeggiation on keyboard, brilliant runs — suits the instrument.

Texture

Texture and harmony

Instrumental textures and harmony:

  • Baroque counterpoint and continuo; Classical homophony with Alberti bass.
  • Functional harmony, cadences and modulation to related keys throughout.
  • Romantic chromaticism and thicker, divided textures.

Appraising

Appraising instrumental music

To appraise instrumental music:

  • Identify the genre and form (sonata, rondo, variations, concerto).
  • Track tonality — keys and modulations across sections.
  • Comment on texture, idiomatic writing and period style.
