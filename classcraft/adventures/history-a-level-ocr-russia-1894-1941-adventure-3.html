Industrialisation

1928-37

The First Five-Year Plan (1928-32) targeted heavy industry; steel output rose from 4 to 17 million tonnes by 1937. Collectivisation forced peasants into kolkhozes; dekulakisation displaced ~5 million. The Holodomor (1932-33) devastated Ukraine. Magnitogorsk and Dneprostroi exemplified industrial gigantism. The Stakhanovite movement (1935) drove productivity.

Terror

1934-38

Kirov's murder (1 December 1934) initiated the spiral. The Moscow Show Trials (1936-38) destroyed Zinoviev, Kamenev, Bukharin and others. Order 00447 (July 1937) targeted 'former kulaks, criminals and other anti-Soviet elements'. The Red Army purge (1937-38) killed three of five marshals. Gulag population rose to about 1.8 million by 1938.

Pact and Barbarossa

1939-41

The Nazi-Soviet Pact (August 1939) divided Poland (September 1939); Soviet annexations followed of the Baltic states, Bessarabia and parts of Romania (1940). The Winter War with Finland (November 1939-March 1940) exposed Red Army weakness. On 22 June 1941 Operation Barbarossa began; Kiev, Minsk and Smolensk fell, the Wehrmacht reached Moscow's outskirts before being halted in December 1941.
