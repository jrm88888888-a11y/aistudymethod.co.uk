Free Will and Moral Responsibility

This mini-lesson covers free will and moral responsibility — the clash between determinism and freedom.

The key question throughout: can we be morally responsible if our choices are caused?

Ethics · the problem

Are we free?

Moral responsibility seems to require free will — that we could have acted otherwise. But is the will free, or are our choices determined? Three positions:

  • Hard determinism — all is determined; there is no free will and no real responsibility.
  • Libertarianism — we have genuine free will; determinism is false of human choices.
  • Compatibilism (soft determinism) — freedom and determinism are compatible.
Ethics · determinism

Hard determinism

Hard determinism holds that every event, including every human choice, is fully caused by prior conditions, so no one could ever have acted otherwise. Its forms:

  • Philosophical/physical — universal causation (Laplace's "demon" could predict everything from present states).
  • Psychological — e.g. Freud: behaviour is driven by unconscious forces.
  • Biological/genetic — genes and brain chemistry shape action.

John Locke's locked room: a man happily stays in a room, not knowing it is locked; he feels free but is not — suggesting our sense of freedom can be an illusion. Consequence: if hard determinism is true, praise, blame and punishment (as desert) seem unjustified.

Ethics · libertarianism

Libertarianism

Libertarianism insists some choices are genuinely free — not merely uncaused randomness, but caused by the agent (self-determination).

  • C. A. Campbell distinguishes the empirical self (personality, shaped by heredity and environment) from the moral self, which in moments of moral effort can resist the strongest desire and choose duty.
  • Our strong sense of deliberation and responsibility is taken as evidence of real freedom.

Challenge: if a choice is not determined by prior causes, is it not just random — and how is randomness any more "responsible" than determinism?

Ethics · compatibilism

Compatibilism (soft determinism)

Compatibilism says determinism and freedom are compatible, because "free" does not mean "uncaused" but "unconstrained".

  • Hume: freedom is acting according to one's own desires without external compulsion; a determined action can still be voluntary.
  • A. J. Ayer: the opposite of "free" is not "caused" but "constrained/compelled". I am free when I could have done otherwise had I chosen to, and no one is forcing me.

So we can keep moral responsibility even in a causal universe.

Ethics · religion

Predestination and free will

Religion adds the problem of divine foreknowledge and predestination:

  • If God is omniscient and knows the future, can our choices be free?
  • Augustine and especially Calvin emphasised predestination — God elects who is saved — which appears to limit human freedom.
  • Others argue God's foreknowledge does not cause our choices, so freedom survives; or that God stands outside time.

Ethics · evaluation

Weighing the positions

  • Does neuroscience (e.g. brain-state studies) support determinism, or only show influences on choice?
  • Is libertarian freedom coherent, or does it collapse into randomness?
  • Does compatibilism save responsibility, or just redefine freedom to dodge the problem?
  • How much responsibility is fair when heredity and upbringing clearly shape us (cf. Darrow's defence pleas)?
