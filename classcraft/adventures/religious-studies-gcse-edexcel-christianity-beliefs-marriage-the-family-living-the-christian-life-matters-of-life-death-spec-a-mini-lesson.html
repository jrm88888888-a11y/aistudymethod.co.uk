The big question

What Christians believe about God

Christianity is monotheistic: there is one God. Christians describe God's nature with several key ideas that run right through the whole topic:

  • God is omnipotent — all-powerful, the maker of everything.
  • God is loving (benevolent) — God cares for people and wants what is good.
  • God is just — God is fair and will judge people rightly.

Spec focus: Edexcel Spec A asks you to describe and explain Christian beliefs — including the Trinity, creation, the person of Jesus (incarnation, crucifixion, resurrection) and sin, salvation and the afterlife — and to apply them to family life and ethics.

Key vocabulary

Words you must be able to define

  • Trinity — the one God as three persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
  • Incarnation — God becoming human in the person of Jesus.
  • Atonement — Jesus' death restoring the relationship between humans and God.
  • Salvation — being saved from sin and its consequences, through Jesus.
  • Sanctity of life — the belief that all life is holy and God-given.

Common mix-up: the Trinity is one God in three persons — not three gods. And don't confuse resurrection (Jesus rising from death) with the ascension (Jesus returning to heaven).

Beliefs & teachings

One God in three persons

Christianity is monotheistic: there is one God. Christians teach that this one God is a Trinity — Father, Son (Jesus Christ) and Holy Spirit — as set out in the Nicene Creed. Each person is fully God, yet there is only one God.

Scripture: "In the beginning God created the heavens and the earth." (Genesis 1:1) — God as Creator, who made the world in six days and rested on the seventh. Many Christians read Genesis 1 as showing that creation is ordered, good and God-given.
Watch out

The Trinity is not three gods

A very common exam error is to say the Trinity means Christians believe in three gods. This is wrong. The Trinity is the teaching that the one God exists as three persons — Father, Son and Holy Spirit — who are distinct but share one divine nature.

Say it like this: "Christianity is monotheistic. The Trinity means one God in three persons — not three separate gods." That single sentence protects you from the most common mistake in this topic.

Beliefs & teachings

Jesus: incarnation to ascension

At the heart of Christian belief is Jesus Christ. Christians teach the incarnation — God became human in Jesus — followed by his crucifixion, resurrection and ascension. These events are the basis of salvation.

Scripture: "The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us." (John 1:14) — the incarnation.

Beliefs & teachings

Sin, salvation and atonement

Christians teach that humans have turned away from God through sin. Through Jesus' death and resurrection, God offers salvation — being saved from sin. This is possible through atonement: Jesus' death restoring the broken relationship between humans and God, and through God's free gift of grace.

Scripture: "For the wages of sin is death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 6:23) — sin, grace and salvation.

Because salvation is a gift of grace (undeserved love), many Christians respond with faith, repentance and living a good life in gratitude — not to "earn" heaven, but out of thanks.

Beliefs & teachings

Afterlife, judgement, heaven & hell

Christians believe in life after death. Because God is just, there will be judgement: people will answer to God for how they lived. Christian teaching speaks of:

  • Heaven — being with God for ever, the reward of the saved.
  • Hell — separation from God (understood by some as a place, by others as a state).

Many Christians point to Jesus' resurrection as the guarantee of eternal life. Interpretations vary: some read heaven and hell literally, others more symbolically.

Careful: not all Christians picture hell the same way — the exam rewards you for showing this range of views, not just one.

Marriage & the family

Marriage, sex and the family

Most Christians teach that marriage is a lifelong, exclusive union — ideally the right setting for sexual relationships and for raising a family. Many Christians therefore see cohabitation and sex before marriage as falling short of this ideal, though views differ.

  • Family is highly valued: parents nurture children and teach the faith.
  • Gender roles: some Christians hold traditional/complementary roles; many others teach the full equality of men and women.

Divorce: the Catholic Church teaches marriage is a sacrament that cannot be dissolved, so it does not permit remarriage in church after divorce. Many Protestants accept that divorce and remarriage can sometimes be the compassionate choice.

Marriage & the family

Contraception & family planning

Christians disagree about contraception:

  • The Catholic Church traditionally teaches that artificial contraception is wrong because sex should be open to new life; it permits natural family planning.
  • Many Protestants accept contraception as a responsible way to plan a family and space children.

Both positions come from taking marriage, love and children seriously — they simply weigh these values differently.

Key point: when a question asks about "different Christian views", contrast Catholic and many Protestant teaching, and give a reason for each.

Matters of life & death

Origins of the universe & life

Science and religion both ask where the universe came from. Christians respond to scientific ideas in different ways:

  • Big Bang & evolution — the scientific accounts of how the universe and life developed.
  • Christian responses — many Christians accept these and say God is the cause behind them; some (Young Earth creationists) read Genesis literally and reject them.

Either way, Christians hold the sanctity of life: all life is holy and God-given, so human life has special value. This underpins their views on abortion and euthanasia.

Key point: "God created" and "the Big Bang happened" are not automatically in conflict — many Christians say God used natural processes to create.

Matters of life & death

Abortion and euthanasia

The sanctity of life shapes how Christians respond to two hard issues. Christians hold a range of views:

  • Abortion: many Christians oppose it because life is God-given from conception; others accept it in cases such as risk to the mother's life, guided by compassion.
  • Euthanasia: most Christians oppose deliberately ending life, trusting God over life and death; they often support hospices and good palliative care instead.
  • Some Christians weigh quality of life and the wish to reduce suffering alongside the sanctity of life.
  • All agree these are serious moral questions about a God-given life, not just personal choices.
