Mission 1

Mission 1: Why can a whole field of crop plants be wiped out by a single new disease?

Make a guess: Why can a whole field of crop plants be wiped out by a single new disease?

Mission 1

Genetic diversity

  • Genetic diversity: the variety of alleles within a species or population.
  • Every member of a species has the same genes. They differ in which alleles they carry.
  • Small, isolated or captive populations often have low genetic diversity.
  • Say: "different alleles of the same genes" — not: "different genes".
Quick check
?Cheetahs (Acinonyx jubatus) show extremely little genetic variation: unrelated cheetahs are so alike that skin grafts between them are not rejected. In a reserve, cheetah numbers have been stable for 20 years, and the species present and their relative abundances have not changed. Which statement about biodiversity in the reserve is correct?
Mission 1

From features to sequences

  • Genetic diversity can be compared by:
  • the frequency of observable or measurable characteristics;
  • the base sequence of DNA or of mRNA;
  • the amino-acid sequence of the protein coded for.
  • Fewer differences = more closely related.
  • Gene technology changed the method: inferring DNA differences from characteristics has been replaced by sequencing DNA directly.
  • Why: characteristics are also affected by the environment, and one characteristic may be controlled by many genes.
  • Say: "compare base sequences" — a base change may not change the amino acid (the code is degenerate), so proteins can hide DNA differences.
Mission 1

Why genetic diversity matters

  • Environments change: new diseases, new climates, new chemicals.
  • High genetic diversity: some individuals are more likely to carry alleles that let them survive. The population persists.
  • Low genetic diversity: if one is susceptible, nearly all are. The population can crash.
  • Example: California condors fell to 22 birds in 1982. All living condors descend from the 27 taken into captivity by 1987.
  • Say: "the population is more likely to contain resistant individuals" — not: "each individual is more resistant".
Quick checkRead the data
?Real data (Zhu et al. 2000). In Yunnan, China, farmers planted rows of disease-susceptible rice varieties among disease-resistant varieties in every rice field of 5 townships (1998) and 10 townships (1999). Plots of the susceptible varieties grown alone (monoculture) were the comparison.
Which conclusion is best supported?
  • Rice blast (a fungal disease) on the susceptible varieties: 94% less severe in mixtures.
  • Yield of the susceptible varieties: 89% greater in mixtures.
Mission check

?Almost all bananas in international trade are one variety, Cavendish. Every plant is grown from a piece of another plant, so all are genetically identical. A fungal wilt disease that kills Cavendish plants is spreading between plantations. Why are biologists concerned for the whole crop?

Numbers do not protect a population; variety does. Genetic diversity is a population's insurance against change.

Mission 2

Mission 2: If two groups of the same animal never meet again, how do they end up as different species?

Make a guess: If two groups of the same animal never meet again, how do they end up as different species?

Mission 2

What counts as a species?

  • Biological species concept: a group of organisms that can interbreed and produce fertile offspring.
  • CCEA wording: a group of individuals of common ancestry that closely resemble each other and are normally capable of interbreeding to produce fertile offspring.
  • Horse × donkey → mule. Offspring, yes; fertile, no. So horse and donkey are two species.
  • Say: "can produce fertile offspring" — not: "can produce offspring".
Quick check
?According to the biological species concept, what is a species?
Mission 2

Where the definition struggles

  • Often it cannot be tested:
    • fossils cannot be crossed;
    • populations that never meet cannot show it in the wild.
  • It does not fit organisms that reproduce asexually.
  • Some pairs of species still produce some fertile hybrids (common in plants).
  • So competing species concepts exist: shared traits, DNA, ancestry. None fits every case.
  • Hence "normally capable of interbreeding" in the CCEA definition.
Mission 2

Speciation means splitting

  • Speciation: one species splits into two or more.
  • The key step: populations become reproductively isolated. Gene flow between them stops.
  • Their gene pools then accumulate differences, until members can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring.
  • Usually gradual. Part-way through, "one species or two?" can be an arbitrary call.
  • A lineage that changes over time without splitting has not speciated.
Mission 2

Allopatric speciation, step by step

  • (1) One population. Gene flow keeps its gene pool mixed.
  • (2) A geographical barrier (river, sea, mountains) splits it. Gene flow stops. This is allopatric speciation.
  • (3) Each gene pool now changes on its own: new mutations, different selection pressures, and genetic drift in small populations. Allele frequencies diverge.
  • (4) In time, the two can no longer interbreed to produce fertile offspring: two species.
  • Say: "isolation stopped gene flow; selection changed allele frequencies" — not: "the river caused mutations" or "they changed in order to adapt".

Mission 2

What keeps species apart

  • Isolating mechanisms stop two gene pools mixing.
  • Before fertilisation (pre-zygotic):
    • geographical: they live in different places;
    • seasonal (temporal): they breed at different times;
    • behavioural: courtship signals differ;
    • morphological (mechanical): reproductive parts or flowers do not fit;
    • gametic: sperm cannot fertilise the other species' eggs.
  • After fertilisation (post-zygotic):
    • the hybrid dies before it can breed;
    • hybrid sterility: the hybrid lives but cannot breed. Mule: 32 horse + 31 donkey chromosomes = 63; the unmatched sets cannot pair properly in meiosis.
  • These reproductive isolating mechanisms maintain the genetic divergence.
Quick check
?Two closely related bird species live in the same forest. The males of each species sing a different song, and females respond only to the song of their own species. What is the significance of this for the two species?
Mission check

?A population of lizards was split when rising sea level cut off an island from the mainland. Many generations later, island and mainland lizards can no longer produce fertile offspring together. Which statement about this speciation is correct?

The barrier only stops gene flow. Mutation, selection and drift do the rest, over many generations.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Two animals look almost identical. How can biologists tell whether they are truly close relatives?

Make a guess: Two animals look almost identical. How can biologists tell whether they are truly close relatives?

Mission 3

Same bones, different jobs

  • Homologous structures: the same basic structure, used for different functions.
  • Pentadactyl limb: one upper bone, two lower bones, a group of wrist bones, five digits.
  • Found in a human arm, a bat wing, a whale flipper.
  • No single job needs this layout. It was inherited from a common ancestor and modified.
Mission 3

Same job, different origins

  • Analogous structures: the same function, different evolutionary origins.
  • They arise by convergent evolution: similar selection pressures favour similar forms in unrelated lineages.
  • Examples: streamlined body and fins of a shark (fish) and a dolphin (mammal); wings of birds and insects.
  • Classification uses homologous features. Analogous features can make unrelated groups look related.
  • Say: "convergent: features became alike" — not: "the species are merging".
Quick check
?Sharks, which are fish, and dolphins, which are mammals, both have streamlined bodies, a dorsal fin and paddle-shaped front fins. Which statement about these features is correct?
Mission 3

  • After two lineages split, differences accumulate in DNA base sequences and amino acid sequences.
  • Fewer differences → more recent common ancestor. P and Q are the closest relatives here.
  • DNA differs more than protein: some base changes do not change the amino acid.
  • At the widest scale, almost all organisms share one genetic code: best explained by a common ancestor of all life.
  • Classification now combines morphology, cell structure, and DNA, RNA and protein comparisons.
  • Say: "P and Q share a more recent common ancestor" — not: "P evolved from Q".
Quick check
?The amino acid sequence of the same protein was compared in four species. Compared with species W, species X differs at 2 positions, species Y at 15 positions and species Z at 31 positions. Which conclusion is supported by the data?
Mission check

?The marsupial mole of Australia and the golden moles of southern Africa look remarkably alike: tiny or hidden eyes, no external ears and spade-like front claws for digging. DNA comparisons place the marsupial mole closest to other Australian marsupials, and the golden moles closest to other African placental mammals. Which conclusion is best supported?

Looks can converge. Inherited structure and molecular sequences show who is really related.

Mission 4

Mission 4: A single cell can feed, move and reproduce on its own. So why are so many living things made of many cells?

Make a guess: A single cell can feed, move and reproduce on its own. So why are so many living things made of many cells?

Mission 4

Protoctists: one cell, two ways to feed

  • Protoctists are eukaryotic: nucleus, mitochondria, membrane-bound organelles.
  • Most are unicellular; some show only limited differentiation.
  • Paramecium: freshwater; heterotroph, takes in organic food made by other organisms. Cilia, oral groove, food vacuoles, contractile vacuoles.
  • Pleurococcus: green alga on damp tree bark and walls; autotroph, makes its own organic molecules by photosynthesis.
  • Pleurococcus: cellulose cell wall, a chloroplast, no cilia or flagella. Cells may stay together in small groups after dividing.
  • Each single cell carries out all its own life processes: feeding, respiration, excretion, reproduction.
Quick check
?Paramecium and Pleurococcus are both protoctists. Which statement is correct?
Mission 4

From one cell to many

  • Unicellular: one cell does every job.
  • Multicellular: cells specialise and share out the jobs.
Mission check

?Volvox is a green alga: a hollow ball of over a thousand cells. Most of its cells beat flagella to swim but never divide; a few large cells only make new colonies. Its close relative Chlamydomonas is a single cell that both swims and reproduces. What does Volvox gain from being multicellular?

One cell can do it all. Many cells can share out the jobs.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Genetic diversity is the variety of alleles in a population; high diversity makes it more likely that some individuals survive a change.

Mission 2

  • A species can interbreed to produce fertile offspring; speciation is one species splitting into two once gene flow between populations stops and their gene pools diverge.
  • Isolating mechanisms (geographical, seasonal, behavioural, morphological, hybrid sterility) keep species' gene pools apart.

Mission 3

  • Homologous structures share an origin but differ in function; analogous structures share a function but arose separately by convergent evolution.
  • Fewer differences in DNA or protein sequences mean a more recent common ancestor.

Mission 4

  • Protoctists are eukaryotic and mostly unicellular: Paramecium is a heterotroph, Pleurococcus an autotroph.
