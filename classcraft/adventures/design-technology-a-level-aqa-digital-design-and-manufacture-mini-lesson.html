Overview

The digital thread

Digital tools connect design to manufacture in one digital thread: a model is created in CAD, tested by simulation, then made by CAM-driven machines — with data flowing all the way to stock control.

CAD

Computer-aided design (CAD)

CAD creates 2D drawings and 3D models on screen. Benefits over hand drawing:

  • Designs are easy to edit and dimension precisely (parametric models update automatically).
  • Photorealistic rendering and 3D views for client presentation.
  • Models can be tested virtually and shared instantly with a global team.
  • Standard component libraries speed up work and aid standardisation.

CAM

Computer-aided manufacture (CAM)

CAM converts CAD data into toolpaths / machine code (e.g. G-code) that drive CNC routers, mills, lathes, laser cutters and 3D printers. The same file makes the part identically every time, so quality is consistent and repeatable.

Simulation

Virtual modelling and simulation

Before anything is made, designs can be tested on a computer:

  • FEA (finite element analysis) predicts stress, deflection and failure under load.
  • Thermal, fluid and motion simulations check performance.
  • Photorealistic rendering evaluates aesthetics.

Why it matters: virtual testing cuts the number of expensive physical prototypes, saving time, material and cost.

Rapid prototyping

Rapid prototyping

Rapid prototyping uses 3D printing to turn a CAD model into a physical part in hours, so ideas can be held, tested and iterated far faster than traditional model-making — a key enabler of iterative design.

CIM & FMS

CIM, FMS and data

  • CIM (computer-integrated manufacture) links design, machining, robotics and stock control into one connected system.
  • FMS (flexible manufacturing system) can be reconfigured quickly to make different products.
  • EPOS (electronic point of sale) feeds live sales data into stock control, triggering re-ordering — enabling JIT.

Balance

Advantages and drawbacks

Advantages: accuracy, speed, easy iteration, global collaboration, less waste. Drawbacks: cost of software and hardware, training needs, reliance on power/IT, and cybersecurity risks to design data.

Recap

The big ideas to know

CAD: 2D/3D modelling — easy edits, rendering, virtual testing, sharing, libraries.

CAM: turns CAD data into toolpaths/G-code for CNC and 3D printers.

Simulation: FEA predicts stress; virtual testing cuts physical prototypes.

Rapid prototyping: 3D printing enables fast iteration.

Systems: CIM integrates the factory; FMS is flexible; EPOS drives stock control/JIT.

Trade-offs: accuracy & collaboration vs cost, training and cybersecurity.
