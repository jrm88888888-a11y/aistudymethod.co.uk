Hypothesis testing · binomial tests

The language of a hypothesis test

  • The null hypothesis H0​H₀ states a value of the population parameter, e.g. H0​:p=0.5H₀colon p = 0.5. The alternative hypothesis H1​H₁ says how it may differ: p>0.5p > 0.5 or p<0.5p < 0.5 (1-tail), or p=0.5p ≠ 0.5 (2-tail).
  • Define pp in context, e.g. "pp is the probability that the coin lands heads".
  • The test statistic is the sample count, X∼B(n,p)X ~ B(n, p) assuming H0​H₀ is true.
  • The significance level is the cut-off probability for "too unlikely if H0​H₀ is true", e.g. 5%.

Worked example Hypothesis testing · binomial tests

A 1-tail test using a p-value

Question
A sweet maker says 30% of its sweets are red. Asha thinks the proportion is higher. A random bag of 25 sweets has 12 red. Test Asha's claim at the 5% level.
  1. 1
    State hypotheses: H0​:p=0.3H₀colon p = 0.3, H1​:p>0.3H₁colon p > 0.3, where pp is the proportion of red sweets
  2. 2
    Model under H0​H₀: X∼B(25,0.3)X ~ B(25, 0.3), where XX is the number of red sweets
  3. 3
    Find the probability of 12 or more: P(X≥12)=1−P(X≤11)=1−0.9558=0.0442P(X ≥ 12) = 1 − P(X ≤ 11) = 1 − 0.9558 = 0.0442
  4. 4
    Compare with the level: 0.0442<0.050.0442 < 0.05, so the result is significant: reject H0​H₀
Answer
There is sufficient evidence at the 5% level to reject H0​H₀. It is likely that more than 30% of the sweets are red.
⚠️ Watch out
the p-value is P(X≥12)P(X ≥ 12), not P(X=12)=0.0268P(X = 12) = 0.0268. You need the result or anything more extreme.
Hypothesis testing · binomial tests

Critical regions and the actual significance level

  • The critical region is the set of values that lead you to reject H0​H₀; a critical value is its boundary value (a 2-tail test has two). The rest is the acceptance region.
  • For a 2-tail test at 5%, each tail has probability at most 2.5%. Compare xx with npnp, the expected number of successes, to see which tail it is in.
  • For discrete counts the actual significance level, P(X in critical region)P(X in critical region), is usually below the stated level.
  • The significance level is the probability of incorrectly rejecting H0​H₀.

    Each tail may have at most 0.025
  2. 2

?A coin lands heads 15 times in 20 throws. Test at the 5% level whether it is biased.

Under H0​:p=0.5H₀colon p = 0.5, X∼B(20,0.5)X ~ B(20, 0.5) and P(X≥15)=0.0207<0.025P(X ≥ 15) = 0.0207 < 0.025, so there is evidence at the 5% level that the coin is biased. How sure? Hypothesis testing · correlation and the normal mean

The sample mean as a random variable (A-level only)

  • Each random sample of size nn gives a different mean, so the sample mean XˉX̄ is a random variable.
  • If X∼N(μ,σ2)X ~ N(μ, σ²), then
Xˉ∼N(μ,nσ2​)X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n)
  • Its standard deviation is n​σ​σ/(√n): averages vary much less than single values.
  • To test a mean, state H0​:μ=μ0​H₀colon μ = μ₀ and H1​H₁ in terms of μμ, then standardise:
Z=σ/n​Xˉ−μ0​​∼N(0,1)Z = (X̄ − μ₀)/(σ / √n) ~ N(0, 1)

A test for a normal mean

Question
Journey times on a route are normally distributed with mean 42 minutes and standard deviation 6 minutes. After roadworks, a random sample of 16 journeys has mean 44.1 minutes. Assuming the standard deviation is unchanged, test at the 5% level whether the mean journey time has increased.
  1. 1
    State hypotheses: H0​:μ=42H₀colon μ = 42, H1​:μ>42H₁colon μ > 42, where μμ is the mean journey time in minutes
  2. 2
    Under H0​H₀: Xˉ∼N(42,1662​)X̄ ~ N(42, 6²/16), so the standard deviation of XˉX̄ is 46​=1.56/4 = 1.5
  3. 3
    Find the p-value: P(Xˉ≥44.1)=P(Z≥1.4)=0.0808P(X̄ ≥ 44.1) = P(Z ≥ 1.4) = 0.0808
  4. 4
    Compare: 0.0808>0.050.0808 > 0.05, so the result is not significant: do not reject H0​H₀
Answer
There is insufficient evidence at the 5% level that the mean journey time has increased.
⚠️ Watch out
using σ=6σ = 6 instead of 16​6​6/(√16) gives z=0.35z = 0.35. The test is about the mean of 16 journeys, not one journey.

Hypothesis testing · correlation and the normal mean

Testing for correlation (A-level only)

  • The product moment correlation coefficient (PMCC) rr measures how close points lie to a straight line: −1≤r≤1−1 ≤ r ≤ 1; r=±1r = ± 1 means all on a line.
  • rr is the sample value; ρρ the population value. Test H0​:ρ=0H₀colon ρ = 0 against H1​:ρ>0H₁colon ρ > 0, ρ<0ρ < 0 or ρ=0ρ ≠ 0.
  • Compare ∣r∣|r| with the tabled critical value for nn. Reject H0​H₀ if ∣r∣≥|r| ≥ critical value (sign matching H1​H₁), or if the p-value is at most the level.
  • The data are assumed to be from a bivariate normal distribution.

A 1-tail correlation test

Question
On 12 randomly chosen days, a garden records hours of sunshine and number of visitors. The PMCC is r=0.53r = 0.53. The critical value for n=12n = 12 at the 5% level (1-tail) is 0.4973. Test at the 5% level whether there is positive correlation.
  1. 1
    State hypotheses: H0​:ρ=0H₀colon ρ = 0, H1​:ρ>0H₁colon ρ > 0, where ρρ is the population PMCC between sunshine and visitors
  2. 2
    Compare: 0.53>0.49730.53 > 0.4973, so rr is in the critical region
  3. 3
    Reject H0​H₀
Answer
There is evidence at the 5% level of positive correlation between hours of sunshine and number of visitors.
⚠️ Watch out

Recap

Big ideas

  • State H0​H₀ and H1​H₁ about the population parameter pp (defined in context); the test statistic X∼B(n,p)X ~ B(n, p) assumes H0​H₀.
  • Reject H0​H₀ if the p-value is at most the significance level, or if xx is in the critical region. For a 2-tail test, halve the level for each tail.
  • Conclude in context and without certainty: "there is evidence at the 5% level that …". The significance level is the probability of incorrectly rejecting H0​H₀.
  • If X∼N(μ,σ2)X ~ N(μ, σ²) then Xˉ∼N(μ,nσ2​)X̄ ~ N(μ, σ²/n); test a mean with Z=σ/n​Xˉ−μ0​​Z = (X̄ − μ₀)/(σ/√n).
  • Test correlation with H0​:ρ=0H₀colon ρ = 0; compare ∣r∣|r| with the tabled critical value for nn (or use the p-value), choosing the 1-tail or 2-tail column.
  • Conclude in context, without certainty, and remember that correlation is not causation.
