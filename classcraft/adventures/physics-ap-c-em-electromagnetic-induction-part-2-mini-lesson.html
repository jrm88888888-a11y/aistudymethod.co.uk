Mission 1

Mission 1: Close a switch in a circuit with a large coil. Why does the current take time to reach its full value?

Make a guess: Close a switch in a circuit with a large coil. Why does the current take time to reach its full value?

Mission 1

Switching on an LR circuit

  • Battery ε, resistor R, ideal inductor L, in series. Switch closed at t = 0.
  • Loop rule: ε − IR − L dI/dt = 0, so ε = IR + L dI/dt.
  • Just after closing: I = 0, so the inductor's emf is ε, opposing the battery. dI/dt = ε/L.
  • Long after: I steady, dI/dt = 0. The inductor acts as a plain wire. I = ε/R.
Quick check
?A series circuit contains an ideal 9.0 V battery, a 25 Ω resistor, an ideal inductor and a switch. The switch is closed at t = 0. At a later instant, the current in the circuit is 0.24 A. What is the magnitude of the potential difference across the inductor at that instant?
Mission 1

Solving the loop equation

  • Solution with I(0) = 0: I = (ε/R)(1 − e−t/τ), with τ = L/R.
  • Check: dI/dt = (ε/L)e−t/τ. Then IR + L dI/dt = ε(1 − e−t/τ) + εe−t/τ = ε. ✓
  • Potential difference across the inductor: |ΔVL| = εe−t/τ: starts at ε, decays to zero.
  • Energy stored ½LI² also rises to a steady value, ½L(ε/R)².
Mission 1

What τ means

  • At t = τ, I = (1 − e⁻¹) × final ≈ 63 % of the final current.
  • Rising at its initial rate ε/L, the current would reach ε/R in exactly τ.
  • Bigger L: slower. Bigger R: faster (τ = L/R), and a smaller final current.
  • Use Req: the resistance the inductor's current actually passes through.
Mission 1

Worked example

  • ε = 12 V, R = 20 Ω, L = 0.50 H.
  • τ = 0.50/20 = 25 ms. Final current 12/20 = 0.60 A.
  • At t = 25 ms: I = 0.60(1 − e⁻¹) = 0.38 A.
  • Initial rate: ε/L = 24 A/s. 24 A/s × 0.025 s = 0.60 A: the tangent reaches the final value at τ.
Quick check
?A series circuit contains an ideal battery of emf ε, a resistor of resistance R, an ideal inductor of inductance L and a switch. The switch is closed at t = 0. At what time t is the energy stored in the inductor one-quarter of its final value?
Mission 1

Switching off: decay

  • Inductor carrying I0 is connected across a resistor, with no battery.
  • I = I0e−t/τ, τ = L/Req of the loop it decays through.
  • At t = τ: 37 % of I0 remains.
  • Just after switching, the inductor keeps the current I0 flowing: its emf is I0Req, which can be larger than the battery's emf.
  • Example: 0.60 A in 0.50 H, diverted through 100 Ω. τ = 5.0 ms; emf just after = 0.60 × 100 = 60 V.
Mission 1

Where the energy goes

  • The stored energy ½LI0² is dissipated in the resistors as the current decays.
  • Decay example above: ½ × 0.50 × 0.60² = 0.090 J, all to the 100 Ω resistor.
  • The inductor does not dissipate energy: an ideal inductor has no resistance.
  • No path for the current (switch simply opened): dI/dt is enormous, and the emf can spark across the switch.
Quick check
?An ideal inductor of inductance 0.40 H carries a steady current of 4.0 A. At t = 0 a switch disconnects the battery and, at the same instant, connects the inductor across a 12 Ω resistor, so the current decays in the loop formed by the inductor and the resistor. How much energy is dissipated in the resistor between t = 0 and t = τ, where τ is the time constant of that loop?
Quick check
?In the circuit shown, the battery and the inductor are ideal, and switch S is initially open. S is closed at t = 0. I0 is the current in resistor R2 just after S is closed, and If is the current in R2 a long time after S is closed. Which statement is correct?
Mission check

?The diagram shows three circuits, each with an identical ideal battery. Every resistor has resistance R, and the ideal inductor in each circuit has inductance L. τ1, τ2 and τ3 are the time constants of Circuits 1, 2 and 3. Which ranking of the time constants is correct?

The inductor opposes every change in its current, so the current approaches its final value exponentially, with τ = L/Req. More inductance, or less resistance in the path the inductor's current takes, means a slower rise.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Connect a charged capacitor to a coil, with no battery. Why does the charge swing back and forth?

Make a guess: Connect a charged capacitor to a coil, with no battery. Why does the charge swing back and forth?

Quick check
?Predict first. A charged capacitor is connected across an ideal inductor at t = 0, with no resistance in the circuit. Which describes the charge on the capacitor after t = 0?
Mission 2

One cycle, step by step

  • Start: capacitor charged to Q0, current zero. All energy in the capacitor.
  • It discharges through L. The inductor makes the current grow gradually.
  • Capacitor empty: current maximum. All energy in the inductor's field.
  • The inductor keeps the current going, charging the capacitor the opposite way.
  • Then the current reverses, and the cycle repeats. No resistance: it goes on forever with constant amplitude.
Mission 2

Energy is conserved

  • At every instant: q²/(2C) + ½LI² = Q0²/(2C).
  • Maximum current when q = 0: ½LImax² = Q0²/(2C).
  • Imax = Q0/√(LC). Starting from potential difference ΔV0: Imax = ΔV0√(C/L).
  • Larger L: smaller Imax, since Imax ∝ 1/√L.
Mission 2

Worked example

  • C = 10 μF charged to 20 V, then connected across L = 25 mH.
  • Q0 = CΔV0 = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ C. Energy ½CΔV0² = 2.0 × 10⁻³ J.
  • Imax = Q0/√(LC) = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴/√(2.5 × 10⁻⁷) = 0.40 A.
  • Check: ½LImax² = ½ × 0.025 × 0.40² = 2.0 × 10⁻³ J. ✓
Quick check
?A 20 μF capacitor is charged to a potential difference of 12 V and then connected across an ideal 50 mH inductor, with no other elements in the circuit. What is the maximum current in the inductor?
Mission 2

The SHM equation

  • SHM: any quantity obeying d²x/dt² = −ω²x oscillates as x = A cos ωt, with ω set by the system.
  • Loop rule: q/C + L dI/dt = 0, with I = dq/dt.
  • So d²q/dt² = −q/(LC): the same form as a = −ω²x.
  • ω = 1/√(LC); period T = 2π√(LC).
  • q = Q0 cos ωt; I = dq/dt = −ωQ0 sin ωt. Current peaks when q = 0.
  • Analogy: q ↔ x, I ↔ v, L ↔ m, 1/C ↔ k.
  • Worked example above: ω = 1/√(2.5 × 10⁻⁷) = 2.0 × 10³ rad/s.
Quick check
?An ideal LC circuit has an inductance of 80 mH and a capacitance of 5.0 μF. What is the angular frequency of its oscillations?
Quick check
?Capacitor C in the circuit shown is initially charged, capacitor 2C is initially uncharged, and the inductor is ideal. Switch S is then closed. What is the angular frequency of the resulting oscillations of charge?
Mission 2

Energy sloshing in time

  • UC = (Q0²/2C) cos²ωt; UL = (Q0²/2C) sin²ωt. They always add to Q0²/(2C).
  • Each energy swings between zero and the total twice per cycle of q.
Quick check
?In an ideal LC circuit, at t = 0 the capacitor (capacitance C) has charge Q0 and the current is zero; the inductor has inductance L. At what time t does the energy stored in the inductor first equal the energy stored in the capacitor?
Mission check

?The graph shows the charge q on the capacitor of an ideal LC circuit as a function of time t. Which ranks the magnitudes of the current in the circuit at the instants P, Q and R?

The current is the slope of the q–t graph: largest where the capacitor is empty, zero where it is fully charged. Energy passes back and forth between the capacitor's field and the inductor's field, so the charge swings with ω = 1/√(LC).

Mission 3

Mission 3: Light crosses empty space with nothing to carry it. What keeps the wave going?

Make a guess: Light crosses empty space with nothing to carry it. What keeps the wave going?

Mission 3

Four equations, all of electromagnetism

  • 1. Gauss (E): ∮E·dA = qenc/ε0. Charges are sources of electric field.
  • 2. Gauss (B): ∮B·dA = 0. No magnetic charges.
  • 3. Faraday: ∮E·dℓ⃗ = −dΦB/dt. A changing magnetic flux makes a circulating electric field.
  • 4. Ampère–Maxwell: ∮B·dℓ⃗ = μ0I + μ0ε0 dΦE/dt. Currents and changing electric flux make a circulating magnetic field.
  • With the force on a charge, these fully describe electromagnetism.
Quick check
?Gauss's law is the first of Maxwell's equations, the set of equations that together fully describe electromagnetism. Which statement about Gauss's law is correct?
Mission 3

Equation 1: Gauss's law

  • Holds for every closed surface, any shape, any charges inside or outside.
  • Net flux out depends only on the enclosed charge.
  • Symmetry (sphere, cylinder, plane) only makes it easy to use for finding E.
  • Field lines that start or end do so on charges. (Induced E fields, equation 3, form closed loops instead.)
Mission 3

Equation 2: no magnetic charges

  • ∮B·dA = 0 for every closed surface.
  • Magnetic field lines form closed loops: out of N, around, into S, back through the magnet.
  • Every line that leaves a closed surface comes back in. Net flux: zero.
  • So there are no monopoles: no point where field lines simply begin.
Quick check
?The figure shows a bar magnet and some of its field lines. The dashed line is the edge view of a flat plane that cuts through the middle of the magnet, perpendicular to it, and extends indefinitely. Region 1 is the part of the plane inside the magnet; region 2 is all the rest of the plane. How do the magnitudes of the magnetic flux through region 1 and through region 2 compare?
Mission 3

Equation 3: induced electric field

  • ∮E·dℓ⃗ = −dΦB/dt. The emf around a loop is the circulation of an induced E field.
  • The field exists with or without a wire. A wire just lets charges respond.
  • Its lines are closed loops: not conservative, so no potential can describe it.
  • Long solenoid, radius R, B = βt inside. Circle of radius r about the axis:
  • Inside (r < R): E(2πr) = πr²β → E = rβ/2.
  • Outside (r > R): E(2πr) = πR²β → E = R²β/(2r). Nonzero, though B ≈ 0 there.
  • R = 0.040 m, β = 0.50 T/s: at r = 0.020 m, E = 5.0 × 10⁻³ V/m; at r = 0.10 m, E = 4.0 × 10⁻³ V/m.
Quick check
?An electron is held at rest at a point outside a long solenoid, where the solenoid's magnetic field is negligible. The current in the solenoid begins to increase, and the electron is released. Which describes the force on the electron just after it is released?
Quick check
?A parallel-plate capacitor is being charged, with a steady current in the wires connected to its plates. Which statement about the region between the plates is correct?
Mission 3

Equation 4: a changing E field makes B

  • Ampère: a current makes a circulating B field.
  • Maxwell's addition: a changing electric field also makes one, just as a changing B field makes E.
  • Charging capacitor: no charge crosses the gap, yet the growing E field between the plates is circled by a B field.
  • You will not need to calculate with the changing-E term. Know that it exists and what it does.
Mission 3

Light

  • Equations 3 and 4 together: a changing B makes E, a changing E makes B.
  • In empty space, with no charges or currents, the two can sustain each other and travel: an electromagnetic wave.
  • Maxwell's equations give its speed in free space: c = 1/√(ε0μ0).
  • 1/√(4π × 10⁻⁷ × 8.85 × 10⁻¹²) = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s: the measured speed of light.
  • Same speed for every frequency. No medium needed. Derivation: not required.
Quick check
?Suppose the permittivity of free space were four times its actual value, with the permeability unchanged. What speed would Maxwell's equations then predict for electromagnetic waves in a vacuum?
Mission check

?A region of empty space contains no charges and no currents. At one instant, the electric field throughout the region points in the +x direction and its magnitude is increasing steadily. Which statement follows from Maxwell's equations?

A changing E field makes a circulating B field, and a changing B field makes a circulating E field. In light, each keeps renewing the other, so the wave needs no medium and travels at 1/√(ε0μ0).

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • ε = IR + L dI/dt. Growth I = (ε/R)(1 − e−t/τ); decay I = I0e−t/τ; τ = L/Req.
  • Just after switching, an inductor's current is unchanged; a long time later it is a plain wire. Stored energy ends up dissipated in the resistors.

Mission 2

  • LC circuit: q²/(2C) + ½LI² = constant; Imax = Q0/√(LC).
  • d²q/dt² = −q/(LC): SHM with ω = 1/√(LC).

Mission 3

  • Maxwell's four equations: Gauss (E), Gauss (B) = 0, Faraday (changing B → circulating E), Ampère–Maxwell (current or changing E → circulating B).
  • Together they predict EM waves in free space at c = 1/√(ε0μ0) = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s.
