The literary past

The passé simple — recognise, don't produce

In novels and formal narrative, French replaces the passé composé with the passé simple. You'll never speak it — but you must read it. The regular endings:

  • -er verbs → il parla (he spoke), ils parlèrent (they spoke)
  • -ir / -re verbs → il finit, ils finirent, il vendit

Reading tip: il parla in a novel simply means "he spoke" — mentally read it as the passé composé il a parlé.

When is it used?

Where you'll meet the passé simple

The passé simple belongs to written, literary narrative — novels, short stories, historical accounts. It is not used in speech or informal writing, where the passé composé takes over:

  • Novel (literary): Il entra et vit Marie. — He entered and saw Marie.
  • Speech / everyday: Il est entré et a vu Marie. — same meaning, everyday register.

Exam reality: you only ever read the passé simple. In your own writing, stick with the passé composé.

Literary subjunctive

The imperfect & pluperfect subjunctive

Older and very formal literature also uses the imperfect subjunctive and pluperfect subjunctive. Like the passé simple, these are literary forms you only need to recognise:

  • qu'il fût — a literary form of the subjunctive of être
  • qu'il eût — a literary form of the subjunctive of avoir

Spot the accent: the circumflex on fût / eût marks the literary subjunctive — modern French would use the present subjunctive soit / ait.

Register

tu vs vous — reading the register

French chooses its pronoun for "you" by register:

  • tu — informal / singular: friends, family, children.
  • vous — formal (one person) or plural (more than one person).

Formal writing: essays, letters and the exam favour vous and formal conventions — the choice of pronoun signals the whole tone of a text.

False friends

Faux amis — words that trick you

Some French words look like English words but mean something different. These faux amis cost easy marks:

  • une librairie = a bookshop (not a library)
  • actuellement = currently (not actually)
  • sensible = sensitive (not sensible)
  • assister à = to attend (not to assist)
  • rester = to stay (not to rest)
  • une journée = a day (not a journey)
  • éventuellement = possibly (not eventually)
  • prétendre = to claim (not to pretend)
