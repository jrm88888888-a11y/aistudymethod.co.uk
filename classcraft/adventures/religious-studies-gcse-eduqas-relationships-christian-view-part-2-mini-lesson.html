Mission 1

Mission 1: Why can one married couple use the pill with a clear conscience while another won't?

Make a guess: Why can one married couple use the pill with a clear conscience while another won't?

Mission 1

Methods and reasons

  • Contraception: deliberately preventing pregnancy. Family planning: deciding whether, when and how many children to have.
  • Artificial methods: barrier (condom), hormonal (pill, implant), IUD (coil).
  • Natural methods (natural family planning, NFP): avoiding sex on the woman's fertile days, tracked by body signs.
  • Permanent: sterilisation (vasectomy for men, tubal ligation for women).
  • Some methods may work after conception (some emergency pills, the IUD in some cases). Many believers who accept contraception reject these, seeing them as close to abortion.
  • Reasons: spacing children, health, money, the couple's relationship; condoms also protect against STIs.
Mission 1

Catholic teaching: Humanae Vitae

  • Humanae Vitae (Pope Paul VI, 1968), paraphrase: sex in marriage has two meanings, unitive (joining the couple) and procreative (open to new life). They must never be separated (HV 12).
  • So each act of intercourse must stay open to life (HV 11): artificial contraception and sterilisation are wrong (HV 14).
  • Responsible parenthood (HV 10): couples may space births for serious reasons using NFP, which works with the body's natural rhythm (HV 16).
  • Roots in natural law: Thomas Aquinas (c. 1225–1274) saw reproduction as a basic human purpose.
  • Your spec: Aquinas' second primary precept, to reproduce. Some Christians apply it strictly; others say it applies to marriage as a whole, not to every act.

?A Catholic married couple have serious health reasons to avoid a pregnancy for two years. What does Catholic teaching allow?
Mission 1Diversity

Other Christians

  • Church of England: the Lambeth Conference of 1930 first accepted contraception within marriage for good reasons; by 1958 it called family planning a matter of the couple's conscience.
  • Methodists and most Protestants: responsible use of contraception within marriage is good stewardship; it can strengthen the couple's love.
  • Orthodox: many allow non-abortive methods within marriage after pastoral guidance.
  • Some conservative evangelicals: leave family size to God and avoid contraception.
  • All these Christians reject methods that act after conception if they see them as ending a life.
  • Many Catholics use contraception in practice, though the teaching has not changed.
Mission 1Diversity

Islam

  • Muslims believe children are a gift from Allah; having a family is a strong Sunnah.
  • Hadith report that Companions practised 'azl (withdrawal) and the Prophet did not forbid it.
  • So most scholars allow reversible methods within marriage, with both spouses' consent, for reasons such as health or spacing.
  • Permanent sterilisation: generally only for serious health reasons. Methods acting after conception: widely rejected.
  • Some Muslims oppose contraception from fear of poverty, citing: "Slay not your children, fearing a fall to poverty, We shall provide for them and for you." (Qur'an 17:31, Pickthall)
Mission 1Diversity

Judaism

  • "Be fruitful and multiply" (Genesis 1:28) is counted the first mitzvah (commandment).
  • Orthodox Jews: contraception allowed with a rabbi's guidance, especially for the wife's health. Methods used by the woman (the pill) are preferred to the condom, which wastes seed (compare Onan, Genesis 38:9).
  • Reform and Liberal Jews: a responsible choice for the couple; health and family welfare come first.
  • Both put the mother's life and health first.
Mission 1Diversity

Hindu, Buddhist and Sikh views

  • Hindus: the householder stage (grihastha) includes the duty to have children. Many Hindus accept contraception once that duty is met; ahimsa (non-harm) rules out methods that destroy an embryo.
  • Bhagavata Purana 5.5.8 (paraphrase): attraction between man and woman binds people to home, possessions and worldly life. Your spec also asks how sex relates to karma.
  • Buddhists: no ban. Preventing conception harms no being, so most accept it. Methods that act after conception may break the First Precept (not harming life). Intention (cetana) matters.
  • Sikhs: no Guru forbids it; a matter for the couple. Kaam (lust) is one of the five thieves, so sex should be disciplined within marriage. Methods that act after conception are opposed.
  • Your spec names Guru Granth Sahib 335 for Sikh teaching on sex and contraception.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Two people who love each other can marry in law. Why do believers reading the same texts still disagree?

Make a guess: Two people who love each other can marry in law. Why do believers reading the same texts still disagree?

Mission 2

Words and the law

  • Sexuality / sexual orientation: who a person is attracted to. Heterosexual: the opposite sex. Homosexual (gay, lesbian): the same sex. Bisexual: both.
  • Civil partnership: legal union, not called marriage. UK-wide since 2005 (Civil Partnership Act 2004).
  • Same-sex marriage: England and Wales from 2014 (Marriage (Same Sex Couples) Act 2013); Scotland 2014; Northern Ireland 2020.
  • No religious group is forced to hold same-sex weddings. Groups may opt in; the Church of England and Church in Wales are barred by the Act unless the law changes.
Mission 2

The traditional Christian view

  • Marriage is a lifelong union of one man and one woman: "they will be one flesh" (Genesis 2:24); Jesus repeats it (Mark 10:6–8).
  • Sex belongs only within that marriage, where it unites the couple and can create life.
  • Texts read as forbidding same-sex acts: Leviticus 18:22; Romans 1:26–27; 1 Corinthians 6:9–10; 1 Timothy 1:10.
  • Most who hold this view separate orientation (not a sin) from sexual acts (sinful outside man–woman marriage).
  • So: same-sex attracted Christians are called to celibacy; same-sex marriage is not a true marriage in God's eyes.
Mission 2

Catholic teaching

  • The Catechism (CCC 2357–2359, paraphrase): same-sex sexual acts cannot be approved; people with same-sex attraction must be treated with respect, compassion and sensitivity; unjust discrimination must be avoided; all are called to chastity.
  • Marriage can only be between a man and a woman, open to children.
  • 2023, Fiducia Supplicans: priests may give a short, informal blessing to couples in "irregular" situations, including same-sex couples, but never in a form like a wedding, and without approving the union.
Mission 2Read the source

Two ways of reading

"You shall not lie with a man as with a woman. That is detestable." (Leviticus 18:22, WEBBE)

  • Traditional reading: a moral law, repeated in the New Testament (1 Corinthians 6:9–10), so it still applies.
  • Liberal reading: part of a holiness code that also bans wearing two kinds of material (Leviticus 19:19); it addresses acts in an ancient setting, not loving, faithful partnerships.
  • Your spec also names Leviticus 20:13 (the same act, with the death penalty) and 1 Timothy 1:8–10 (a list of wrongdoers that includes "homosexuals" in WEBBE; some scholars argue the Greek word meant exploitative sex).

Q1 Which other verse might a liberal Christian use to argue Leviticus should not be applied word for word?

Show answer

Leviticus 19:19: Christians don't keep that law either.

Q2 How would a traditionalist reply?

Show answer

The moral ban is repeated in the New Testament; food and fabric laws are not.

Mission 2Diversity

The range of Christian views

  • Catholic and Orthodox: acts sinful; persons respected; no same-sex marriage.
  • Many evangelicals: same view, from the authority of the Bible's plain sense.
  • Church of England: does not marry same-sex couples; since 2023 its Prayers of Love and Faith can be used to pray God's blessing on them in ordinary services.
  • Have opted in to same-sex weddings: Quakers, United Reformed Church (2016), Scottish Episcopal Church (2017), Methodist Church (2021, where a local church agrees), Church of Scotland (2022, ministers who opt in).
  • Liberal Christians: "God is love" (1 John 4:8); God made people as they are; faithful, loving relationships reflect God whatever the couple's sex.

Mission 2Diversity

Judaism and Islam

  • Orthodox Jews: Leviticus 18:22 forbids the act; marriage is between a man and a woman. Many stress welcome and dignity for gay Jews (UK Chief Rabbi's guidance for schools, 2018).
  • Reform and Liberal Jews: read Leviticus in context; every person is made in God's image (Genesis 1:27). Their synagogues in Britain hold same-sex weddings.
  • Most Muslims: same-sex acts are forbidden. The Qur'an tells of the people of Lut: "Lo! ye come with lust unto men instead of women." (Qur'an 7:81, Pickthall)
  • Marriage (nikah) is between a man and a woman; sex belongs only within it (Qur'an 23:5–6).
  • Many Muslims distinguish feelings from actions, and reject abuse of gay people.
  • A minority of Muslims (e.g. the UK group Imaan) read the Lut story as condemning violence, not loving relationships. Most scholars reject this reading.
Mission 2Diversity

Hindu, Buddhist and Sikh views

  • Hindus: no single authority. Many see marriage and the householder stage (grihastha ashrama), with its duty to have children, as the right path, so same-sex relationships clash with householder dharma.
  • Others point to old texts that speak of a "third nature" (tritiya prakriti) and stories of gods who change sex. India decriminalised gay sex in 2018; its Supreme Court declined to create same-sex marriage in 2023.
  • Your spec contrasts traditional Hinduism with Hinduism in Britain, where same-sex relationships are widely accepted.
  • Buddhists: no official view. The Third Precept forbids sexual misconduct: sex that harms, exploits or betrays. Some count same-sex acts as misconduct; many Western Buddhists (e.g. Triratna) do not, if the relationship is loving and faithful.
  • Sikhs: the Lavan (wedding hymns) see married life between husband and wife as the ideal; nine of the ten Gurus married. In 2005 the head (Jathedar) of the Akal Takht told Sikhs to oppose same-sex marriage. Some Sikhs stress that God's light is in every person.

Mission 2Worked answer

Two beliefs that differ

Question: Explain two contrasting beliefs in contemporary British society about same-sex relationships.

  • Belief 1: many Catholics believe same-sex sexual acts are wrong. Development: the Catechism teaches that sex is for a married man and woman and open to new life, so they call gay people to chastity, while respecting them.
  • Belief 2: many liberal Christians and Humanists believe same-sex relationships are as good as heterosexual ones. Development: for liberal Christians "God is love" (1 John 4:8); for Humanists, consenting adults harm no one, so same-sex couples should marry.
Mission check

?A same-sex couple in England asks four places of worship to hold their wedding. Which could legally marry them there?

Law and religion now differ: the state allows same-sex marriage, and each faith decides whether to hold weddings, guided by how it reads its texts.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Equal but different, or equal and the same: who should do what in a family?

Make a guess: Equal but different, or equal and the same: who should do what in a family?

Mission 3

Roles then and now

  • Traditional roles: man as breadwinner and head; woman as homemaker and main carer.
  • Today: most couples both work; more fathers share childcare or stay at home; roles are negotiated.
  • Gender equality: men and women have equal worth, rights and opportunities.
  • Two religious approaches:
  • Complementarian: equal in worth, different and complementary roles.
  • Egalitarian: equal in worth, roles shared or interchangeable, decided by gifts and circumstances.
Mission 3

Genesis: equal in God's image

  • "God created man in his own image. In God's image he created him; male and female he created them." (Genesis 1:27)
  • Both share God's image and the same blessing and task (Genesis 1:26–31).
  • Genesis 2:18: the woman is "a helper comparable to him". The Hebrew word (ezer) is also used of God helping Israel, so it does not mean inferior.
  • Genesis 3:16: after the Fall, "he will rule over you". Many Christians and Jews read this as the result of sin, not God's original plan.
Mission 3Diversity

Christian views on roles

  • Complementarian (many evangelicals, some traditional Christians): the husband is a loving, serving head; the wife supports and respects him (Ephesians 5:22–25).
  • Egalitarian (many Anglicans, Methodists, liberal Christians): 5:21 means mutual submission; "neither male nor female" (Galatians 3:28); roles by gifts.
  • Catholic: man and woman are equal in dignity and complementary. John Paul II (Mulieris Dignitatem, 1988) read Ephesians 5 as mutual submission.
Mission 3Diversity

Islam

  • Equal before Allah: "Whosoever doeth right, whether male or female, and is a believer..." (Qur'an 16:97, Pickthall); both created "from a single soul" (Qur'an 4:1).
  • Different responsibilities: the husband must provide for the family (Qur'an 4:34); a wife's own earnings and property remain hers; mahr (dowry) is paid to her.
  • "they (women) have rights similar to those (of men) over them in kindness, and men are a degree above them." (Qur'an 2:228, Pickthall) Scholars differ on what the "degree" means (e.g. extra responsibility).
  • Diversity: traditional Muslims stress distinct roles; many British Muslims share work and childcare.
Mission 3Diversity

Judaism

  • "A woman of valour who can find? for her price is far above rubies." (Proverbs 31:10, JPS 1917) This poem (31:10–31) is sung in many Jewish homes on Friday night, often by the husband; the woman runs a home, trades and buys a field (31:16).
  • Orthodox Jews: equal worth, different roles. Women are exempt from many time-bound commandments, partly to allow for family duties; the home is a woman's special sphere of holiness (e.g. Shabbat candles).
  • Reform and Liberal Jews: egalitarian: roles in home and synagogue are shared.
Mission 3Diversity

Hindu, Buddhist and Sikh views

  • Hindus: Manusmriti 3.55–60 (paraphrase): women must be honoured by fathers, brothers and husbands; where women are honoured the gods rejoice, and where they are not, rites bring no reward.
  • The wife is ardhangini ("half of the body") of her husband; stridharma (women's duty) traditionally centred on home and family. Many Hindus today share roles; others keep traditional ones.
  • Buddhists: Sigalovada Sutta: the husband honours her, does not look down on her, is faithful, gives her authority in the home and gives her gifts (ornaments); the wife manages the home well, is hospitable to relatives, faithful, protects the family's wealth and is skilful.
  • Sikhs: Guru Granth Sahib 788 (paraphrase): those who merely sit together are not husband and wife; husband and wife are those who have one light in two bodies. Equal partners in grihasth.

Mission 4

Mission 4: If a faith teaches that women and men are equal, why can't women lead worship everywhere?

Make a guess: If a faith teaches that women and men are equal, why can't women lead worship everywhere?

Mission 4

Prejudice, discrimination, equality

  • Prejudice: judging someone unfairly before knowing them (an attitude). Sexism: prejudice based on sex.
  • Discrimination: treating someone unfairly because of a prejudice (an action).
  • Gender equality: equal rights, status and opportunities for women and men.
  • UK law: Equal Pay Act 1970; Sex Discrimination Act 1975; now the Equality Act 2010.
  • Examples: unequal pay for the same work; women passed over for promotion; girls denied schooling (Malala Yousafzai, shot in 2012 for campaigning for girls' education).
Mission 4

Christian teaching on equality

  • Both sexes are made in God's image (Genesis 1:27).
  • "There is neither Jew nor Greek, there is neither slave nor free man, there is neither male nor female; for you are all one in Christ Jesus." (Galatians 3:28)
  • Jesus treated women as disciples and witnesses: Mary Magdalene first told the others he had risen (John 20:18).
Mission 4Read the source

Equality and leadership

"But I don't permit a woman to teach, nor to exercise authority over a man, but to be in quietness." (1 Timothy 2:12)

  • Compare Galatians 3:28: "neither male nor female".
  • Your spec pairs 1 Timothy 2:11–12 with Galatians 3:27–29.

Q1 What does 1 Timothy 2:12 forbid?

Show answer

Women teaching or having authority over men.

Q2 How might an Anglican who supports women priests read it?

Show answer

As advice for one church in Paul's time, while Galatians 3:28 states the lasting principle.

Mission 4Diversity

Women's ministry in the churches

  • Catholic: only men can be ordained priests. John Paul II (Ordinatio Sacerdotalis, 1994): the Church has no authority to ordain women. Reasons: Jesus chose twelve men as apostles; the priest acts in the person of Christ.
  • Catholics stress this is about roles, not worth; women lead as religious sisters, teachers, theologians; since 2021 they can be formally installed as readers and acolytes.
  • Orthodox: male priesthood, for similar reasons of tradition.
  • Church of England: women priests since 1994, bishops since 2014 (first, Libby Lane, 2015); in 2025 Sarah Mullally was chosen as the first woman Archbishop of Canterbury.
  • Methodists, Salvation Army, URC, many Baptists: women ministers; the Salvation Army from its start (Catherine Booth).
  • Some evangelical churches: women lead in many ways but not as senior pastors (1 Timothy 2:12).

Mission 4Diversity

Islam

  • Equal reward for faith and good deeds: Qur'an 33:35 lists "men who surrender unto Allah, and women who surrender" through ten pairs (Qur'an 33:35, Pickthall).
  • Qur'an 4:1 (one soul), 40:40 (whoever does right, "whether male or female"), 2:228 (rights similar, and a "degree").
  • Worship: men and women pray in separate rows or rooms; women may lead other women; most scholars teach only men lead mixed congregational prayer. Attending the mosque is a duty for men at Jumu'ah, optional for women.
  • Equality in action: Khadijah, successful businesswoman; Aisha, a leading scholar; Fatima al-Fihri founded al-Qarawiyyin in Fez (859), often called the oldest university still working.
  • Many Muslims say discrimination against women comes from culture, not Islam; groups such as Muslim Women's Network UK campaign for women's rights.
Mission 4Diversity

Judaism

  • Both made in God's image (Genesis 1:27).
  • Orthodox: separate seating (mechitzah); only men count for a minyan (prayer quorum of ten) and lead public prayer; rabbis are men. Women's learning and leadership roles are growing.
  • Reform and Liberal: mixed seating; women rabbis and cantors. First woman rabbi: Regina Jonas (Germany, 1935); in Britain, Jackie Tabick (1975).
Mission 4Diversity

Hindu, Buddhist and Sikh views

  • Hindus: the same atman (self) is in all, so all deserve equal regard. The Gita's true yogi "sees the Life-Soul resident / In all things living" (Bhagavad Gita 6.29, Arnold).
  • God is worshipped as female too (Durga, Lakshmi, Saraswati). Women increasingly train as priests; some temples still limit women's roles.
  • Your spec compares Manusmriti 2.67–68 and 5.148–155 (women under men's protection, marriage as women's sacrament) with 9.72–81 and 9.192–194 (marriage duties; a woman's own property).
  • Buddhists: women and men can both reach enlightenment; the Buddha founded an order of nuns.
  • Theravada hierarchy: monks, nuns, laymen, laywomen; nuns keep more rules than monks (311 vs 227 in the Pali Vinaya). The Theravada nuns' line died out; it was revived from 1998, though not all accept it. Mahayana nuns' lines continue.
  • Sikhs: Guru Granth Sahib 473 (paraphrase): we are born of woman, and through woman the family goes on; so why call her inferior, from whom kings are born? Women may lead any service, read the Guru Granth Sahib and serve as granthi.
  • Divergent Sikh practice: few women in senior Sikh bodies; women are not permitted to perform kirtan inside the Harmandir Sahib. Bibi Jagir Kaur became the first woman president of the SGPC (1999).

Mission 4Evaluate

"Religions treat women as equals."

  • Reasons for: equal image of God (Genesis 1:27), Galatians 3:28, Qur'an 33:35, GGS 473, Soma Sutta; women priests, rabbis and leaders in many traditions; faiths campaign against sex discrimination.
  • Reasons against: male-only priesthood (Catholic, Orthodox) and rabbinate (Orthodox Judaism); separate roles in mosques; some texts (1 Timothy 2:12; some Manusmriti verses); practice often lags behind teaching.
  • Another view: equal worth is taught everywhere; the debate is whether different roles are fair.
  • Judge it by: Equal worth or equal roles? Teaching or practice? Which tradition or group?
Mission check

?On one street, the Anglican church's vicar is a woman, while the Catholic parish next door has only ever had male priests. Both churches teach that women and men are made equally in God's image. What best explains the difference?

Faiths teach equal worth and oppose sex discrimination; they disagree about whether every role in worship should be open to both, and why.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Catholics (Humanae Vitae): only natural family planning, for serious reasons; most other Christians accept contraception within marriage.
  • Islam ('azl hadith) and Judaism (first mitzvah, pill preferred) allow it with conditions; Hindus, Buddhists and Sikhs mostly accept it but reject methods that act after conception.

Mission 2

  • UK law allows civil partnership (2005) and same-sex marriage (2014; NI 2020); faiths can opt in or out.
  • Traditional Christians, Catholics, Orthodox Jews and most Muslims forbid same-sex acts while calling for respect; liberal Christians, Reform Jews, many Western Buddhists and Humanists accept same-sex relationships.
  • In Dharmic traditions there is no single view: householder dharma, the Third Precept and the 2005 Akal Takht edict are key points.

Mission 3

  • Equal in God's image (Genesis 1:27); Ephesians 5 read as headship (complementarian) or mutual submission (egalitarian).
  • Other faiths: Qur'an 2:228 and 4:34; Proverbs 31; Manusmriti 3.55–60; Sigalovada Sutta.
  • Sikhs: GGS 788, one light in two bodies.

Mission 4

  • Prejudice is an attitude; discrimination an action; sex discrimination is illegal under the Equality Act 2010.
  • Christians: Genesis 1:27, Galatians 3:28; Catholic and Orthodox churches ordain only men; Anglicans and most Protestants ordain women.
  • Other faiths: Qur'an 33:35; Orthodox vs Reform Judaism; Gita 6.29; Buddhist nuns; practice does not always match teaching.
  • Sikhs: GGS 473; women may lead any service.
