Paper 2: Externally Set Assignment

This mini-lesson walks you through Paper 2 (the Externally Set Assignment, or "ESA") of Edexcel International GCSE Art & Design: what the external theme is, how to use the preparatory period, how to produce a timed final outcome, and how all four assessment objectives are marked.

Work through each screen, answer the questions as you go (some are wordy, some ask you to plan) and collect ⭐ stars. Press Start when you're ready.

What Paper 2 is

The Externally Set Assignment

Paper 2 is your second and final component. Pearson (Edexcel) releases an external theme — a paper of starting points that is the same for every candidate worldwide. You respond to it over a preparatory period, then produce a final outcome in a set number of hours of supervised, timed work.

  • The theme is set by the exam board, not your teacher.
  • Your response must be your own personal interpretation of that theme.
  • You submit preparatory studies and a resolved personal response (the final piece).

Big idea: the ESA is not a mystery exam question you sit cold — you get weeks to prepare, then the final piece is made under exam conditions.

The marking framework

The four Assessment Objectives

Every mark in the ESA comes from the same four AOs as the Portfolio. Learn them as a cycle — you weave them together, not tick them off in a line:

AO1 · AO2 · AO3 · AO4Develop · Refine (experiment with media) · Record · Present a personal response
  • AO1 — Develop: research the theme; explore sources, ideas and artists/designers.
  • AO2 — Refine: experiment with media, materials, techniques and processes, reviewing as you go.
  • AO3 — Record: record ideas, observations and insights (drawings, photos, annotation).
  • AO4 — Present: present a personal, meaningful response that realises your intentions (the final piece).

Each AO is equally weighted. A brilliant final piece with weak research still loses a quarter of the marks.

Reading the theme

Interpreting the external theme

The exam paper gives a theme (a single word or phrase) plus some suggested starting points — quotations, images or ideas to spark you. You may pick a starting point or read the theme in your own way.

  • Don't take it literally. A theme like "Fragments" isn't only broken glass — think memory, ruins, torn paper, mosaic, split identity.
  • List meanings, moods and materials the word suggests before committing.
  • Choose an angle that lets you show all four AOs and play to your strengths.

Tip: the theme is a launchpad, not a rulebook. Examiners reward a personal, considered interpretation over the obvious first idea.

The ESA timeline

How the assignment runs

The ESA has a clear shape: the theme is released, you have a preparatory period, then you make the final outcome under supervised, timed conditions:

Note: there is no fixed length to the preparatory period, but the final outcome is made under a set, supervised time and can only use your prep — you can't start new source material in the exam.

Prep · Step 1

Preparatory period — research (AO1)

Open your response by developing ideas. This is where AO1 lives:

  • Mind-map the theme, then focus on one or two strands.
  • Study relevant artists or designers — analyse how they work, not just what they made.
  • Gather your own first-hand sources (photos, objects, places) wherever you can.

Depth beats breadth: one artist studied and genuinely applied to your work beats five copied images.

Prep · Step 2

Preparatory period — experiment (AO2)

Now refine by experimenting. AO2 rewards trying media and reviewing what happens:

  • Test a range of media, materials, techniques and processes tied to your idea.
  • Annotate each experiment: what worked, what you'll take forward, what to drop.
  • Let experiments steer your final outcome — this is refining, not decorating.

Watch out: a wall of untested media with no comment is not AO2. The review is what earns the marks.

Prep · Step 3

Preparatory period — record (AO3)

Recording runs right through the prep. AO3 is about capturing ideas and observations that are relevant to your intentions:

  • Observational drawings and studies from your own sources.
  • Photographs, quick sketches, colour notes and written annotation.
  • Record as you go, so your thinking is visible — not a tidy write-up at the end.

Key: recording must be purposeful — tied to the theme and to where your final piece is heading.

Prep · Step 4

Plan the final outcome first

Before any timed session, your prep should point clearly at a final piece. Planning is part of AO2/AO4 and stops you improvising under pressure:

  • Produce design ideas / mock-ups of possible final outcomes.
  • Decide the size, media, layout and colours in advance.
  • Do a rehearsal or study of the trickiest part so nothing is new on the day.

Misconception: "I'll decide the final piece when the timed session starts." No — you plan the final piece during prep. The timed session is for making it, not inventing it.

The supervised time

Managing the timed sessions

The final outcome is produced in a set number of hours of supervised work. It may be split across a few sessions in a short window, but it is your unaided work under exam conditions:

  • Bring only your prep — no new research or outside help during the timed time.
  • Pace yourself: block out the time (e.g. background → main forms → detail → check).
  • Work from your plan — resist changing the whole idea mid-session.

Tip: leave a little time at the end to resolve — step back, adjust and finish edges rather than starting something new.

The personal response (AO4)

A resolved, personal final outcome

The final piece is your AO4 — you present a personal, meaningful response that realises your intentions and pulls together your prep:

  • It should connect clearly to your research, experiments and recording.
  • "Resolved" means finished and considered — not rushed or abandoned.
  • It should look and feel like yours: a genuine personal interpretation of the theme.

Remember: AO4 is one of four equal AOs. A strong final piece and strong prep is how you reach the top band.

Planning your time

Time management across the ESA

Because the prep period has no fixed length, it is easy to run out of time. Plan backwards from the timed sessions:

  • Front-load research (AO1) so you commit to an idea early.
  • Leave clear time to experiment and record (AO2/AO3) before planning the final piece.
  • Finish design ideas and a rehearsal before the supervised time begins.

Rule of thumb: if you're still choosing your idea the week before the timed session, you've spent too long exploring and too little refining.

Clear these up

Common ESA misconceptions

Three myths trip students up every year — don't be one of them:

  • ❌ "Only the final piece is marked." ✅ Your prep is assessed too — AO1–AO3 mostly come from prep.
  • ❌ "The ESA is worth different AOs from the Portfolio." ✅ It uses the same four AOs, each equally weighted.
  • ❌ "I'll plan the final piece in the timed session." ✅ Plan it during prep; the timed time is for making it.

One more: the ESA is 50% of the whole qualification — it matters exactly as much as your coursework, so treat the prep seriously.

The ESA in a nutshell

What it is: Paper 2, the Externally Set Assignment — a board-set theme, prep period, then a timed final outcome.

Weighting: 50% of the qualification (Portfolio is also 50%).
