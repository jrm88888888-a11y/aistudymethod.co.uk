Structure

Pop song structures

Pop songs are built from repeating sections, most often in a verse–chorus design:

  • Intro · verse (tells the story) · pre-chorus (builds) · chorus (the hook, highest energy) · bridge / middle 8 (contrast) · outro.
  • The 12-bar blues underpins rock and roll and much early pop.
  • Strophic songs repeat the same music for each verse; a hook is the catchy repeated idea that sells the song.

The band

The band and its instruments

A standard pop/rock line-up divides into the rhythm section and the front line:

  • Drum kit and bass guitar lock the groove; the bass also outlines the harmony.
  • Rhythm guitar or keyboards supply chords; lead guitar plays riffs, fills and solos.
  • Lead and backing vocals carry melody and harmony; effects (distortion, delay, wah) shape the timbre.

Backline = the amplified instruments (guitars, bass, keys) behind the singer.

Harmony & melody

Harmony, riffs and melody

Pop harmony is usually diatonic and repetitive:

  • Common loops such as I–V–vi–IV; power chords (root plus fifth) in rock.
  • Riffs — short repeated instrumental patterns; ostinato and hooks drive memorability.
  • Melodies often use the pentatonic or blues scale, with blue notes (flattened 3rd, 5th, 7th) and vocal melisma or falsetto.

Rhythm & groove

Rhythm and groove

Groove is everything in pop:

  • The backbeat — snare accent on beats 2 and 4 — drives most pop and rock.
  • Syncopation (off-beat accents) and anticipation give music its drive.
  • Straight, swung or dotted feels change the character; the tempo and drum pattern define the style.

Production

Production and technology

In pop, the recording studio is an instrument:

  • Multitracking layers separately recorded parts; overdubbing adds to an existing take.
  • Effects: reverb, delay/echo, chorus, compression, auto-tune.
  • Sampling, looping, drum machines and MIDI sequencing shape modern production.
  • Panning and mixing place sounds in the stereo field and balance the track.

Styles & appraising

Style development and appraising

Pop is a family of styles that evolve — rock and roll, Motown and soul, rock, funk, disco, synth-pop, hip-hop and beyond. Rather than memorising one set song, learn to place an extract by its features:

  • Instrumentation and timbre (guitars vs synths vs samples).
  • Groove, tempo and production sound.
  • Harmonic and melodic vocabulary (blues, pentatonic, loops).

Appraising tip: describe how the arrangement builds across the song — instruments added, texture thickened, dynamics raised into each chorus.

Recap

The big ideas to carry into the exam

Structure: intro–verse–pre-chorus–chorus–bridge–outro; 12-bar blues; hooks

The band: rhythm section (bass + drums) vs front line (lead, vocals)

Harmony/melody: diatonic loops, power chords, riffs, pentatonic/blues scales

Rhythm: backbeat, syncopation, groove and feel

Production: multitracking, reverb, panning, sampling, MIDI

Skill: place a style from sound; describe how a track builds
