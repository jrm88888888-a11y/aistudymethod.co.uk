Health and Safety

This mini-lesson covers health and safety — the difference between a hazard and a risk, the 5-step risk assessment, the hierarchy of control, PPE, COSHH and safety signs.

Overview

Designing and working safely

Health and safety is a legal duty, governed by laws such as the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 (HASAWA), the COSHH Regulations 2002, PUWER (work equipment) and RIDDOR (reporting incidents). Employers and employees share responsibility, and products must be safe for users too.

Hazard vs risk

Hazard vs risk

  • A hazard is anything with the potential to cause harm (a spinning blade, a solvent, a trailing cable).
  • Risk is the likelihood that the hazard causes harm, combined with the severity of that harm.

A sharp knife locked in a drawer is a hazard with low risk; the same knife left on a busy bench is a hazard with high risk.

Risk assessment

The 5 steps of a risk assessment

The HSE sets out five steps:

  • 1. Identify the hazards.
  • 2. Decide who might be harmed and how.
  • 3. Evaluate the risks and decide on control measures.
  • 4. Record findings and put controls in place.
  • 5. Review and update regularly.

Hierarchy

The hierarchy of control

Control measures are ranked most-to-least effective — always try the top first:

  • Eliminate the hazard entirely.
  • Substitute it for something safer.
  • Engineering controls — guards, extraction, interlocks.
  • Administrative — training, signage, safe procedures.
  • PPE — the last resort, protecting the individual only.

COSHH & PPE

COSHH and PPE

  • COSHH requires employers to assess, control, label and store hazardous substances (solvents, dusts, adhesives) and provide safety data.
  • Hazard pictograms (CLP/GHS) warn of toxic, corrosive, flammable or harmful substances.
  • PPE includes eye protection, gloves, ear defenders, respirators, aprons and safety footwear.
Safety signs

Safety signs — colour and shape

  • Prohibition — red circle with a diagonal bar ("do not…").
  • Mandatory — blue circle ("you must…", e.g. wear eye protection).
  • Warning — yellow triangle ("caution…").
  • Safe condition — green square/rectangle (fire exit, first aid).

Duties

Employer & employee duties

Under HASAWA, employers must provide a safe workplace, training, equipment and risk assessments. Employees must take reasonable care of themselves and others, use equipment correctly and not misuse safety provisions. Serious incidents are reported under RIDDOR.

Recap

The big ideas to know

Law: HASAWA 1974, COSHH 2002, PUWER, RIDDOR — shared employer/employee duties.

Hazard vs risk: hazard = potential to harm; risk = likelihood × severity.

Assessment: 5 steps — identify, who, evaluate/control, record, review.

Hierarchy: eliminate > substitute > engineering > administrative > PPE (last).

Signs: red prohibition · blue mandatory · yellow warning · green safe condition.

Risk rating: likelihood × severity.
