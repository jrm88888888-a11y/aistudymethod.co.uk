CCEA A-level Biology · Biochemistry, Genetics & Evolution

Biochemistry, Genetics & Evolution
Now in 4 mini-lessons.

This topic is taught in the mini-lessons below. Each mission starts from a question: make a guess, learn the idea, then check it. About 20 minutes each. No login.

A2 2 - 5.1 Respiration

  1. Your cells already get energy from sugar. Why do they spend it through a different molecule first?
  2. Burning sugar releases all its energy in one flash. How do your cells release it slowly enough to use?
  3. Pyruvate enters the mitochondrion with three carbon atoms. Where do they go, and what does the cell gain?
  4. Why would a cell pump protons uphill across a membrane, only to let them flood straight back?
  5. When oxygen runs out, muscles make lactate and yeast makes alcohol. What do they gain from it?

Start this mini-lesson →

A2 2 - 5.3 DNA as the genetic code

  1. DNA has only four letters. How can a gene spell out a protein built from twenty different building blocks?
  2. Your DNA never leaves the nucleus. So how do its instructions reach the place where proteins are made?
  3. A ribosome never sees a single amino acid's name. So how does it build the right protein?
  4. Nearly every cell in your body carries the same genes. So why doesn't a skin cell act like a nerve cell?
  5. Identical twins start life with the same genes. Why do they become more different as they age?

Start this mini-lesson →

A2 2 - 5.5 Genes and patterns of inheritance

  1. Two tall pea plants can produce a short one. Where was the short plant hiding?
  2. Cuttings from one plant share every gene. So why don't they all grow to the same height?
  3. If two genes sit on the same chromosome, can they ever be separated?
  4. Why do some inherited conditions turn up far more often in sons than in daughters?
  5. How can one gene decide whether another gene gets any say at all?

Start this mini-lesson →

A2 2 - 5.6 Population genetics

  1. A harmful allele can hide in healthy carriers. How can you work out how many people carry it?
  2. Change one letter in a gene: does the protein always change?
  3. Can a living thing ever mutate because it needs to?
  4. One extra chromosome can change a whole body. So how do some plants thrive with doubled sets?
  5. If no animal can change its own genes, how can a whole population change over time?
  6. Why doesn't selection always push a population in the same direction?
  7. Nobody designs a drug-resistant germ, so how do whole populations of them appear so quickly?
