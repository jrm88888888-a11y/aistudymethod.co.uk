Mission 1

Acids and esters: the structures

  • Carboxylic acid: –COOH. Ethanoic acid, CH3COOH.
  • Ester: –COO– with a carbon on both sides. RCOOR′.
  • An ester is an acid with the O–H hydrogen replaced by an alkyl group.
  • Name: alkyl (from the alcohol) + -oate (from the acid).
  • Methyl ethanoate, CH3COOCH3. Ethyl propanoate, CH3CH2COOCH2CH3.
Mission 1

Weak acids that still make salts

  • CH3COOH ⇌ CH3COO⁻ + H⁺: only partly dissociates. A weak acid.
  • Alkali: CH3COOH + NaOH → CH3COONa + H2O
  • Carbonate: 2CH3COOH + Na2CO3 → 2CH3COONa + CO2 + H2O
  • Hydrogencarbonate: CH3COOH + NaHCO3 → CH3COONa + CO2 + H2O
  • Salts are named -oate: sodium ethanoate.
Mission 1

The carbonate test

  • Add sodium carbonate: effervescence.
  • Eduqas test: sodium hydrogencarbonate: effervescence.
  • The gas turns limewater milky: CO2.
  • Alcohols, aldehydes, ketones and esters: no fizzing.
Quick check
?0.010 mol of ethanoic acid reacts with excess sodium carbonate. What amount of carbon dioxide forms?
Mission 1

Order of acidity

  • Carboxylic acid > phenol > water > alcohol.
  • The more stable the negative ion left behind, the stronger the acid.
  • RCOO⁻: charge delocalised over two O atoms. Most stable.
  • C6H5O⁻: charge partly delocalised into the ring.
  • RO⁻: the alkyl group pushes electrons onto the O. Least stable.
  • Show it: NaHCO3 fizzes only with the acid.
  • Show it: NaOH(aq) dissolves phenol (sodium phenoxide forms); ethanol shows no change.
  • Show it: sodium fizzes more vigorously with water than with ethanol.
Mission 1

Making carboxylic acids

  • Oxidise a primary alcohol or an aldehyde: reflux with excess acidified K2Cr2O7 (orange → green).
  • CH3CH2OH + 2[O] → CH3COOH + H2O
  • Side-chain oxidation: reflux methylbenzene with alkaline KMnO4, then acidify.
  • C6H5CH3 + 3[O] → C6H5COOH + H2O (benzoic acid)
Mission 1

Reducing, chlorinating and decarboxylating acids

  • LiAlH4 in dry ether reduces the acid to a primary alcohol.
  • CH3COOH + 4[H] → CH3CH2OH + H2O
  • NaBH4 is not strong enough to reduce acids.
  • PCl5: CH3COOH + PCl5 → CH3COCl + POCl3 + HCl (steamy fumes).
  • Decarboxylation: heat the sodium salt with soda lime. One carbon is lost.
  • CH3COONa + NaOH → CH4 + Na2CO3
Quick check
?Propanoic acid, CH3CH2COOH, is treated with LiAlH4 in dry ether, followed by dilute acid. What is the organic product?
Mission 1

Esterification

  • Carboxylic acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water
  • CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH ⇌ CH3COOCH2CH3 + H2O
  • Catalyst: concentrated H2SO4. Warm or reflux.
  • Reversible: an excess of one reactant gives a higher yield.
  • The water forms from the acid's –OH and the alcohol's H.
  • Esters also form from acyl chlorides and alcohols (see Mission 2).
Mission 1

Preparing a liquid ester

  • Example: ethyl ethanoate, b.p. 77 °C.
  • 1. Reflux ethanoic acid, ethanol and a few drops of conc H2SO4 (anti-bumping granules).
  • 2. Distil off the crude ester.
  • 3. Shake with Na2CO3(aq) in a separating funnel to remove acid; vent (CO2 forms). Run off the lower aqueous layer: the ester is the upper layer.
  • 4. Dry with anhydrous calcium chloride.
  • 5. Redistil, collecting at about 74–79 °C.
Quick check
?Propanoic acid is warmed with methanol and a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid. What ester forms?
Mission 1

Hydrolysis: splitting the ester

  • Acid: reflux with dilute H2SO4(aq). Reversible; does not go to completion.
  • CH3COOCH2CH3 + H2O ⇌ CH3COOH + CH3CH2OH
  • Alkali: reflux with NaOH(aq). Goes to completion.
  • CH3COOCH2CH3 + NaOH → CH3COONa + CH3CH2OH
  • The carboxylate ion cannot react with the alcohol, so it does not reverse.
  • Add strong acid afterwards to get the free carboxylic acid.
Quick check
?Methyl propanoate, CH3CH2COOCH3, is heated under reflux with sodium hydroxide solution. What are the products?
Mission check

?Two bottles, X and Y, each hold a liquid of formula C3H6O2. One is propanoic acid; the other is methyl ethanoate.
X: boils at 141 °C, fizzes with sodium carbonate.
Y: boils at 57 °C, smells fruity, no fizzing.
Which statement is correct?

Replacing the acid's O–H hydrogen with a carbon group removes both its acidity and its hydrogen bonding. What is left is a volatile, fruity ester.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Why does ethanoyl chloride fume in moist air, when ethanoic acid just sits in the bottle?

Make a guess: Why does ethanoyl chloride fume in moist air, when ethanoic acid just sits in the bottle?

Mission 2

Meet the acid derivatives

  • Swap the –OH of a carboxylic acid for another group: an acid derivative.
  • Acyl chloride: –COCl. Ethanoyl chloride, CH3COCl. Name ends -oyl chloride.
  • Amide: –CONH2. Ethanamide, CH3CONH2 (a primary amide).
  • N-substituted amides, e.g. CH3CONHCH3, form from amines.
Mission 2

Making an acyl chloride

  • Start from the carboxylic acid. Dry conditions: water destroys the product.
  • PCl5 (from Mission 1): CH3COOH + PCl5 → CH3COCl + POCl3 + HCl
  • Steamy fumes of HCl. The acyl chloride is separated by distillation.
  • SOCl2 also works: CH3COOH + SOCl2 → CH3COCl + SO2 + HCl
Mission 2

Why so reactive?

  • The carbonyl carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms: O and Cl.
  • So it is strongly δ+: a target for any nucleophile with a lone pair.
  • Chlorine then leaves easily as Cl⁻: a good leaving group.
  • So acyl chlorides react at room temperature, often violently. No catalyst.
  • In a carboxylic acid the C is less δ+, and –OH is a poor leaving group.
  • So, the opening question: water vapour in the air hydrolyses it. The "fumes" are HCl.
Quick check
?Ethanoyl chloride reacts with cold water within seconds. Ethanoic acid can stay in water for years without changing. Which explains the difference?
Mission 2

Four nucleophiles, one pattern

  • The nucleophile swaps in for Cl. HCl is the other product.
  • Water → carboxylic acid: CH3COCl + H2O → CH3COOH + HCl
  • Alcohol → ester: CH3COCl + CH3CH2OH → CH3COOCH2CH3 + HCl. Fast, and not reversible.
  • Ammonia → primary amide:
  • CH3COCl + 2NH3 → CH3CONH2 + NH4Cl. The second NH3 takes up the HCl.
  • Primary amine → N-substituted amide: CH3COCl + 2CH3NH2 → CH3CONHCH3 + CH3NH3⁺Cl⁻
  • Phenol → ester: CH3COCl + C6H5OH → CH3COOC6H5 + HCl (phenyl ethanoate). Carboxylic acids barely esterify phenol.
  • So acids → acid chlorides → esters or amides: a quick two-step route.
Quick check
?Ethanoyl chloride is added dropwise to an excess of ethylamine, CH3CH2NH2. Which two products form?
Mission check

?Chloroethane, CH3CH2Cl, and ethanoyl chloride, CH3COCl, are each added to cold water. What do you see, and why?

Two electronegative atoms on one carbon make acyl chlorides the most reactive acid derivatives. Even water, a weak nucleophile, attacks at once.

Mission 3

Mission 3: How can a chemist make a molecule's carbon chain exactly one atom longer?

Make a guess: How can a chemist make a molecule's carbon chain exactly one atom longer?

Mission 3

Building the skeleton

  • Most organic reactions only swap functional groups. The carbon chain stays the same length.
  • To make a longer chain, you need a reaction that forms a new C–C bond.
  • The trick: a carbon nucleophile, a carbon atom with a lone pair or a δ− charge.
  • Cyanide, ⁻:C≡N, is the simplest: it adds one carbon.
Mission 3

Cyanide + halogenoalkane

  • Reagent: KCN in ethanol, heat under reflux.
  • CH3CH2Br + CN⁻ → CH3CH2CN + Br⁻
  • Product: propanenitrile. The chain grows from 2 C to 3 C.
  • Naming: count the C≡N carbon in the chain. CH3CH2CN is propanenitrile.
  • Mechanism: nucleophilic substitution. Lone pair on the C of CN⁻ attacks the δ+ C; the C–Br electrons go to Br.
  • KCN is highly toxic. Acid turns cyanide into HCN gas, so cyanide and acid only ever meet in a fume cupboard, in small amounts, under controlled conditions.
Mission 3

Cyanide + carbonyl

  • Aldehydes and ketones add cyanide across C=O: hydroxynitriles.
  • Reagent: HCN, with KCN to supply CN⁻.
  • CH3CHO → CH3CH(OH)CN, 2-hydroxypropanenitrile. Chain: 2 C → 3 C.
  • Mechanism: nucleophilic addition. Product is racemic when the new C is chiral.
  • Hazards: HCN is a very toxic gas, and cyanide salts are toxic too. Fume cupboard, gloves, small amounts.
Quick check
?1-Bromobutane is heated under reflux with potassium cyanide in ethanol. What is the organic product?
Mission 3

Nitrile → carboxylic acid

  • Acid hydrolysis: heat under reflux with dilute HCl.
  • CH3CH2CN + 2H2O + HCl → CH3CH2COOH + NH4Cl
  • The C≡N carbon becomes the –COOH carbon.
  • Alkaline hydrolysis: reflux with NaOH(aq). CH3CH2CN + NaOH + H2O → CH3CH2COONa + NH3
  • This gives the salt. Add dilute acid to release the carboxylic acid.
  • From a hydroxynitrile: CH3CH(OH)CN → CH3CH(OH)COOH, 2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid).
Mission 3

Nitrile → amine

  • Reduction with LiAlH4 in dry ether: CH3CH2CN + 4[H] → CH3CH2CH2NH2
  • Product: propylamine (propan-1-amine), one carbon longer than bromoethane.
  • Unlike ammonia + halogenoalkane, this gives only the primary amine: no further substitution.
Mission 3

Amides and nitriles

  • Acid → amide: make the acyl chloride (e.g. with PCl5), then add concentrated ammonia. CH3COCl + 2NH3 → CH3CONH2 + NH4Cl
  • Amide → nitrile: heat with phosphorus(V) oxide, P4O10, which removes water. CH3CONH2 → CH3CN + H2O
  • This route keeps the same number of carbons. Cyanide + halogenoalkane adds one.
  • Amides hydrolyse like nitriles. Acid gives RCOOH + NH4⁺; alkali gives RCOO⁻ + NH3.
Quick check
?2-Hydroxypropanenitrile, CH3CH(OH)CN, is heated under reflux with dilute hydrochloric acid. What is the organic product?
Mission check

?Bromomethane, CH3Br, has one carbon. Which route turns it into ethylamine, CH3CH2NH2?

Cyanide adds the extra carbon, then you choose what the C≡N becomes: an amine, or (by hydrolysis) an acid.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Acid → ester: the O–H hydrogen is replaced by an alkyl group. No acidity, no hydrogen bonding between molecules.
  • Esters form with an acid catalyst (reversible) and hydrolyse with acid (reversible) or alkali (complete, gives the salt).

Mission 2

  • Acyl chlorides: δ+ carbonyl C and Cl⁻ leaving group. Water → acid, alcohol → ester, NH3 → amide, amine → N-substituted amide; HCl formed.

Mission 3

  • CN⁻ adds one carbon: with halogenoalkanes (substitution) or carbonyls (addition).
  • Nitriles can be reduced to amines.
  • Nitriles can be hydrolysed to carboxylic acids.
