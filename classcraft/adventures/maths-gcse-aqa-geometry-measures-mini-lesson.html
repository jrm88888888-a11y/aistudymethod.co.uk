Geometry · angles and polygons

Triangles and quadrilaterals

  • Angles in a triangle sum to 180°. Proof: draw a line through one corner parallel to the opposite side; the two alternate angles and the corner angle make a straight line.
  • An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
  • Isosceles: two equal sides and two equal base angles. Equilateral: three equal sides, every angle 60°.
  • Angles in a quadrilateral sum to 360°: a diagonal splits it into two triangles.

Geometry · angles and polygons

Angles in polygons

  • From one corner, an nn-sided polygon splits into n−2n − 2 triangles, so the interior angle sum is
(n−2)×180°(n−2) × 180°
  • The exterior angles of any polygon sum to 360° (walk round it and you turn once).
  • In a regular polygon all angles are equal: exterior angle =360°÷n= 360° ÷ n, and interior angle =180°−= 180° − exterior angle (they sit on a straight line).

Mission check

?Why can identical regular pentagons never tile a floor?

Tiles meeting at a point must have angles adding to exactly 360°. A regular pentagon's interior angle is 180°−360°÷5=108°180° − 360° ÷ 5 = 108°, and 360÷108=3.33…360 ÷ 108 = 3.33…, so they never fit. Only equilateral triangles (60°), squares (90°) and regular hexagons (120°) tile on their own.

Geometry · bearings and constructions

Three-figure bearings

  • A bearing is an angle measured clockwise from north, written with three figures: 065°, 008°, 300°.
  • "The bearing of BB from AA": stand at AA, face north, turn clockwise until you face BB.
  • Compass points as bearings: N 000°, NE 045°, E 090°, SE 135°, S 180°, SW 225°, W 270°, NW 315°.
  • The back bearing (the bearing of AA from BB) differs by exactly 180°, because the north lines at AA and BB are parallel.
Worked example Geometry · bearings and constructions

  2. 2
    300° is more than 180°, so subtract 180°: 300°−180°=120°300° − 180° = 120°
  3. 3
    Write each answer with three figures
Answer
AA from BB is 245°; PP from QQ is 120°
⚠️ Watch out
"the bearing of AA from BB" means you stand at BB. Reading the question the wrong way round gives the original bearing back.

Geometry · bearings and constructions

Scale drawings: fixing a point from two bearings

  • A scale: "1 cm represents 1 km", or 1 : 100,000.
  • Two bearings fix a point: draw a north line at each known point, measure each bearing clockwise, draw the lines. The point is where they cross.
  • Measure the drawn distance; convert with the scale.
  • Triangle from three sides: draw one side; from its ends draw arcs of the other two lengths; join to the crossing. Leave the arcs.

Geometry · bearings and constructions

Ruler-and-compass constructions

  • Perpendicular bisector of ABAB: equal arcs (radius over half ABAB) from AA and BB cross on both sides; join the crossings. Its points are equidistant from AA and BB.
  • Angle bisector: an arc from the vertex cuts both arms; equal arcs from those points cross; join the vertex to the crossing.
  • Perpendicular from PP to a line: an arc from PP cuts the line twice; bisect that segment. The perpendicular distance is the shortest.
  • Leave every arc visible.
Geometry · bearings and constructions

Loci

  • A locus is the set of all points that obey a rule.
  • Fixed distance from a point: a circle. "Within 5 m" means inside it.
  • Fixed distance from a line segment: two parallel lines joined by semicircles (a racetrack shape).
  • Equidistant from two points: the perpendicular bisector. "Closer to AA than BB" is the side containing AA.
  • Equidistant from two lines that meet: the angle bisector.
  • Shade the region that obeys every condition, and label it.

?Tower BB is 8 km due east of tower AA. A fire is on a bearing of 045° from AA and 315° from BB. Where is the fire?

Areas of triangles, parallelograms and trapezia

  • Perimeter is the total distance round the edge (cm). Area is the space inside (cm²).

Measures · perimeter and area

Circles

  • Circumference C=πd=2πrC = π d = 2π r. Area A=πr2A = π r². Learn both.
  • Halve a diameter before using πr2π r²: squaring the diameter is the commonest slip.
  • Exact answers are left in terms of ππ: for r=3r = 3, A=9πA = 9π cm².
  • A semicircle's perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter.

?Each lane on a track is 1.2 m wide, so lane 2's bends have a radius 1.2 m bigger than lane 1's. The straights are the same. About how much further is one lap in lane 2?

A lap is two straights plus two semicircular bends, which together make a full circle. Moving out one lane adds 2π×1.2≈7.52π × 1.2 ≈ 7.5 m per lap, whatever the size of the track, so each outer lane starts further round to make every runner's distance the same.

Measures · volume and surface area

Prisms and cylinders

  • A prism has the same cross-section all the way through. A cuboid has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.
  • Volume of any prism:
V=area of cross-section×lengthV = area of cross-section × length
  • Cuboid: V=l×w×hV = l × w × h. Cylinder: the cross-section is a circle, so V=πr2hV = π r² h.
  • Units: cm³ or m³. 1 litre =1000= 1000 cm³, and 1 m³ =1000= 1000 litres.

Pyramids, cones and spheres

  • Pyramid: V=31​×base area×hV = 1/3 × base area × h.
  • Cone: V=31​πr2hV = 1/3π r² h, a third of the cylinder with the same base and height. Curved surface =πrl= π r l, where the slant height ll comes from Pythagoras: l2=r2+h2l² = r² + h².

Geometry · Pythagoras' theorem

The hypotenuse and the theorem

  • In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse. It is always the longest side.
  • Pythagoras' theorem: if the shorter sides are aa and bb and the hypotenuse is cc, then
a2+b2=c2a² + b² = c²
  • The theorem is about the squares of the sides, not the sides themselves.
  • It works the other way too: sides 6, 8 and 10 give 36+64=10036 + 64 = 100, so that triangle is right-angled. Sides 6, 8 and 11 do not.

Geometry · Pythagoras' theorem

Finding a shorter side

  • Rearrange the theorem: a2=c2−b2a² = c² − b².
  • Square, subtract, square-root. Subtract because the hypotenuse is the biggest square.
  • Check: your answer must be shorter than the hypotenuse.
  • First spot the hypotenuse: it is opposite the right angle, wherever the triangle is turned.

Geometry · Pythagoras' theorem

Pythagoras in 3-D (Higher)

  • To find the longest diagonal of a cuboid, use Pythagoras twice.
  • First the diagonal across the base: d2=a2+b2d² = a² + b². Then the base diagonal and the height make a right angle: g2=d2+c2g² = d² + c².
  • Together: g2=a2+b2+c2g² = a² + b² + c², so g=a2+b2+c2​g = √(a² + b² + c²).
  • A box 1 m by 0.8 m by 0.5 m deep: g=1+0.64+0.25​=1.89​≈1.37g = √(1 + 0.64 + 0.25) = √1.89 ≈ 1.37 m.

Geometry · trigonometry

Naming the sides and the three ratios

  • Mark the angle θθ you know or want. The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle, the opposite faces θθ, the adjacent is next to θθ (and not the hypotenuse).

Geometry · trigonometry

Angles of elevation and depression

  • The angle of elevation is measured up from the horizontal to the line of sight.
  • The angle of depression is measured down from the horizontal.
  • Looking up from A to B and down from B to A gives equal angles (alternate angles between parallel horizontals).
  • Measure from where your eye is: add the eye height back on at the end.

Finding the centre of a rotation

  • The centre is the one point that does not move: an invariant point.
  • Every other point stays the same distance from the centre: CP=CP′CP = CP'.
  • So the centre lies on the perpendicular bisector of PP′PP'. Two such bisectors cross at the centre.
  • On a grid: pin tracing paper at a guess and turn it. If the shape lands on the image, that is the centre.

Geometry · congruence and similarity

Congruent or similar?

  • Congruent shapes are the same shape and size: one fits exactly on the other (after turning or flipping).
  • Similar shapes are the same shape: corresponding angles are equal and every length is multiplied by the same scale factor kk.
  • k=corresponding length on the originallength on the new shape​k = (length on the new shape)/(corresponding length on the original)
  • A 4 by 6 rectangle and a 6 by 9 rectangle are similar (k=1.5k = 1.5); a 4 by 6 and a 6 by 8 are not.
  • Triangles with equal angles are similar. Give a reason for each equal angle.
Geometry · congruence and similarity

Proving triangles congruent

  • Two triangles are congruent if they match by one of these conditions:
  • SSS: three sides equal. SAS: two sides and the angle between them.
  • ASA: two angles and a corresponding side. RHS: right angle, hypotenuse and one other side.
  • AAA proves only similarity; two sides and a non-included angle is not a condition.
  • In a proof, list the three equal facts with a reason for each, then name the condition.

Geometry · circle theorems

The parts of a circle

  • Radius: centre to the circle. Diameter: a chord through the centre, twice the radius.
  • Chord: a straight line joining two points on the circle. Tangent: a straight line touching the circle at one point.
  • Arc: part of the circumference. Sector: the slice between two radii and an arc. Segment: the region between a chord and an arc.
  • Higher: a quadrilateral with all four corners on a circle is a cyclic quadrilateral.
Geometry · circle theorems

Angle in a semicircle (Higher)

  • AB is a diameter and C is any point on the circle. Then angle ACB=90∘ACB = 90°: the angle in a semicircle is a right angle.
  • Why: join C to the centre O. OA=OB=OCOA = OB = OC (radii), so triangles AOCAOC and BOCBOC are isosceles.
  • Call the base angles a,aa, a and b,bb, b. Triangle ABCABC gives 2a+2b=180∘2a + 2b = 180°, so a+b=90∘a + b = 90°.
  • Exam reason: "angle in a semicircle is 90°".

Geometry · circle theorems

Tangents and chords (Higher)

  • A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
  • Two tangents from the same outside point are equal in length (by RHS congruence), so they make a kite with the two radii.
  • The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord. The converse holds too: the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to it.

Geometry · circle theorems

Four angle theorems (Higher)

  • The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc.
  • Angles in the same segment are equal (they stand on the same chord, on the same side).
  • Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.
  • Alternate segment theorem: the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment.
  • In an exam, quote the theorem in words for every step. "Circle theorem" alone earns no reason mark.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Every angle needs a reason: angles at a point (360°), on a straight line (180°), vertically opposite, alternate, corresponding and co-interior angles.
  • Triangle 180°, quadrilateral 360°, any polygon (n−2)×180°(n−2) × 180°; exterior angles of a polygon always sum to 360°.
  • For a regular polygon, find the exterior angle 360°÷n360° ÷ n first, then interior =180°−= 180° − exterior.
  • Bearings are measured clockwise from north and written with three figures; a back bearing differs by 180°.
  • Two bearing lines from two known points cross at exactly one place: that is how you fix a position on a scale drawing.
  • Loci: fixed distance from a point is a circle; equidistant from two points is the perpendicular bisector; equidistant from two lines is the angle bisector.
  • Area: rectangle lwlw, triangle 21​bh1/2bh, parallelogram bhbh, trapezium 21​(a+b)h1/2(a+b)h, always with the perpendicular height.
  • Circle: C=2πr=πdC = 2π r = π d, A=πr2A = π r²; give exact answers in terms of ππ when asked; split compound shapes into parts.
  • Sector =360θ​= θ/360 of the circle for both arc length and area.
  • Prism volume == cross-section area ×× length; cylinder V=πr2hV = π r² h; 1 litre =1000= 1000 cm³.
  • Surface area is the sum of the faces: use a net; a cylinder's curved surface unrolls to a 2πr2π r by hh rectangle.
  • Pyramid and cone volumes are a third of the matching prism or cylinder; sphere V=34​πr3V = 4/3π r³, SA=4πr2SA = 4π r².
  • The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and is the longest side: a2+b2=c2a² + b² = c².
  • Hypotenuse: square, add, square-root. Shorter side: square, subtract, square-root.
  • Higher: in a cuboid use Pythagoras twice, g=a2+b2+c2​g = √(a² + b² + c²).
  • Label hyp, opp and adj from the angle; choose the ratio with SOH CAH TOA.
  • Unknown side: write the ratio and rearrange. Unknown angle: use sin−1sin⁻¹, cos−1cos⁻¹ or tan−1tan⁻¹.
  • Know the exact values for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°; in 3-D, find a right-angled triangle inside the solid (Higher/Extended).
  • Label each side with the same letter as its opposite angle.
  • Sine rule for a side–angle pair; cosine rule for two sides with the included angle, or three sides.
  • Area =21​absinC= 1/2 absin C with the angle between the two sides.
  • Describe fully: reflection (mirror line equation), rotation (centre, angle, direction), translation (column vector), enlargement (centre, scale factor).
