Mission 1

Mission 1: How can a French sentence with "ne" in it mean "only" instead of "not"?

Make a guess: How can a French sentence with "ne" in it mean "only" instead of "not"?

Mission 1

ne … que: only

  • Looks negative, but means only.
  • Je n'ai que dix euros. – I only have ten euros.
  • que goes right before the thing that is limited.
  • que → qu' before a vowel: Elle ne parle qu'anglais. – She only speaks English.
  • seulement means the same: J'ai seulement dix euros.
  • Watch out: it isn't a true negative, so the article stays. Il ne mange que des légumes. – He only eats vegetables (not que de légumes).
  • Your turn: what does Nous n'avons qu'une heure mean?
    Show answer

    We only have one hour.

Quick check
?What does this mean? Je n'ai qu'une sœur.
Mission 1

ne … pas encore: not yet

  • ne + verb + pas encore = not yet.
  • Il n'est pas encore là. – He isn't here yet.
  • Mon petit frère ne sait pas encore lire. – My little brother can't read yet.
  • Short answer: Tu es prêt ? — Pas encore ! – Are you ready? — Not yet!
  • encore on its own = again, still. pas encore = not yet.
  • de after it, like pas: Je n'ai pas encore de portable. – I haven't got a mobile yet.
  • Your turn: what does Ma sœur ne travaille pas encore mean?
    Show answer

    My sister isn't working yet.

  • Now say it: "She (elle) isn't ready yet."
    Show answer

    Elle n'est pas encore prête.

Mission 1

ne … ni … ni: neither … nor

  • ne before the verb; ni before each item. No pas.
  • Je n'aime ni le thé ni le café. – I like neither tea nor coffee.
  • le, la, les stay.
  • un, une, du, de la, des disappear:
  • Je n'ai ni frère ni sœur. – I have neither a brother nor a sister.
  • Elle ne mange ni viande ni poisson. – She eats neither meat nor fish.
  • Watch out: Je n'aime pas ni le thé ni le café. ni replaces pas.
  • Your turn: what does Je n'ai ni portable ni ordinateur mean?
    Show answer

    I have neither a mobile nor a computer.

  • Now say it: "I like neither football nor tennis."
    Show answer

    Je n'aime ni le foot ni le tennis.

Quick check
?What does this mean? Mon père ne boit ni thé ni café.
Mission 1

ne … aucun(e): not a single

  • aucun + masculine noun, aucune + feminine noun. Always singular.
  • Je n'ai aucune idée. – I have no idea.
  • Il n'y a aucun problème. – There's no problem at all.
  • Je n'ai aucun ami ici. – I don't have a single friend here.
  • Stronger than pas de.
  • Your turn: what does Il n'y a aucune voiture dans la rue mean?
    Show answer

    There isn't a single car in the street.

  • Now say it: "There's no bus." (le bus)
    Show answer

    Il n'y a aucun bus.

Mission 1

Nobody and nothing as the subject

  • An earlier lesson: Je ne vois personne. – personne comes after the verb (I don't see anybody).
  • As the subject: Personne ne / Rien ne + verb. No pas.
  • Personne ne parle. – Nobody is speaking.
  • Rien ne change. – Nothing changes.
  • Personne ne veut sortir. – Nobody wants to go out.
  • The verb is in the il form: Personne ne sait. – Nobody knows.
  • Watch out: Personne ne parle pas → Personne ne parle.
  • Your turn: what does Rien ne m'intéresse mean?
    Show answer

    Nothing interests me.

Mission 2

Mission 2: How do you say "I had already eaten" in French, and why does it matter when you tell a story?

Make a guess: How do you say "I had already eaten" in French, and why does it matter when you tell a story?

Mission 2

One step further back

  • English: "I had eaten" = one past action before another.
  • French: imperfect of avoir or être + past participle.
  • j'avais mangé – I had eaten
  • elle était partie – she had left
  • Quand je suis arrivé(e), le film avait commencé. – When I arrived, the film had started.
  • First the film started; later I arrived.
  • Your turn: what does j'avais fini mean?
    Show answer

    I had finished.

Mission 2

With avoir

  • Compare: j'ai joué – I played; j'avais joué – I had played.
  • Same participles as the perfect, irregulars too: j'avais fait, j'avais vu, j'avais pris, j'avais eu, j'avais été.
  • Negative: ne … pas goes round avais: Je n'avais pas mangé. – I hadn't eaten.
  • Watch out: only avoir changes. j'avais mangeais → j'avais mangé.
  • Your turn: which one is "we had finished": nous avons fini or nous avions fini ?
    Show answer

    nous avions fini

Mission 2

With être

  • Verbs that take être in the perfect take être here too: étais, était, étions, étiez, étaient.
  • J'étais allé(e). – I had gone.
  • Elle était partie. – She had left.
  • Nous étions arrivé(e)s. – We had arrived.
  • Ils étaient tombés. – They had fallen.
  • Same agreement as the perfect: -e for feminine, -s for plural.
  • Reflexive verbs too: Je m'étais levé(e) tôt. – I had got up early.
  • Watch out: j'avais allé → j'étais allé(e).
  • Your turn: what does Elles étaient sorties mean?
    Show answer

    They (f) had gone out.

Mission 2

Where it helps

  • Two past actions? The earlier one goes in the pluperfect.
  • Quand nous sommes arrivés, le train était déjà parti. – When we arrived, the train had already left.
  • J'étais triste parce que j'avais perdu mon portable. – I was sad because I had lost my phone.
  • déjà sits between the two parts: avait déjà commencé.
  • Watch out: English sometimes skips "had" ("I was sad because I lost my phone"). French normally keeps the pluperfect for the earlier action.
  • Your turn: what does Le film avait déjà commencé mean?
    Show answer

    The film had already started.

Quick check

Mission 3

Mission 3: Why does French say "I live here since three years" when English says "I have been living here"?

Make a guess: Why does French say "I live here since three years" when English says "I have been living here"?

Mission 3

depuis + present

  • English: I have been living here for three years.
  • French: present + depuis: J'habite ici depuis trois ans.
  • Why the present? The action is still going on now.
  • depuis = "for" (a length of time) or "since" (a starting point).
  • J'apprends le français depuis quatre ans. – I have been learning French for four years.
  • Il pleut depuis lundi. – It has been raining since Monday.
  • Je joue du piano depuis 2020. – I have been playing the piano since 2020.
  • Watch out: don't copy the English past. J'ai habité ici depuis trois ans. → J'habite ici depuis trois ans.
  • Your turn: what does Elle attend depuis vingt minutes mean?
    Show answer

    She has been waiting for twenty minutes.

  • Your turn: "I have been living in London for two years."
    Show answer

    J'habite à Londres depuis deux ans.

Mission 3

Asking; depuis or pendant?

  • Depuis quand … ? – Since when … ?
  • Depuis combien de temps … ? – How long … (so far)?
  • Tu apprends le français depuis combien de temps ? – How long have you been learning French?
  • pendant = for a finished period: J'ai habité à Lyon pendant trois ans. – I lived in Lyon for three years (I don't now).
  • Still true now → present + depuis. Finished → perfect + pendant.
  • Your turn: which one means "I lived in Paris for two years (then I moved)"? J'habite à Paris depuis deux ans or J'ai habité à Paris pendant deux ans ?
    Show answer

    J'ai habité à Paris pendant deux ans.

Quick check
?Choose the best French for: "I have been learning French for four years."
Mission 3

depuis + imperfect

  • Move the whole thing into the past: imperfect + depuis = "had been …ing".
  • J'attendais le bus depuis dix minutes quand il est arrivé. – I had been waiting for the bus for ten minutes when it arrived.
  • Elle habitait à Nice depuis un an. – She had been living in Nice for a year.
  • Pattern: still going on now → present. Still going on then → imperfect.
  • Your turn: "We had been playing for an hour."
    Show answer

    Nous jouions depuis une heure.

Mission 3

venir de: just done

Recognise only

  • English: I have just eaten.
  • French: venir (present) + de + infinitive: Je viens de manger.
  • Word for word: "I come from eating".
  • venir: je viens, tu viens, il / elle / on vient, nous venons, vous venez, ils / elles viennent.
  • Le film vient de commencer. – The film has just started.
  • Nous venons d'arriver. – We have just arrived. (de → d' before a vowel)
  • Watch out: infinitive, not past participle. Je viens de mangé → Je viens de manger.
  • Watch out: je vais manger = I'm going to eat; je viens de manger = I've just eaten.
  • Your turn: what does Elle vient de partir mean?
    Show answer

    She has just left.

Quick check
?What does this mean? Nous venons de finir nos devoirs.
Mission 3

être en train de: right in the middle

  • The French present already means "I am doing": je mange – I eat / I'm eating.
  • To stress right now, in the middle of it: être en train de + infinitive.
  • Je suis en train de faire mes devoirs. – I'm busy doing my homework.
  • Ils sont en train de manger. – They're in the middle of eating.
  • Watch out: French has no "-ing" tense. Je suis faisant → Je fais or Je suis en train de faire.
  • Your turn: what does Mon père est en train de faire la cuisine mean?
    Show answer

    My dad is busy cooking.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Why does French say "without to pay" where English says "without paying"?

Make a guess: Why does French say "without to pay" where English says "without paying"?

Mission 4

After a preposition: the infinitive

  • English: preposition + -ing. without paying.
  • French: preposition + infinitive. sans payer.
  • pour + infinitive = (in order) to: Je travaille le samedi pour gagner de l'argent. – I work on Saturdays to earn money.
  • sans + infinitive = without …ing: Il est entré sans payer. – He went in without paying.
  • Watch out: English -ing is not French -ant. J'aime chanter. – I like singing. Je lis. – I am reading.
  • Your turn: "She went out without eating."
    Show answer

    Elle est sortie sans manger.

Mission 4

avant de: before doing

  • "Before …ing" = avant de + infinitive.
  • Avant de partir, je ferme la porte. – Before leaving, I close the door.
  • Je fais mes devoirs avant de regarder la télé. – I do my homework before watching TV.
  • de → d' before a vowel: avant d'aller au collège – before going to school.
  • Watch out: don't drop de. avant partir → avant de partir.
  • Both actions are done by the same person.
  • Your turn: "Before going out, I eat."
    Show answer

    Avant de sortir, je mange.

Quick check
?Choose the best French for: "She left without saying goodbye."
Mission 4

après avoir: after doing

  • "After …ing" = après avoir + past participle.
  • English says "after eating" or "after having eaten". French always says "after having eaten".
  • Après avoir mangé, j'ai regardé un film. – After eating, I watched a film.
  • Après avoir fini mes devoirs, je sors. – After finishing my homework, I go out.
  • The action after après happened first.
  • Watch out: write après avoir mangé, not après manger.
  • Watch out: no agreement with avoir: Après avoir mangé, Léa est sortie.
  • Your turn: "After buying a ticket, I took the train."
    Show answer

    Après avoir acheté un billet, j'ai pris le train.

Mission 4

après être: verbs that take être

  • Verbs that take être in the perfect use après être + past participle.
  • The participle agrees with the subject, as in the perfect tense.
  • Après être arrivée à la gare, Léa a acheté un billet. – After arriving at the station, Léa bought a ticket. (arrivée: Léa is feminine)
  • Après être rentrés, nous avons mangé. – After getting home, we ate. (rentrés: masculine plural)
  • Your turn: what does Après être sorties, elles sont allées au cinéma. mean? Who went out?
    Show answer

    After going out, they went to the cinema. sorties: a group of girls or women.

?Choose the best French for: "We eat before watching the film."

Mission 5

Mission 5: How does French say "the cake is eaten by the dog", and why do the French often avoid it?

Make a guess: How does French say "the cake is eaten by the dog", and why do the French often avoid it?

Mission 5

Who does the action?

Recognise only

  • Active: the subject does the action. Le chien mange le gâteau. – The dog eats the cake.
  • Passive: the subject has the action done to it. Le gâteau est mangé par le chien. – The cake is eaten by the dog.
  • Formula: subject + être (present) + past participle + par + doer.
  • par = by.
  • The doer is often left out: Le gâteau est mangé. – The cake is eaten.
  • Your turn: what does Le déjeuner est préparé par mon père. mean?
    Show answer

    Lunch is prepared by my dad.

Mission 5

Mission 5

Passive or perfect?

Recognise only

  • être + participle also makes the perfect tense of a small group of verbs (aller, arriver, partir, sortir, tomber…).
  • Elle est sortie. – She went out. → perfect: she did it.
  • Elle est invitée. – She is invited. → passive: someone does it to her.
  • Test 1: is the verb in the être group? If not, it's passive.
  • Test 2: can you add par + a doer? Elle est invitée par Paul. ✓ → passive.
  • Your turn: perfect or passive? La porte est fermée par le professeur.
    Show answer

    Passive: fermer takes avoir in the perfect, and there is a doer (par le professeur).

Mission 5

Find the doer

Recognise only

  • To understand a passive sentence, ask three questions.
  • 1: Who does it? → the person after par.
  • 2: What has it done to it? → the subject.
  • 3: When? → look at être: est/sont = is/are.
  • La fête est organisée par les élèves. → doers: the pupils · thing: the party · now.
  • English can also say it the active way: The pupils organise the party.
  • Your turn: who does the correcting? Les devoirs sont corrigés par le professeur.
    Show answer

    The teacher.

Recognise only

Mission 5

The French shortcut: on

  • French uses the passive less than English.
  • Instead, it often uses on + active verb.
  • On parle français au Québec. – French is spoken in Quebec.
  • Ici, on vend des billets. – Tickets are sold here.
  • On m'a invité(e) à la fête. – I was invited to the party.
  • Watch out: English "I was given a present" can't be copied. Use on: On m'a donné un cadeau.
  • Your turn: what does Ici, on recycle le papier. mean?
    Show answer

    Paper is recycled here.

Mission 6

Mission 6: Why does French sometimes change a verb's form just because of the words in front of it?

Make a guess: Why does French sometimes change a verb's form just because of the words in front of it?

Mission 6

Spot the change

Recognise only

  • Je fais mes devoirs. – I do my homework.
  • Il faut que je fasse mes devoirs. – I have to do my homework.
  • Same verb (faire), same person (je), different form.
  • This form is the subjunctive. Certain expressions + que trigger it.
  • You only need to recognise it: spot it, find the verb, understand the sentence.
  • Good news: the subjunctive rarely changes the English meaning.

Mission 6

Infinitive or que?

Recognise only

  • Same person → infinitive. Je veux partir. – I want to leave.
  • Different person → que + subjunctive. Je veux que tu partes. – I want you to leave.
  • English "want someone to…" = vouloir que + subjunctive.
Same personDifferent person
Je veux sortir. – I want to go out.Mes parents veulent que je sorte. – My parents want me to go out.
avant de partir – before leaving (see Mission 4)avant que tu partes – before you leave

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • ne … que = only (articles stay); ne … pas encore = not yet; ne … ni … ni = neither … nor (un/du/des drop).
  • ne … aucun(e) + singular noun = not a single.
  • Personne ne / Rien ne + verb, with no pas.

Mission 2

  • "had done" = avais / était … + past participle.
  • Same auxiliary and agreement rules as the perfect.
  • Use it for the earlier of two past actions.

Mission 3

  • depuis + present = have been …ing (still going on). Finished → perfect + pendant.
  • venir de + infinitive = have just done.
  • être en train de + infinitive = to be busy doing, right now.

Mission 4

  • pour, sans, avant de + infinitive: pour gagner, sans payer, avant de partir.
  • après avoir + past participle: après avoir mangé.
  • après être + participle that agrees: après être arrivée.
  • en + -ant (from the nous form) = while / by / on …ing: en faisant.

Mission 5

  • Passive = être + past participle (agrees with the subject) + par + doer.
  • être + participle with a non-être verb (préparer, inviter) = passive, not perfect.
  • French often uses on instead: On parle français ici.
  • Recognise: the tense is in être: a été (was), était (was), sera (will be).

Mission 6

  • Triggers + que (il faut que, vouloir que, bien que, pour que, je ne pense pas que) → subjunctive.
  • Regular: ils stem + -e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent; irregular: sois, aie, aille, fasse, puisse.
  • Same person → infinitive; different person → que + subjunctive.
