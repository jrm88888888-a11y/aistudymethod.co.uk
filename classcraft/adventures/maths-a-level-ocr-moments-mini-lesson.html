Mechanics · moments

The moment of a force (A-level only)

  • The moment of a force about a point is its turning effect:
moment=F×dmoment=F × d
  • dd is the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force. Units: N m.
  • State the sense: clockwise or anticlockwise. A force through the point has zero moment.
  • A uniform rod's weight acts at its midpoint; a light rod has no weight.
  • A uniform rectangular lamina's weight acts at its centre (point of symmetry).
Quick check
?A light rod ABAB of length 4 m is horizontal with AA on the left. Forces act perpendicular to it: 10 N upwards at AA, 6 N downwards 1 m from AA, and 8 N downwards at BB. Find the total moment about the midpoint MM.
Mechanics · moments

Rigid bodies in equilibrium

  • A rigid body is in equilibrium when the resultant force is zero and the resultant moment about any point is zero.
  • So: total up = total down, and clockwise moments = anticlockwise moments about any point you choose.
  • Take moments about the point where an unknown force acts: that force has no moment there.
  • A beam on the point of tilting about one support has zero reaction at the other support.

Mission check

?A seesaw is pivoted at its centre. A 30 kg child sits 2.4 m from the pivot. Where must a 60 kg adult sit for it to balance?

Moments about the pivot must balance: 30g×2.4=60g×d30g × 2.4=60g × d, so the adult sits 1.2 m from the pivot, half as far as the child. The plank's own weight acts at the pivot, so it has no moment there.

Mechanics · moments: ladders and hinges

The moment of a force at an angle (A-level only)

  • Moment = force × perpendicular distance to its line of action.
  • If a force FF acts at the end of a rod of length ll, at angle θθ to the rod, its moment about the other end is
FlsinθFlsin θ
  • Equivalent view: split FF into FsinθFsin θ perpendicular to the rod (moment Fsinθ×lFsin θ × l) and FcosθFcos θ along the rod (no moment, as its line passes through the point).

Mechanics · moments: ladders and hinges

Modelling a ladder

  • Smooth wall: reaction SS is perpendicular to the wall (horizontal). No friction there.
  • Rough ground: normal reaction RR upwards and friction FF along the ground, towards the wall (it stops the foot sliding out).
  • A uniform ladder's weight acts at its midpoint.
  • Method: resolve vertically and horizontally, then take moments about the foot, which removes RR and FF.
  • About to slip: limiting equilibrium, F=μRF=μ R. Otherwise F≤μRF≤μ R.

Mechanics · moments: ladders and hinges

Hinged rods

  • A hinge can push or pull in any direction. Model its force by two unknown components, horizontal XX and vertical YY.
  • Take moments about the hinge first: XX and YY drop out, leaving one unknown (often a tension).
  • Then resolve horizontally and vertically to find XX and YY.
  • Magnitude of the hinge force: X2+Y2​√(X²+Y²); direction from tan−1XY​tan⁻¹Y/X.

Mission check

?A person climbs a ladder resting on rough ground against a smooth wall. Why is it more likely to slip when they are near the top?

Vertically, RR is the total weight wherever you stand, so the most friction available, μRμ R, is fixed. But moments about the foot show the wall reaction, and so the friction needed, grows as you climb. Near the top the friction needed can exceed μRμ R, and the ladder slips.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Moment = force × perpendicular distance, with a sense (clockwise or anticlockwise), in N m.
  • Equilibrium of a rigid body: resultant force zero and resultant moment zero about any point; take moments about an unknown force's point.
  • On the point of tilting, the reaction at the other support is zero.
  • The moment of a force uses the perpendicular distance to its line of action: FlsinθFlsin θ for a force at angle θθ to a rod.
  • Ladder: smooth wall gives a horizontal reaction; rough ground gives RR and friction; take moments about the foot, then resolve.
  • Hinge: unknown components XX and YY; take moments about the hinge first.
