Mission 1

Your papers

  • Component 1: Foundational Catholic Theology (Origins and Meaning · Good and Evil).
  • Component 2: Applied Catholic Theology (Life and Death · Sin and Forgiveness).
  • Each: 1 hour 30 minutes, 90 marks + 6 for SPaG (spelling, punctuation, grammar and specialist terms).
  • Two compulsory questions per component, one per theme, each worth 45 marks. Parts are worth (a) 2 · (b) 5 · (c) 8 · (d) 15, and some parts are set twice (e.g. (b)(i) and (b)(ii)).
  • Answer from Catholic Christianity, with Judaism where appropriate and non-religious views where a question asks.
  • About a mark a minute: roughly 45 minutes per theme.
Mission 1

Four parts, four tasks

  • (a) 2 marks: "What is meant by…?" Define a key concept. AO1.
  • (b) 5 marks: Describe. Accurate detail. AO1.
  • (c) 8 marks: Explain. Reasons, developed, with sources. AO1.
  • (d) 15 marks: a statement to discuss. Analyse and evaluate different views; reach a judgement. AO2.
  • AO1 and AO2 carry equal weight across the paper. Half your marks are for argument.
Mission 1

(a): define the concept

  • Each theme has eight concepts you must be able to explain and apply.
  • E.g. Origins and Meaning: creation ex nihilo, evolution, imago Dei, inspiration, omnipotence, revelation, stewardship, transcendence.
  • Two marks: a clear, accurate meaning + an example or a short development.
  • Do not use the word to define itself ("Commitment means being committed").
  • Learn all eight for every theme. They are guaranteed vocabulary for (b), (c) and (d) too.
Quick check
?"What is meant by free will?" [2] Which answer would earn both marks?
Mission 1

(b): describe

  • 5 marks, AO1: what a belief, teaching or practice is, in accurate detail.
  • Go step by step: what happens, who, what is said, what objects or words are used.
  • Use specialist terms correctly.
  • You do not need reasons or evaluation; "why" belongs in (c), judgement in (d).
  • Two or three well-detailed points beat a long list of one-word facts.
Mission 1

(c): explain

  • 8 marks, AO1: why believers think or act as they do.
  • Each point: belief → reason → development → source of wisdom and authority.
  • Watch the wording: "Explain from two religious traditions…", "Explain Christian teaching about…". Answer exactly that.
  • Show diversity where it exists (e.g. Catholic and Protestant; Orthodox and Reform).
  • Name Catholic sources: the Bible, the Catechism, Vatican II documents, papal encyclicals, theologians such as St Augustine.
Mission 1Read the source

What counts as a source

Your specification says sacred texts and sources of wisdom might include, for example:

> "the Bible; extracts from the documents of Vatican II or other ecumenical councils, extracts from Papal encyclicals and exhortations, extracts from the work of key theologians and thinkers such as St Augustine of Hippo as well as the views of past and current philosophers"

Q1 Name two kinds of Catholic source besides the Bible.

Show answer

Council documents, e.g. Vatican II; papal encyclicals and exhortations; theologians such as Augustine.

Q2 A student cites a papal encyclical in a (c) answer on the environment. Is that a valid source?

Show answer

Yes: papal encyclicals are listed, e.g. Laudato Si', 2015.

Mission 1

(d): discuss the statement

  • 15 marks, AO2. Usually: "Discuss this statement showing that you have considered more than one point of view."
  • Some (d) questions add: refer to religious and non-religious beliefs, such as those held by Humanists and atheists. Then you must.
  • Shape: one side with reasons and sources → the other side with reasons and sources → weigh them → justified conclusion.
  • Evaluate, don't just list: "This is a strong argument because…", "This is weaker because…".
  • SPaG: 6 marks on a marked (d) answer. Spell key terms correctly.
Mission 1Worked answer

One weighed (d) paragraph

Statement: "Free will explains why there is evil in the world." Discuss.

  • For: St Augustine (354–430) taught that evil is not a thing God made but a privation (a lack) of good; it entered the world through the misuse of free will. So God is not its author.
  • Weigh: this works well for moral evil: murder and war are human choices.
  • Against: it seems weaker for natural evil: an earthquake is not anyone's choice.
  • Reply: some Christians link natural evil to the Fall; others argue a world with fixed natural laws is needed for real choices.
  • Non-religious view: many Humanists and atheists say suffering has natural causes and needs no religious explanation.
  • Conclusion: "Free will explains some evil well, but…" Then judge, and give your reason.
Quick check
?A (d) question says: "You must refer to religious and non-religious beliefs, such as those held by Humanists and atheists." A student gives only Catholic and Protestant views, both well argued. What is the main problem?
Mission 1

Common slips

  • (a) an example with no meaning; or the meaning of a different concept.
  • (b) explaining "why" at length instead of describing "what".
  • (c) describing, with no reasons; or no source of wisdom and authority.
  • (d) one side only; no evaluation; a conclusion that ignores the reasons given.
  • Timing: an 8-mark essay for a 2-mark definition. Let the marks set the length.
Quick check
