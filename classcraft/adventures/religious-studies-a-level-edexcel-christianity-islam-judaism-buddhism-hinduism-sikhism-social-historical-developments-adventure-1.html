Edexcel A-level Religious Studies · Christianity / Islam / Judaism / Buddhism / Hinduism / Sikhism: Social & Histori

Christianity / Islam / Judaism / Buddhism / Hinduism / Sikhism: Social & Histori
Now in 6 mini-lessons.

This topic is taught in the mini-lessons below. Each mission starts from a question: make a guess, learn the idea, then check it. About 20 minutes each. No login.

Paper 4A Buddhism – 4 Social and historical developments

  1. If calling a Buddha's name could carry you to a better world, would that still be the Buddha's path?
  2. If awakening cannot be put into words, why does Zen have so many books?
  3. Can you be fully ordained as a Buddhist while keeping your job, your home and your partner?
  4. The Buddha left his wife and newborn son. What do Buddhists teach about marriage, family and sex?
  5. Can a woman become a Buddha? Why do Buddhist texts give different answers?
  6. Why can a woman become a fully ordained nun in Taiwan, but not easily in Thailand?

Paper 4B Christianity – 4 Social and historical developments (part 1 of 2)

  1. Galileo, Darwin, the Big Bang: did each discovery push God out, or change how Christians read the Bible?
  2. Is belief in God just wishful thinking, and would society be happier without it?
  3. If fewer Britons go to church, why are new churches springing up in cafés, homes and woods?
  4. In the 1960s, priests in Latin America said God takes the side of the poor. What did they mean?
  5. Liberation theologians borrowed ideas from Marx, an atheist. How did the Catholic Church respond?
  6. Christians believe Jesus came to save all people. But Jesus was a man: can a male saviour save women?

Start this mini-lesson →

Paper 4C Hinduism – 4 Social and historical developments; Paper 4C Hinduism – 5 Works of scholars

  1. Under British rule, two Hindu reformers both rejected image worship. Why did they still disagree?
  2. How did a temple priest who spoke no English shape how the world sees Hinduism?
  3. What makes someone a Hindu: belief, birth, or loving India as a holy land?
  4. Gandhi said his politics came from his religion. Was he a saint, a politician, or both at once?
  5. Ancient hymns, ancient astronomers, a universe billions of years old: does science fit Hindu belief?
  6. If only one reality exists, why do we see a world full of separate things?
  7. Is the soul one with God, part of God, or forever different from God?

Start this mini-lesson →

Paper 4D Islam – 4 Social and historical developments (part 1 of 2)

  1. The Prophet died in 632. Who should lead next, and why does the answer still divide Muslims?
  2. For 1,300 years there was a caliph, then in 1924 there wasn't. What did a caliph actually do?
  3. Most Shi'a Muslims say a guide chosen by God has been hidden for over 1,000 years. Who leads them now?
  4. A battle lost in 680 is mourned by millions every year. Why do Shi'a Muslims keep remembering it?
  5. Is Sufism a separate branch of Islam, or the heart of the same faith?

Start this mini-lesson →

Paper 4E Judaism – 4 Social and historical developments (part 1 of 2)

  1. How have Jews in Britain stayed Jewish for 350 years without either hiding or shutting themselves away?
  2. For most of 1,900 years few Jews lived in the Land of Israel. Why did it stay at the centre of Judaism?
  3. In a society where religion seems optional, should Jews live apart, blend in, or something in between?
  4. Judaism does not call wealth a sin. So why does it set so many limits on what you do with it?
  5. The Jewish calendar counts about 5,800 years since creation. Science says 13.8 billion. Must one be wrong?

Start this mini-lesson →

Paper 4F Sikhism – 4 Social and historical developments (part 1 of 2)

  1. A Sikh carries a blade to school and marries outside the faith. Who decides what is allowed?
  2. Guru Nanak said no one knows when the world began. Does that leave room for the Big Bang?
  3. When do Sikhs believe a war can be righteous, and why do they honour those who refused to fight back?
  4. Why do some Sikhs want a country of their own, while many others reject the idea?
