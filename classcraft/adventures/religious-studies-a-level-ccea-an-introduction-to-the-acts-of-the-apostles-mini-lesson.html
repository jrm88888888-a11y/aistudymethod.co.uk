An Introduction to the Acts of the Apostles

Area 1: Textual Studies · Unit AS 2 (AS level)

This mini-lesson covers Unit AS 2: An Introduction to the Acts of the Apostles — the context and authorship of Acts, the early Christian community, the beginnings of the church at Pentecost, and the missionary work of Peter, Stephen, Philip and Paul.

Learning focus

The context of Acts

The Acts of the Apostles traces the Gospel's journey from Jerusalem, into Judea and Samaria, and out into the Gentile world. On authorship, candidates weigh internal and external evidence, including the famous 'we' passages where the narrator suddenly writes as an eyewitness travelling with Paul.

  • Date: arguments for an early, intermediate or late composition;
  • Purpose: defence of the faith, unity of Jew and Gentile, and a record of the Spirit's work;
  • Characteristics: speeches, summaries, travel narrative; and
  • Challenges to the traditional authorship and to the book's historical accuracy.
Learning focus

The early Christian community

Acts paints an idealised picture of the first believers:

  • Church life and organisation — a fellowship who shared their goods (2:42–47, 4:23–37);
  • Discipline — the sobering story of Ananias and Sapphira (5:1–11); and
  • the role of miracles in aiding the spread of the Gospel (3:1–10, 5:12–16).

Evaluation: is Luke describing history, or presenting an ideal of the Spirit-filled community to inspire his readers?

Learning focus

The beginnings of the church

Two foundational events launch the mission:

  • the Ascension (1:1–11), where the risen Jesus commissions the mission before departing; and
  • Pentecost (2:1–13), the coming of the Holy Spirit that empowers the apostles and births the Church.

These scenes are theologically decisive: the Spirit, not human effort alone, drives the Gospel outward.

Learning focus

Peter and John

Peter emerges as the leading voice:

  • his Pentecost speech and its dramatic response (2:14–41);
  • his teaching and miracles (3:1–26, 4:1–22, 5:12–42); and
  • the apostles' attitude towards opposition — bold witness in the face of the authorities.

Stephen and Philip

As the church grows, tension between Hellenist and Hebrew believers leads to the appointing of the seven helpers (6:1–7).

  • Stephen's speech and martyrdom (6:8–8:1) — often read as a Hellenist defence, and the first Christian martyrdom; and
  • Philip's evangelising activity (8:4–40), carrying the Gospel into Samaria and beyond.

Stephen and Philip mark the Gospel's decisive move beyond Jerusalem.

Learning focus

Peter and the Gentiles

A turning point comes when Peter takes the Gospel to Cornelius, a Gentile (10:1–48):

  • the implications for continuing missionary work — the Gospel is for all;
  • Peter's report to the Jerusalem church (11:1–18); and
  • his miraculous escape from prison (12:1–19).

Learning focus

Paul the Apostle

Paul dominates the second half of Acts:

  • his conversion and its significance (9:1–19);
  • the three missionary journeys that spread the Gospel across the Roman world; and
  • his speeches — notably at Pisidian Antioch (to a synagogue) and at the Areopagus in Athens (to Greek philosophers), which reveal his skill in addressing different audiences.

Recap

The big ideas to know

Context: Authorship debate, the 'we' passages, date, purpose and challenges to accuracy

Early community: Sharing of goods (2:42–47); discipline (Ananias and Sapphira, 5:1–11); miracles

Beginnings: Ascension (1:1–11) and Pentecost (2:1–13) — the Spirit births the Church

Peter: Pentecost speech; Cornelius (10:1–48); escape from prison (12:1–19)

Stephen and Philip: The Seven (6:1–7); first martyrdom; mission beyond Jerusalem

Paul: Conversion (9:1–19); three missionary journeys; speeches at Pisidian Antioch and Athens
