Dialogues · what is this unit?

Connecting ethics and religion

This dialogue unit asks how far ethical studies (Component 1) and your chosen religion (Component 2) connect, agree and challenge one another.

  • Which ethical theories does the tradition accept or reject?
  • Does secular ethics challenge religious morality — or depend on it?
  • How does the religion respond on applied issues (life, death, animals)?

Apply this to your religion; examples below are illustrative.

Dialogues · normative theories

Which theories fit the tradition?

  • Natural law (Aquinas) grew within Christianity and fits religions that see purpose in creation — but sits awkwardly with non-theistic Buddhism.
  • Situation ethics (Fletcher) is a Christian theory of agape — welcomed by some believers, resisted by others as too relativist.
  • Virtue ethics resonates with traditions that stress character (e.g. the virtues, the Eightfold Path, the mitzvot as habit-forming).
  • Kant and utilitarianism are secular theories a tradition may accept in part or criticise.

Dialogues · divine command

Divine command and the Euthyphro dilemma

A central question: is something good because God commands it (divine command theory), or does God command it because it is good?

This is Plato's Euthyphro dilemma:

  • If good is whatever God commands, morality looks arbitrary (could cruelty become good?).
  • If God commands things because they are good, then goodness seems independent of God.

Traditions reply variously — e.g. that God's nature is good, so the dilemma is a false one.

Dialogues · autonomy & meta-ethics

Autonomy, meta-ethics and challenge

  • Autonomy: Kant grounds morality in reason, not divine command — a challenge to traditions that root ethics in revelation. Some argue religious ethics can be heteronomous (obeying an external will); others reply that following God is following the good.
  • Meta-ethics: emotivism (Ayer) reduces "murder is wrong" to feeling — a challenge to religions that assert objective, God-grounded moral truths. Religious ethics tends toward moral realism.
  • Secular vs religious: can there be a fully secular ethics, or does morality need a religious foundation? Traditions and secular philosophers disagree.
Dialogues · freedom & conscience

Free will, responsibility and conscience

  • Free will matters for moral responsibility (Component 1) and for religious ideas of judgement. But some traditions also teach predestination or divine foreknowledge (e.g. Calvin, Islamic qadar) — a tension to explore.
  • Conscience: is it the voice of God (Newman, Butler), of reason (Aquinas), or the super-ego (Freud)? The religion's view of conscience meets the psychological challenge head-on.

Dialogues · applied

Applied ethics: where they meet

Applied issues put the dialogue to the test:

  • Life and death: sanctity-of-life teaching vs quality-of-life and autonomy arguments on abortion and euthanasia.
  • Animals: stewardship/dominion vs Singer's equal consideration and Regan's rights.
  • Here the religion's ethics and secular theories may agree (compassion, justice) or clash (autonomy vs sanctity).
Dialogues · evaluation

Reaching a judgement

  • Does the religion need philosophical ethics, or does it already have its own resources?
  • Is secular ethics a rival to, or a partner with, religious morality?
  • Which theory best fits — and best challenges — the tradition you study?
  • Reach a reasoned overall judgement, citing thinkers on both sides.
