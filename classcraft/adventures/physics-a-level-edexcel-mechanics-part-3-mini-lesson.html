Mission 1

Mission 1: Can an object's balance point be in empty space, where there is no material at all?

Make a guess: Can an object's balance point be in empty space, where there is no material at all?

Mission 1

Centre of mass

  • Centre of mass: the single point where the whole mass can be treated as concentrated.
  • A mass-weighted average position of all the bits of the object.
  • Uniform and regular (sphere, cylinder, cube, uniform beam): at the geometric centre.
  • If the mass is spread symmetrically, it lies on every line of symmetry.
  • Uniform ring: at the centre of the hole. No material there.
Quick check
?A cylindrical rod is made of a 0.50 m aluminium section joined end to end to a 0.50 m steel section of the same diameter. Each section is uniform, and the steel section has the greater mass. How does the location of the rod's centre of mass compare with the rod's midpoint?
Mission 1

Centre of gravity

  • Centre of gravity: the point where the whole weight of the body can be taken to act.
  • In a uniform gravitational field it is at the centre of mass.
  • Uniform beam: draw its weight at the middle. This is how you take moments for an extended body.
Mission 1

Finding it by experiment

  • Hang a flat card (a lamina) freely from a pin near its edge.
  • At rest, its centre of gravity is directly below the pin: otherwise its weight would turn it.
  • Hang a plumb line from the pin; draw along it.
  • Hang from a second point; draw again.
  • The centre of gravity is where the lines cross. Check with a third point.
Quick check
?A rectangular wooden sign hangs from a single hook through a hole at the middle of its top edge. A heavy metal letter is fixed near the sign's left end. How does the sign hang at rest?
Mission check

?A high jumper clears the bar by arching her back, so that her body is draped over the bar in an upside-down U shape, with her head and arms hanging on one side and her legs on the other. Which statement about her centre of mass, at the instant her hips are directly above the bar, can be correct?

Yes. The centre of mass is an average position of the mass. For a ring, a horseshoe or an arched jumper, that average lies in empty space.

Mission 2

Mission 2: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Make a guess: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Mission 2

The turning effect of a force

  • Moment of a force about a point = force × perpendicular distance from the point to the force's line of action. Moment = Fx.
  • Unit: N m.
  • Same force, longer perpendicular distance: bigger turning effect.
  • A force whose line of action passes through the pivot has zero turning effect.
  • Each moment is clockwise or anticlockwise.
Quick check
?A mechanic pulls with a 20 N force on the end of a tool handle, 0.25 m from the centre of a nut. The force is in the plane of the handle's turn, but at 30° to the handle instead of at 90°. What is the turning effect of the force about the centre of the nut?
Mission 2

Balanced turning effects

  • Principle of moments: for a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point = the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.
  • Full equilibrium needs both: resultant force = 0 and resultant moment = 0.
  • See-saw: child of weight W at 3d balances an adult of weight 3W at d. W × 3d = 3W × d.
Mission 2

Worked example

  • Uniform plank, 3.0 m long, weight 120 N, rests on a pivot 1.0 m from end A.
  • Its weight acts at its centre of gravity, the midpoint: 1.5 m from A, so 0.50 m beyond the pivot.
  • Moment of the plank's weight about the pivot = 120 × 0.50 = 60 N m.
  • Weight W hung at A balances it: W × 1.0 = 60, so W = 60 N.
  • Resultant force also zero: pivot pushes up with 120 + 60 = 180 N.
  • Tip: take moments about the point where an unknown force acts. Its moment there is zero.
Quick check
?A uniform beam of length 5.0 m and weight 400 N rests horizontally on two supports, one at each end, P and Q. A person of weight 600 N stands on the beam 1.0 m from Q. What is the upward force from support P?
Mission check

?A uniform board of weight W rests horizontally on two supports: support P at its left end and support Q one-quarter of the board's length from its right end. The diagram shows a student's force diagram for the board: FP and FQ are the forces exerted on the board by the supports, and W is drawn at the board's centre. Which statement about the student's diagram is correct?

The support farther from the board's midpoint pushes up with the smaller force. On the see-saw, the child sits three times as far from the pivot: W × 3d balances 3W × d.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Hold a heavy bag still for ten minutes and your arms ache. How much work have you done on the bag?

Make a guess: Hold a heavy bag still for ten minutes and your arms ache. How much work have you done on the bag?

Mission 3

Work is energy transferred

  • A force does work when its point of application moves in the direction of the force.
  • Work done = force × distance moved in the direction of the force.
  • Work done = energy transferred. Both in joules.
  • 1 J = the work done when a force of 1 N moves its point of application 1 m in its own direction.
Mission 3

A force at an angle

  • Only the component along the displacement does work.
  • ΔW = FΔs, using the component of F along Δs: F cos θ.
  • θ = 0: W = Fs. θ = 90°: W = 0. The force changes only the direction of motion.
Quick check
?A child pulls a sledge 25 m along level snow using a rope held at 30° above the horizontal. The tension in the rope is a constant 80 N. How much work does the tension do on the sledge?
Mission 3

Positive, negative or zero

  • Work is a scalar. Its sign shows which way energy goes.
  • θ < 90°: positive work. The force transfers energy to the object.
  • θ > 90°: negative work. The force takes energy away: friction, braking.
  • θ = 90°: zero. A crate slides along a level floor: its weight and the normal force do no work.
  • No displacement: zero. Holding a load still does no work on it.
Quick check
?A worker uses a rope to lower a box straight down at constant speed. Which statement about the work done on the box during the descent is correct?
Mission 3

Worked example: pushing a crate

  • A 150 N push at 40° below the horizontal moves a crate 6.0 m along a level floor. Friction is 90 N.
  • Push: W = 150 × 6.0 × cos 40° = +690 J.
  • Friction (θ = 180°): W = 90 × 6.0 × cos 180° = −540 J.
  • Weight and normal force (θ = 90°): 0 J each.
  • Each force's work is found separately. Use the angle between that force and the displacement.
Mission check

?A student carries a bag of weight 15 N up a flight of stairs at constant velocity. The bag moves 4.0 m horizontally and rises 3.0 m, so it travels 5.0 m along the stairs. How much work does the student's upward force do on the bag?

Work needs a displacement along the force. The held bag does not move, so you do no work on it. Your arms ache because your muscles use energy internally to keep exerting the force.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Two smooth slides drop the same height: one steep, one gentle. Which gives you more speed at the bottom?

Make a guess: Two smooth slides drop the same height: one steep, one gentle. Which gives you more speed at the bottom?

Mission 4

Kinetic energy

  • Kinetic energy: the energy an object has because it is moving.
  • Eₖ = ½mv²
  • A scalar: never negative; no direction.
  • Double the mass → double the kinetic energy. Double the speed → four times the kinetic energy.
Mission 4

Gravitational potential energy

  • Gravitational potential energy: energy due to position in a gravitational field.
  • ΔEgrav = mgΔh
  • Valid near the surface, where g is roughly constant.
Quick check
?Object A has mass m and moves at speed 2v. Object B has mass 2m and moves at speed v. What is the kinetic energy of object A divided by the kinetic energy of object B?
Mission 4

Conservation of energy

  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed.
  • It is transferred between stores.
  • Stores include kinetic, gravitational, elastic, internal (thermal), chemical and nuclear.
  • No friction or drag: mechanical energy stays constant. Loss of potential energy = gain of kinetic energy.
  • With friction or drag: some becomes internal energy and sound. The total is still conserved.
  • Loss of GPE = gain of KE + work done against resistive forces.
Mission 4

Worked example: a falling ball

  • A ball is dropped from 1.8 m. Air resistance is negligible.
  • Loss of GPE = gain of KE: mgh = ½mv². m cancels: v = √(2gh).
  • v = √(2 × 9.81 × 1.8) = 5.9 m s⁻¹.
  • Only the vertical drop matters. A smooth slide of any shape, same drop: the same speed.
Quick check
?A child slides from rest down a straight slide with negligible friction and reaches a speed v at the bottom. A second straight slide has the same steepness, but its top is four times as high above its bottom. What speed does the child reach at the bottom of the second slide?
Mission check

?Three identical stones are thrown from the top of a sea cliff with the same speed: one straight up, one horizontally and one straight down. Air resistance is negligible. Which stone hits the sea with the greatest speed?

Only the drop in height matters, not the path. Two smooth slides of the same height give the same speed at the bottom; the gentle one just takes longer.

Mission 5

Mission 5: Double a car's speed. How much longer is its braking distance?

Make a guess: Double a car's speed. How much longer is its braking distance?

Mission 5

Net work changes kinetic energy

  • The work done by the resultant force = the change in kinetic energy.
  • From F = ma and v² = u² + 2as: Fs = ½mv² − ½mu².
  • Net work positive: speeds up. Negative: slows down. Zero: constant speed.
Mission 5

Worked example: braking

  • A 1200 kg car at 20 m s⁻¹ brakes with a constant 8000 N resultant force.
  • Final KE = 0, so F × s = ½mu².
  • s = ½ × 1200 × 20² ÷ 8000 = 30 m.
  • At 40 m s⁻¹: s = ½ × 1200 × 40² ÷ 8000 = 120 m.
  • Braking distance ∝ u²: double the speed, four times the distance.
Quick check
?A child on a toboggan slides across level snow. The child and toboggan have a total mass of 50 kg and slow down from 8.0 m s⁻¹ to 6.0 m s⁻¹. What is the magnitude of the net work done on the child–toboggan system during this time?
Mission 5

Friction and drag dissipate energy

  • Friction and drag do negative work on a moving object.
  • That mechanical energy becomes internal (thermal) energy and sound. The total is still conserved.
  • Loss of GPE = gain of KE + work done against resistive forces.
Mission 5

Worked example: a toboggan run

  • A 50 kg toboggan and rider start from rest. They descend 12 m vertically along a 40 m slope and reach 12 m s⁻¹.
  • GPE lost = 50 × 9.81 × 12 = 5890 J. KE gained = ½ × 50 × 12² = 3600 J.
  • Work done against resistive forces = 5890 − 3600 ≈ 2.3 kJ. Average resistive force = 2290 ÷ 40 ≈ 57 N.
Quick check
?A skier of mass 60 kg starts from rest at the top of a slope and descends through a vertical height of 20 m. At the bottom her speed is 16 m s⁻¹. Take g = 9.81 m s⁻². What is the total work done on the skier by friction and air resistance during the descent?
Mission check

?A block on a level, frictionless surface is sped up by a constant horizontal force. The work needed to speed the block up from rest to speed v is W. How many times W is needed to speed the block up from v to 3v?

Work changes kinetic energy, and kinetic energy ∝ v². Braking from twice the speed removes four times the KE, so the same braking force needs four times the distance.

Mission 6

Mission 6: Identical twins climb the same stairs, one running, one walking. Who does more work? Who is more powerful?

Make a guess: Identical twins climb the same stairs, one running, one walking. Who does more work? Who is more powerful?

Mission 6

Power

  • Power = rate of doing work = rate of energy transfer.
  • P = E/t = W/t
  • Unit: the watt. 1 W = 1 J s⁻¹.
  • Same work in half the time: twice the power.
Mission 6

Worked example: climbing stairs

  • A 60 kg student climbs stairs 3.0 m high in 4.0 s.
  • Work done = mgΔh = 60 × 9.81 × 3.0 = 1.8 kJ (1766 J).
  • Power = 1766 ÷ 4.0 = 440 W.
  • Walk up in 8.0 s: the same work, half the power.
Mission 6

Efficiency

  • Efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input.
  • Or: useful power output ÷ total power input.
  • Multiply by 100 for a percentage.
  • Always less than 100%. The rest is wasted, mostly as thermal energy in the surroundings.
Mission 6

Worked example: a motor

  • An electric motor takes 200 W. It lifts a 20 kg load 3.0 m in 5.0 s.
  • Useful power = mgΔh/t = 20 × 9.81 × 3.0 ÷ 5.0 = 118 W.
  • Efficiency = 118 ÷ 200 = 0.59, or 59%.
  • Wasted power = 200 − 118 = 82 W, mostly heating the motor and its surroundings.
Quick check
?A Sankey diagram for a car engine has an input arrow representing 200 kJ of chemical energy from the fuel. It divides into four output arrows: 60 kJ of useful mechanical work, 100 kJ of thermal energy carried away by the exhaust gases, 30 kJ of thermal energy removed by the cooling system, and 10 kJ of sound. What is the efficiency of the engine?
Mission check

?Motor A raises a 200 N load through 3.0 m in 6.0 s. Motor B raises a 100 N load through 6.0 m in 4.0 s. Both lift at constant speed. Which statement is correct?

Work depends on force and distance, not time. The runner and the walker do the same work against gravity; the runner does it faster, so develops more power.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Centre of mass: the mass-weighted average position; at the centre of a uniform regular object, on every line of symmetry, and possibly outside the material.
  • Centre of gravity: where the whole weight acts.
  • Find it by hanging the body from two points with a plumb line.

Mission 2

  • Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action. In equilibrium: Σ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments, and resultant force = 0.

Mission 3

  • ΔW = FΔs, using the component of F along Δs; work done = energy transferred.

Mission 4

  • Eₖ = ½mv²; ΔEgrav = mgΔh. Without resistive forces, PE lost = KE gained: only the height dropped matters.

Mission 5

  • Work done by the resultant force = change in kinetic energy: Fs = ½mv² − ½mu².
  • Friction and drag turn mechanical energy into internal energy; total energy is conserved.

Mission 6

  • P = W/t; efficiency = useful output ÷ total input (energy or power).
