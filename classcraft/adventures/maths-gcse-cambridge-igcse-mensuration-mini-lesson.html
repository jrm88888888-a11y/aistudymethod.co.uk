Measures · perimeter and area

Areas of triangles, parallelograms and trapezia

  • Perimeter is the total distance round the edge (cm). Area is the space inside (cm²).
  • Always use the perpendicular height, measured at 90° to the base, never a slanted side.
  • Split compound shapes (including kites and rhombuses) into rectangles and triangles, then add or subtract.

Worked example Measures · perimeter and area

Area of a trapezium

Question
A trapezium has parallel sides 7 cm and 13 cm. Its perpendicular height is 6 cm and each slanted side is 6.7 cm. Find its area.
  1. 1
    Pick out the parallel sides and the perpendicular height: a=7a = 7, b=13b = 13, h=6h = 6
  2. 2
    Add the parallel sides: 7+13=207 + 13 = 20
  3. 3
    Multiply by the height and halve: 21​×20×6=601/2 × 20 × 6 = 60
Answer
60 cm²
⚠️ Watch out
using the slanted side 6.7 cm as the height gives 67 cm², which is wrong. The 6.7 cm is extra information.

Measures · perimeter and area

Circles

  • Circumference C=πd=2πrC = π d = 2π r. Area A=πr2A = π r². Both are on the List of formulas.
  • Halve a diameter before using πr2π r²: squaring the diameter is the commonest slip.
  • Exact answers are left in terms of ππ: for r=3r = 3, A=9πA = 9π cm².
  • A semicircle's perimeter is half the circumference plus the diameter.
Worked example Measures · perimeter and area

A track-shaped perimeter and area

Question
A shape is a 10 cm by 6 cm rectangle with a semicircle on each 6 cm end. Find its perimeter and its area, exactly and to 1 decimal place.
  1. 1
    The two semicircles make one circle of diameter 6 cm: curved length =π×6=6π= π × 6 = 6π
  2. 2
    Add the two straight sides: perimeter =20+6π≈38.8= 20 + 6π ≈ 38.8 cm
  3. 3
    The two semicircles make one circle of radius 3 cm: area =π×32=9π= π × 3² = 9π
  4. 4
    Add the rectangle: area =60+9π≈88.3= 60 + 9π ≈ 88.3 cm²
Answer
perimeter 20+6π≈38.820 + 6π ≈ 38.8 cm; area 60+9π≈88.360 + 9π ≈ 88.3 cm²
⚠️ Watch out
the 6 cm sides are inside the shape, not on its edge, so they are not part of the perimeter.

Measures · perimeter and area

Arcs and sectors

  • A sector is a "pizza slice" cut by two radii; the arc is its curved edge.
  • A sector with angle θθ is the fraction 360θ​θ/360 of the whole circle:
arc length=360θ​×2πrsector area=360θ​×πr2arc length = θ/360 × 2π r sector area = θ/360 × π r²
  • The perimeter of a sector is the arc plus two radii.
  • Core questions use angles that divide 360° (such as 90°, 60°, 40°); Extended uses any angle, including major sectors.

Mission check

?Each lane on a track is 1.2 m wide, so lane 2's bends have a radius 1.2 m bigger than lane 1's. The straights are the same. About how much further is one lap in lane 2?

A lap is two straights plus two semicircular bends, which together make a full circle. Moving out one lane adds 2π×1.2≈7.52π × 1.2 ≈ 7.5 m per lap, whatever the size of the track, so each outer lane starts further round to make every runner's distance the same.

Measures · volume and surface area

Prisms and cylinders

  • A prism has the same cross-section all the way through. A cuboid has 6 faces, 12 edges and 8 vertices.
  • Volume of any prism:
V=area of cross-section×lengthV = area of cross-section × length
  • Cuboid: V=l×w×hV = l × w × h. Cylinder: the cross-section is a circle, so V=πr2hV = π r² h.
  • Units: cm³ or m³. 1 litre =1000= 1000 cm³, and 1 m³ =1000= 1000 litres.
  • Extended: recognise planes of symmetry and axes of symmetry of prisms, cylinders, pyramids and cones.
Worked example Measures · volume and surface area

Volume of a triangular prism

Question
A prism has a right-angled triangle as its cross-section, with shorter sides 6 cm and 8 cm. The prism is 15 cm long. Find its volume.
  1. 1
    Area of the cross-section: 21​×6×8=241/2 × 6 × 8 = 24 cm²
  2. 2
    Multiply by the length: 24×15=36024 × 15 = 360
Answer
360 cm³
⚠️ Watch out
forgetting to halve for the triangle gives 6×8×15=7206 × 8 × 15 = 720 cm³, double the true volume.

Measures · volume and surface area

Surface area, nets, plans and elevations

  • Surface area is the total area of the faces. A net shows them unfolded flat.
  • Cuboid: SA=2(lw+lh+wh)SA = 2(lw + lh + wh).
  • A cylinder's net is two circles and a rectangle whose width is the circumference 2πr2π r. Curved surface =2πrh= 2π r h; total =2πr2+2πrh= 2π r² + 2π r h.

Measures · volume and surface area

Pyramids, cones and spheres

  • Pyramid: V=31​×base area×hV = 1/3 × base area × h.
  • Cone: V=31​πr2hV = 1/3π r² h, a third of the cylinder with the same base and height. Curved surface =πrl= π r l, where the slant height ll comes from Pythagoras: l2=r2+h2l² = r² + h².
  • Sphere: V=34​πr3V = 4/3π r³ and SA=4πr2SA = 4π r². Half a sphere is a hemisphere.
  • Extended: a frustum is a cone with its top cut off: volume == large cone −− small cone.
  • All these formulas are on the List of formulas.
Worked example Measures · volume and surface area

Volume and curved surface of a cone

Question
A cone has base radius 3 cm and perpendicular height 4 cm. Find its volume and its curved surface area, in terms of ππ and to 1 decimal place.
  1. 1
    Volume: 31​×π×32×4=12π≈37.71/3 × π × 3² × 4 = 12π ≈ 37.7 cm³
  2. 2
    Compare: a cylinder with the same base and height holds π×32×4=36ππ × 3² × 4 = 36π, three times as much
  3. 3
    Slant height by Pythagoras: l=32+42​=5l = √(3² + 4²) = 5 cm
  4. 4
    Curved surface: πrl=π×3×5=15π≈47.1π r l = π × 3 × 5 = 15π ≈ 47.1 cm²
Answer
volume 12π≈37.712π ≈ 37.7 cm³; curved surface area 15π≈47.115π ≈ 47.1 cm²
⚠️ Watch out
πrlπ r l needs the slant height. Using the perpendicular height gives π×3×4=12ππ × 3 × 4 = 12π cm², which is too small.

Mission check

?One 20 cm by 30 cm sheet is rolled into a tube two ways: tall (30 cm high) or short (20 cm high). Which holds more?

The edge that wraps round becomes the circumference, so r=2πC​r = C/2π. Tall: r=2π20​r = 20/2π, V=πr2×30=π3000​≈955V = π r² × 30 = 3000/π ≈ 955 cm³. Short: r=2π30​r = 30/2π, V=π4500​≈1432V = 4500/π ≈ 1432 cm³. Because the radius is squared, the wide tube wins, holding 1.5 times as much.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Area: rectangle lwlw, triangle 21​bh1/2bh, parallelogram bhbh, trapezium 21​(a+b)h1/2(a+b)h, always with the perpendicular height.
  • Circle: C=2πr=πdC = 2π r = π d, A=πr2A = π r²; give exact answers in terms of ππ when asked; split compound shapes into parts.
  • Sector =360θ​= θ/360 of the circle for both arc length and area.
  • Prism volume == cross-section area ×× length; cylinder V=πr2hV = π r² h; 1 litre =1000= 1000 cm³.
  • Surface area is the sum of the faces: use a net; a cylinder's curved surface unrolls to a 2πr2π r by hh rectangle.
  • Pyramid and cone volumes are a third of the matching prism or cylinder; sphere V=34​πr3V = 4/3π r³, SA=4πr2SA = 4π r².
