Mission 1

Mission 1: Hold a heavy bag still for ten minutes and your arms ache. How much work have you done on the bag?

Make a guess: Hold a heavy bag still for ten minutes and your arms ache. How much work have you done on the bag?

Mission 1

Work is energy transferred

  • A force does work when its point of application moves in the direction of the force.
  • Work done = force × distance moved in the direction of the force.
  • Work done = energy transferred. Both in joules.
  • 1 J = the work done when a force of 1 N moves its point of application 1 m in its own direction.
  • Work transfers energy into or out of a system by a force exerted on it.
Mission 1

A force at an angle

  • Only the component along the displacement does work.
  • W = F∥d = Fd cos θ, θ = angle between the force and the displacement.
  • θ = 0: W = Fd. θ = 90°: W = 0. The force changes only the direction of motion.
Quick check
?The diagram shows a sled pulled to the right across level snow by a rope. It gives the tension in the rope, the angle the rope makes with the horizontal, and the sled's displacement. Use sin 37° = 0.60 and cos 37° = 0.80. How much work does the tension do on the sled?
Mission 1

Positive, negative or zero

  • Work is a scalar. Its sign shows which way energy goes.
  • θ < 90°: positive work. The force transfers energy to the object.
  • θ > 90°: negative work. The force takes energy away: friction, braking.
  • θ = 90°: zero. A crate slides along a level floor: its weight and the normal force do no work.
  • No displacement: zero. Holding a load still does no work on it.
Quick check
?A worker uses a rope to lower a box straight down at constant speed. Which statement about the work done on the box during the descent is correct?
Mission 1

Worked example: pushing a crate

  • A 150 N push at 40° below the horizontal moves a crate 6.0 m along a level floor. Friction is 90 N.
  • Push: W = 150 × 6.0 × cos 40° = +690 J.
  • Friction (θ = 180°): W = 90 × 6.0 × cos 180° = −540 J.
  • Weight and normal force (θ = 90°): 0 J each.
  • Each force's work is found separately. Use the angle between that force and the displacement.
Mission 1

A force that varies

  • Force changes with position: work = area under the force–displacement graph.
  • Plot F∥ (the component along the displacement) against position x.
  • Constant force: a rectangle, F × d. Force rising steadily from 0: a triangle, ½Fd.
  • Area below the axis counts as negative work.
Quick check
?A student's hand exerts a horizontal force on a block as the block moves in the +x direction along a level surface. The graph shows the force component F as a function of the block's position x. What is the total work done on the block by the hand's force from x = 0 to x = 6.0 m?
Mission check

?A car drives at constant speed around a flat circular bend of radius 50 m. The friction force from the road on the car is 4000 N and acts toward the center of the bend. How much work does this force do on the car while it goes a quarter of the way around?

Work needs a displacement along the force. The held bag does not move, so you do no work on it. Your arms ache because your muscles use energy internally to keep exerting the force.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Two smooth slides drop the same height: one steep, one gentle. Which gives you more speed at the bottom?

Make a guess: Two smooth slides drop the same height: one steep, one gentle. Which gives you more speed at the bottom?

Mission 2

Kinetic energy

  • Kinetic energy: the energy an object has because it is moving.
  • K = ½mv²
  • A scalar: never negative; no direction.
  • Double the mass → double the kinetic energy. Double the speed → four times the kinetic energy.
Mission 2

Gravitational potential energy

  • Gravitational potential energy: energy due to position in a gravitational field.
  • ΔUg = mgΔy
  • Valid near the surface, where g is roughly constant.
Quick check
?Object A has mass m and moves at speed 2v. Object B has mass 2m and moves at speed v. What is the kinetic energy of object A divided by the kinetic energy of object B?
Mission 2

Elastic potential energy

  • A stretched or compressed spring stores elastic potential energy.
  • Us = ½k(Δx)², Δx = distance from the spring's equilibrium length.
  • It is the area under the line of spring force against Δx (a triangle), for an ideal spring.
Mission 2

Conservation of energy

  • Energy cannot be created or destroyed.
  • It changes form, or is transferred into or out of a system.
  • Mechanical energy = kinetic + gravitational potential + elastic potential.
  • No friction or drag: mechanical energy stays constant. Loss of potential energy = gain of kinetic energy.
  • With friction or drag: some becomes internal energy and sound. The total is still conserved.
Mission 2

Worked example: a falling ball

  • A ball is dropped from 1.8 m. Air resistance is negligible.
  • Loss of Ug = gain of K: mg|Δy| = ½mv². m cancels: v = √(2g|Δy|).
  • v = √(2 × 10 × 1.8) = 6.0 m/s.
  • Only the vertical drop matters. A smooth slide of any shape, same drop: the same speed.
Quick check
?A child slides from rest down a straight slide with negligible friction and reaches a speed v at the bottom. A second straight slide has the same steepness, but its top is four times as high above its bottom. What speed does the child reach at the bottom of the second slide?
Mission 2

Worked example: a spring launcher

  • A spring (k = 400 N/m) is compressed 0.050 m, then fires a 0.020 kg ball along a smooth, level track.
  • Energy stored = ½ × 400 × 0.050² = 0.50 J.
  • All of it becomes kinetic energy: ½ × 0.020 × v² = 0.50 → v = 7.1 m/s.
  • Fired straight up instead: spring potential energy → kinetic energy → gravitational potential energy. At the top, ½k(Δx)² = mgΔy.
Quick check
?A spring launcher points straight up. When its ideal spring is compressed by Δx, it launches a ball of mass m that rises to a maximum height H above its release point. The spring is then compressed by 2Δx and used to launch a ball of mass 2m. Air resistance is negligible. How high above its release point does the second ball rise?
Mission check

?Three identical stones are thrown from the top of a sea cliff with the same speed: one straight up, one horizontally and one straight down. Air resistance is negligible. Which stone hits the sea with the greatest speed?

Only the drop in height matters, not the path. Two smooth slides of the same height give the same speed at the bottom; the gentle one just takes longer.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Lift a book onto a shelf. Where exactly is its new potential energy stored?

Make a guess: Lift a book onto a shelf. Where exactly is its new potential energy stored?

Mission 3

Kinetic energy: scalar, and frame-dependent

  • K = ½mv² is a scalar: never negative, no direction.
  • Total K of a system = the sum of each object's K. Directions do not cancel.
  • v depends on the reference frame, so K does too.
  • A 60 kg passenger sits in a bus moving at 10 m/s. For the driver: K = 0.
  • For someone at the roadside: K = ½ × 60 × 10² = 3.0 × 10³ J. Both are right.
Quick check
?A 0.50 kg ball rolls forward along the floor of a train car at 2.0 m/s relative to the car. The train moves along a straight, level track at a constant 20 m/s relative to the ground. What is the ball's kinetic energy as measured by an observer standing beside the track?
Mission 3

Potential energy belongs to a system

  • Potential energy needs two or more objects that interact by a conservative force: gravity, an ideal spring.
  • Lifted book: the energy is in the book–Earth system, not "in the book".
  • U depends on the configuration: the positions of the objects relative to each other.
  • A scalar. A system of one object can have only K.
  • Friction is not conservative. There is no potential energy for friction.
Quick check
?Each system below contains only the objects named. In which system do the objects interact only through conservative forces, so that the system has a potential energy associated with their interaction?
Mission 3

Choosing the zero; adding the pairs

  • You choose where Ug = 0: the floor, the table, the shelf. Any level works.
  • Below your zero, Ug is negative. That is fine.
  • ΔUg is the same for every choice. Only changes have physical meaning.
  • Several interactions: total U = sum over every interacting pair.
  • Block hanging on a spring: U = Ug (block–Earth) + Us (block–spring).
Quick check
?A 1.5 kg book is moved from a table 0.80 m above the floor to a shelf 2.0 m above the floor. A student chooses Ug = 0 for the book on the shelf. Using g = 10 m/s², what are Ug for the book on the table and ΔUg for the move?
Mission 3

Energy bar charts

  • One bar per energy: K, Ug, Us at the start; the same at the end.
  • A W bar between them: work done on the system by external forces (+ in, − out).
  • Rule: initial bars + W = final bars.
  • Ball–Earth system, ball thrown upward to its top: K bar shrinks, Ug bar grows; W = 0, as gravity is internal.
Quick check
?A ball is launched from level ground at an angle above the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible. A student draws the energy bar chart shown for the ball–Earth system, from the instant of launch to the instant the ball is at the highest point of its path. Which statement about the chart is correct?
Mission 3

Energy is always conserved

  • Energy is conserved in every interaction. Mechanical energy is not always.
  • Trampoline: stretched, it springs back. The system has Us.
  • A car's crumple zone: it stays crumpled. The car's K becomes thermal energy (and sound), not U.
  • Nonconservative forces dissipate mechanical energy as thermal energy and sound.
Mission check

?A rock rests on the edge of a cliff 20 m above a beach. Which statement about its gravitational potential energy is correct?

Potential energy belongs to a system of objects interacting by a conservative force. The lifted book's energy is in the book–Earth system, not in the book alone.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Double a car's speed. How much longer is its braking distance?

Make a guess: Double a car's speed. How much longer is its braking distance?

Mission 4

Net work changes kinetic energy

  • The work done by the net force = the change in kinetic energy.
  • Work–energy theorem: ΔK = ΣW = ΣF∥d. Add the work of every force.
  • Net work positive: speeds up. Negative: slows down. Zero: constant speed.
Mission 4

Worked example: braking

  • A 1200 kg car at 20 m/s brakes with a constant 8000 N net force.
  • Final K = 0, so F × d = ½mv0².
  • d = ½ × 1200 × 20² ÷ 8000 = 30 m.
  • At 40 m/s: d = ½ × 1200 × 40² ÷ 8000 = 120 m.
  • Braking distance ∝ v0²: double the speed, four times the distance.
Quick check
?A child on a toboggan slides across level snow. The child and toboggan have a total mass of 50 kg and slow down from 8.0 m/s to 6.0 m/s. What is the magnitude of the net work done on the child–toboggan system during this time?
Mission 4

Friction and drag dissipate energy

  • Friction and drag do negative work on a moving object.
  • That mechanical energy becomes internal (thermal) energy and sound. The total is still conserved.
  • ΔEmech = Ff d cos θ; θ = 180°, so it is negative.
Mission 4

Worked example: a toboggan run

  • A 50 kg toboggan and rider start from rest. They descend 12 m vertically along a 40 m slope and reach 12 m/s.
  • ΔUg = −50 × 10 × 12 = −6000 J. ΔK = ½ × 50 × 12² = +3600 J.
  • ΔEmech = −2400 J = −Ff × 40 → average Ff = 60 N.
Quick check
?A 5.0 kg box slides 4.0 m down a ramp inclined at 37° to the horizontal. The coefficient of kinetic friction between the box and the ramp is 0.25. Use g = 10 m/s², sin 37° = 0.60 and cos 37° = 0.80. How much mechanical energy is dissipated by friction during the slide?
Mission 4

Conservative and nonconservative forces

  • Conservative force (gravity, ideal spring): its work depends only on the start and end points, not the path.
  • Round trip back to the start: zero work. A potential energy can be defined.
  • Nonconservative force (friction, air resistance): its work depends on the path taken.
  • No potential energy exists for a nonconservative force.
Quick check
?Two identical boxes slide from rest all the way down two ramps, starting from the same height h above the floor. Ramp S is steep and ramp G is gentle, and the coefficient of kinetic friction is the same for both. How does the mechanical energy dissipated by friction compare for the two slides?
Mission 4

Choosing the system

  • Ball alone: a single object has only K. Gravity is external: it does work on the ball.
  • Ball + Earth: gravity is internal. The system has K + Ug; gravity does no external work.
  • Change in a system's energy = work done by external forces: ΔE = Wext.
  • Choose a system with no external work, and its total energy is constant.
  • Friction inside the system: mechanical energy → thermal energy. The total is still constant.
Quick check
?A person pushes a box across a rough, level floor at constant speed. The system is the box and the floor. Which statement correctly describes the change in the total energy of the system?
Mission check

?A block on a level, frictionless surface is sped up by a constant horizontal force. The work needed to speed the block up from rest to speed v is W. How many times W is needed to speed the block up from v to 3v?

Work changes kinetic energy, and kinetic energy ∝ v². Braking from twice the speed removes four times the KE, so the same braking force needs four times the distance.

Mission 5

Mission 5: Identical twins climb the same stairs, one running, one walking. Who does more work? Who is more powerful?

Make a guess: Identical twins climb the same stairs, one running, one walking. Who does more work? Who is more powerful?

Mission 5

Power

  • Power = rate of doing work = rate of energy transfer.
  • Pavg = ΔE/Δt = W/Δt
  • Unit: the watt. 1 W = 1 J/s.
  • Same work in half the time: twice the power.
Mission 5

Worked example: climbing stairs

  • A 60 kg student climbs stairs 3.0 m high in 4.0 s.
  • ΔUg = mgΔy = 60 × 10 × 3.0 = 1800 J.
  • Average power = 1800 ÷ 4.0 = 450 W.
  • Walk up in 8.0 s: the same work, half the power.
Mission 5

Power and speed: P = Fv

  • A force F moves its point of application a distance d in time Δt: W = Fd.
  • P = W/Δt = F × d/Δt = Fv.
  • Force at angle θ to the velocity: P = Fv cos θ. Only the component along v delivers power.
  • Car at a steady 30 m/s: driving force = resistive forces = 600 N.
  • Power needed = 600 × 30 = 18 kW.
Quick check
?A student pulls a box across a level floor at constant speed with a rope. The diagram shows the tension in the rope, the angle of the rope above the horizontal, and the box's velocity. Use cos 37° = 0.80 and sin 37° = 0.60. What is the power delivered to the box by the tension in the rope?
Quick check
?A cart starts from rest on a level track with negligible friction. A constant horizontal force is exerted on the cart, and no other horizontal force acts on it. At time t after the cart starts, the power delivered to the cart by the force is P. The power delivered by the force at time 2t is how many times P?
Mission check

?Motor A raises a 200 N load through 3.0 m in 6.0 s. Motor B raises a 100 N load through 6.0 m in 4.0 s. Both lift at constant speed. Which statement is correct?

Work depends on force and distance, not time. The runner and the walker do the same work against gravity; the runner does it faster, so develops more power.

Mission 6

Mission 6: If you know only how stored energy changes with position, can you find the force?

Make a guess: If you know only how stored energy changes with position, can you find the force?

Mission 6

Work with vectors

  • Work done by a force along a path from a to b: W = ∫ F · dr (from a to b).
  • Dot product: A · B = AB cos θ. The result is a scalar, not a vector.
  • In components: A · B = AₓBₓ + AyBy + AzBz.
  • Only the component of F parallel to the displacement does work. A perpendicular force does none.
  • Constant force, straight path: W = F · Δr = Fd cos θ.
Quick check
?A constant force F = (5.00î − 2.00ĵ) N is exerted on an object while the object undergoes a displacement Δr = (3.00î + 4.00ĵ) m. How much work does the force do on the object?
Mission 6

Worked example: a force that varies

  • A force along the x-axis: Fx = (20 − 4x) N, with x in m. The object moves from x = 0 to x = 5 m.
  • W = ∫0⁵ (20 − 4x) dx = [20x − 2x²]0⁵
  • W = 100 − 50 = 50 J.
  • Check: area under the F–x graph = triangle ½ × 5 m × 20 N = 50 J.
  • Never use "end force × distance" (0 J here) or "start force × distance" (100 J).
Quick check
?An ideal spring is stretched slowly from its relaxed length by an external force. Stretching it from 0 to an extension x takes work W. Stretching it further, from an extension x to an extension 2x, takes work nW. What is n?
Mission 6

Conservative forces: the round-trip test

  • Conservative force (gravity, ideal spring): work depends only on the start and end positions, not the path.
  • Test with a round trip. Ideal spring, Fx = −kx, out to x = A and back: W = ∫0ᴬ (−kx) dx + ∫ᴬ0 (−kx) dx = −½kA² + ½kA² = 0.
  • Friction of constant size f on the same trip opposes the motion both ways: W = −fA − fA = −2fA, never zero.
  • So a potential energy U can be defined, but only for conservative forces.
  • No "friction potential energy" exists.
Mission 6

From force to potential energy and back

  • Conservative force → potential energy: ΔU = −∫ Fc · dr (from a to b).
  • The minus sign: when the force does positive work, U falls.
  • In one dimension, reverse it: Fx = −dU/dx.
  • The force is minus the slope of the U(x) graph. It points toward lower U.
  • Example: U(x) = 3x² (J, x in m) → Fx = −6x. At x = 2 m: Fx = −12 N (toward x = 0, where U is lowest).
Quick check
?The graph shows the potential energy U of a system as a function of the position x of an object in the system. What is the x-component of the conservative force exerted on the object at x = 4 m?
Mission 6

Reading a U(x) graph

  • Slope = 0 → Fx = 0 → equilibrium.
  • Minimum of U: stable. Push it slightly: the force points back.
  • Maximum of U: unstable. Push it slightly: the force points away.
  • Draw the total mechanical energy E as a horizontal line. Kinetic energy = E − U.
  • Where U = E the object stops and turns: a turning point. Where U > E it can never go.
Quick check
?The graph shows the potential energy U of a system as a function of the position x of an object in the system, with four labeled points. At which labeled point could the object be at rest in unstable equilibrium?
Mission 6

Instantaneous power

  • P = dW/dt: the rate at which a force does work at an instant.
  • Average power = W/Δt; it hides changes during the interval.
  • Since dW = F · dr: P = F · v = Fv cos θ.
  • Energy delivered = ∫P dt = area under a P–t graph.
Quick check
?A car of mass m starts from rest on a level road. Its engine delivers a constant power P to the car, and friction and air resistance are negligible. What is the car's speed at time t?
Mission check

?The potential energy of a system is U(x) = ax² − bx³, where a and b are positive constants and x is the position of an object in the system. What is the x-component of the conservative force exerted on the object when it is at x = d?

Yes. Fx = −dU/dx: the force is minus the slope of the energy graph, so U(x) alone gives the force at every point (for a conservative force).

Mission 7

Mission 7: How much energy does it take to lift a probe far from a planet, or to free it completely?

Make a guess: How much energy does it take to lift a probe far from a planet, or to free it completely?

Mission 7

Zero at infinity

  • Choose zero gravitational potential energy when the masses are infinitely far apart.
  • Gravity attracts. As two masses come together from infinity, gravity does positive work: energy is released.
  • So at any finite separation the potential energy is below zero: negative.
  • Closer → more negative. Moving apart → rises toward zero, and that needs work done against gravity.
Mission 7

Potential energy of two masses

  • Ug = −Gm1m2/r
  • r: center to center. The energy belongs to the pair, not to one of the masses.
  • Three or more objects: add Ug for every pair, each pair once.
Mission 7

Worked example: when is constant g good enough?

  • Lift a 1000 kg probe from Earth's surface (R = 6.37 × 10⁶ m) to a height R, so r goes from R to 2R. M = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg.
  • Gain = GMm/R − GMm/2R = GMm/2R = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ × 1000 ÷ (2 × 6.37 × 10⁶) = 3.1 × 10¹⁰ J.
  • Using mgΔy = 1000 × 10 × 6.37 × 10⁶ = 6.4 × 10¹⁰ J: about twice too big.
  • mgΔy assumes g stays constant. Here g falls to a quarter. Use mgΔy only when Δy ≪ R.
Quick check
?Three identical small spheres A, B and C, each of mass m, are fixed in a straight line far from all other objects, with the center-to-center spacings shown in the diagram. Which expression gives the total gravitational potential energy of the three-sphere system?
Quick check
?A 4.0 × 10² kg satellite is moved from an orbit at an altitude of 1.6 × 10⁶ m above Earth's surface to an orbit at an altitude of 1.36 × 10⁷ m. Earth's mass is 6.0 × 10²⁴ kg and its radius is 6.4 × 10⁶ m. What is the change in the gravitational potential energy of the Earth–satellite system? Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg².
Mission check

?A probe is moved from a distance R to a distance 3R from the center of a planet of radius R. What fraction of the energy needed to move it from R all the way to infinity is this?

Far from the surface g falls, so mgΔy overestimates the energy. Use Ug = −Gm1m2/r: two-thirds of the way to "free" is reached at only 3R.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • W = Fd cos θ: a scalar; positive, negative or zero; area under F∥–x.

Mission 2

  • K = ½mv²; ΔUg = mgΔy; Us = ½k(Δx)². No external work and no nonconservative forces: K + U is constant.

Mission 3

  • K is a scalar that depends on the frame. U belongs to a system with conservative interactions; you choose its zero; total U sums every pair. Bar charts: initial + W = final.

Mission 4

  • ΔK = net work; friction dissipates Ff d; ΔEsystem = Wexternal.
  • Friction and drag turn mechanical energy into internal energy; total energy is conserved.

Mission 5

  • Pavg = ΔE/Δt = W/Δt; Pinst = Fv cos θ.

Mission 6

  • W = ∫F · dr (a dot product, a scalar); only the parallel component does work.
  • Conservative forces: path-independent work, ΔU = −∫F · dr and Fx = −dU/dx; minima of U are stable, maxima unstable. P = dW/dt = F · v.

Mission 7

  • Gravitational potential energy is zero at infinity and negative at any finite separation.
  • Ug = −Gm1m2/r; use mgΔy only when Δy ≪ R.
