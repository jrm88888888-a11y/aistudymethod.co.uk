Before you start

You need from the core lesson

  • pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]; one pH unit is a tenfold change in [H⁺].
  • Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 298 K.
  • Strong acids and bases ionize completely; weak ones only partly.
  • A conjugate pair differs by one H⁺: CH₃COOH / CH₃COO⁻, NH₄⁺ / NH₃.
Mission 1

Mission 1: What is the pH of boiling pure water?

Make a guess: What is the pH of boiling pure water?

Mission 1

pOH

  • pOH = −log₁₀[OH⁻] (in the data booklet), so [OH⁻] = 10⁻ᵖᴼᴴ.
  • At 298 K, pH + pOH = 14.00, because Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴.
  • Worked: 0.025 mol dm⁻³ KOH. [OH⁻] = 0.025, pOH = 1.60, pH = 12.40.
  • Group 2 hydroxides release two OH⁻ per formula unit.
Quick check
?An aqueous solution at 298 K has [H⁺] = 5.0 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³. What is the pOH of the solution?
Calculate
1A solution at 298 K has pH 10.40. Calculate [OH⁻], in mol dm⁻³.
Mission 1

Kw changes with temperature

  • H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻ is endothermic, so Kw rises as temperature rises.
  • At 333 K, Kw ≈ 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴. Neutral [H⁺] = √Kw = 3.1 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³: pH 6.51.
  • Neutral means [H⁺] = [OH⁻]. It means pH 7.00 only at 298 K.
  • pH + pOH = 14.00 also holds only at 298 K.
Calculate
1At 273 K, Kw = 1.14 × 10⁻¹⁵. Calculate the pH of pure water at 273 K.
Mission 1

Ka and pKa

  • Weak acid: HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻. Ka = [H⁺][A⁻]/[HA].
  • pKa = −log₁₀Ka. Larger Ka, smaller pKa, stronger weak acid.
  • Methanoic acid (pKa 3.75) is stronger than ethanoic acid (pKa 4.76). (Literature values.)
  • Ka describes weak acids. A strong acid is fully ionized: its Ka is very large and is not used.
Mission 1

Kb and pKb

  • Weak base: B + H₂O ⇌ BH⁺ + OH⁻. Kb = [BH⁺][OH⁻]/[B].
  • Water is the solvent, so it is left out of the expression.
  • pKb = −log₁₀Kb. Smaller pKb, stronger base.
  • Ammonia: pKb 4.75. Methylamine: pKb 3.34, a stronger base. (Literature values.)
Quick check
?At 298 K the pKa of methanoic acid, HCOOH, is 3.75 and the pKa of ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH, is 4.76. Which statement about 0.10 mol dm⁻³ solutions of the two acids is correct?
Mission 1

Ka × Kb = Kw

  • For a conjugate pair HA / A⁻: Ka × Kb = ([H⁺][A⁻]/[HA]) × ([HA][OH⁻]/[A⁻]).
  • Everything cancels except [H⁺][OH⁻]: Ka × Kb = Kw.
  • Take logs: pKa + pKb = 14.00 at 298 K.
  • Stronger acid, weaker conjugate base.
  • Worked: methanoic acid, pKa 3.75, so methanoate ion HCOO⁻ has pKb 10.25.
Quick check
?At 298 K the pKa of the ammonium ion, NH₄⁺, is 9.25 and the pKa of the methylammonium ion, CH₃NH₃⁺, is 10.66. Which statement about the bases NH₃ and CH₃NH₂ is correct?
Quick check
?Phenol, C₆H₅OH, has pKa = 9.99 at 298 K (literature value). What is Kb for its conjugate base, C₆H₅O⁻?
Mission check

?At 373 K, Kw ≈ 5.4 × 10⁻¹³. What is the pH of pure water at 373 K?

About 6.1. Kw rises with temperature, so [H⁺] = √Kw is larger at 373 K. [H⁺] and [OH⁻] are still equal.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Why is a solution of sodium ethanoate alkaline when no hydroxide was put in?

Make a guess: Why is a solution of sodium ethanoate alkaline when no hydroxide was put in?

Mission 2

pH of a weak acid: two approximations

  • HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻. Very little HA ionizes, so [HA]eqm ≈ [HA]initial.
  • Each HA that ionizes gives one H⁺ and one A⁻, so [H⁺] ≈ [A⁻].
  • So Ka ≈ [H⁺]²/[HA], giving [H⁺] = √(Ka × [HA]).
  • No quadratic is needed.
Mission 2

Weak acid pH (worked example)

  • 0.050 mol dm⁻³ methanoic acid, pKa 3.75.
  • Ka = 10⁻³·⁷⁵ = 1.78 × 10⁻⁴.
  • [H⁺] = √(1.78 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.050) = 2.98 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³.
  • pH = 2.53. A strong acid at 0.050 mol dm⁻³ would have pH 1.30.
Calculate
1Benzoic acid has Ka = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ at 298 K. Calculate the pH of 0.10 mol dm⁻³ benzoic acid.
Calculate
1A 0.20 mol dm⁻³ solution of a weak acid, HA, has pH 2.70 at 298 K. Calculate Ka.
Mission 2

pH of a weak base

  • B + H₂O ⇌ BH⁺ + OH⁻. Same approximations: [OH⁻] = √(Kb × [B]).
  • Then pOH, then pH = 14.00 − pOH.
  • Worked: 0.10 mol dm⁻³ methylamine, pKb 3.34. Kb = 4.57 × 10⁻⁴.
  • [OH⁻] = √(4.57 × 10⁻⁵) = 6.76 × 10⁻³. pOH 2.17, so pH = 11.83.
Calculate
1pKa of NH₄⁺ = 9.25 at 298 K. Calculate the pH of 0.050 mol dm⁻³ NH₃(aq).
Mission 2

Salt hydrolysis

  • Dissolving gives ions. Hydrolysis: an ion then donates H⁺ to water or accepts H⁺ from it.
  • Conjugate acid of a weak base → acidic: NH₄⁺ + H₂O ⇌ NH₃ + H₃O⁺.
  • Conjugate base of a weak acid → basic: RCOO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ RCOOH + OH⁻.
  • Ions of strong acids and strong bases (Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻, NO₃⁻) do not hydrolyse measurably.
Mission 2

Carbonate and hydrogencarbonate

  • CO₃²⁻ + H₂O ⇌ HCO₃⁻ + OH⁻: basic.
  • HCO₃⁻ + H₂O ⇌ H₂CO₃ + OH⁻: also basic, but more weakly.
  • HCO₃⁻ is amphiprotic, but its Kb is larger than its Ka, so it acts mainly as a base.
  • Rule of thumb: strong acid + weak base → pH < 7; weak acid + strong base → pH > 7; strong + strong → 7.
Quick check
?Which equation explains why aqueous potassium carbonate, K₂CO₃(aq), is alkaline?
Quick check
?Which equation represents the hydrolysis reaction that determines the pH of aqueous ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl(aq)?
Quick check
?Which 0.10 mol dm⁻³ salt solution has a pH above 7 at 298 K?
Mission check

?Which equation represents the hydrolysis reaction that determines the pH of aqueous sodium ethanoate, CH₃COONa(aq)?

No hydroxide was added. The ethanoate ion takes H⁺ from water, leaving OH⁻ behind, so [OH⁻] > [H⁺].

Mission 3

Mission 3: Two acids at the same concentration need the same volume of alkali. How can a titration show which one is weak?

Make a guess: Two acids at the same concentration need the same volume of alkali. How can a titration show which one is weak?

Mission 3

Four curves, four shapes

Base added to 25.0 cm³ of acid, all 0.100 mol dm⁻³:

  • Strong acid + strong base: starts near pH 1; long steep section; equivalence at pH 7.
  • Weak acid + strong base: starts higher; buffer region; equivalence above 7.
  • Strong acid + weak base: starts near pH 1; equivalence below 7; levels off well below the strong-base curve.
  • Weak acid + weak base: no sharp steep section.
  • Acid added to base gives the same features, with the curve falling.
Mission 3

Reading the key points

  • Intercept: the pH of the flask solution before any titrant is added.
  • Equivalence point: the middle of the steep section, where amounts match the equation.
  • Buffer region: the gentle slope before equivalence, where HA and A⁻ are both present.
  • Half-equivalence: [HA] = [A⁻], so Ka = [H⁺] and pH = pKa.
  • Weak base titrated with acid: at half-equivalence, pOH = pKb.
Quick check
?25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid (pKa = 4.76) is titrated with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH(aq) at 298 K. What is the pH after 12.5 cm³ of NaOH(aq) has been added?
Quick check
?The graph shows the pH curve for the titration of 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ of a weak monoprotic acid, HA, with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH(aq) at 298 K. Three points on the curve are labelled with their pH. What is the Ka of HA?
Calculate
1A weak base is titrated with HCl(aq) at 298 K. At half-equivalence the pH is 10.80. Calculate the pKb of the base.
Mission 3

Why equivalence isn't always at pH 7

  • At equivalence, the flask holds the salt and water only.
  • Weak acid + strong base: A⁻ hydrolyses → pH > 7.
  • Strong acid + weak base: BH⁺ hydrolyses → pH < 7.
  • The equivalence volume depends on amounts, not strength: strong and weak acids need the same.
Quick check
?A pH curve starts at pH 1.0. It rises slowly, then steeply from about pH 3 to pH 7, with the equivalence point at about pH 5.5. It then levels off below pH 10. Which titration does it show?
Mission check

?Separate 20.0 cm³ portions of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ HCl and of a 0.200 mol dm⁻³ weak acid, HA, are each titrated with 0.200 mol dm⁻³ NaOH(aq). Which feature of the pH curves shows that HA is weak?
Mission 4

Mission 4: How does a colourless dye suddenly turn pink at the end of a titration?

Make a guess: How does a colourless dye suddenly turn pink at the end of a titration?

Mission 4

Indicators are weak acids

  • HInd(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + Ind⁻(aq). HInd and Ind⁻ have different colours.
  • Add acid: [H⁺] rises, so the equilibrium shifts left: HInd's colour shows.
  • Add alkali: OH⁻ removes H⁺, so it shifts right: Ind⁻'s colour shows.
  • Only a few drops are used, so the indicator does not noticeably change the pH.
Mission 4

Where the colour changes

  • Ka = [H⁺][Ind⁻]/[HInd], so [Ind⁻]/[HInd] = Ka/[H⁺].
  • At pH = pKa, [HInd] = [Ind⁻]: a mixed colour. This is the end point.
  • Below pKa, HInd dominates; above pKa, Ind⁻ dominates.
  • The change is seen over about pKa ± 1. Ranges are in the data booklet.
Mission 4

Universal indicator

  • A mixture of indicators with different pKa values.
  • Their changes follow one another, giving a different colour at most pH values.
  • Good for estimating pH; no use in a titration, because it has no single sharp change.
Quick check
?Universal indicator shows a different colour at almost every whole-number pH from about 1 to 14. What is the reason for this?
Calculate
1An indicator, HInd, has pKa = 6.8. Calculate the ratio [Ind⁻]/[HInd] in a solution of pH 8.8.
Mission 4

End point vs equivalence point

  • Equivalence point: acid and base have reacted in the stoichiometric ratio.
  • End point: the indicator changes colour.
  • Pick an indicator whose range lies within the steep section, so the two coincide.
  • Weak acid + strong base → phenolphthalein. Strong acid + weak base → methyl orange.
  • Strong + strong: either works. Weak + weak: no indicator gives a sharp end point.
Quick check
?Which statement correctly distinguishes the end point of a titration from its equivalence point?
Quick check
?25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid (pKa = 4.76) is titrated with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH(aq) at 298 K. Three indicators are available, with these pH ranges: methyl orange 3.1–4.4; bromothymol blue 6.0–7.6; phenolphthalein 8.0–10.0. Which choice of indicator, with its reason, is correct?
Mission check

?Phenolphthalein can be written HInd(aq) ⇌ H⁺(aq) + Ind⁻(aq), where HInd is colourless and Ind⁻ is pink. NaOH(aq) is added to an acid containing a few drops of phenolphthalein. Why does the pink colour appear near the end?
Mission 5

Mission 5: Why does your blood stay at about pH 7.4, even after a sprint?

Make a guess: Why does your blood stay at about pH 7.4, even after a sprint?

Mission 5

What a buffer is

  • A buffer resists a change in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are added.
  • Acidic buffer: a weak acid plus its conjugate base, e.g. CH₃COOH with CH₃COONa.
  • Basic buffer: a weak base plus its conjugate acid, e.g. NH₃ with NH₄Cl.
  • Or part-neutralize: excess weak acid with some NaOH, or excess weak base with some HCl.
Mission 5

How a buffer works

  • Acidic buffer, add H⁺: CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ → CH₃COOH.
  • Add OH⁻: CH₃COOH + OH⁻ → CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O.
  • Basic buffer: NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺, and NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ → NH₃ + H₂O.
  • The ratio of the pair changes only a little, so the pH changes only slightly.
Quick check
?Which mixture, made up to 1.00 dm³ with water, is a buffer solution?
Quick check
?A buffer solution contains 0.10 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COOH(aq) and 0.10 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COONa(aq). A small amount of HCl(aq) is added. Which statement about the buffer's response is correct?
Mission 5

Buffer pH

  • Rearrange Ka: [H⁺] = Ka × [HA]/[A⁻].
  • In logs: pH = pKa + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA]).
  • [HA] = [A⁻] gives pH = pKa.
  • Worked: 0.10 mol dm⁻³ HCOOH with 0.25 mol dm⁻³ HCOONa, pKa 3.75. pH = 3.75 + log₁₀2.5 = 4.15.
  • Basic buffer: pOH = pKb + log₁₀([BH⁺]/[B]), then pH = 14.00 − pOH.
Quick check
?A buffer solution at 298 K contains 0.20 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COOH and 0.10 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COONa. Ka(CH₃COOH) = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵. What is the pH of the buffer?
Calculate
150.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ propanoic acid (pKa 4.87) is mixed with 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH(aq) at 298 K. Calculate the pH of the buffer formed.
Calculate
1A basic buffer at 298 K contains 0.15 mol dm⁻³ NH₃ and 0.30 mol dm⁻³ NH₄Cl. pKb(NH₃) = 4.75. Calculate the pH.
Mission 5

Diluting a buffer

  • Dilution lowers [HA] and [A⁻] by the same factor.
  • The ratio is unchanged, so the pH stays about the same.
  • But each cm³ now holds less HA and A⁻: the capacity to absorb added acid or alkali falls.
  • Compare: diluting a strong acid tenfold raises its pH by 1.
Quick check
?A basic buffer of NH₃ and NH₄Cl is diluted to ten times its volume with water. Which statement is correct, to a first approximation?
Mission check

?Blood contains a buffer of carbonic acid, H₂CO₃, and hydrogencarbonate ions, HCO₃⁻, which helps keep its pH between 7.35 and 7.45. During a sprint, muscles release extra H⁺ into the blood. Which reaction removes most of the added H⁺?
Recap

The big ideas

  • pH + pOH = 14.00 at 298 K only. Kw rises with temperature; neutral means [H⁺] = [OH⁻].
  • Larger Ka, smaller pKa, stronger weak acid. Ka × Kb = Kw for a conjugate pair.
  • Weak acid: [H⁺] = √(Ka × [HA]). Weak base: [OH⁻] = √(Kb × [B]).
  • Salts of a weak acid with a strong base are basic; of a weak base with a strong acid, acidic.
  • Half-equivalence: pH = pKa. Choose an indicator whose range falls within the steep section.
  • A buffer's conjugate base removes H⁺ and its weak acid removes OH⁻. pH = pKa + log₁₀([A⁻]/[HA]).
