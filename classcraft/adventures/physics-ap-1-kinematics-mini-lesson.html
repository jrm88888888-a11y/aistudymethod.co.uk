Mission 1Topics 1.1, 1.5

Mission 1: When does 3 + 4 = 5 in physics?

Make a guess: When does 3 + 4 = 5 in physics?

Mission 1

Scalars and vectors

  • Scalar: magnitude (size) only. Vector: magnitude and direction.
  • Scalars: distance, speed, mass, time, energy, temperature.
  • Vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum.
  • Position is a vector too.
  • Draw a vector as an arrow: length ∝ magnitude, pointing in its direction.
  • Symbol with an arrow, v. Its magnitude: v or |v|.
Quick check
?A student records four facts about a school bus trip. Which fact describes a vector quantity?
Mission 1

Adding along a line

  • Choose a positive direction. The opposite direction is negative.
  • 3 m east, then 8 m west. East positive: +3 + (−8) = −5 m, i.e. 5 m west.
  • The sign gives the direction, not the size: −5 m is 5 m long.
  • Two 5 N forces: same direction → 10 N. Opposite directions → 0 N.
Quick check
?A hiker walks 5 m east and then 9 m west along a straight path. Student 1 takes east as the positive direction; Student 2 takes west as the positive direction. Each finds the hiker's resultant displacement as a signed component. Which statement correctly compares their results?
Mission 1

Adding at right angles

  • Draw the vectors tip to tail. The resultant runs from the first tail to the last tip.
  • 3.0 N east + 4.0 N north:
  • Size: √(3.0² + 4.0²) = 5.0 N.
  • Direction: tan θ = 4.0 ÷ 3.0 → θ = 53° north of east.
  • So 3 + 4 = 5, but only for vectors at right angles.
  • Other angles: resolve each vector into components, add the components.
Mission 1

Resolving into components

  • Any vector = two perpendicular components.
  • F at angle θ to the horizontal:
  • Horizontal: F cos θ (side adjacent to θ).
  • Vertical: F sin θ (side opposite θ).
  • 20 N at 30°: 20 cos 30° = 17.3 N; 20 sin 30° = 10.0 N.
  • The two components together replace the original vector.
  • Adding at any angle: add the x-components, add the y-components, then use the Pythagorean theorem.
Mission 1

Components on a slope

  • Choose axes along and perpendicular to the slope.
  • Slope at angle θ: the angle between the weight and the perpendicular to the slope is also θ.
  • Gravitational force Fg = mg. Along the slope: mg sin θ. Into the slope: mg cos θ.
Quick check
?The diagram shows the velocity of a ball just after it is kicked, drawn with its tail at the origin of an x-y coordinate system with x horizontal and y vertical. The velocity has magnitude v and makes an angle θ with the vertical y-axis. Which expression gives the horizontal component vx of the velocity?
Quick check
?A small block moves up a straight ramp inclined at 30° to the horizontal. Student 1 resolves the block's velocity into horizontal and vertical components. Student 2 resolves the same velocity using an x-axis along the ramp and a y-axis perpendicular to the ramp. Which statement about their results is correct?
Mission check

?A robot vacuum moves 1.1 m east and then 1.6 m north, each along a straight line. What is the magnitude of its displacement from its starting point?

At right angles, sizes do not simply add: √(1.1² + 1.6²) = 1.9 m, not 1.1 + 1.6.

The Pythagorean theorem combines them. That is why 3 + 4 can make 5.

Mission 2Topics 1.1, 1.2

Mission 2: A runner finishes a lap exactly where she started. What was her average velocity?

Make a guess: A runner finishes a lap exactly where she started. What was her average velocity?

Mission 2

Position, displacement, distance

  • Model the moving thing as an object: a point carrying all its mass.
  • Position: where it is, measured from an origin along an axis. The sign shows which side.
  • Displacement: the change in position. A vector.
  • Δx = x − x0
  • Distance: total length of the path. A scalar, never negative.
  • 40 m out, 10 m back: distance 50 m; displacement 30 m outward.
Quick check
?A runner moves along a straight track. Her position x is measured from a marker on the track. She starts at x = +30 m, runs to x = +70 m, then turns around and runs to x = −20 m, where she stops. What is her displacement?
Mission 2

Speed and velocity

  • Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. A scalar.
  • Average velocity = displacement ÷ time. A vector, in the direction of the displacement.
  • vavg = Δx/Δt
  • Unit: m/s.
Mission 2

Acceleration

  • Acceleration = rate of change of velocity. Unit: m/s².
  • aavg = Δv/Δt
  • Velocity changes if the speed or the direction changes. A ball that bounces straight back at the same speed has accelerated.
  • a in the same direction as v: speeding up. Opposite: slowing down.
  • A negative acceleration does not always mean slowing down.
Quick check
?An object moves along a straight line, with positive taken to the right. At one instant its velocity is −3.0 m/s and its acceleration is +2.0 m/s². The acceleration stays constant. Which describes the motion of the object?
Mission 2

Average and instantaneous

  • Average value: over a time interval.
  • Average velocity and average acceleration use only the initial and final states.
  • Average speed uses the whole path: total distance ÷ total time.
  • Instantaneous value: at one instant.
  • The average over a very short interval approximates the instantaneous value.
  • A speedometer shows instantaneous speed.
  • A card of length L passes a photogate in time Δt: v ≈ L ÷ Δt.
  • Uniform acceleration: velocity changes by the same amount every second. Non-uniform: the acceleration itself changes.
Quick check
?The table shows the position x of an object moving along a straight line at four instants. Which value is the best estimate of the object's instantaneous velocity at t = 2.00 s?
Mission 2

Worked example

  • A cyclist rides 600 m north in 120 s, then 200 m south in 40 s.
  • Distance = 600 + 200 = 800 m. Displacement = 600 − 200 = 400 m north.
  • Time = 160 s.
  • Average speed = 800 ÷ 160 = 5.0 m/s.
  • Average velocity = 400 ÷ 160 = 2.5 m/s north.
  • Both legs are at 5.0 m/s, but the velocity changes: north, then south.
Quick check
?The diagram shows the velocities of four carts, P, Q, R and S, moving along straight tracks, at t = 0 and at t = 2.0 s. The positive direction is to the right, and each arrow is labeled with its speed. Which cart has the greatest magnitude of average acceleration between t = 0 and t = 2.0 s?
Mission check

?A car travels 3.0 km along a straight road at a constant speed of 10 m/s, then returns along the same road to its starting point at a constant speed of 15 m/s. What are its average speed and its average velocity for the whole journey?

Back at the start means zero displacement, so zero average velocity, for the car and for the runner on her lap. Average speed uses total distance ÷ total time: 6000 m ÷ 500 s, not the mean of the two speeds.

Mission 3Topic 1.3

Mission 3: On a velocity–time graph, where is the distance covered hiding?

Make a guess: On a velocity–time graph, where is the distance covered hiding?

Mission 3

Position–time graphs

  • Position–time (x–t) graph.
  • Slope = velocity.
  • Straight line: constant velocity. Steeper: faster. Horizontal: at rest.
  • Negative slope: moving in the negative direction.
  • Curve: velocity changing. Slope of the tangent = instantaneous velocity.
Mission 3

Motion diagrams

  • Dots show the object's position at equal time intervals.
  • Equal spacing: constant velocity. Spacing grows: speeding up. Spacing shrinks: slowing down.
  • Acceleration points the way the velocity is changing.
  • Narrative, motion diagram, graph and equation: four views of one motion. Translate between them.
Quick check
?The graph shows the position x of an object moving along a straight line as a function of time t. Points A, B and C are on the curve; the curve is horizontal at B. Which ranks the object's speeds at A, B and C, from greatest to least?
Mission 3

Velocity–time graphs

  • Slope = acceleration. Straight line: uniform acceleration. Curve: non-uniform.
  • Area between the line and the time axis = displacement.
  • Area below the axis: negative displacement (moving backward).
  • Distance = total area, every part counted as positive.
  • Line crosses the time axis: velocity zero, the object reverses.
  • Non-uniform acceleration: describe it from how the slope changes.
Mission 3

Worked example

  • A car: 0 to 12 m/s in 4.0 s; steady 12 m/s for 6.0 s; then to rest in 3.0 s.
  • Accelerations: 12 ÷ 4.0 = 3.0 m/s²; 0; −12 ÷ 3.0 = −4.0 m/s².
  • Displacement = area: ½ × 4.0 × 12 + 6.0 × 12 + ½ × 3.0 × 12
  • = 24 + 72 + 18 = 114 m.
  • Average velocity = 114 ÷ 13.0 = 8.8 m/s.
Quick check
?The graph shows the velocity v of an object moving along a straight line as a function of time t. A student claims that the object's acceleration is zero at t = 3 s, because the object is at rest at that instant. Which statement correctly evaluates the claim?
Quick check
?The graph shows the velocity v of a car as a function of time t as the car pulls away from rest along a straight, level road. Which statement describes the car's motion during the time shown?
Mission 3

Acceleration–time graphs

  • Area under an a–t graph = change in velocity.
  • Horizontal line: uniform acceleration. Line on the time axis: constant velocity.
  • Final velocity = starting velocity + area. You need the starting velocity.
  • Area below the axis: a negative change in velocity.
Quick check
?The graph shows the acceleration a of a cart moving along a straight track as a function of time t. At t = 0 the cart's velocity is +2 m/s. What is the cart's velocity at t = 4 s?
Mission check

?The graph shows the velocity v of a cart moving along a straight track as a function of time t. What is the cart's displacement from t = 0 to t = 3 s?

Distance and displacement hide in the area under a velocity–time graph. Area below the time axis counts as negative displacement but still adds to distance.

Mission 4Topic 1.3

Mission 4: A dropped stone falls a distance d in its first second. How far does it fall in the next second?

Make a guess: A dropped stone falls a distance d in its first second. How far does it fall in the next second?

Mission 4

Five quantities, one condition

  • Use these equations only when the acceleration is constant: same size, same direction, along a straight line.
  • Δx = x − x0 displacement · vx0 initial velocity · vx final velocity · ax acceleration · t time.
  • Every quantity except t has a direction along the line: pick a positive direction and give each one a sign.
Mission 4

The equations

  • vx = vx0 + ax t (no Δx)
  • x = x0 + vx0 t + ½ax t² (no vx)
  • vx² = vx0² + 2ax(x − x0) (no t)
  • Choose the equation that leaves out the quantity you neither know nor want.
Mission 4

Worked example

  • A cyclist moving at 4.0 m/s accelerates uniformly at 1.5 m/s² for 6.0 s. Find the final velocity and the displacement.
  • Known: vx0 = 4.0 m/s, ax = 1.5 m/s², t = 6.0 s. Want: vx, Δx.
  • vx = vx0 + ax t = 4.0 + 1.5 × 6.0 = 13 m/s
  • Δx = vx0 t + ½ax t² = 4.0 × 6.0 + ½ × 1.5 × 6.0² = 24 + 27 = 51 m
  • Check with the third equation: 13² = 4.0² + 2 × 1.5 × 51 → 169 = 169 ✓
Quick check
?A car traveling at 16 m/s brakes with uniform deceleration and comes to rest in 4.0 s. What distance does the car travel while braking?
Mission 4

Free fall

  • Free fall: gravity is the only force acting (air resistance negligible).
  • Acceleration = g, downward, the same for every mass.
  • Take g = 10 m/s² near Earth's surface.
  • A ball thrown upward has acceleration g downward while rising, at the top and while falling.
  • At the top its velocity is zero; its acceleration is not.
Quick check
?A cart moves up a straight ramp, slowing down at a steady rate. It turns around at its highest point and then speeds up at the same steady rate as it rolls back down. The direction up the ramp is positive. Which description matches the cart's velocity–time graph?
Mission 4

Worked example: thrown upward

  • A ball is thrown straight up at 12.0 m/s. How high does it rise, and when does it reach the top?
  • Take up as positive: vy0 = +12.0 m/s, ay = −g. At the top, vy = 0.
  • Height: 0 = vy0² − 2gΔy → Δy = vy0²/2g
  • Time: 0 = vy0 − gt → t = vy0/g
  • Δy = 12.0² ÷ (2 × 10) = 7.20 m · t = 12.0 ÷ 10 = 1.20 s
Mission check

?A toy car is released from rest on a straight slope and speeds up with constant acceleration. It travels 0.60 m in the first 2.0 s. How far does it travel between t = 4.0 s and t = 6.0 s?

A dropped stone does the same. From rest, distance fallen ∝ t², so after T, 2T, 3T it has fallen d, 4d, 9d: the distances in successive equal times go 1 : 3 : 5.

With g = 10 m/s²: 5 m in the first second, then 15 m in the next.

Mission 5Topic 1.4

Mission 5: On a fast train, you throw a ball forward. How fast is the ball moving?

Make a guess: On a fast train, you throw a ball forward. How fast is the ball moving?

Mission 5

Who is measuring?

  • A reference frame is an observer with a ruler and a clock.
  • Position and velocity are measured relative to a frame.
  • Different frames can give different values, all correct.
  • No frame is "the true one". Always say which frame you mean.
Mission 5

Adding velocities in a line

  • v(ball rel. ground) = v(ball rel. train) + v(train rel. ground)
  • Choose a positive direction. Train: +30 m/s relative to the ground.
  • Ball thrown forward at +10 m/s relative to the train → +40 m/s relative to the ground.
  • Thrown backward (−10 m/s) → +20 m/s relative to the ground.
Quick check
?A cyclist rides east at 6 m/s relative to the road. Observer X stands beside the road. Observer Y is in a car that moves east along the same road at a constant 10 m/s. Which statement correctly compares the cyclist's velocity as measured by Y with the velocity measured by X?
Mission 5

Acceleration is the same in both frames

  • Two frames moving at constant velocity relative to each other.
  • Every velocity measured in one differs by the same constant from the other.
  • So every change in velocity is the same → the acceleration is the same in both frames.
  • A ball dropped in a steady train falls with acceleration g for the passenger and for the platform.
Quick check
?A cart moves along a straight track, speeding up relative to the track. Observer G stands beside the track. Observer R rides on a platform that moves along the track at constant velocity. The table shows the cart's velocity v measured by each observer at four instants t. Which claim is supported by the data?
Mission check

?A moving walkway in an airport moves at constant velocity vW relative to the floor. A passenger walks on the walkway with velocity vP relative to the walkway. A worker walks on the floor beside the walkway at constant velocity vF relative to the floor. All three velocities are components along the same straight line. Which expression gives the passenger's velocity as measured by the worker?

The ball in the hook moves at 10 m/s relative to you and 40 m/s relative to the track. Both are right: velocity belongs to a frame.

Mission 6Topic 1.5

Mission 6: Fire a bullet horizontally and drop another at the same instant. Which hits the ground first?

Make a guess: Fire a bullet horizontally and drop another at the same instant. Which hits the ground first?

Mission 6

Two motions, one clock

  • No air resistance: the only force is weight, straight down.
  • Horizontally: no force → constant velocity.
  • Vertically: acceleration g downward → free fall.
  • The two motions are independent. They share only the time, t.
Mission 6

Launched horizontally

  • A ball rolls off a table 0.80 m high at 2.5 m/s. Where does it land?
  • Vertical first: it starts with zero vertical velocity. 0.80 = ½gt².
  • t = √(2 × 0.80 ÷ 10) = 0.40 s
  • Then horizontal: x = 2.5 × 0.40 = 1.0 m from the table.
  • Recipe: find t from the vertical motion; use it in the horizontal motion.
Quick check
?A ball rolls off the edge of a horizontal ledge 1.8 m above the floor with a horizontal speed of 4.0 m/s. Air resistance is negligible. Use g = 10 m/s². What is the horizontal distance from the edge of the ledge to the point where the ball lands on the floor?
Mission 6

Launched at an angle

  • Launch at a given speed and angle θ to the horizontal. Resolve at the start:
  • horizontal vx0 = v0 cos θ · vertical vy0 = v0 sin θ
  • Horizontal component: never changes.
  • Vertical component: changes by g every second, as in free fall.
  • Below the horizontal: the vertical component starts downward. Watch the sign.
Mission 6

The top and the landing

  • At the highest point: vertical velocity = 0. Velocity = the horizontal component, not zero.
  • Acceleration at the top: still g downward.
  • Over level ground (no air resistance): the path is symmetrical. Time up = time down.
  • Range = horizontal velocity × total time of flight.
Mission 6

Worked example: a kicked ball

  • A ball is kicked at 18.0 m/s, 35.0° above level ground. Find the maximum height and the range.
  • Horizontal: 18.0 cos 35.0° = 14.7 m/s. Vertical: 18.0 sin 35.0° = 10.3 m/s.
  • Top: vertical velocity 0 → time to top = 10.3/g; height = 10.3²/2g.
  • Landing (level ground): time of flight = 2 × time to top. Range = 14.7 × time of flight.
  • g = 10 m/s²: top at 1.03 s · height 5.33 m · flight 2.06 s · range 30.4 m
  • Unrounded values carried through; round only at the end.
Quick check
?A ball is launched into the air. The horizontal and vertical components of its velocity, vx and vy, are shown as functions of time t in the graphs, with upward positive. Air resistance is negligible. Which claim about the ball's motion is supported by the graphs?
Quick check
?A ball is thrown from the top of a vertical cliff 25 m above the sea, with speed 20 m/s at 37° above the horizontal (sin 37° = 0.60, cos 37° = 0.80). Air resistance is negligible. Use g = 10 m/s². How long after it is thrown does the ball reach the sea?
Mission check

?Ball X is thrown horizontally at 8.0 m/s from a height of 5.0 m above level ground. At the same instant, ball Y is dropped from rest from a height of 20 m. Air resistance is negligible. How does the time Y takes to reach the ground compare with the time X takes?

The bullets are the same. Fired horizontally or dropped, each starts with zero vertical velocity and falls with acceleration g, so both hit level ground together (ignoring air resistance). The fired one just lands much farther away.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Vectors have direction: in a line, signs show it; at right angles, combine by the Pythagorean theorem and tan θ.
  • Resolve: F cos θ along the side adjacent to θ, F sin θ along the opposite side.
  • Any angle: add components.

Mission 2

  • Distance and speed are scalars; displacement, velocity and acceleration are vectors.
  • Average velocity = displacement ÷ time; average speed = distance ÷ time. Return to the start: average velocity zero.
  • Acceleration = Δv/Δt: a change of speed or of direction. Instantaneous = average over a very short interval.

Mission 3

  • Position–time graph: slope = velocity. v–t graph: slope = acceleration, area = displacement.
  • a–t graph: area = change in velocity.
  • Curves: tangent for instantaneous values.

Mission 4

  • Constant acceleration only: choose the constant-acceleration equation that leaves out the quantity you neither know nor want; give every vector a sign.
  • Free fall: a = g ≈ 10 m/s², downward, at every point of the flight, including the top.

Mission 5

  • Velocity relative to the ground = velocity relative to the moving frame + velocity of that frame; acceleration is the same in frames moving at constant velocity relative to each other.

Mission 6

  • Projectile, no air resistance: horizontal velocity constant, vertical acceleration g; the two share only the time t.
  • Resolve the launch velocity; at the top the vertical velocity is zero but the speed is not.
