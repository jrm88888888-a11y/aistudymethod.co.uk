Individual Oral

The Individual Oral (IO) is a supervised 10-minute prepared talk followed by 5 minutes of examiner questions. You examine how a global issue of your choice is presented, through content and form, in two of the literary works you have studied.

  • One work must be originally written in the language of the course and one must be a work in translation.
  • You bring a short extract (around 40 lines) from each work as a springboard to the whole works.
  • Worth 30% of the SL grade, assessed against four criteria out of 40.

Work through each screen, answer the questions as you go and collect ⭐ stars. Any named work appears only as a swappable, illustrative example — the IB reading lists are chosen by your school and rotate, so this lesson teaches the skills and ideas, never a set text. Press Start when you are ready.

The task

The fixed task

The task is fixed. The official prompt runs, in effect:

Examine the ways in which the global issue of your choice is presented through the content and form of two of the works you have studied.illustrative wording — always use the current prompt supplied by your teacher

Everything you say must serve that sentence: a global issue, two works, and attention to both content and form.

Global issue

What makes an issue global

A global issue is not any topic. IB says it should have three properties.

  • Wide significance — it matters to many people, not just to one plot.
  • Transnational impact — it reaches across borders and cultures.
  • Local relevance — it is felt in everyday, specific lived experience.

Too narrow: the fate of one character. Too broad: war. Just right: how propaganda reshapes private conscience — wide, transnational, and grounded in specifics you can analyse.

Fields of inquiry

The five fields of inquiry

To find and frame an issue, IB gives five fields of inquiry — broad areas, not issues themselves.

  • Culture, identity and community
  • Beliefs, values and education
  • Politics, power and justice
  • Art, creativity and the imagination
  • Science, technology and the environment

Remember the distinction: a field is a global area; you must narrow it to a specific global issue, and then to a precise line of inquiry.

Line of inquiry

The line of inquiry

The line of inquiry is the precise, arguable focus you carve from a broad issue — the difference between wandering and arguing.

  • Issue: how power silences dissent. Line of inquiry: how both works use the form of the interrupted or unfinished utterance to enact political silencing.
  • A good line of inquiry names a claim you can prove from the extracts, and points at form, not just content.

Extracts

Extracts as springboards

The extracts are springboards, not the whole task.

  • Analyse each extract closely — this is where your form analysis earns marks.
  • Then reach outward to how the global issue plays across the whole work.
  • A common failure is to stay trapped inside the 40 lines and never connect them to the larger work or the issue.

Balance the two works: roughly equal time and depth. An IO that is 80% one work and 20% the other is penalised on focus and organisation.

Content and form

Content and form together

Content and form together is the phrase to tattoo on your hand.

  • Content — what the work says about the issue: character, event, idea.
  • Form — how the craft delivers it: structure, imagery, voice, genre, staging, sound.

Why examiners insist on form: anyone can summarise a theme. Only a literature student can show how the writing constructs that theme. Form analysis is what separates the top bands from the rest.

Criteria

The four assessment criteria

Know the four assessment criteria (10 marks each, 40 total).

  • A — Knowledge, understanding and interpretation: of the works and the global issue.
  • B — Analysis and evaluation: of the writers’ choices and form.
  • C — Focus and organisation: a balanced, structured, coherent talk.
  • D — Language: clear, accurate, appropriate academic register.

Craft

Preparation and delivery

Preparation and delivery.

  1. Prepare ten bullet points only — no full script may be read.
  2. Rehearse to hit 10 minutes; leave the examiner room to ask real questions.
  3. Structure clearly: issue and line of inquiry → work one (content and form) → work two (content and form) → synthesis.
  4. Choose an issue you can see in the form of both extracts — not one you must force on.

Do not double-dip: works you use for the IO cannot be reused in Paper 2. Plan your nine works across the whole assessment set.

Recap

The big ideas to take away

The task: a 10-minute oral (+5 min questions) on a global issue across TWO works — content and form

Two works: one originally in the language, one in translation; a ~40-line extract from each

Global issue: wide significance + transnational impact + local relevance

Five fields of inquiry: broad AREAS — narrow to an issue, then to a line of inquiry

Extracts are springboards — analyse form closely, then reach out to the whole works

Criteria: A understanding · B analysis of form · C focus & balance · D language (40 marks)

No double-dipping: IO works cannot reappear in Paper 2

The IO rewards one thing above all: showing how the writing itself carries the issue. Press Finish to see your score.
