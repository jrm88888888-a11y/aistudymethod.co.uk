Mission 1

The passive: être + past participle

  • Active: the subject does the action. Passive: the subject receives it.
  • Le Parlement vote la loi. → La loi est votée par le Parlement. – The law is passed by Parliament.
  • Form: être + past participle. The participle agrees with the subject, like an adjective.
  • Les élèves sont encouragés par leurs professeurs. – The pupils are encouraged by their teachers.
  • Cette chanteuse est admirée de tous. – This singer is admired by everyone.
  • "by" = par (an action); de with feelings and states: aimé de, respecté de, entouré de, couvert de.
  • Watch out: don't drop the agreement: Les décisions sont prises (not pris).

Mission 1

"was": a été or était?

  • English "was + -ed" hides two meanings. French separates them.
  • An event (someone did it, at a moment) → perfect: a été.
  • La porte a été fermée à 22 h. – The door was closed (by someone) at 10 pm.
  • A state (how things were) → imperfect: était + participle as an adjective.
  • Quand je suis arrivé(e), la porte était fermée. – When I arrived, the door was (already) closed.
  • Dates and "by…" usually signal an event: a été construit en…, a été élu par….
  • Watch out: Elle est arrivée is not passive. It's the perfect of an être verb. Test: can you add par + agent? arrivée par makes no sense.

Mission 1

Only a direct object can become the subject

  • English turns the person into the subject: "I was told", "She was asked", "We were given".
  • French verbs like these take à + person (indirect object): dire à, demander à, donner à, permettre à, conseiller à, offrir à.
  • An indirect object cannot become the subject of a passive.
  • J'ai été dit → On m'a dit – I was told
  • Elle a été demandée de partir → On lui a demandé de partir – She was asked to leave
  • Nous avons été permis de sortir → On nous a permis de sortir – We were allowed to go out
  • The thing can still be the subject in formal French: Une bourse lui a été accordée. – A grant was awarded to him/her.

Mission 1

Four ways French avoids the passive

  • French uses the passive less than English, especially in speech. Natural alternatives:
  • 1. on: On a fermé l'usine. – The factory was closed. Ici, on parle français. – French is spoken here.
  • 2. Pronominal se (general facts, no agent): Le français se parle au Québec. – French is spoken in Quebec. Les billets se vendent en ligne. – Tickets are sold online. Ça ne se fait pas. – That isn't done.
  • 3. A noun: après la fermeture de l'usine – after the factory was closed.
  • 4. Active with the agent as subject: Le Parlement a voté la loi.
  • Also se faire + infinitive: Il s'est fait voler son portable. – He had his phone stolen.
  • Watch out: se + verb only works when no agent is named: Le français se parle par les Québécois.

Mission 1

Worked example: translating both ways

  • Into English: on and se often become an English passive.
  • On a découvert une nouvelle espèce en Guyane. (example text) → A new species has been discovered in French Guiana.
  • Ce produit se vend partout. → This product is sold everywhere.
  • Into French: step 1: who did it? step 2: event or state? step 3: is "I/we/he" really an indirect object?
  • "Thousands of trees are cut down every year." → Des milliers d'arbres sont abattus chaque année. or On abat des milliers d'arbres chaque année.
  • "The data was stolen." → event, données feminine plural → Les données ont été volées.
  • "We were told the truth." → dire à → On nous a dit la vérité.
  • Translation into French: a passive with "I/we were told/asked/given" is a classic trap; translation into English: render on naturally, often as a passive.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Passive = être (any tense) + participle agreeing with the subject; par (action), de (feeling, state); été never agrees.
  • "was" = a été (event) or était (state).
  • Indirect objects can't become passive subjects: On m'a dit. Avoid the passive with on, se, a noun or an active verb.
