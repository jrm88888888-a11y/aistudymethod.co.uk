Data presentation · single-variable data

Histograms: area means frequency

  • In a histogram the area of each bar represents the frequency, so bars can have unequal widths.
  • Plot frequency density on the vertical axis:
frequency density=class widthfrequency​frequency density = frequency/(class width)
  • Frequency = frequency density × class width. If a question says area is proportional to frequency, find the scale factor from one bar first.
  • To estimate a frequency over part of a class, take the matching fraction of that bar's area.
  • Box plots and cumulative frequency diagrams show the median and quartiles directly.

?A histogram of journey times tt minutes has a bar of frequency density 2.4 for 20≤t<3020 ≤ t < 30 and a bar of frequency density 0.9 for 30≤t<5030 ≤ t < 50. Estimate the number of journeys with 25≤t<4025 ≤ t < 40.

Variance and standard deviation

  • The standard deviation measures spread: roughly the typical distance of a value from the mean. The variance, its square, is the mean squared deviation.
  • From summary statistics, with Sxx​=∑(x−xˉ)2=∑x2−n(∑x)2​S_xx = Σ (x − x̄)² = Σ x² − ((Σ x)²)/n:
xˉ=n∑x​,σ2=n∑(x−xˉ)2​=n∑x2​−xˉ2=nSxx​​x̄ = (Σ x)/n, σ² = (Σ (x − x̄)²)/n = (Σ x²)/n − x̄² = S_xx/n
  • For a frequency table use ∑fxΣ fx and ∑fx2Σ fx² over ∑fΣ f. With grouped data use midpoints: the results are estimates.
  • To compare distributions, compare a measure of location and a measure of spread, in context.

Standard deviation from summary statistics

Question

  1. 1
    Mean: xˉ=202400​=120x̄ = 2400/20 = 120
  2. 2
    Mean of the squares: 20289620​=14481(289 620)/20 = 14 481

Coding

Question
Lengths xx mm are coded using y=10x−150​y = (x − 150)/10. The coded data have yˉ​=2.4ȳ = 2.4 and standard deviation σy​=1.5σ_y = 1.5. Find the mean and standard deviation of xx.
  1. 1
    Rearrange the code: x=10y+150x = 10y + 150
  2. 2
    The mean follows the code exactly: xˉ=10(2.4)+150=174x̄ = 10(2.4) + 150 = 174
  3. 3
    Adding 150 shifts every value but not the spread; multiplying by 10 scales it: σx​=10×1.5=15σ_x = 10 × 1.5 = 15
Answer
xˉ=174x̄ = 174 mm and σx​=15σ_x = 15 mm.
⚠️ Watch out
do not add 150 to the standard deviation. A shift changes location only.
Data presentation · single-variable data

Quartiles, interpolation and outliers

  • Q1​Q₁, median and Q3​Q₃ cut ordered data into quarters. IQR =Q3​−Q1​= Q₃ − Q₁.
  • For grouped data, estimate the median (or a percentile) by linear interpolation in its class:

median≈L+f2n​−F​×wmedian ≈ L + (n/2 − F)/f × w (LL lower boundary, FF cumulative frequency below, ff class frequency, ww width.)

  • A possible outlier lies more than 1.5×1.5 × IQR beyond the nearer quartile, or more than 2 standard deviations from the mean. The question states the rule.
  • Cleaning data: remove errors, handle missing values, and comment on genuine outliers.

?Two classes both average 62%. Why might one have far more fails?

Class P scored 55, 58, 60, 62, 62, 64, 66, 69 and class Q scored 38, 45, 52, 62, 66, 72, 78, 83. Q's standard deviation is about 14.9; P's is about 4.2.

a mean only tells you the centre. Class Q's standard deviation (about 14.9 against 4.2) shows its marks are much more spread out, so it has marks far below 62: two of them, 38 and 45, are below a 50% pass mark, while class P has none.

Data presentation · bivariate data

Scatter diagrams and correlation

  • Plot the explanatory (independent) variable across and the response (dependent) variable up.
  • Describe correlation by direction (positive, negative or none) and strength (strong or weak): how closely the points follow a straight line.
  • Look for distinct sections: two groups (say, adults and children) can each show no pattern, yet together look correlated.
  • A point far from the pattern is a possible outlier: check it is not an error.

Data presentation · bivariate data

Using a regression line

  • A regression line of yy on xx, y=a+bxy = a + bx, is used to predict yy from a given xx.
  • Gradient bb: the change in yy for each increase of 1 in xx, in context and with units.
  • Intercept aa: the predicted yy when x=0x = 0; often meaningless if x=0x = 0 is outside the data.
  • Interpolation (inside the range of xx) is fairly reliable. Extrapolation (outside it) is not.
  • A line fitted to logylog y against xx (or logxlog x) converts back to y=kbxy = kb^x (or y=axny = axⁿ).

Interpreting a regression line

Question
For 30 students, revision time hh hours (from 2 to 12) and test mark mm (out of 100) give the regression line m=28+4.5hm = 28 + 4.5h. Interpret 4.5 and 28, and comment on the predictions for h=8h = 8 and h=30h = 30.
  1. 1
    Gradient: each extra hour of revision is linked with about 4.5 more marks.
  2. 2
    Intercept: a student doing no revision is predicted to score 28, but h=0h = 0 is outside the data, so treat this with caution.
  3. 3
    Interpolate: m=28+4.5×8=64m = 28 + 4.5 × 8 = 64, reliable because 8 is within 2 to 12.
  4. 4
    Extrapolate: m=28+4.5×30=163m = 28 + 4.5 × 30 = 163, which is impossible for a mark out of 100.
Answer
about 4.5 marks per extra hour; 28 marks predicted with no revision (unreliable); 64 is a sensible prediction, 163 is not.
⚠️ Watch out
interpret in context with units ("marks per hour"), not just "the gradient is 4.5".

Data presentation · bivariate data

Correlation is not causation

  • Two variables can be correlated without one causing the other.
  • A third (confounding) variable may drive both. Ice cream sales and sea rescues are positively correlated: hot weather increases both.
  • The correlation may be coincidence, especially with few data points or many variables compared.
  • Data about groups (countries, schools) may not describe individuals within them.
  • To claim cause you need a controlled experiment or a clear mechanism, not just a scatter diagram.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Histogram bars: area represents frequency, with frequency density = frequency ÷ class width.
  • σ2=n∑x2​−xˉ2σ² = (Σ x²)/n − x̄²; take the square root for the standard deviation. Compare data sets with a measure of location and a measure of spread, in context.
  • Interpolate grouped data at position 2n​n/2; outliers lie beyond 1.5×1.5 × IQR from the nearer quartile (or a stated number of standard deviations from the mean).
  • Describe correlation by direction and strength; watch for distinct sections and outliers.
  • Interpret the regression gradient and intercept in context; interpolate, don't extrapolate.
  • Correlation does not imply causation: look for a third variable.
