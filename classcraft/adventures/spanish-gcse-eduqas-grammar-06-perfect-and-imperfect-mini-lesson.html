The perfect and imperfect

To tell a story about the past you need two past tenses working together. This lesson builds them: the present perfect ("have done") with haber, the imperfect for backgrounds and habits, and — the key exam skill — the preterite vs imperfect contrast for describing childhood, routines and things that happened.

Work through each screen and answer the questions as you go — some ask you to recognise the right form, some ask you to produce it by typing. Collect ⭐ stars and press Start.

The present perfect

"have / has done" — haber + participle

The present perfect talks about a recent past that is still connected to now: "I have eaten", "she has seen it". It is made of two parts — the present tense of haber plus a past participle:

haber (present) + past participlehe · has · ha · hemos · habéis · han  +  -ado / -ido
  • He comido. — I have eaten.
  • ¿Has visto la película? — Have you seen the film?
  • Hemos hablado con el profesor. — We have spoken to the teacher.

Two rules that never break: the two parts never separate (nothing goes between he and comido), and the participle never agrees here — it always ends in -o, whoever the subject is.

Building the participle

-ado and -ido

To make a regular past participle, chop the infinitive ending and add:

-ar → -ado
hablarhablado
trabajartrabajado
estudiarestudiado
-er / -ir → -ido
comercomido
vivirvivido
beberbebido

Now attach the right haber form: he hablado (I have spoken), has comido (you have eaten), han vivido (they have lived).

Note: the perfect tense uses the present of haber — he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han. (The pluperfect, había hablado, swaps in the imperfect of haber — a separate, higher-level structure.)

The imperfect tense

The imperfect endings

The imperfect is the tense of "used to", descriptions and backgrounds. It is beautifully regular — almost no exceptions. Chop the infinitive and add:

-AR · hablar

túhablabas
él / ellahablaba
nosotros Higherhablábamos
vosotros Higherhablabais
ellos Higherhablaban

yocomía
túcomías
él / ellacomía
nosotros Highercomíamos
vosotros Highercomíais

Tier note: the singular persons (yo, tú, él) are the most common; the Higher plural persons (hablábamos, comíais, vivían…) carry the badge as stretch — learn them all. -ir verbs (vivir → vivía) use exactly the same endings as -er.

When to use the imperfect

Four jobs of the imperfect

Reach for the imperfect whenever the past is unfinished, repeated or described:

  • Habits — "used to / would": De niño jugaba al fútbol. — As a child I used to play football.
  • Descriptions & background: Hacía sol y la casa era grande. — It was sunny and the house was big.
  • Ongoing "was doing": Comía cuando llegaste. — I was eating when you arrived.
  • Time & age in the past: Eran las tres; Tenía diez años. — It was three o'clock; I was ten years old.

Storytelling tip: the imperfect paints the scene — the weather, the place, how people felt, what usually happened. It answers "what was going on?" rather than "what happened next?".

The key skill · preterite vs imperfect

Two pasts, two jobs

Spanish has two simple past tenses and choosing between them is the heart of storytelling. The rule of thumb:

Preterite — the event

A completed, single, finished action. Answers "what happened?" — fui, comí, sonó, llegó.

Imperfect — the background

Ongoing description, habit, "was doing". Answers "what was going on?" — era, comía, hacía, jugaba.

They combine in the classic "interrupted action" sentence — the imperfect sets the scene, the preterite drops in the event:

  • Comía cuando sonó el teléfono. — I was eating (background) when the phone rang (event).
  • Cuando era pequeño, vivía en Madrid. — When I was little, I used to live in Madrid. (both imperfect — habit & description)
  • Ayer fui al cine. — Yesterday I went to the cinema. (preterite — one finished event)

Recap

Your perfect & imperfect toolkit

Present perfect: haber (he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han) + past participle. Parts never split; participle stays -o.

Regular participles: -ar → -ado (hablado), -er/-ir → -ido (comido, vivido).

Irregular participles: hecho, visto, escrito, dicho, puesto, vuelto, abierto, roto, muerto.

Imperfect endings: -ar → -aba…; -er/-ir → -ía…. Plural persons are Higher.

Imperfect irregulars: ser → era, ir → iba, ver → veía.

Imperfect uses: habits ("used to"), descriptions, "was doing", time & age.

Preterite vs imperfect: preterite = the event ("what happened"); imperfect = the background ("what was going on"). Comía cuando sonó el teléfono.
