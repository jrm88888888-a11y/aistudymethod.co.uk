A-level skills · unseen proseScreen 1

What an unseen prose question tests

  • How the writer shapes meaning (AO2): what was chosen, and what each choice does.
  • In prose, look at narrative voice (who tells, how close to a character's mind), structure (where the passage turns), sentence shapes, imagery, and what is withheld.
  • You can't lean on knowing the book: everything you need is on the page.
  • A list of techniques isn't an answer; an argument about what the passage does is.
A-level skills · unseen proseScreen 2

How your exam asks this

  • OCR Component 02 Section 1 (closed text): a close reading of one unseen prose extract from your topic, such as the Gothic or Women in Literature, 30 marks, assessed for AO2, AO1 and AO3: close analysis, plus how attitudes and values show in the extract.

A-level skills · unseen proseScreen 7

From choices to an argument

  • Lead with a claim about the whole passage, e.g. ‘Chopin lets us inside a desire her heroine can't act on.’
  • Follow the passage's movement (longing, then retreat), not a list of features.
  • Notice free indirect style: "she did not care what" is her thought in the narrator's voice.
  • Hedge where it's open: suggests, perhaps, may.
  • Say what attitudes and values show, e.g. ideas of a wife's duty in the 1890s.

?A passage from a story you've never read. How do you find the choices?

Read for movement first: what happens, whose mind you're in, and where it turns. Then ask which choices (voice, sentence shape, imagery, repetition) make that turn felt, and build one argument about what the passage does.

Recap

Big ideas

  • An unseen prose question tests how the writer shapes meaning: choices and their effects, argued, not listed.
  • Read three times: what happens and whose mind; where it turns; which choices make the turn felt.
  • In Chopin's passage, free indirect style lets us share Mrs Baroda's longing, and a balanced sentence ties desire to retreat.
  • OCR's close reading is assessed for AO2, AO1 and AO3, including attitudes and values from your topic.
