Trigonometry · sine and cosine rules

The sine rule

  • Label the triangle so that side aa is opposite angle AA, side bb opposite BB and side cc opposite CC.
  • The sine rule:
sinAa​=sinBb​=sinCc​a/(sin A) = b/(sin B) = c/(sin C)
  • Use it when you know a side and its opposite angle, plus one more side or angle.
  • To find an angle, use it upside down: asinA​=bsinB​(sin A)/a = (sin B)/b.

Trigonometry · sine and cosine rules

The cosine rule

  • The cosine rule:
a2=b2+c2−2bccosAa² = b² + c² − 2bccos A

Graphs, symmetry and period

  • y=sinxy = sin x and y=cosxy = cos x have period 360∘360° and take values from −1−1 to 11. y=tanxy = tan x has period 180∘180° and asymptotes at x=90∘,270∘,…x = 90°, 270°, …

Trigonometry · radians

What is a radian? (A-level only)

  • 1 radian is the angle at the centre of a circle when the arc length equals the radius.
  • The circumference is 2πr2π r, so a full turn is 2π2π radians: 2π rad=360∘2π rad = 360° and π rad=180∘π rad = 180°.
  • Degrees to radians: multiply by 180π​π/180. Radians to degrees: multiply by π180​180/π. So 1 rad ≈57.3∘≈ 57.3°.
  • Exact values: 30∘=6π​30° = π/6, 45∘=4π​45° = π/4, 60∘=3π​60° = π/3, 90∘=2π​90° = π/2. So sin6π​=21​sin π/6 = 1/2.

Arc length and sector area (A-level only)

s=rθA=21​r2θs = rθ A = 1/2r²θ

Trigonometry · reciprocal and inverse functions

Three reciprocal functions (A-level only)

  • Secant, cosecant and cotangent are reciprocals:
secθ=cosθ1​,cosecθ=sinθ1​,cotθ=tanθ1​=sinθcosθ​sec θ = 1/(cos θ), cosec θ = 1/(sin θ), cot θ = 1/(tan θ) = (cos θ)/(sin θ)
  • Memory aid: the third letter of sec, cosec and cot names its partner (c for cos, s for sin, t for tan).
  • Each is undefined where its partner is zero, e.g. sec90∘sec 90° does not exist.
  • Signs follow the partner: cos120∘<0cos 120° < 0, so sec120∘<0sec 120° < 0.
