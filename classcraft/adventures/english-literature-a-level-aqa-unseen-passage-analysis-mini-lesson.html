What an unseen prose question tests

  • How the writer shapes meaning (AO2): what was chosen, and what each choice does.
  • In prose, look at narrative voice (who tells, how close to a character's mind), structure (where the passage turns), sentence shapes, imagery, and what is withheld.
  • You can't lean on knowing the book: everything you need is on the page.
  • A list of techniques isn't an answer; an argument about what the passage does is.
A-level skills · unseen proseScreen 2

How your exam asks this

  • AQA Literature A, Paper 2 Section B (open book for set texts): one compulsory question on an unseen prose extract, 25 marks. The extract comes from your option (WW1 and its aftermath, or Modern times), so you read it through that shared context. AS Paper 2 Section A is an unseen prose extract on love (25 marks).

A-level skills · unseen proseScreen 7

From choices to an argument

  • Lead with a claim about the whole passage, e.g. ‘Chopin lets us inside a desire her heroine can't act on.’
  • Follow the passage's movement (longing, then retreat), not a list of features.
  • Notice free indirect style: "she did not care what" is her thought in the narrator's voice.
  • Hedge where it's open: suggests, perhaps, may.
  • Link to your option's shared context only where the extract invites it.

Form as meaning

  • Drayton: a sonnet in iambic pentameter, three quatrains and a couplet (abab cdcd efef gg). Eight brisk lines of goodbye; lines 9–12 slow into a deathbed scene; the couplet reverses everything.
  • Hardy: three five-line stanzas of short, mostly three-stress lines, rhymed abccb. Each opening line chimes across the poem (dear, career, disappear), linking habit to death.
  • Hardy's last line sits in speech marks: the speaker's guess at her meaning, voiced as hers.

A-level skills · unseen poetry and comparisonScreen 7

Comparing, and weighing readings

  • A comparative claim: both speakers face a parting they won't quite accept. Drayton performs indifference to win the lover back; Hardy turns a wife's habit into a way to bear her death.
  • Hold two readings of each ending: Drayton's couplet as a sincere plea or a clever move; Hardy's last line as reproach or tenderness.
  • Place each in love poetry over time: the Renaissance sonnet of persuasion; the elegy.

Recap

Big ideas

  • An unseen prose question tests how the writer shapes meaning: choices and their effects, argued, not listed.
  • Read three times: what happens and whose mind; where it turns; which choices make the turn felt.
  • In Chopin's passage, free indirect style lets us share Mrs Baroda's longing, and a balanced sentence ties desire to retreat.
  • The AQA extract is read through your option's shared context; AS has an unseen prose extract on love.
  • A-level adds an argued reading, form and structure as meaning, comparison throughout and alternative readings.
  • Drayton's sonnet stages a brisk goodbye and then reverses it in the couplet; Hardy's short stanzas turn a habit into an elegy.
  • Hold two readings of an ending, and say which the evidence favours.
  • Compare two unseen love poems, using all five AOs, including love poetry over time.
