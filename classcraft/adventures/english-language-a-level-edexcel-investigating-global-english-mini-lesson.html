Investigating Language: Global English

English is now a global language with many varieties. This topic explores World Englishes, how English spread, and the debate over standards and ownership.

Global · spread

How English became global

English spread worldwide through colonisation, trade, science and technology, and now through media and the internet.

  • It is widely used as a lingua franca — a common language between speakers of different first languages.
  • There is no longer a single English but many World Englishes, each with its own features and prestige.

David Crystal has written extensively on English as a global language and the scale of its spread.

Global · Kachru

Kachru's three circles

Braj Kachru modelled World Englishes as three concentric circles:

  • Inner circle — English as a first language (UK, USA, Australia): norm-providing.
  • Outer circle — English with official or historical status (India, Nigeria, Singapore): norm-developing.
  • Expanding circle — English as a foreign language (China, Brazil): norm-dependent.

Attribute: the three circles are Kachru's model. It has been debated but remains a key framework.

Global · varieties

Pidgins, creoles and lingua franca

Contact between languages produces new varieties:

  • Pidgin — a simplified contact language with no native speakers, used for trade or work.
  • Creole — a pidgin that has become a community's first language, developing full grammar.
  • English as a Lingua Franca (ELF) — English used between non-native speakers, often flexibly, prioritising communication over native norms.

Key distinction: a pidgin has no native speakers; a creole does.

Global · attitudes

Standards, ownership and attitudes

Global English raises debates about ownership and standards:

  • Who owns English now that most speakers are non-native? Many argue no single variety is correct.
  • Prestige attaches unevenly: inner-circle varieties often carry more overt prestige, which is contested.
  • Concerns about English as a killer language displacing others sit alongside celebration of local, hybrid Englishes.

Evaluation: weigh the benefits of a shared global language against threats to linguistic diversity.
