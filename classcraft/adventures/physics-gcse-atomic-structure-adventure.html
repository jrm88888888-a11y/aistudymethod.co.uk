Stop 1 · The basics

What is an atom?

Everything is made of tiny particles called atoms.

Your chair, the water you drink, the air you breathe — and even you — are all built from atoms. 🌍💧🪑

An atom is the smallest unit of an element that still has the properties of that element.

Stop 3 · Scale

Tiny, but not equally tiny

If an atom were a football stadium 🏟️, the nucleus would be a single grain of rice at the centre spot. Almost the whole atom is empty space.

Yet the nucleus holds almost all the mass — because protons and neutrons are about 1836 times heavier than electrons.

Stop 4 · Atomic number

The atom's identity number

The number of protons in an atom is called its atomic number (Z). It defines which element the atom is.

Z = 1 → Hydrogen Z = 6 → Carbon Z = 8 → Oxygen Z = 11 → Sodium Z = 26 → Iron

An atom is neutral overall: the number of electrons equals the number of protons. So protons = electrons in a neutral atom.

Stop 5 · Mass number

Mass number: protons + neutrons

The mass number (A) is the protons + neutrons added together. Electrons are too light to count.

We write it like this:

2311Na
mass = 23 · Z = 11

So sodium-23 has 11 protons, 11 electrons, and 23 − 11 = 12 neutrons.

Tip: number of neutrons = mass number − atomic number.

Stop 6 · Isotopes

What are isotopes?

Isotopes are atoms of the same element (same number of protons) but with different numbers of neutrons — so different mass numbers.

Carbon has three natural isotopes:

¹²C
6 p · 6 n
mass 12
¹³C
6 p · 7 n
mass 13
¹⁴C
6 p · 8 n
mass 14

All three are still carbon — they all have 6 protons. They only differ in the neutrons.

Stop 7 · Ions

Ions: when atoms gain or lose electrons

An atom is normally neutral. But if it loses electrons it becomes a positive ion (cation). If it gains electrons it becomes a negative ion (anion).

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻ (lost 1 electron) Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻ (gained 1 electron)

The number of protons never changes when an atom becomes an ion — only the electrons change. So the element stays the same.
