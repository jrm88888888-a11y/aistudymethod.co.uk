Mission 1

Mission 1: An electron and a proton feel the same force between charged plates. Which swerves more, and by how much?

Make a guess: An electron and a proton feel the same force between charged plates. Which swerves more, and by how much?

Mission 1

The force on a charge in a field

  • F = qE, the same everywhere in a uniform field.
  • Positive charge: force along the field (towards the negative plate).
  • Negative charge: force against the field (towards the positive plate).
  • Between parallel plates: E = V/d. 400 V across 2.0 cm → E = 2.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹.
  • Electron or proton there: |F| = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁴ = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ N. Same size, opposite directions.
Mission 1

Same force, different acceleration

  • Constant force → constant acceleration: a = F/m.
  • Electron: a = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 3.5 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻².
  • Proton: a = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ / 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ = 1.9 × 10¹² m s⁻².
  • The electron's mass is about 1/1800 of the proton's, so its acceleration is about 1800 times greater.
  • Gravity is negligible: the electron's weight is about 10⁻²⁹ N, some 10¹⁴ times smaller than 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ N.
Quick check
?Two horizontal parallel plates, 2.5 cm apart in a vacuum, have a potential difference of 150 V between them. What is the magnitude of the acceleration of an electron between the plates? Gravity is negligible. Take e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and me = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg.
Mission 1

Accelerating through a potential difference

  • Work done by the field = qV = gain in kinetic energy.
  • From rest: ½mv² = eV (electron or proton).
  • Worked example: electron through 2000 V.
  • v = √(2eV/m) = √(2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2000 / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 2.65 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹.
  • That is about 9% of the speed of light, so ½mv² is still accurate.
Quick check
?Two parallel plates, 2.0 cm apart in a vacuum, have a potential difference of 500 V between them. An electron is released from rest at the negative plate and accelerates across the gap. What is its speed when it reaches the positive plate? Take e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and me = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg.
Quick check
?An electron moves horizontally into the region between two horizontal parallel plates, midway between them and at right angles to the uniform electric field between the plates. It is deflected towards the positive plate. Gravity is negligible. Which describes the motion of the electron while it is between the plates?
Mission 1

Deflection: a projectile in an electric field

  • Particle enters at right angles to the field (parallel to the plates).
  • Along the plates: no force → constant velocity v.
  • Across the plates: constant force → constant acceleration a = F/m.
  • Path between the plates: a parabola, like a ball thrown horizontally.
  • Time between plates: t = L/v. Sideways shift: y = ½at².
  • After the plates: no force → straight line.
Mission 1

Worked example: deflection

  • Electron at 2.65 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ enters midway between plates 5.0 cm long, 2.0 cm apart, 400 V.
  • a = eV/(md) = 3.5 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻² (as for the electron on the earlier screen).
  • t = L/v = 0.050 / 2.65 × 10⁷ = 1.9 × 10⁻⁹ s.
  • y = ½at² = ½ × 3.5 × 10¹⁵ × (1.9 × 10⁻⁹)² = 6.3 mm. Less than 10 mm, so it misses the plate.
  • Sideways velocity at exit: vy = at = 6.6 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹.
  • A proton at the same speed: about 1800 times less deflection.
Mission check

?A proton and an alpha particle are placed, one at a time, at the same point in a uniform electric field. The alpha particle has twice the charge of the proton and about four times its mass. Gravity is negligible. What is the ratio (acceleration of the alpha particle) / (acceleration of the proton)?

Same field, acceleration = charge × E ÷ mass. The electron and proton feel equal forces, but the electron has about 1/1800 of the mass: about 1800 times the acceleration, so it swerves far more.

Mission 2

Mission 2: A wire carrying a current lies between the poles of a magnet. Which way is it pushed?

Make a guess: A wire carrying a current lies between the poles of a magnet. Which way is it pushed?

Mission 2

The motor effect

  • A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force.
  • The force is at right angles to both the current and the field.
  • No force when the current is parallel to the field.
  • Reverse the current or the field: the force reverses.
Mission 2

Which way?

  • Right-hand rule: fingers along B, thumb along I; the force comes out of your palm.
Quick check
?A straight wire lies in the plane of the page and carries a current towards the right of the page. A uniform magnetic field in the plane of the page is directed towards the top of the page. What is the direction of the force on the wire?
Mission 2

How big?

  • F = BIL sin θ
  • θ = angle between the current and the field. Largest at 90°, zero at 0°.
  • The length is the length inside the field.
  • B is in tesla (T).
Mission 2

Magnetic flux density

  • B is the magnetic field strength.
  • Rearranged (current ⟂ field): B = F/(IL): force per unit current per unit length.
  • 1 tesla: the field in which a wire carrying 1 A at right angles to the field feels 1 N on each metre.
  • 1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹.
Mission 2

Worked example

  • A 4.0 cm length of wire carries 2.5 A at right angles to a 0.15 T field.
  • Force = 0.15 × 2.5 × 0.040 = 0.015 N.
  • Turn the wire to 30° to the field: F = 0.015 × sin 30° = 0.0075 N.
  • Backwards: 0.012 N on a 5.0 cm wire carrying 3.0 A at right angles. B = force ÷ (current × length) = 0.012 ÷ (3.0 × 0.050) = 0.080 T.
Quick check
?A straight wire 0.40 m long carries a current of 3.0 A. Only 0.25 m of the wire lies inside a uniform magnetic field of strength 0.080 T, and the wire makes an angle of 30° with the direction of the field. What is the magnitude of the force on the wire?
Mission check

?A long straight wire carries a current vertically upwards. It is in a uniform horizontal magnetic field that points due north. In which direction is the magnetic force on the wire?

The force is at right angles to both the current and the field, and its size is B × current × length when they are perpendicular: so a wire between the poles is pushed sideways, out of the gap or into it, never towards a pole.

Mission 3

Mission 3: A magnetic field can steer a beam of charged particles. Can it also make them go faster?

Make a guess: A magnetic field can steer a beam of charged particles. Can it also make them go faster?

Mission 3

Force on a moving charge

  • A current is moving charge, so a single moving charge in a field also feels a force.
  • F = qvB sin θ
  • θ = angle between v and B.
  • Zero force on a charge at rest, or moving along the field.
  • Example: an electron at 3.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ at right angles to a 0.20 T field: F = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 3.0 × 10⁶ × 0.20 = 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ N.
Mission 3

Which way?

  • Right-hand rule for a positive charge: fingers along B, thumb along v, force out of the palm. Negative charge: force the other way.
  • The force is always ⟂ to the velocity, so it does no work: the speed and kinetic energy never change.
Quick check
?An electron moves towards the right of the page, in the plane of the page, through a uniform magnetic field directed into the page. What is the direction of the magnetic force on the electron at that instant?
Mission 3

Circular paths

  • Only the direction changes. Uniform field, v ⟂ B: a circle.
  • The magnetic force is the centripetal force: magnetic force = mv²/r.
  • r = mv/(Bq)
  • Faster or heavier → bigger circle. Stronger field or more charge → tighter circle.
  • Time for one orbit: T = 2πr/v = 2πm/(Bq).
Mission 3

Worked example

  • A proton (m = 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, q = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) at 2.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹, at right angles to a 0.50 T field.
  • Radius: r = mv/(Bq) = (1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ × 2.0 × 10⁶) ÷ (0.50 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 0.042 m.
  • Period: T = 2πm/(Bq) = 1.3 × 10⁻⁷ s.
  • Double the field: r halves to 0.021 m, and T halves too, to 6.6 × 10⁻⁸ s.
Quick check
?A proton moves in a circle in a uniform magnetic field that is perpendicular to its velocity. A second proton moves at twice the speed of the first, perpendicular to the same field. Compared with the first proton, what are the radius of the second proton's circle and the time it takes to complete one revolution?
Mission 3

Charge-to-mass ratio from the path

  • Accelerate the particles through a pd V: qV = ½mv².
  • Send them at right angles into a uniform field B and measure the radius r of the circle.
  • r = mv/(qB) and v² = 2qV/m combine to give q/m = 2V/(B²r²).
  • Electrons in a fine-beam tube: V, B (from the coil current) and r (from a scale) give e/mₑ ≈ 1.76 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹.
Quick check
?Positive ions of a single type, initially at rest, are accelerated through a potential difference of 1500 V. They then enter a uniform magnetic field of strength 0.25 T at right angles to their velocity, travel through a semicircle and strike a detector 6.3 cm from the point where they entered the field. What is the charge-to-mass ratio q/m of the ions?
Quick check
?In a velocity selector, uniform electric and magnetic fields act at right angles to each other and to the velocity of the particles, so that the electric and magnetic forces on a proton are in opposite directions. Protons moving at speed v0 pass straight through undeflected. Gravity is negligible. Which statement about this velocity selector is correct?
Mission 3

Crossed fields

  • In both E and B fields, a charge feels two independent forces: qE (any speed) and Bqv (depends on speed).
  • E and B at right angles, both ⟂ to the beam, forces opposite. Straight through only if qE = Bqv: v = E/B. This is a velocity selector.
  • Faster particles: magnetic force wins. Slower: electric force wins.
  • Example: plates 1.0 cm apart, 400 V, B = 0.050 T. E = 4.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹; v = E/B = 8.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹.
Mission check

?Two protons enter two separate regions at the same speed. Region 1 contains only a uniform electric field; region 2 contains only a uniform magnetic field. Each proton enters at right angles to the field lines. Gravity is negligible. A short time later, which statement is correct?

The magnetic force is always at right angles to the velocity. It changes direction, never speed, so a magnetic field alone can bend a beam but never accelerate it to a higher energy. That is why particle accelerators use electric fields to speed particles up; magnetic fields only steer them.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Two parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. Do they attract or repel, and why?

Make a guess: Two parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. Do they attract or repel, and why?

Mission 4

Force between parallel wires

  • Wire 1 makes a field B1 at wire 2, ⟂ to wire 2. It is proportional to I1 and falls as 1/r.
  • Wire 2 carries I2 in that field, so it feels F = B1I2L.
  • Per unit length: F/L = μ0I1I2/(2πr), r = separation.
  • Same direction → attract. Opposite directions → repel.
Mission 4

Worked example

  • Two long parallel wires 5.0 cm apart each carry 10 A in the same direction.
  • F/L = μ0I1I2/(2πr) = 2 × 10⁻⁷ × 10 × 10 ÷ 0.050 = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ N m⁻¹.
  • On 2.0 m of wire: 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ N, towards the other wire.
Quick check
?Two long straight parallel wires in air are 4.0 cm apart and carry currents of 5.0 A and 8.0 A. What is the magnitude of the magnetic force on a 1.5 m length of the wire carrying 8.0 A? Take μ0 = 4π × 10⁻⁷ T m A⁻¹ for air.
Quick check
?Two long straight parallel wires, X and Y, are 5.0 cm apart in air. X carries 2.0 A and Y carries 6.0 A, in the same direction. What is the magnetic force on a 1.0 m length of each wire?
Mission check

?A light, loosely wound coil of wire, like a soft spring, hangs vertically. A large steady current is passed through it. What happens to the length of the coil?

Parallel currents in the same direction attract: each wire sits in the other's field, and the force on it points towards the other wire. Opposite currents repel.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • In a uniform field F = qE (positive charges along E, negative against it), so a = qE/m is constant; light particles accelerate most.
  • Through a potential difference V, a charge gains kinetic energy qV.
  • Entering at right angles: constant velocity along the plates, constant acceleration across them, so a parabola.

Mission 2

  • Force on a current in a field: at right angles to both; zero when parallel.
  • Direction: right-hand rule.
  • F = BIL sin θ.
  • 1 T: 1 N on 1 m of wire carrying 1 A at right angles to the field.

Mission 3

  • F = qvB sin θ.
  • The force is ⟂ to v, so speed and kinetic energy never change.
  • v ⟂ B gives a circle: r = mv/(Bq) = p/(Bq); the period does not depend on speed.
  • Crossed fields pass undeflected only particles with v = E/B.

Mission 4

  • F/L = μ0I1I2/2πr.
  • Parallel wires: same direction attract, opposite repel; equal and opposite forces.
