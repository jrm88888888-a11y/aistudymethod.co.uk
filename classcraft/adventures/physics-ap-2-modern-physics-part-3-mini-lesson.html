Mission 1

Mission 1: A helium nucleus is lighter than the protons and neutrons that make it. Where did the missing mass go?

Make a guess: A helium nucleus is lighter than the protons and neutrons that make it. Where did the missing mass go?

Mission 1

Mass is a form of energy

  • E = mc²: every mass m has a rest energy mc².
  • c² is huge: 1 kg ↔ 9.0 × 10¹⁶ J.
  • Every energy change changes mass, chemical ones too; there Δm is far too small to measure.
  • Nuclear reactions: Δm is big enough to measure. The energy released leaves as kinetic energy of the products and as photons.
Mission 1

Units for tiny masses

  • Atomic mass unit: 1 u = 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
  • 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.
  • 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J.
  • 1 u = 931 MeV/c².
  • A mass in MeV/c²: multiply by c² and you have the energy in MeV. No c needed.
Quick check
?A nucleus of mass M, initially at rest, splits into two fragments of masses m1 and m2 and no other particles. Which expression gives the total kinetic energy of the two fragments just after the split?
Mission 1

Where the released energy goes

  • Total mass falls → energy released, as kinetic energy of the products and as photons.
  • Fission: a heavy nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei.
  • Nucleus at rest splits in two: momentum before = 0, so the products have equal and opposite momenta.
  • Same size of momentum: the lighter product moves faster and gets most of the kinetic energy.
  • Binding energy: energy needed to pull a nucleus apart into its nucleons.
  • Products with more total binding energy than the parent: energy is released; the split can happen on its own.
  • Products with less total binding energy: the split needs an energy input.
Quick check
?The bar chart shows the total binding energy of each of two nuclei, X and Y, and the total binding energy of the fragments that each nucleus would form by fission. Which claim is supported by the chart?
Mission check

?In one fusion reaction, a deuterium nucleus and a tritium nucleus fuse: ²1H + ³1H → ⁴2He + ¹0n. The atomic masses are ²H: 2.01410 u, ³H: 3.01605 u and ⁴He: 4.00260 u (the electron masses on the two sides cancel), and the mass of a neutron is 1.00866 u. How much energy is released in one reaction? Use 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s.

The missing mass left as energy: here 0.01889 u of mass became 2.82 × 10⁻¹² J of kinetic energy of the products.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Splitting a uranium nucleus gives out energy. How can breaking something apart release energy?

Make a guess: Splitting a uranium nucleus gives out energy. How can breaking something apart release energy?

Mission 2

Nuclear fission

  • Fission: a heavy nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei of comparable mass, plus a few neutrons (2 or 3 for uranium-235).
  • Spontaneous fission: no trigger (e.g. californium-252). Rare for most nuclei.
  • Induced fission: the nucleus absorbs a neutron first.
  • ²³⁵92U + ¹0n → ²³⁶92U → ¹⁴¹56Ba + ⁹²36Kr + 3 ¹0n
  • Balance it: nucleon numbers 235 + 1 = 141 + 92 + 3(1) = 236; proton numbers 92 = 56 + 36.
Quick check
?Which of the following is an example of nuclear fission?
Mission 2

Where the energy comes from

  • The fragments are more tightly bound: their total binding energy is greater than the parent's.
  • More binding → less total mass → the mass difference is released: E = Δmc².
  • About 200 MeV per fission, mostly as kinetic energy of the fragments; the rest as neutrons, γ photons and later decays.
  • Fragments less tightly bound than the parent: no release; that split would need an energy input.
Mission check

?Plutonium-240 nuclei can split on their own into two fragments and two free neutrons. In one such split, the total binding energy rises from about 1810 MeV (the plutonium nucleus) to about 2010 MeV (the two fragments). How much energy is released, and why?

Breaking a heavy nucleus apart releases energy because the pieces are more tightly bound than the original. Their total mass is less, and the mass lost leaves as about 200 MeV per fission.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Joining nuclei releases energy, and so does splitting them. How can both be true?

Make a guess: Joining nuclei releases energy, and so does splitting them. How can both be true?

Mission 3

Nuclear fusion

  • Fusion: two light nuclei join to form a heavier nucleus, often with a spare particle.
  • ²1H + ³1H → ⁴2He + ¹0n + 17.6 MeV
  • Per nucleon, fusion gives more: 17.6 MeV ÷ 5 = 3.5 MeV, against about 0.85 MeV for uranium fission.

Quick check

Mission check

?The fusion of two hydrogen-2 nuclei and the fission of a uranium-235 nucleus both release energy. Which statement explains this for both?

In both fusion and fission the products are more tightly bound, so their total mass is less: E = Δmc². Both processes move nuclei toward the most tightly bound size, near iron. Light nuclei fuse up to it; heavy nuclei split down to it. Either way the total mass falls, and that mass leaves as energy.

Mission 4

Antimatter

  • Every particle has an antiparticle: the same mass, the opposite charge.
  • Positron, e⁺ (or β⁺): the antiparticle of the electron. Charge +e, electron's mass.
  • Antiparticles are real: positrons come out of β⁺ decay, and hospitals use them in PET scans.
Quick check

Quick check

Mission check

?A beam of electrons enters the region between two oppositely charged parallel plates with velocity v, as shown, and follows track 3. A beam of positrons then enters with the same velocity. The figure shows four possible tracks, numbered 1 to 4 (not drawn to scale). Which track do the positrons follow?

An antiparticle has the same mass as its particle but the opposite charge. In a field it bends by the same amount, the opposite way: a mirror-image track.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Every energy change changes mass: E = mc². Nuclear reactions release energy because the products have less mass.
  • Products share the released energy as kinetic energy (equal and opposite momenta) and photons.

Mission 2

  • Fission: a heavy nucleus splits into two smaller nuclei plus neutrons; spontaneous or neutron-induced.
  • Energy is released because the fragments are more tightly bound: less total mass, E = Δmc², about 200 MeV per fission.

Mission 3

  • Fusion: light nuclei join; the products are more tightly bound, so their total mass is less and energy is released.

Mission 4

  • Every particle has an antiparticle with the same mass (and rest energy) and the opposite charge.
  • α = helium-4 nucleus (+2e); positron e⁺ = antielectron (+e, electron's mass); ν and ν̄ are uncharged with negligible mass and feel only the weak force and gravity.
