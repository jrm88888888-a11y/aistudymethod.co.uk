Mission 1

Mission 1: A cannon fires a ball forward. Why does the cannon roll backward, and more slowly than the ball?

Make a guess: A cannon fires a ball forward. Why does the cannon roll backward, and more slowly than the ball?

Mission 1

Momentum

  • Momentum p = mv: mass × velocity.
  • Unit: kg·m/s (the same as N s).
  • A vector: same direction as the velocity. Choose a positive direction; the other way is negative.
  • A 0.20 kg ball hits a wall at 5.0 m/s and bounces straight back at 5.0 m/s. Its momentum changes, from +1.0 to −1.0 kg·m/s, though its speed does not.
  • Change in momentum: Δp = p − p0.
Quick check
?A baseball of mass m moving horizontally with speed v is struck by a bat. It leaves the bat moving horizontally with speed 3v/2 in the opposite direction. Taking the ball's initial direction of motion as positive, what is the change in the ball's momentum?
Mission 1

Conservation of momentum

  • The total momentum of a system stays constant unless a net external force acts on it.
  • Why: in a collision, A pushes B and B pushes A with equal and opposite forces (Newton's third law), for the same time.
  • So A's change in momentum is equal and opposite to B's. The total does not change.
  • Total momentum before = total momentum after. Add the momenta with signs.
Mission 1

Worked example: a collision

  • Glider A, 1.5 kg, moves at 4.0 m/s along an air track into glider B, 0.50 kg, at rest. They stick together.
  • Before: p = 1.5 × 4.0 + 0.50 × 0 = 6.0 kg·m/s.
  • After: (1.5 + 0.50) × v = 2.0v.
  • 2.0v = 6.0, so v = 3.0 m/s, in A's original direction.
Quick check
?Two carts collide on a level track. Friction and air resistance are negligible. During the collision, Earth exerts a gravitational force and the track exerts a normal force on each cart. Is the total momentum of the two-cart system constant during the collision?
Mission 1

Explosions

  • Everything starts at rest: total momentum = 0. So it is still 0 afterward.
  • Two pieces: they move off in opposite directions, with equal and opposite momenta.
  • 2.0 kg and 3.0 kg blocks pushed apart by a spring. The 3.0 kg block moves at 1.2 m/s.
  • 2.0 × v = 3.0 × 1.2 = 3.6, so v = 1.8 m/s the other way. Lighter piece: faster.
  • Kinetic energy increases (0 → 5.4 J): it comes from energy stored in the compressed spring (or in the explosive, for a cannon).
Quick check
?Two blocks, of masses 1.0 kg and 4.0 kg, are held at rest on a horizontal surface with negligible friction, with a compressed spring between them. When they are released, the 1.0 kg block moves off at 2.0 m/s. Which statement about the 4.0 kg block is correct?
Mission 1

Elastic or inelastic?

  • Kinetic energy K = ½mv².
  • Momentum is conserved in every collision (no net external force). Kinetic energy is not always.
  • Elastic: total kinetic energy unchanged.
  • Inelastic: total kinetic energy decreases. It becomes internal (thermal) energy, sound, and energy of permanent deformation.
  • Perfectly inelastic: the objects stick together and share one velocity. Biggest loss of kinetic energy.
  • Total energy is always conserved; only the kinetic part changes.
  • Test it with the glider collision: kinetic energy before = ½ × 1.5 × 4.0² = 12 J; after = ½ × 2.0 × 3.0² = 9.0 J. 3.0 J transformed into other forms: inelastic.
Quick check
?A 0.50 kg cart moving at 5.0 m/s collides with a 3.0 kg cart at rest on a level track with negligible friction. After the collision, the 0.50 kg cart moves backward at 1.0 m/s and the 3.0 kg cart moves forward at 1.0 m/s. How much of the system's kinetic energy is transformed into other forms of energy?
Mission check

?A skater of mass M stands at rest on level ice, where friction is negligible, and throws a ball of mass m horizontally with speed v relative to the ice. Which expression gives the skater's velocity immediately after the throw?

Total momentum starts at zero, so it stays zero: equal and opposite momenta. The cannon recoils for the same reason: same mv as the ball, much bigger m, so a much smaller v.

Mission 2

Force is the rate of change of momentum

  • Fnet = dp/dt: net external force = rate of change of momentum.
  • Constant mass: Δp/Δt = mΔv/Δt = ma. F = ma is the special case.
  • On a p–t graph, the slope is the net force. Flat line: zero net force.
Mission 2

Impulse

  • Impulse J = Favg Δt (in general, J = ∫F dt).
  • J = Δp: the impulse equals the change in momentum.
  • Unit: N s, the same as kg·m/s.
  • A vector: in the direction of the net force, which need not be the direction of motion.
Mission 2

Worked example: a rebound

  • A 0.060 kg ball hits a wall at 25 m/s and rebounds at 15 m/s. Contact time 0.020 s.
  • Take away from the wall as positive.
  • v0 = −25 m/s, v = +15 m/s.
  • Δp = m(v − v0) = 0.060 × (15 − (−25)) = 2.4 N·s, away from the wall.
  • Average force = 2.4 ÷ 0.020 = 120 N, away from the wall.
  • Trap: 0.060 × (15 − 25) = −0.60 N·s. Velocity is a vector; the direction reverses.
Quick check
?Pucks A and B are at rest on level ice with negligible friction. Puck A has mass m, and puck B has mass 2m. Each puck is pushed by the same constant horizontal force F for the same time interval Δt. After the push, puck A has momentum of magnitude pA. What is the magnitude of puck B's momentum after the push?
Mission 2

Area under a force–time graph

  • Real impacts: the force rises and falls. It is not constant.
  • Impulse = area under the F–t graph = change in momentum.
  • Average force = area ÷ contact time.
  • Area below the time axis = impulse in the negative direction.
  • The area is J = ∫F dt.
Quick check
?A cart of mass 1.5 kg is at rest on a level track at t = 0. The graph shows the net force F exerted on the cart as a function of time t. What is the speed of the cart at t = 4.0 s?
Quick check
?The graph shows the momentum p of a cart on a straight track as a function of time t. The cart starts from rest at t = 0, and the direction in which it then moves is positive. Which claim about the net force exerted on the cart is supported by the graph?
Mission 2

Longer contact, smaller force

  • Stopping: the change in momentum is fixed by the mass and the speed.
  • Longer stopping time → smaller average force.
  • A 70 kg occupant stops from 14 m/s: Δp = 980 N·s.
  • Seat belt and crumple zone, 0.12 s: F ≈ 8.2 kN. Hard dashboard, 0.012 s: F ≈ 82 kN.
  • Same idea: airbags, bicycle helmets, packaging foam, bending your knees on landing.
Mission check

?Two identical 0.50 kg gliders on an air track each move at 2.0 m/s toward an obstacle. Glider 1 hits a steel buffer and bounces straight back at 2.0 m/s; the contact lasts 0.010 s. Glider 2 hits a foam pad and stops; the contact lasts 0.040 s. How many times larger is the average force on glider 1 than the average force on glider 2?

Same change in momentum, longer time, smaller force. The airbag stops the performer over a much longer time than concrete, so the average force is many times smaller.

Mission 3

Mission 3: A dropped ball speeds up as it falls. Where does its extra momentum come from?

Make a guess: A dropped ball speeds up as it falls. Where does its extra momentum come from?

Mission 3

Momentum of a system

  • Total momentum of a system = vector sum of its parts' momenta: Σp = p1 + p2 + …
  • Signs matter: two equal carts moving toward each other at equal speeds have zero total momentum.
Quick check
?Two carts move along a straight track. Cart 1, of mass 3.0 kg, moves at +2.0 m/s, and cart 2, of mass 2.0 kg, moves at −1.0 m/s. What is the total momentum of the two-cart system?
Mission 3

Internal forces and the collision model

  • Internal forces come in third-law pairs, so they give equal and opposite impulses: ΔpA = −ΔpB, whatever the masses.
  • Collision model: during a short collision the internal forces are much larger than external ones (friction, air resistance). Ignore the external ones for that instant.
  • Each object can be an object model: only its motion just before and just after matters.
  • An explosion is the same, with stored energy inside the system pushing the parts apart.
Quick check
?Cart 1, of mass 1.0 kg, moves in the positive direction along a level track with negligible friction and collides with cart 2, of mass 2.0 kg, which is moving in the negative direction at 1.0 m/s. The graph shows the force exerted on cart 1 by cart 2 as a function of time t during the collision. What is the momentum of cart 2 just after the collision?
Mission 3

External forces move momentum across the boundary

  • Momentum is conserved in every interaction: never created or destroyed, only transferred.
  • Net external force zero: the system's total momentum is constant.
  • Net external force not zero: momentum flows to or from the environment: Δpsystem = Fnet Δt, with Fnet the net external force.
  • Falling ball, system = ball: gravity is external, so the ball's momentum grows.
  • System = ball + Earth: gravity is internal. Earth gains equal upward momentum; the total stays constant. (Earth's velocity change is tiny: huge mass.)
Quick check
?A cart of mass m moves with speed v along a level track toward a wall. It hits the wall and rebounds along the same line with speed v/3. Taking the cart's initial direction of motion as positive and the cart as the system, which expression gives the momentum transferred to the system from its surroundings during the collision?
Mission 3

Momentum and the center of mass

  • A system has one center-of-mass velocity: vcm = Σp / Σm.
  • No net external force: Σp is constant, so vcm is constant, through any collision or explosion.
  • 2.0 kg cart at +2.0 m/s and 1.0 kg cart at −1.0 m/s: vcm = (4.0 − 1.0) ÷ 3.0 = +1.0 m/s, before and after they collide.
Quick check
?Cart 1 rolls along a level track toward an identical cart 2, which is at rest. After they collide, cart 1 is at rest and cart 2 moves off with cart 1's original velocity. A student says that the collision cannot be elastic, because cart 1's kinetic energy changed. Which response to the student is correct?
Mission 3

Kinetic energy: parts and the whole

  • In an elastic collision each object's own K can change: a light cart that bounces back off a heavy cart at rest gives some of its K to the heavy cart.
  • Inelastic: nonconservative internal forces (deforming, rubbing) transform K into internal (thermal) energy and sound.
  • Momentum is still conserved: those forces are internal, in equal and opposite pairs.
  • Energy is never "turned into momentum": they are different quantities, each conserved in its own way.
Mission check

?A box slides across a level floor and comes to rest because of friction. Which statement about the box's momentum is correct?

Same idea, opposite direction. The falling ball's extra downward momentum comes from the Earth, which gains an equal upward momentum. For the ball–Earth system, the total never changes.

Mission 4

Mission 4: When two pucks collide and fly off at angles, what exactly stays the same?

Make a guess: When two pucks collide and fly off at angles, what exactly stays the same?

Mission 4

Momentum in two dimensions

  • Momentum is a vector. Split each momentum into x and y components.
  • With no net external force (in that direction), each component of the total momentum is conserved separately.
  • Total px before = total px after. Total py before = total py after.
  • As a picture: the momentum vectors after the collision, added tip to tail, rebuild the momentum vector before.
  • Speeds are not conserved, and kinetic energy only in an elastic collision.
Quick check
?On a level air table, where friction is negligible, puck 1 of mass m and puck 2 of mass 2m slide toward the collision point shown in the diagram, each with speed v. The pucks collide and stick together. How does the y-component of the combined pucks' momentum immediately after the collision compare with its x-component?
Quick check
?On a level air table with negligible friction, puck 1 slides in the +x-direction toward puck 2, which is at rest. After they collide, puck 1 moves off at an angle above the +x-axis (a positive y-component of velocity). Which statement about puck 2's motion just after the collision must be true?
Mission 4

Worked example: a glancing collision

  • Puck A, 0.20 kg, at 3.0 m/s along +x hits identical puck B at rest. A leaves at 1.5 m/s, 60° above the x-axis.
  • x: 0.20 × 3.0 = 0.20 × 1.5 cos 60° + 0.20 × (B's vx) → B's vx = 2.25 m/s.
  • y: 0 = 0.20 × 1.5 sin 60° + 0.20 × (B's vy) → B's vy = −1.30 m/s.
  • vB = √(2.25² + 1.30²) = 2.6 m/s, at 30° below the x-axis.
  • Check: K = 0.90 J before and after, so elastic; equal masses then leave at 90° to each other.
Quick check
?An object of mass 4m at rest on a frictionless horizontal surface explodes into three pieces that slide along the surface. Immediately after the explosion, a piece of mass m moves at speed v in the +x-direction and a piece of mass 2m moves at speed 2v in the +y-direction. What is the speed of the third piece, of mass m?
Mission check

?An open cart of mass 3m rolls at speed v0 along a level track with negligible friction. A ball of mass m falls vertically into the cart and stays in it; just before it lands, the ball's speed is 4v0. What is the speed of the cart, with the ball in it, just after the ball lands?

Conserve each component on its own, wherever no net external force acts in that direction. For the pucks: total px and total py. Here only the horizontal component is conserved: the track's upward push is an external force, so vertical momentum passes to Earth.

Mission 5

Mission 5: A belt carries sand along at a steady speed. Why must its motor keep pushing?

Make a guess: A belt carries sand along at a steady speed. Why must its motor keep pushing?

Mission 5

Impulse from a changing force

  • J = ∫Fnet(t) dt, from t1 to t2. The area under F–t, done exactly.
  • J = Δp: the impulse–momentum theorem.
  • So, starting from v0: v(t) = v0 + (1/m)∫0ᵗ Fnet dt (constant m).
  • Average force = J/Δt.
Mission 5

Worked example

  • A 2.0 kg puck starts from rest. F(t) = 12t − 3t² (N, t in s), from t = 0 to t = 4.0 s.
  • J = ∫0⁴ (12t − 3t²) dt = [6t² − t³]0⁴ = 96 − 64 = 32 N·s.
  • v = J/m = 32/2.0 = 16 m/s.
  • Peak force at dF/dt = 0: t = 2.0 s, F = 12 N. Average force = 32/4.0 = 8.0 N.
  • Check the sign of F over the interval: here F ≥ 0 throughout, so no area cancels.
Quick check
?The net force on an object moving along the x-axis is F(t) = 3.0 + 6.0t², where F is in newtons and t is in seconds. What is the impulse delivered to the object from t = 0 to t = 2.0 s?
Mission 5

Force from a momentum function

  • Fnet = dp/dt: differentiate p(t).
  • Example: p = 3.0t² + 2.0t (p in kg·m/s, t in s). F = dp/dt = 6.0t + 2.0. At t = 2.0 s: F = 14 N.
  • If p is decreasing, dp/dt is negative: the net force opposes the motion.
  • Constant mass: dp/dt = m dv/dt = ma. Newton's second law is the special case.
Quick check
?A cart moving in the +x direction is slowed by a resistive force, and its momentum is p(t) = p0e−t/τ, where p0 and τ are positive constants and t is time. What is the magnitude of the net force on the cart at the instant its momentum is p0/2?
Mission 5

When the mass changes

  • p = mv. If m changes: dp/dt = m dv/dt + v dm/dt.
  • Constant velocity, growing mass: Fnet = (dm/dt)v.
  • Sand drops onto a belt with no horizontal velocity. The belt moves at constant v.
  • Each second, mass dm/dt must be brought from 0 to v. That needs a force.
  • Gravel at 50 kg/s onto a belt at 2.0 m/s: F = 50 × 2.0 = 100 N.
  • The belt has a = 0, so the net force on the belt is zero: the motor's push balances the sand's backward friction on it.
  • That friction speeds up the sand. F = ma still holds grain by grain; F = v dm/dt adds up the grains.
Quick check
?For which of the following systems does Fnet = ma, where m is the mass of the system and a is its acceleration, give an incorrect value for the net external force on the system?
Mission 5

Where the motor's energy goes

  • Motor power: P = Fv = v² dm/dt.
  • Kinetic energy given to the sand: d(½mv²)/dt = ½v² dm/dt. Only half.
  • The other half: dissipated as the sand slides on the belt before it reaches speed v.
  • Gravel example: P = 100 × 2.0 = 200 W; kinetic energy gained at 100 W.
  • So assuming all the work becomes kinetic energy gives half the true force. Use momentum.
Quick check
?Sand falls vertically onto a horizontal conveyor belt, landing with no horizontal velocity, and a motor keeps the belt moving at a constant speed v. Starting at t = 0, the rate at which sand lands on the belt increases with time t as dm/dt = βt, where β is a positive constant. No sand leaves the belt during the time considered, and friction in the belt's rollers is negligible. What is the magnitude of the horizontal impulse the motor exerts on the belt from t = 0 to t = T? g is the acceleration due to gravity.
Mission check

?A hose sends water horizontally at 2.0 kg/s and 15 m/s against a vertical wall. The water stops at the wall and runs straight down it. What is the magnitude of the horizontal force the water exerts on the wall? Use g = 10 m/s².

The belt's push is not fighting roller friction or holding up the sand: each second, new sand must be given momentum, F = v dm/dt. The wall does the reverse: it takes the water's momentum away at the same kind of rate.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • p = mv, a vector. With no net external force, total momentum is conserved in every collision and explosion.
  • Elastic: total K unchanged. Inelastic: total K decreases; perfectly inelastic objects stick together.

Mission 2

  • J = Favg Δt = Δp: the area under F–t; the slope of p–t is the net force.

Mission 3

  • Momentum is conserved in every interaction; a system's momentum changes only when a net external force transfers momentum across its boundary.
  • Internal forces give equal and opposite impulses, so vcm = Σp/Σm stays constant without external force. Elastic collisions keep total K, not each object's K.

Mission 4

  • In 2D, each component of the total momentum is conserved separately when no net external force acts in that direction.

Mission 5

  • J = ∫F dt = Δp; v(t) follows by integrating F(t)/m. Fnet = dp/dt; for constant mass it is ma.
  • Constant velocity, changing mass: F = v dm/dt. Half the motor's work goes into kinetic energy; half is dissipated.
