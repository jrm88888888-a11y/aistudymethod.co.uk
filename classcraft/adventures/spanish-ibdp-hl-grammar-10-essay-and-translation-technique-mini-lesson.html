Essay & translation technique

The final backbone lesson turns grammar into marks. A strong IB Spanish B essay is glued together with connectives, argues with nuance (and the subjunctive), and avoids the classic translation traps. Master these and your writing reads like a fluent native's, not a word-for-word conversion. In IB Diploma Spanish B HL, this grammar powers your work across the five prescribed themes — Identities, Experiences, Human ingenuity, Social organization and Sharing the planet — and the two literary works you study, especially when you write across all five themes.

Connectives · contrast

Markers of contrast

Sophisticated essays signpost every turn. To introduce a contrast, reach for:

  • sin embargo — however
  • no obstante — nevertheless
  • en cambio — on the other hand / by contrast
  • aunque — although
  • a pesar de — in spite of

Signpost, don't list: a well-placed sin embargo tells the examiner you are building an argument, not just piling up sentences.

Connectives · add & conclude

Addition, consequence & structure

Beyond contrast, you need markers to add, to show consequence, and to structure the essay:

  • Addition: además (moreover), asimismo (likewise), también (also).
  • Consequence: por lo tanto (therefore), por consiguiente (consequently), así que (so).
  • Structure: por un lado… por otro lado (on one hand… on the other), en primer lugar (firstly), en resumen (in short), para concluir (to conclude).

Frame your essay: open with en primer lugar, weigh both sides with por un lado… por otro lado, and close with en resumen / para concluir.

Opinion with nuance

creo que vs no creo que

Here the subjunctive earns you marks. An affirmed opinion takes the indicative; a denied one takes the subjunctive:

  • Creo que es verdad. — I think it's true. (indicative)
  • No creo que sea verdad. — I don't think it's true. (subjunctive)
  • Es evidente que tiene razón. — It's obvious he's right. (indicative)

Exam gold: writing no creo que sea… shows you can flip a certainty into doubt with the subjunctive — a reliable IB-level flourish.

Translation · false traps

Don't translate word-for-word

The biggest translation losses come from copying English directly. Watch these:

  • False friends: "actually" ≠ actualmente (that means "currently"). Use en realidad.
  • ser vs estar: choose by trait vs state.
  • por vs para: cause vs goal.
  • Gender & agreement: adjectives must agree.
  • Word order: Spanish is more flexible — don't force the English order.

Rule: translate the meaning, not the words. If it sounds like English in Spanish clothing, rework it.

Translation · time expressions

"I have been studying for 3 years"

English present-perfect-continuous with "for" does NOT map onto Spanish tenses directly. Use one of two structures:

  • Llevo tres años estudiando. — literally "I carry three years studying".
  • Estudio desde hace tres años. — literally "I study since three years ago".

Never use a past tense here — Spanish keeps the verb in the present for an action still going on.

Recap

Your essay & translation toolkit

Contrast: sin embargo, no obstante, en cambio, aunque, a pesar de.

Add / consequence: además, asimismo, por lo tanto, por consiguiente, así que.

Structure: por un lado… por otro lado, en primer lugar, en resumen, para concluir.

Nuanced opinion: creo que + indicative but no creo que + subjunctive (no creo que sea); es evidente que + indicative.

Translation traps: false friends (en realidad not actualmente), ser/estar, por/para, agreement, word order; ongoing time = llevo tres años estudiando / estudio desde hace tres años.
