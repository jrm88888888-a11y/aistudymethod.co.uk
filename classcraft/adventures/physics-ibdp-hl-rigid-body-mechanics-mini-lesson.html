Mission 1

Mission 1: Every point on a spinning wheel turns through the same angle. So why does the rim move fastest?

Make a guess: Every point on a spinning wheel turns through the same angle. So why does the rim move fastest?

Mission 1

Angular displacement

  • Angle in radians: θ = s/r (arc length ÷ radius).
  • 1 revolution = 2π rad = 360°.
  • Angular displacement Δθ = θ − θ0: the angle turned about an axis.
  • Choose one sense as positive, usually counter-clockwise; clockwise is then negative.
  • Rigid system: keeps its shape, but different points move in different directions.
Quick check
?A disc on a fixed axle rotates 1.0 revolution counter-clockwise and then 0.25 revolution clockwise. Counter-clockwise is taken as positive. What is the disc's angular displacement?
Mission 1

Angular velocity and angular acceleration

  • Angular speed ω = Δθ/Δt, in rad s⁻¹.
  • Angular acceleration α = Δω/Δt, in rad s⁻².
  • Each has a linear twin: θ ↔ x, ω ↔ v, α ↔ a.
  • Graphs work the same way: gradient of θ–t = ω; gradient of ω–t = α; area under ω–t = Δθ; area under α–t = Δω.
Mission 1

Constant angular acceleration

  • Same equations as for constant linear acceleration, with angular symbols:
  • ωf = ωi + αt
  • Δθ = ωi t + ½αt²
  • ωf² = ωi² + 2αΔθ
  • Δθ = ((ωf + ωi)/2)t
  • Example: a wheel speeds up from 5.0 to 25 rad s⁻¹ in 4.0 s.
  • α = (25 − 5.0) ÷ 4.0 = 5.0 rad s⁻².
  • Δθ = average ω × t = 15 × 4.0 = 60 rad (about 9.5 revolutions).
Quick check
?A flywheel rotating at 1500 revolutions per minute is brought to rest with uniform angular deceleration in 20 s. Through what angular displacement does it turn while stopping? (1 revolution = 2π rad.)
Mission 1

Linking angular and linear

  • In a rigid body, every point has the same ω and α.
  • A point at distance r from the axis:
  • arc length Δs = rΔθ
  • speed v = rω
  • tangential acceleration aT = rα
  • So linear quantities grow in proportion to r. The rim moves fastest.
  • θ must be in radians for these to work.
Quick check
?A light string is wrapped around a pulley of radius 0.0600 m that turns on a fixed axle. The string is pulled so that it unwinds without slipping while the pulley turns through 20.0 rad. What length of string unwinds from the pulley?
Mission check

?A turntable speeds up with constant angular acceleration. A student claims that a dot near the edge of the turntable has a greater angular acceleration than a dot near the centre, because the edge dot's speed increases by more each second. Which statement correctly evaluates the claim?

Same angle, same ω, same α for every point. But a point at radius r covers arc rΔθ, so its speed is v = rω: the rim is fastest.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Why is a door handle on the edge far from the hinges, and not right next to them?

Make a guess: Why is a door handle on the edge far from the hinges, and not right next to them?

Mission 2

Torque

  • Torque τ: the turning effect of a force about an axis. Unit N m.
  • Only the force component perpendicular to the line from the axis (r) turns the body: F⊥ = F sin θ.
  • τ = Fr sin θ
  • θ = angle between F and r (r from the axis to where the force acts).
  • θ = 90°: greatest torque. θ = 0° or 180°: line of action passes through the axis, no torque.
  • Torque has a sense: clockwise or counter-clockwise.
Quick check
?A wheel turns on a fixed axle through its centre. Four forces, each of 20 N and each in the plane of the wheel, act on it one at a time. Which force exerts no torque about the axle?
Mission 2

The lever arm

  • Two ways to get the same torque:
  • r × (F sin θ): distance × perpendicular component of the force.
  • F × (r sin θ): force × lever arm.
  • Lever arm r⊥ = perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of action of the force.
  • Pick whichever is easier from the diagram.
Mission 2

Worked example

  • A spanner: force 60 N at the end, 0.25 m from the bolt, at 50° to the handle.
  • τ = rF sin θ = 0.25 × 60 × sin 50°
  • τ = 15 × 0.766 = 11 N m
  • Check with the lever arm: r⊥ = 0.25 × sin 50° = 0.19 m; 60 × 0.19 = 11 N m.
  • Trap: using cos 50° gives 9.6 N m. Wrong angle.
Quick check
?A door is pushed at its handle, 0.80 m from the hinge line, with a force of 25 N. The force makes an angle of 60° with the line from the hinge to the handle, and lies in the plane perpendicular to the hinge line. What is the magnitude of the torque of this force about the hinge?
Mission check

?The diagram shows four forces, A, B, C and D, exerted on a rod that can rotate about a fixed axis through point P, perpendicular to the page. The arrows are drawn to scale and start where each force is exerted. Which force exerts the torque of greatest magnitude about P?

Torque = force × lever arm. At the far edge of a door, the lever arm is largest, so the same torque needs the smallest push.

Mission 3

Mission 3: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Make a guess: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Mission 3

Rotational equilibrium on a see-saw

  • Add torques with signs: counter-clockwise +, clockwise − (or the reverse).
  • Rotational equilibrium: the resultant torque about any axis is zero: Στ = 0.
  • Full equilibrium also needs ΣF = 0. Then the torques balance about any point.
  • See-saw: child of weight W at 3d balances an adult of weight 3W at d. W × 3d = 3W × d.
  • The pivot pushes up with 4W, so the forces balance too.
Mission 3

Worked example

  • Uniform plank, 3.0 m long, weight 120 N, rests on a pivot 1.0 m from end A.
  • Its weight acts at its centre of mass, the midpoint: 1.5 m from A, so 0.50 m beyond the pivot.
  • Torque of the plank's weight about the pivot = 120 × 0.50 = 60 N m.
  • Weight W hung at A balances it: W × 1.0 = 60, so W = 60 N.
  • Resultant force also zero: pivot pushes up with 120 + 60 = 180 N.
  • Tip: take torques about the point where an unknown force acts. Its torque there is zero.
Quick check
?A uniform beam of length 5.0 m and weight 400 N rests horizontally on two supports, one at each end, P and Q. A person of weight 600 N stands on the beam 1.0 m from Q. What is the upward force from support P?
Mission 3

Balanced does not mean still

  • Zero resultant torque means no angular acceleration.
  • The body is at rest, or turns at constant angular velocity.
  • A turntable turning steadily: the motor's torque balances the friction torque at the bearing.
  • Rotational and translational equilibrium are independent:
  • Two equal, opposite forces at opposite ends of a rod: resultant force zero, resultant torque not zero. It starts to spin.
  • Two equal forces in the same direction at the ends: resultant torque zero, resultant force not zero. It accelerates without turning.
Quick check
?An electric fan is switched on, and after a few seconds its blades turn at a constant angular velocity. Which statement about the torques exerted on the blades while they turn at this constant angular velocity is correct?
Mission check

?A uniform board of weight W rests horizontally on two supports: support P at its left end and support Q one-quarter of the board's length from its right end. The diagram shows a student's force diagram for the board: FP and FQ are the forces exerted on the board by the supports, and W is drawn at the board's centre. Which statement about the student's diagram is correct?

The support farther from the board's midpoint pushes up with the smaller force. On the see-saw, the child sits three times as far from the pivot: W × 3d balances 3W × d.

Mission 4

Mission 4: A hoop and a disc have the same mass and radius. Which is harder to start spinning, and why?

Make a guess: A hoop and a disc have the same mass and radius. Which is harder to start spinning, and why?

Mission 4

Resistance to turning

  • Moment of inertia I: how strongly a body resists a change in its rotation.
  • The rotational twin of mass. Unit kg m².
  • Depends on the mass and how far that mass is from the axis.
  • Depends on the axis chosen. Does not depend on how fast it spins.
Quick check
?A bicycle wheel can rotate about its axle. Which change would increase the wheel's moment of inertia about the axle?
Mission 4

Point masses

  • One small mass at distance r from the axis: I = mr².
  • Several masses: add them. I = Σmr².
  • r = perpendicular distance from the axis.
  • Example: light rod, axis at one end. 0.40 kg at 0.20 m, 0.30 kg at 0.50 m, 0.20 kg at 0.80 m.
  • I = 0.40 × 0.20² + 0.30 × 0.50² + 0.20 × 0.80²
  • I = 0.016 + 0.075 + 0.128 = 0.22 kg m²
  • The lightest mass gives the biggest share: r is squared.
Quick check
?A system of small masses rotates about a fixed axis. Which single change doubles its moment of inertia about that axis?
Mission 4

Shape matters

  • Same mass M, same radius R, axis through the centre:
  • Hoop: all mass at R → I = MR².
  • Disc: much of the mass near the axis → I = ½MR².
  • Rod (length L): about its centre ML²/12; about one end ML²/3.
  • Mass farther out → larger I → harder to start or stop spinning.
  • The formula for a particular shape is given when needed.
Quick check
?The diagram shows three objects, each of which can rotate about a fixed axis through its centre, perpendicular to the page. Each object's type and mass are labelled. Which ranking of their moments of inertia about these axes, I1, I2 and I3, is correct?
Mission check

?Two thin rods, X and Y, have the same mass and length. In rod X most of the mass is concentrated equally near the two ends; in rod Y most of the mass is concentrated near the middle. Each rod can rotate about an axis through its midpoint, perpendicular to the rod. Which claim about the rods' moments of inertia about these axes is correct?

I = Σmr² weights mass by distance squared. A hoop puts all its mass at R (I = MR²); a disc keeps much of it near the axis (½MR²). The hoop is twice as hard to spin up.

Mission 5

Mission 5: A bucket falls down a well, unwinding rope from a heavy drum. Why does it fall with less than g?

Make a guess: A bucket falls down a well, unwinding rope from a heavy drum. Why does it fall with less than g?

Quick check
?A rigid wheel turns on a fixed axle. The resultant torque exerted on the wheel is not zero. Which statement about the wheel must be true?
Mission 5

Unbalanced torque

  • Resultant torque zero → angular velocity constant (perhaps zero).
  • Resultant torque not zero → angular velocity changes.
  • Torque in the same sense as the rotation: speeds up.
  • Torque opposite to the rotation: slows down (and may reverse).
  • No torque is needed to keep something spinning, only to change its spin.
Mission 5

Newton's second law for rotation

  • τ = Iα (τ = resultant torque)
  • Twin of F = ma: torque ↔ force, I ↔ mass, α ↔ a.
  • α is in the same sense as the resultant torque.
  • Same torque, double I → half the angular acceleration.
Mission 5

Worked example

  • A flywheel, I = 0.80 kg m², starts from rest.
  • A rope pulls tangentially on its rim: 30 N at r = 0.20 m. Friction torque at the axle: 1.2 N m.
  • Driving torque = 30 × 0.20 = 6.0 N m.
  • Resultant torque = 6.0 − 1.2 = 4.8 N m.
  • α = 4.8 ÷ 0.80 = 6.0 rad s⁻².
  • After 3.0 s: ω = 0 + 6.0 × 3.0 = 18 rad s⁻¹.
Quick check
?A flywheel of moment of inertia 0.50 kg m² is driven by a belt that exerts a tangential force of 40 N on its rim, 0.30 m from the axis. A frictional torque of 2.0 N m acts at the bearing, opposing the rotation. What is the angular acceleration of the flywheel?
Mission 5

Linear and rotational together

  • Bucket (mass m) on a rope wound around a drum that turns on a frictionless axle (I, radius R).
  • Bucket, linear: mg − T = ma
  • Drum, rotational: TR = Iα
  • Rope does not slip: a = Rα
  • Solve: a = mg / (m + I/R²). Less than g.
  • The rope must pull up on the bucket (T > 0) to spin the drum up.
Mission 5

Worked numbers

  • m = 2.0 kg, drum I = 0.090 kg m², R = 0.15 m. So I/R² = 4.0 kg.
  • a = 2.0 × 9.8 ÷ (2.0 + 4.0) = 3.3 m s⁻²
  • T = m(g − a) = 2.0 × (9.8 − 3.27) = 13 N
  • T < mg: the bucket accelerates down. T > 0: the drum spins up.
Quick check
?A wheel is mounted on a frictionless axle. Starting from rest, a constant resultant torque acts on it for a few seconds and is then removed. How does the wheel move after the torque is removed?
Mission check

?A block on a level, frictionless table is pulled by a string that passes over a pulley at the table's edge to a hanging mass. The light pulley is then replaced by a uniform solid pulley of the same radius but large mass. The string does not slip on the pulley. The system is again released from rest. Which statement is correct?

The drum resists spinning up (τ = Iα), so the rope must pull up on the bucket. Resultant force mg − T < mg, so a < g.

Mission 6

Mission 6: A spinning skater pulls in her arms and whirls faster. Where does the extra spin come from?

Make a guess: A spinning skater pulls in her arms and whirls faster. Where does the extra spin come from?

Mission 6

Angular momentum

  • Angular momentum: L = Iω for a rigid object rotating about a fixed axis.
  • I = moment of inertia about that axis.
  • Unit: kg m² s⁻¹ (the same as N m s).
  • Same ω, more mass far from the axis → larger I → larger L.
  • The rotational partner of linear momentum p = mv.
Mission 6

Worked example

  • A bicycle wheel: I = 0.12 kg m², spinning at ω = 25 rad s⁻¹.
  • L = Iω = 0.12 × 25 = 3.0 kg m² s⁻¹.
  • ω must be in rad s⁻¹. Convert from rev per second or per minute first (1 rev = 2π rad).
Quick check
?A thin hoop (I = MR²) and a uniform solid disc (I = ½MR²) have the same mass M and the same radius R. The hoop rotates about its central axis at angular speed ω; the disc rotates about its central axis at 2ω. How do their angular momenta compare?
Mission 6

Angular impulse

  • A resultant torque acting for a time changes L.
  • Angular impulse = τΔt = ΔL, for a constant (or average) torque.
  • ΔL = L − L0: final minus initial. Choose a positive sense (e.g. counter-clockwise) and keep signs.
  • Rearranged: τ = ΔL/Δt. With I constant: τ = IΔω/Δt = Iα.
Mission 6

Worked example

  • A wheel, I = 0.50 kg m², spins counter-clockwise at 12 rad s⁻¹. A brake exerts a clockwise torque of 1.5 N m for 2.0 s.
  • Counter-clockwise positive. L0 = 0.50 × 12 = +6.0 kg m² s⁻¹.
  • Angular impulse = τΔt = (−1.5)(2.0) = −3.0 N m s.
  • L = L0 + ΔL = 6.0 − 3.0 = +3.0 kg m² s⁻¹ → ω = 3.0 ÷ 0.50 = 6.0 rad s⁻¹, still turning the same way.
Quick check
?A wheel of moment of inertia 0.50 kg m² rotates counter-clockwise at 12 rad s⁻¹. A torque acts on it for 2.0 s, after which the wheel rotates clockwise at 4.0 rad s⁻¹. What is the magnitude of the average torque on the wheel?
Mission 6

Conservation of angular momentum

  • No resultant external torque → L stays constant.
  • Torques that parts of a system exert on each other come in equal and opposite pairs (Newton's third law). They cancel: internal torques cannot change the total L.
  • So: I1ω1 = I2ω2 for one object that changes shape.
Mission 6

Changing shape

  • Skater, arms out: I1 = 3.6 kg m², ω1 = 2.0 rad s⁻¹.
  • Arms in: I2 = 1.2 kg m². Ice and air exert negligible torque about her vertical axis.
  • ω2 = I1ω1/I2 = (3.6 × 2.0) ÷ 1.2 = 6.0 rad s⁻¹.
  • L is unchanged. I falls, so ω rises. Nothing creates angular momentum.
Mission 6

Coupled pairs

  • A turntable (I = 0.030 kg m²) spins freely at 4.0 rad s⁻¹. A disc (I = 0.010 kg m²), not spinning, drops onto it on the same axis.
  • Friction between them is internal. No external torque about the axis.
  • Before: 0.030 × 4.0 + 0 = 0.12 kg m² s⁻¹. After: (0.030 + 0.010)ω.
  • ω = 0.12 ÷ 0.040 = 3.0 rad s⁻¹.
  • Kinetic energy is not conserved here: friction while they slip transfers some to internal energy.
Quick check
?Disc 1, with moment of inertia 0.40 kg m², rotates counter-clockwise at 5.0 rad s⁻¹ on a vertical axle. Disc 2, with moment of inertia 0.20 kg m², rotates clockwise at 4.0 rad s⁻¹ on the same axle, just above disc 1. Friction at the axle is negligible. Disc 2 drops onto disc 1, and the two discs soon rotate together. What is the angular speed of the two discs when they rotate together?
Mission check

?A playground roundabout with a moment of inertia of 240 kg m² about its axis turns freely at 1.5 rad s⁻¹. A child of mass 40 kg, moving straight towards the axis, steps onto the edge, 1.5 m from the axis. Treat the child as a point mass. What is the new angular speed?

No external torque about the axis, so Iω is constant. The skater is the same story: pulling mass inwards cuts I, so ω must rise. The "extra spin" is only extra ω; the angular momentum never changed.

Mission 7

Mission 7: Race a hoop and a solid cylinder down a slope. Which wins, and does their mass matter?

Make a guess: Race a hoop and a solid cylinder down a slope. Which wins, and does their mass matter?

Mission 7

Rotational kinetic energy

  • Each bit of a spinning object moves at v = rω, so has ½mr²ω².
  • Add them up: Σ½mr²ω² = ½(Σmr²)ω².
  • Ek = ½Iω² = L²/2I (L = Iω)
Quick check
?A uniform grinding wheel spins at constant angular velocity on a fixed axle, so its centre of mass stays at rest. A student claims: "The velocities of points on opposite sides of the wheel are equal in size and opposite in direction, so they cancel, and the wheel has no kinetic energy." Which response to the student's claim is correct?

A spinning wheel on a fixed axle has kinetic energy even though its centre of mass is at rest. Kinetic energy is a scalar: the same spinning either way.

Quick check
?The diagram shows a wheel rolling without slipping along level ground; the arrow shows the velocity v of its centre C. Points P and B are on the rim, as shown. Which statement describes the velocities of P and B relative to the ground at the instant shown?
Mission 7

Rolling without slipping

  • The contact point is momentarily at rest on the ground.
  • So the centre moves exactly one circumference per turn:
  • v = ωr, a = αr, distance moved = rΔθ (centre of mass)
  • Top of the wheel: 2vcm. Bottom: 0.
  • Static friction acts at a point that is not sliding: in the ideal case it does no work and dissipates no energy.
Mission 7

Total kinetic energy

  • A rolling body moves and spins:
  • total Ek = ½Mv² + ½Iω², v the speed of the centre of mass
  • Example: solid cylinder, M = 2.0 kg, I = ½MR², rolling at 3.0 m s⁻¹.
  • Translational: ½ × 2.0 × 3.0² = 9.0 J.
  • Rotational: ½(½MR²)(v/R)² = ¼Mv² = 4.5 J.
  • Total 13.5 J. R cancels.
Mission 7

Worked example: down a slope

  • Solid cylinder (I = ½MR²) rolls from rest down a slope, dropping h = 0.80 m.
  • Energy: Mgh = ½Mv² + ½(½MR²)(v/R)² = ¾Mv²
  • v = √(4gh/3). M and R cancel.
  • v = √(4 × 9.8 × 0.80 ÷ 3) = 3.2 m s⁻¹
  • A block sliding without friction: √(2gh) = 4.0 m s⁻¹. The cylinder is slower: some energy goes into spin.
Mission 7

The race

  • Write I = kMR². Then v = √(2gh/(1 + k)).
  • Hoop: k = 1. Half its kinetic energy is rotational.
  • Solid cylinder: k = ½. One third rotational.
  • Solid sphere: k = 2/5. Two sevenths rotational.
  • Smaller k → less energy locked in spin → faster.
  • Mass and radius cancel. Only the shape (k) matters.
Quick check
?A thin-walled hollow ball (I = (2/3)MR²) is released from rest and rolls without slipping down a ramp. Its centre of mass descends a vertical height of 1.2 m. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻². What is the speed of its centre of mass at the bottom?
Mission check

?A solid ball (I = (2/5)MR²) and a thin-walled hollow ball (I = (2/3)MR²) have the same mass M and the same radius R. Both roll without slipping along a level floor at the same speed. Which ball must have more energy removed to bring it to rest?

The bigger the share of energy locked in spin, the slower an object rolls down a slope. The cylinder (k = ½) beats the hoop (k = 1), whatever their masses.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Angles in radians; every point of a rigid body shares θ, ω and α.
  • Constant α: the constant-acceleration equations with θ, ω, α in place of x, v, a.
  • Δs = rΔθ, v = rω, aT = rα: linear quantities grow with distance from the axis.

Mission 2

  • τ = Fr sin θ: only the perpendicular component turns; or force × lever arm.

Mission 3

  • Torque uses the perpendicular distance (lever arm). Rotational equilibrium: resultant torque = 0, so the body is at rest or turns at constant angular velocity.

Mission 4

  • Moment of inertia I = Σmr²: mass far from the axis counts most; the hoop (MR²) beats the disc (½MR²).

Mission 5

  • τ = Iα: an unbalanced torque causes angular acceleration; zero resultant torque keeps ω constant.
  • Coupled systems: write F = ma for each mass, τ = Iα for each wheel, and link them with a = Rα.

Mission 6

  • L = Iω. A resultant torque changes it: ΔL = τΔt (angular impulse).
  • No external torque → L constant. Smaller I → larger ω (skaters, divers, coupled discs).

Mission 7

  • Ek = ½Iω² = L²/2I; total Ek = ½Mv² + ½Iω².
  • Rolling without slipping: vcm = rω; ideal static friction does no work. Down a slope, smaller I/(MR²) wins; mass and radius cancel.
