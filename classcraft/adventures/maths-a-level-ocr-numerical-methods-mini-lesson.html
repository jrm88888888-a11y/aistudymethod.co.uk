Numerical methods · roots and iteration

Change of sign (A-level only)

  • If ff is continuous on [a,b][a, b] and f(a)f(a) and f(b)f(b) have opposite signs, then f(x)=0f(x) = 0 has a root between aa and bb.
  • To show a root is 2.09 correct to 2 decimal places, check the signs at the bounds 2.085 and 2.095.
  • Always finish with a conclusion: "sign change and ff is continuous, so there is a root in the interval."

Numerical methods · roots and iteration

When change of sign misleads

  • Two roots (or a touching point) in the interval: f(x)=(x−1)(x−2)f(x) = (x − 1)(x − 2) has f(0)=2f(0) = 2 and f(3)=2f(3) = 2. No sign change, yet two roots. Use a smaller interval.
  • A discontinuity: f(x)=x−21​f(x) = 1/(x − 2) has f(1)=−1f(1) = −1 and f(3)=1f(3) = 1. A sign change, but no root: there is a vertical asymptote at x=2x = 2.
  • So the method needs ff to be continuous on the interval, and the interval to be small.

Staircases, cobwebs and convergence

  • Near the root αα, the iteration converges if ∣g′(α)∣<1|g'(α)| < 1: the graph of y=g(x)y = g(x) is less steep than y=xy = x. If ∣g′(α)∣>1|g'(α)| > 1 it diverges, even from a start close to αα.
  • Different rearrangements of one equation can behave differently.
  • Draw y=xy = x and y=g(x)y = g(x). From x0​x₀ go to the curve, across to y=xy = x, back to the curve, and repeat.
  • g′(α)>0g'(α) > 0 gives a staircase; g′(α)<0g'(α) < 0 gives a cobweb, with values either side of αα.

The Newton–Raphson method (A-level only)

  • To solve f(x)=0f(x) = 0, start from an estimate x0​x₀ near the root.
  • Draw the tangent at x0​x₀. Where it crosses the xx-axis is a better estimate x1​x₁.
  • The tangent has gradient f′(x0​)f'(x₀), which gives the Newton–Raphson formula:
xn+1​=xn​−f′(xn​)f(xn​)​xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − (f(xₙ))/(f'(xₙ))
  • Repeat until successive estimates agree to the accuracy you need.
  • When it works, the method converges very fast: the number of correct digits roughly doubles each step.

Numerical methods · Newton–Raphson and trapezium rule

When Newton–Raphson fails

  • If f′(xn​)=0f'(xₙ) = 0 the tangent is horizontal and never meets the xx-axis: you cannot divide by zero. This happens when xn​xₙ is at a stationary point.
  • If f′(xn​)f'(xₙ) is small (near a stationary point), the tangent is nearly flat and the next estimate can jump a long way off.
  • The estimates may then converge to a different root, or not settle at all.
  • Fix: start close to the root, away from turning points.

The trapezium rule (A-level only)

  • Split [a,b][a, b] into nn strips of equal width h=nb−a​h = (b − a)/n. That needs n+1n + 1 ordinates y0​,y1​,…,yn​y₀, y₁, …, yₙ.
∫ab​ydx≈2h​[y0​+yn​+2(y1​+y2​+⋯+yn−1​)]∫_a^b y dx ≈ h/2[y₀ + yₙ + 2(y₁ + y₂ + … + yₙ₋₁)]
  • If the curve is convex (bends upwards, f′′(x)>0f''(x) > 0) the trapezia sit above it: an overestimate.
  • If the curve is concave (f′′(x)<0f''(x) < 0) the result is an underestimate.
  • More strips give a better estimate.
  • To trap the area between bounds, use rectangles: on an increasing curve, left-hand heights give a lower bound and right-hand heights an upper bound.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Sign change of a continuous ff on a small interval shows a root; use the bounds (e.g. 2.085 and 2.095) to prove a rounded value.
  • Change of sign fails with repeated or paired roots (no change) and with asymptotes (change, but no root).
  • xn+1​=g(xn​)xₙ₊₁ = g(xₙ) converges to αα when ∣g′(α)∣<1|g'(α)| < 1; the limit solves x=g(x)x = g(x).
  • g′>0g' > 0 gives a staircase diagram, g′<0g' < 0 a cobweb.
  • Newton–Raphson: xn+1​=xn​−f′(xn​)f(xn​)​xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − (f(xₙ))/(f'(xₙ)). It fails at a stationary point and can go astray where the gradient is small.
  • Trapezium rule: 2h​[y0​+yn​+2(y1​+⋯+yn−1​)]h/2[y₀ + yₙ + 2(y₁ + … + yₙ₋₁)] with h=nb−a​h = (b−a)/n for nn strips.
  • Convex curve: overestimate. Concave curve: underestimate.
