Paper 2: Comparative Essay

Paper 2 is a single comparative essay written in 1 hour 45 minutes. You choose one of four general questions and answer it by comparing and contrasting two of the works you have studied — entirely from memory, with no texts in the room.

  • The questions are general — applicable to many works — so you must fit your chosen works to the question, not the reverse.
  • It is the assessment home of Intertextuality: connecting texts.
  • Worth 25% of the HL grade.

Work through each screen, answer the questions as you go and collect ⭐ stars. Any named work appears only as a swappable, illustrative example — the IB reading lists are chosen by your school and rotate, so this lesson teaches the skills and ideas, never a set text. Press Start when you are ready.

The question

Answer the question set

The single biggest Paper 2 failure is answering a prepared essay instead of the question on the paper.

  • Read all four questions; choose the one your two works genuinely fit.
  • Identify the question’s key term and define it in your own words — that definition frames the whole essay.
  • Bend your material to this question; never recite a rehearsed answer to a different one.

Structure

Integrate, do not sequence

Comparison must be integrated, not sequential.

  • Sequential (weak): everything about work one, then everything about work two — two essays glued together.
  • Integrated (strong): each paragraph built around a point of comparison, holding both works at once.

The structural test: if your paragraphs are titled by work rather than by idea, you are not comparing — you are describing in turn.

Choosing works

Choosing your two works

Everything depends on the choice of two works.

  • Pick works with rich points of contact (a shared theme, form or technique) and illuminating differences.
  • Two works that are too similar yield a thin essay; two with nothing in common force strained links.
  • Ideally choose works of different form or origin so comparison has real traction.

📚 Good preparation

Closed book

Writing from memory

Paper 2 is closed book. You write from memory.

  • Memorise a bank of short, exact quotations from each work — and never misattribute them.
  • Know each work’s structure, key scenes and techniques well enough to summon evidence under pressure.
  • Rehearse flexible comparative frames (power, freedom, memory, form) you can adapt to whatever question appears.

Balance

Keeping the works in balance

Keep the two works in balance.

  • Roughly equal attention and depth — an essay that neglects one work loses focus marks.
  • Alternate or interleave evidence so both works are continuously in play.
  • Use connectives of comparison (whereas, similarly, by contrast) to signal the relationship explicitly.

The rule

The no-double-dipping rule

The no-double-dipping rule shapes your whole plan.

  • A work used for the Individual Oral or the HL essay cannot be used again in Paper 2.
  • So map your thirteen works across all components before the exam season, reserving strong, comparable works for Paper 2.

Plan early: discovering in the exam that your best two works are already spent is a self-inflicted disaster.

Criteria

The four assessment criteria

Know the four criteria (30 marks total).

  • A — Knowledge, understanding and interpretation: of both works, in service of the question.
  • B — Analysis and evaluation: of authorial choices in both works.
  • C — Focus and organisation: a balanced, coherent comparative structure.
  • D — Language: clear, accurate, appropriately formal.

Note the weighting: Paper 2 is 25% at HL (lower than SL, because HL adds the HL essay).

Game plan

A Paper 2 game plan

A Paper 2 game plan.

  1. 10 minutes: choose the question, define its key term, pick the two works, plan points of comparison.
  2. Body: three to four paragraphs, each a shared point, each holding both works.
  3. Evidence: brief, exact, correctly attributed; analyse the method in both.
  4. Close: synthesise — what the comparison finally reveals about the question.
Recap

The big ideas to take away

The task: one comparative essay on TWO studied works, 1 hour 45 minutes, closed book (25%)

Answer the set question — define its key term; never recite a prepared essay

Integrated comparison: organise by point, holding both works in every paragraph

Choose works with rich contact and real contrast — ideally different form or origin

Closed book: memorise short, correctly attributed quotations and each work’s structure

No double-dipping: plan works across all components before the exams

Criteria: A understanding · B analysis · C balance & organisation · D language (30 marks)
