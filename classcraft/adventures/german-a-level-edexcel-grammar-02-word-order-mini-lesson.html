Word order

German word order is rule-governed, not free. Two ideas run the whole system: the finite verb sits in position 2 in a main clause (V2), and it jumps to the very end in a subordinate clause. Around that, the verbal bracket and TeKaMoLo keep the middle of the sentence in order.

V2 in main clauses · verb-final in subordinate clauses…, weil ich müde bin. — the verb goes to the end after weil

The V2 rule

The finite verb is element number two

In a statement the finite (conjugated) verb is always the second element — "element", not "word". Whatever you put first, the verb still comes second, so the subject slides in after it (inversion):

  • Ich fahre morgen nach Berlin. — subject first.
  • Morgen fahre ich nach Berlin. — time first → verb still 2nd, subject 3rd.
  • Nach Berlin fahre ich morgen. — place first → same rule.

The tell: exactly one element may stand before the verb. If you front a time or place phrase, do not also put the subject before the verb — that gives the classic *"Morgen ich fahre" error.

The verbal bracket

Satzklammer — verb parts split apart

When a verb has two parts, the finite part stays in position 2 and the non-finite part (infinitive, past participle or separable prefix) goes to the very end, forming a bracket around the middle:

  • Ich habe gestern einen Film gesehen. — perfect: participle at the end.
  • Ich muss heute schwer arbeiten. — modal: infinitive at the end.
  • Ich stehe jeden Tag um sieben auf. — separable verb aufstehen: prefix at the end.

Everything else lives inside the bracket — the "Mittelfeld". Getting the two verb-halves to their correct positions is the single biggest word-order marker at A-Level.

Ordering the middle field

When several adverbials pile up inside the bracket, arrange them Te–Ka–Mo–Lo:

slotquestionexample
Te — temporalwann?morgen
Ka — kausalwarum?wegen der Arbeit
Mo — modalwie?mit dem Zug
Lo — lokalwohin?nach Berlin

Put together: Ich fahre morgen wegen der Arbeit mit dem Zug nach Berlin. — Te, Ka, Mo, Lo in one clean line.

Objects in the middle

Dative before accusative — unless a pronoun

With two noun objects the order is dative before accusative. But a pronoun jumps left, and an accusative pronoun beats a dative noun:

  • Ich gebe dem Kind das Buch. — Dat noun before Akk noun.
  • Ich gebe es dem Kind. — Akk pronoun before Dat noun.
  • Ich gebe es ihm. — two pronouns: Akk before Dat.

Rule of thumb: "nouns = dative first; pronouns = accusative first, and pronouns always slide toward the verb."

Coordinating conjunctions

ADUSO — the "position-zero" joiners

The coordinating conjunctions aber, denn, und, sondern, oder (mnemonic ADUSO) join two main clauses and do not count as an element — normal V2 order continues after them:

  • Ich bleibe zu Hause, denn ich bin müde. — denn + normal order (ich bin).
  • Ich lerne Deutsch und ich höre Musik. — no inversion.
  • Er ist nicht faul, sondern er ist krank. — sondern = "but rather", after a negative.

Don't confuse denn and weil: both mean "because", but denn is coordinating (verb stays 2nd) while weil is subordinating (verb to the end).

Subordinating conjunctions

Verb kicked to the end

A subordinating conjunction (weil, dass, wenn, als, obwohl, da, damit, ob, bevor, nachdem, während) opens a subordinate clause and sends the finite verb to the very end:

  • Ich bleibe zu Hause, weil ich müde bin.
  • Ich weiß, dass er den Film gesehen hat. — perfect: participle then finite verb, both at the end.
  • Ich hoffe, dass ich morgen arbeiten muss. — modal: infinitive then finite verb last.

Order at the end: non-finite part first, finite verb absolutely last — …gesehen hat, …arbeiten muss.

Subordinate clause first

Verb-comma-verb

If the subordinate clause comes first, the whole clause counts as element 1, so the main-clause verb must come next — two verbs meet across the comma:

  • Weil es regnet, bleiben wir zu Hause. — …regnet, bleiben… (verb, verb).
  • Wenn ich Zeit habe, gehe ich ins Kino.
  • Als ich jung war, wohnte ich in Köln.

The clean test: if a subordinate clause opens the sentence, the very next word after the comma is a finite verb. Miss that and the sentence collapses.

Recap

Your word-order toolkit

V2: finite verb second in a main clause; front one element and the subject inverts after the verb.

Satzklammer: non-finite part (infinitive / participle / separable prefix) to the very end.

TeKaMoLo: temporal → kausal → modal → lokal in the middle field; dative noun before accusative noun (pronouns first).

Coordinating (ADUSO): aber, denn, und, sondern, oder keep normal V2 order.

Subordinating: weil, dass, wenn, obwohl… send the finite verb last; fronted clause → verb-comma-verb.
