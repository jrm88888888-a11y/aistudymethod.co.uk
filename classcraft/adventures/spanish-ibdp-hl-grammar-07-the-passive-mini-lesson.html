The passive & how to avoid it

In the active voice the subject does the action; in the passive the subject receives it. Spanish has a true passive with ser — but far more often it dodges it, using the versatile se. This lesson covers all four moves examiners reward. In IB Diploma Spanish B HL, this grammar powers your work across the five prescribed themes — Identities, Experiences, Human ingenuity, Social organization and Sharing the planet — and the two literary works you study, especially when you report impersonally on Sharing the planet.

The true passive · ser

ser + past participle (+ por)

The true passive is ser + past participle, optionally naming the agent with por. Crucially, the participle agrees with the subject in gender and number:

  • La casa fue construida (por el arquitecto). — "The house was built (by the architect)." (feminine singular)
  • Los libros fueron vendidos. — "The books were sold." (masculine plural)

Agreement is the tell: because the participle behaves like an adjective here, it takes -o / -a / -os / -as to match the subject — unlike the perfect tense, where the participle never changes.

The se passive

The Spanish favourite

Far more common than ser + participle is the se passive / impersonal. Use se + the verb in the 3rd person; the verb agrees with the thing "done":

  • Se habla español. — "Spanish is spoken." (singular subject)
  • Se venden casas. — "Houses are sold." (plural subject → plural verb)
  • Se dice que… — "It is said that…"

Why Spanish loves it: it lets you leave the doer unnamed and sounds far more natural than a heavy ser passive. Reach for it in essays.

ser vs estar + participle

The action vs the resulting state

This is the classic advanced trap. Both use a participle, but they mean different things:

  • ser + participle = the ACTION (something happened): La puerta fue cerrada (por el guardia). — "The door was closed (by the guard)."
  • estar + participle = the RESULTING STATE (how it now is): La puerta está cerrada. — "The door is closed."

Test yourself: if you can add por + a doer, it's the ser passive (the action). If you're just describing how something looks now, it's estar (the state).

Avoiding the passive

The third-person-plural trick

The most natural way to render an English passive with an unknown doer is to use the 3rd-person plural active — "they":

  • Dicen que… — "It is said that…" / "They say that…"
  • Construyeron la casa en 1990. — "The house was built in 1990."
  • Me robaron el móvil. — "My phone was stolen." (lit. "They stole my phone.")

Three tools, one idea: to say "it is said that…" you can use Se dice que… (se passive) or Dicen que… (3rd-person plural). Both dodge the clumsy ser passive.

The four moves side by side

Same idea, four structures

  • True passive: La casa fue construida por el arquitecto. (the action, agent named)
  • se passive: Se construyó la casa. (doer irrelevant)
  • estar + participle: La casa está construida. (the resulting state)
  • 3rd-person plural: Construyeron la casa. (natural, active)

Exam gold: knowing when to swap the heavy ser passive for se or a 3rd-person-plural active is exactly the fluency IB markers reward.

Recap

Your passive toolkit

True passive: ser + participle (+ por + agent); the participle agrees (La casa fue construida; Los libros fueron vendidos).

se passive: far commoner — se + 3rd-person verb agreeing with the thing done (Se habla español; Se venden casas).

ser vs estar: ser + participle = the action; estar + participle = the resulting state (La puerta está cerrada).

Irregular participles: escrito, hecho, roto, abierto, visto.

Avoiding it: 3rd-person plural — Dicen que… ("It is said that…").
