Mission 1

Mission 1: If the electric field at a point is zero, must the electric potential there be zero too?

Make a guess: If the electric field at a point is zero, must the electric potential there be zero too?

Mission 1

Electric potential

  • Electric potential at a point: the work done per unit charge to bring a small positive test charge from infinity to that point.
  • Zero at infinity, by definition.
  • A scalar: it has a sign but no direction.
  • Unit: J C⁻¹ = volt (V).
  • Set by the source charges, not by the test charge: any test charge at P, of any size or sign, sees the same V.
Mission 1

Potential of a point charge

  • V = Q/4πε0r
  • Falls as 1/r, not 1/r² (field strength falls as 1/r²).
  • Positive charge: V positive, highest near the charge. Negative charge: V negative.
  • Example: +4.0 nC. At 0.20 m, V = 180 V.
Mission 1

Potentials add as numbers

  • Several charges: add the potentials, with their signs. No angles, no components.
  • Two charges of +4.0 nC, 0.30 m apart. Midpoint M is 0.15 m from each.
  • Field at M: equal and opposite → E = 0.
  • Potential at M: 240 V + 240 V = 480 V. Not zero.
  • E = 0 does not mean V = 0. The field depends on how V changes, not on its value.
Mission 1

Work and potential difference

  • Work done moving charge Q: ΔW = QΔV
  • Worked example: move +2.0 nC from 0.20 m to 0.10 m from the +4.0 nC charge.
  • ΔV = 360 − 180 = 180 V. Work done on it by an external force (moved slowly) = 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 180 = 3.6 × 10⁻⁷ J.
  • Released at 0.10 m, it is pushed outwards. By 0.20 m it has gained 3.6 × 10⁻⁷ J of kinetic energy.
  • A negative charge does the opposite: it speeds up moving towards higher V.
  • Only the start and end potentials matter, not the path.
Quick check
?A point charge of +5.0 nC is fixed in place. A second charge of +2.0 nC is moved slowly from a point 0.30 m from the fixed charge to a point 0.10 m from it. Take 1/(4πε0) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻². What is the work done on the +2.0 nC charge by the external force?
Mission 1

Equipotentials

  • Equipotential: a surface (or line on a 2D map) where V is the same everywhere.
  • Moving along one: ΔV = 0, so no work is done.
  • Always perpendicular to field lines.
  • The field points towards lower potential.
  • Point charge: concentric spheres. Equal steps of V get closer together near the charge.
  • Uniform field between plates: parallel planes, evenly spaced.
Mission 1

How the field follows from the potential

  • E = ΔV/Δr (size of the potential gradient)
  • Direction: the field points down the potential gradient, towards lower V.
  • The field depends on how V changes, not on its value.
  • Uniform field: E = V/d. 400 V across 2.0 cm → 2.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹.
  • Point charge: E at r = −(gradient of the tangent) to the V–r curve. Its size is how steep the curve is.
  • Steep curve → strong field. Flat (maximum or minimum) → E = 0, whatever V is.
  • ΔV = area under the E–r graph between two distances.
Quick check
?In a region of uniform electric field of strength 4.0 × 10³ V m⁻¹ directed in the +x direction, point A at x = 0 has an electric potential of +500 V. What is the electric potential at point B, at x = 0.050 m?
Quick check
?Which statement is true for the electric field of a point charge, but never true for the gravitational field of a point mass?
Mission 1

Electric and gravitational fields compared

  • Source: mass ↔ charge.
  • Force: F = Gm1m2/r², always attractive ↔ F = Q1Q2/4πε0r², attractive or repulsive.
  • Field strength: g = GM/r² ↔ E = Q/4πε0r². Both inverse-square.
  • Potential: V = −GM/r, always negative ↔ V = Q/4πε0r, sign of Q.
  • Shared: potential zero at infinity; field lines; equipotentials ⟂ field lines; no work along an equipotential.
  • g = −ΔV/Δr; E = ΔV/Δr.
  • Between two protons, the electric force is about 10³⁶ times the gravitational force.
Mission check

?Point charges +Q and −Q are fixed a short distance apart, and M is the midpoint between them. A student claims: 'The potential at M is zero, so no work is needed to bring a small test charge from infinity to M, and the test charge feels no electric force at M.' Which evaluation of the claim is correct?

Potential and field strength are different things. Between two equal positive charges, E = 0 but V is not zero; between +Q and −Q, V = 0 but E is not zero.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Mass, charge and moving charge all make fields. Why is a uniform electric field simply V divided by d?

Make a guess: Mass, charge and moving charge all make fields. Why is a uniform electric field simply V divided by d?

Mission 2

Fields: one idea, several kinds

  • A field is a region where an object feels a non-contact force.
  • Gravitational field: acts on mass. Created by mass.
  • Electric field: acts on charge. Created by charge.
  • Magnetic field: acts on moving charge. Created by moving charge (currents).
  • Field strength is always force per unit of what the field acts on: a vector.
Quick check
?A beam of electrons passes between the poles of a strong magnet. A charged dust grain sits at rest between the same poles. Which of them feels a force from the magnetic field?
Mission 2

Why E = V/d

  • A charge Q crosses the gap d between parallel plates with p.d. V.
  • The field is uniform, so the force F = QE is constant. Work done: W = Fd = QEd.
  • By the definition of p.d., the work done is also W = QV.
  • QEd = QV, so E = V/d.
  • Units agree: V m⁻¹ = J C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = N C⁻¹.
Mission 2

Worked example: two routes, one answer

  • Plates 3.0 mm apart, p.d. 12 V. An electron (charge e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) crosses the gap.
  • Field: E = 12 ÷ (3.0 × 10⁻³) = 4.0 × 10³ V m⁻¹.
  • Route 1, force × distance: F = eE = 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁶ N; W = Fd = 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁶ × 3.0 × 10⁻³ = 1.9 × 10⁻¹⁸ J.
  • Route 2, charge × p.d.: W = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 12 = 1.9 × 10⁻¹⁸ J.
  • The two routes agree. That agreement is E = V/d.
Quick check
?Two parallel plates 5.0 mm apart have a p.d. of 2.0 kV between them. A proton (charge 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) crosses from one plate to the other. How much work does the field do on it?
Mission check

?Two parallel plates are connected to a fixed p.d. They are pulled apart until the gap between them is doubled. For a proton crossing the whole gap, what happens to the force on it and to the work done on it by the field?

The work done crossing depends only on Q and V. Spread the same p.d. over a wider gap and the force is weaker in proportion: that is why E = V/d.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Potential = work done per unit positive charge from infinity; V = Q/4πε0r; a scalar, so potentials simply add.
  • The field is the potential gradient: equipotentials are ⟂ to field lines, and E = 0 does not mean V = 0.
  • Electric potential takes the sign of Q; gravitational potential is always negative.

Mission 2

  • Fields: gravitational (mass), electric (charge), magnetic (moving charge); field strength is force per unit mass or per unit charge.
  • E = V/d follows from W = Fd = QEd and W = QV: the work done crossing the gap is QV, whatever the gap.
