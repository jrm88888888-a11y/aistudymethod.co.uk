Theme A: Relationships & Families

This mini-lesson works through the AQA GCSE theme Relationships and Families: the nature and purpose of marriage, sex, divorce, cohabitation and same-sex relationships; contraception; the nature and purpose of families; and gender equality. Throughout, we present a range of views — Christian, Muslim and non-religious (atheist/Humanist).

Work through each screen, answer the questions as you go and collect ⭐ stars. Remember: there is rarely one "religious view" — always show a range and say which group holds it. Press Start when you're ready.

Marriage

The nature and purpose of marriage

Marriage is a legal union of two people as partners. Many religious believers see it as a God-given or sacred commitment. In Christianity it is often described as a lifelong covenant — a solemn promise before God. In Islam marriage is a contract (nikah) between the couple, with agreed rights and responsibilities.

  • Purposes often given: lifelong faithful companionship, having and raising children (procreation), and providing a stable home.
  • In Christianity, husband and wife are said to become "one flesh".
  • Many non-religious people also value marriage — but see it as a personal, human commitment rather than a religious one.
Scripture: "The two shall become one flesh." (Mark 10:8 / Genesis 2:24) The Qur'an describes spouses as "garments" for one another (Qur'an 2:187).
Sex & commitment

Views on sex before and outside marriage

Traditions differ on when sexual relationships are appropriate. Many Christians and Muslims teach that sex belongs within marriage — sex before marriage is often discouraged, and sex outside marriage (adultery) is widely regarded as wrong across the traditions.

  • Adultery (sex outside marriage): the commandment "You shall not commit adultery" (Exodus 20:14) is shared teaching; the Qur'an also forbids it.
  • Some liberal Christians and many non-religious people accept sex within a loving, committed relationship, married or not.
  • Humanists judge sexual relationships by consent, love and mutual respect rather than by religious rules.

Careful: don't write "all Christians think…". Show the range — traditional and liberal positions both exist.

Divorce

Different views on divorce

Divorce is the legal ending of a marriage. Religious views differ — even within the same faith.

  • Roman Catholic Church: marriage is a sacrament and indissoluble — it does not permit divorce and remarriage in church while a spouse lives (an annulment is different).
  • Many Protestant churches: permit divorce and, in some cases, remarriage — recognising that some marriages break down.
  • Islam: divorce (talaq) is permitted but discouraged — a hadith calls it "the most hated of permitted things"; steps to reconcile are encouraged first.
  • Non-religious/Humanist: divorce can be the compassionate choice when a relationship has ended; the couple's wellbeing matters most.
Scripture: "What God has joined together, let no one separate." (Mark 10:9) — used by Christians who oppose divorce. Others point to teaching allowing divorce in cases such as unfaithfulness.
Cohabitation & same-sex relationships

A range of views

Cohabitation means living together (and often having a sexual relationship) without being married. Same-sex relationships are relationships between people of the same sex; same-sex marriage is legal in the UK.

  • Some Christians and Muslims hold that sex belongs within heterosexual marriage, so cohabitation and same-sex sexual relationships are against their teaching.
  • Other Christians and denominations welcome and affirm same-sex relationships, and accept cohabitation, emphasising love and commitment.
  • Humanists and many non-religious people judge relationships by love, consent and commitment, and support equality for same-sex couples.

Respect & accuracy: there is no single "religious view" here. Represent conservative and accepting positions both — fairly and without caricature.

Contraception

Differing views on contraception

Contraception is the deliberate prevention of pregnancy. Views differ, and the reasons matter as much as the conclusion.

  • Roman Catholic Church: traditionally opposes artificial contraception, teaching that sex should be open to procreation; natural family planning is permitted.
  • Many Protestants and many Muslims: permit contraception for responsible planning — spacing children, protecting a mother's health, or caring for the family they already have.
  • Non-religious/Humanist: support contraception as a matter of personal choice, health and responsible parenthood.
Reasons: those who oppose it stress procreation as a purpose of sex; those who permit it stress responsible planning and the wellbeing of the family.

The family

The nature and purpose of families

Most traditions see the family as very important. Purposes often given include procreation (having children), giving children stability and love, and — for religious families — educating children in the faith.

  • Types of family: nuclear (two parents + children), extended (wider relatives), single-parent, same-sex parent, and blended/step-families.
  • Role of parents: to care for, protect and teach children — and, for many believers, to raise them in their faith.
  • Role of children: to honour their parents ("Honour your father and your mother", Exodus 20:12) and, later, to care for elderly relatives.
Diversity of view: some believers especially value the nuclear family; others fully affirm many family types. Non-religious people generally judge a family by the love and care within it.
Gender equality

Equality of men and women

Gender equality means treating men and women as of equal worth. Gender prejudice is unfair pre-judging by sex; gender discrimination is acting on it. Many religious believers teach that men and women are equal before God.

  • Christianity: many point to "there is neither male nor female, for you are all one in Christ Jesus" (Galatians 3:28) to affirm equality.
  • Islam: the Qur'an teaches men and women were created from a single soul and are equal in worth before God.
  • Differing views on roles: some traditions hold men and women have complementary/different roles (e.g. in worship or family), while others affirm identical roles, including women as religious leaders.

Key skill: distinguish equality of worth (widely affirmed) from views about roles (where believers differ).

Recap

The ideas to know

Marriage: lifelong covenant (Christianity) / contract — nikah (Islam); purposes include companionship, procreation, stability.

Sex: many Christians & Muslims teach sex belongs within marriage; adultery widely condemned; liberal & Humanist views judge by love, consent, commitment.

Divorce: Catholic Church — indissoluble, no remarriage; many Protestant churches permit it; Islam permits but discourages ("most hated of permitted things").

Cohabitation & same-sex: a range — some believers restrict sex to heterosexual marriage; others fully affirm same-sex relationships; Humanists judge by love & commitment.

Contraception: Catholic Church opposes artificial (allows natural planning); many Protestants & Muslims permit for responsible planning.

Family: procreation, stability, raising children (in faith); nuclear, extended, single-parent, same-sex parent, blended; honour parents; care for elderly.

Gender equality: many affirm equal worth before God; traditions differ on roles (complementary vs identical).
