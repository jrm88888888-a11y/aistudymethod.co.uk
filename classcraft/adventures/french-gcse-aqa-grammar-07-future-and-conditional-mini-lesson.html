Future & conditional

These two tenses are a matched pair. The simple future (le futur) says what will happen — je parlerai. The conditional (le conditionnel) says what would happen — je voudrais. They share the same stem and differ only in the endings, so learning one gives you the other.

The simple future

"Will": infinitive + endings

The simple future is one of the easiest tenses. Take the infinitive itself as the stem and add these endings:

infinitive + -ai · -as · -a · -ons · -ez · -ont(for -re verbs, drop the final -e first)
  • parler → je parlerai — I will speak
  • finir → tu finiras — you will finish
  • vendre → il vendra — he will sell (drop the -e)

Spot the pattern: the future endings are almost the present tense of avoir (ai, as, a, ons, ez, ont) glued onto the infinitive. Only -re verbs change — they lose the final -e before adding the ending.

Keep the r: every future form keeps the r of the infinitive — that r is the "sound of the future". Je parlerai (future) vs je parlais (imperfect) differ by that r.

The conditional

"Would": same stem, imperfect endings

The conditional is the future's twin. It uses the same stem as the future, but takes the imperfect endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient). It means "would":

future stem + imperfect endings= "would" — je voudrais, j'aimerais, on pourrait
  • vouloir → je voudrais — I would like
  • aimer → j'aimerais — I would love/like
  • pouvoir → on pourrait — we could

Politeness superpower: the conditional is how you sound polite in French. Je voudrais ("I would like") is far softer than je veux ("I want"). Learn je voudrais, j'aimerais, ce serait, il faudrait — they will lift every speaking answer.

Telling them apart

-ai (will) vs -ais (would)

Because they share a stem, the only difference is the ending. Compare them side by side for parler:

futur — "will"
je parlerai
tu parleras
il parlera
nous parlerons
vous parlerez
ils parleront
conditionnel — "would"
je parlerais
tu parlerais
il parlerait
nous parlerions
vous parleriez
ils parleraient

The tiny "s": je parlerai (no s) = "I will speak"; je parlerais (with s) = "I would speak". One letter changes the whole meaning — worth double-checking in writing.

Si clauses · "if"

Matching tenses after "si"

These tenses shine in si ("if") sentences. There are two fixed patterns to learn:

  • si + present → future: Si j'ai le temps, je viendrai. — If I have time, I will come.
  • si + imperfect → conditional: Si j'avais le temps, je viendrais. — If I had time, I would come.

Keep the pair together: present goes with future, imperfect goes with conditional. Never mix them — si j'aurais is a classic slip. The conditional lives in the main clause, not after si.

Polite requests

Could you…? Would you…?

The conditional is the natural tense for soft, polite requests — English "could" and "would":

  • Pourriez-vous m'aider ? — Could you help me? (pouvoir, vous)
  • Tu pourrais m'aider ? — Could you help me? (pouvoir, tu)
  • Est-ce que tu voudrais venir ? — Would you like to come?

Why it matters: using pourriez-vous or tu pourrais instead of a blunt command instantly raises the tone. Est-ce que vous pouvez… works too, but the conditional is more courteous — a genuine mark-earner in speaking.

Watch out

The classic future/conditional slips

  • Building irregulars from the infinitive: "I will be" is je serai, not je êtrai.
  • Dropping the r: the future always keeps the r — je parlerai, not je parlai.
  • Mixing up the tiny s: -ai = will, -ais = would. Je voudrai vs je voudrais mean different things.
  • Conditional after "si": never si j'aurais — it is si + imperfect → conditional (Si j'avais…, je viendrais).
Recap

Your future & conditional toolkit

Simple future: infinitive + -ai/-as/-a/-ons/-ez/-ont (drop the -e of -re verbs). je parlerai, tu finiras, il vendra.

Irregular future stems: être→ser-, avoir→aur-, aller→ir-, faire→fer-, venir→viendr-, pouvoir→pourr-, vouloir→voudr-, devoir→devr-, voir→verr-, savoir→saur-, falloir→il faudra.

Conditional: future stem + imperfect endings (-ais…-aient) = "would". je voudrais, j'aimerais, on pourrait, il faudrait, ce serait.

Si clauses: si + present → future (Si j'ai le temps, je viendrai); si + imperfect → conditional (Si j'avais le temps, je viendrais).

Polite: the conditional (je voudrais, pourriez-vous…) softens requests.
