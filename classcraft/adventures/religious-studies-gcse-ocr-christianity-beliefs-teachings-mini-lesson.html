Mission 1

Mission 1: Christians call God all-powerful, all-loving and a fair judge. How can one God be all three?

Make a guess: Christians call God all-powerful, all-loving and a fair judge. How can one God be all three?

Mission 1

One God

  • Christianity is monotheistic: there is only one God.
  • It inherits this from Judaism: "Hear, Israel: The LORD is our God. The LORD is one." (Deuteronomy 6:4)
  • The first commandments in Exodus 20 (verses 2–11) are about God: no other gods, no idols, God's name kept holy, the Sabbath kept for God.
  • Christians believe God is greater than any human idea of God.
  • So they use several words, each naming one attribute (quality) of the one God.
  • (How one God is Father, Son and Holy Spirit: a later mission.)
Mission 1

Power and knowledge

  • Omnipotent: all-powerful. Nothing that can be done is beyond God.
  • The creeds call God Almighty.
  • Genesis 1: God only speaks, and things exist. That is power without effort.
  • Omniscient: all-knowing. God knows every thought, past, present and future.
  • Significance: Christians trust God in prayer, because God both knows the need and can act.
Mission 1

Loving, just, forgiving

  • Benevolent (omni-benevolent): all-loving; God wills only good for creation.
  • Just: God is fair. Wrongdoing matters to God, and God judges rightly.
  • Judge: Christians believe all people will one day be judged by God, through Christ (Matthew 25:31–46).
  • Forgiving: God forgives those who turn back. In the Parable of the Lost Son (Luke 15:11–32), the father runs to welcome his son home.
  • Christians hold these together: God's justice means wrong is real; God's love offers a way back.
Mission 1

Beyond time, beyond the world, close by

  • Eternal: without beginning or end; not limited by time.
  • Transcendent: beyond and outside the created world; not part of nature.
  • Immanent: present and active within the world and in people's lives.
  • Personal: someone who can be known and spoken to, as in "Our Father" (Matthew 6:9).
  • Transcendent and immanent are not opposites Christians choose between: most hold both.
  • Significance: a transcendent God is worshipped with awe; an immanent, personal God is prayed to as a friend.

Mission 1

God in Genesis 1–3

  • Creator: "In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth." (Genesis 1:1)
  • Eternal and transcendent: God is there before anything else, and stands outside what is made.
  • Omnipotent: "God said, 'Let there be light,' and there was light." (Genesis 1:3)
  • Benevolent: "God saw everything that he had made, and, behold, it was very good." (Genesis 1:31)
  • Immanent and personal: in Genesis 2–3 God forms the man from dust, breathes life into him, and is heard walking in the garden (Genesis 3:8).

Mission 1

Father, Creator, Judge

  • Father: Jesus teaches his followers to pray "Our Father in heaven" (Matthew 6:9–13). God cares for and provides for people like a loving parent.
  • Creator: God made everything that exists and keeps it in being (Genesis 1).
  • Judge: in the Sheep and the Goats (Matthew 25:31–46), people are judged by how they treated the hungry, the stranger, the sick and the prisoner.
  • So the same God who loves also holds people responsible.
  • Significance: prayer with trust, gratitude for creation, and care for others as if serving God.
Mission 1Diversity

Same God, different emphases

  • All mainstream Christians affirm every attribute. Traditions differ in which they stress.
  • Orthodox: God's transcendence (God beyond and outside the world) and mystery; worship is full of awe; some theology speaks of God mainly by saying what God is not.
  • Catholic: all attributes held together; God can be known through creation and reason as well as through revelation.
  • Reformed (Calvinist) Protestants: God's sovereignty: God's power and control over all things.
  • Evangelical and Pentecostal: God as personal (someone who can be known and spoken to) and immanent (present and active within the world): a living relationship with Jesus; the Holy Spirit at work today.
  • Quakers: God's immanence (God within the world and people): "that of God in every one".
  • These emphases shape how each group reads its sources: e.g. Luke 15 read mainly as forgiveness, Matthew 25 mainly as judgement.

Mission 1Evaluate

"Love is the most important quality of God."

  • Reasons for: "God is love" (1 John 4:8). John 3:16: God's love is the reason for the Incarnation and the cross. Jesus names love of God and neighbour as the greatest commandments (Mark 12:29–31).
  • Reasons against: love without power could not create or save (omnipotence). Love that ignores wrong would fail victims, so justice matters too (Matthew 25). Worship in many traditions, especially Orthodox and Catholic, stresses God's holiness and transcendence (God beyond the world): "Holy, holy, holy" (Isaiah 6:3).
  • Another view: many theologians say God's qualities cannot be ranked: each describes the whole of God.
  • Judge it by: Do the qualities depend on each other? Which most shapes how Christians live? Can God's qualities be ranked at all?
Mission 1Worked answer

Belief, source, development

Question: Explain two Christian beliefs about the nature of God. Refer to a source of Christian belief.

  • Belief 1: God is omnipotent. Development: God made everything simply by speaking ("Let there be light", Genesis 1:3), so Christians trust that God can act when they pray.
  • Belief 2: God is loving. Source: "God so loved the world, that he gave his only born Son" (John 3:16). Development: God's love is shown in sending Jesus, so Christians try to show the same self-giving love (agape) to others.

One God, many attributes: power, love and justice are not rivals but different sides of the same God.

Mission 2

Mission 2: How can Christians believe in only one God, yet pray to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit?

Make a guess: How can Christians believe in only one God, yet pray to the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit?

Mission 2

One God, not three

  • Christians are monotheists, like Jews and Muslims.
  • They hold to the Jewish Shema, which Jesus himself quoted (Mark 12:29): "Hear, Israel: The LORD is our God. The LORD is one." (Deuteronomy 6:4)
  • So the Trinity (Latin trinitas, "threeness": God as three-in-one) never means three gods.
  • The word "Trinity" is not in the Bible. It names a belief Christians find in the Bible.
Mission 2

Three Persons, one God

  • Most Christians believe in one God in three Persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
  • Each Person is fully God, not a third of God.
  • The three are distinct (not the same as each other) but not separate (not three beings).
  • All three are equal and eternal. None was made.
  • Not three gods. Not one God wearing three masks in turn.
Mission 2

Each Person

  • Trinitarian Christians describe each Person like this:
  • Father: Creator and source of all; loves and cares for people.
  • "Therefore you shall be perfect, just as your Father in heaven is perfect." (Matthew 5:48)
  • Son: Jesus Christ, God become human (the Incarnation); saves humanity.
  • Paul: Christ, though in the form of God, emptied himself, became human as a servant, and was obedient to death on a cross (Philippians 2:5–8, paraphrase).
  • Holy Spirit: God present and active in the world now; guides, strengthens and gives life.
  • Jesus promises the Father will send "another Counsellor… the Spirit of truth" (John 14:16–17).
  • Relationships: the Father sends the Son; the Father (through the Son) sends the Spirit; each lives in the others in love.

Mission 2

More roots in scripture

  • Jesus sends his followers to baptise "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit" (Matthew 28:19): one name, three Persons.
  • The Spirit is promised as "another Counsellor" (John 14:16): Christians read "another" as one like Jesus, sent by the Father.
  • So the doctrine grew from texts like these, then was defined by the Church.
Mission 2

The Nicene Creed

  • A priest, Arius (c. 250–336), taught that the Son was created by the Father.
  • The Council of Nicaea (325) rejected this. The Son is "Begotten, not made, Being of one substance with the Father" (Nicene Creed, Book of Common Prayer).
  • Of one substance (Greek homoousios): the same divine being. Catholics say "consubstantial with the Father" at Mass.
  • The Council of Constantinople (381) added more on the Spirit: "the Lord and giver of life", worshipped with the Father and the Son.
  • The Nicene Creed is built in three parts: Father, Son, Holy Spirit.
Quick check
?What was the main point the Council of Nicaea (325) made against the teaching of Arius?
Mission 2

The Trinity in worship and life

  • Baptism is "in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit".
  • Sign of the cross (Catholic, Orthodox, many Anglicans and Lutherans) traces the same words.
  • Prayer is often to the Father, through the Son, in the Spirit. Many hymns and blessings end with all three ("Glory be to the Father…").
  • Daily life: God is a communion of love, so Christians aim to live in loving community; they rely on the Spirit's help.
Mission 2Diversity

Christians who reject the Trinity

  • Mainstream churches (Catholic, Orthodox, most Protestant) treat the Trinity as essential.
  • Unitarians: God is one Person; Jesus is a human teacher and example. Today's Unitarians hold a wide range of beliefs.
  • Christadelphians: the Father alone is God; Jesus is God's Son, but did not exist before his birth; the Holy Spirit is God's power, not a Person.
  • These groups see themselves as Christian and say they follow the Bible's original teaching; mainstream churches disagree.
Quick check
?Which of these beliefs is held by Christadelphians?
Mission 2Evaluate

"The Trinity is a contradiction."

  • Reasons for: one cannot equal three. The word "Trinity" is not in the Bible. Deuteronomy 6:4 stresses that God is one. Unitarians and Christadelphians reject it. Every everyday analogy seems to fail.
  • Reasons against: God is one in being and three in Persons: "one" and "three" count different things, so there is no logical contradiction. Scripture treats Father, Son and Spirit as God (Matthew 28:19; John 10:30; Galatians 4:6). Christians call it a mystery: beyond full understanding, but not against reason.
  • Experience: Christians say they meet God as above them (Father), with them (Son) and within them (Spirit).
  • Judge it by: Do "one" and "three" count the same thing? Must a belief be fully understood to be reasonable? Should belief rest on the Bible alone or also on the Church's councils?
Mission check

?A student describes the Trinity as "like one actor playing three roles in a play, one after another". How would most Christians (Catholic, Orthodox and most Protestants) respond?

One being, three Persons, all at once: that is what keeps the Trinity from being three gods or one God in disguise.

Mission 3

What a creed is

  • A creed is a short statement of shared belief. From Latin credo: "I believe".
  • It sums up what a church teaches about God, Jesus and salvation.
  • Said aloud together, it shows who belongs: "this is what we believe".
  • The two most used: the Apostles' Creed and the Nicene Creed.
  • Both are built in three parts: Father, Son, Holy Spirit.
Mission 3

Where the Apostles' Creed came from

  • It grew from the questions asked of new Christians at baptism in Rome: "Do you believe in God the Father…?"
  • An early Roman form was written down by about 340.
  • Its final form was settled in Western Europe by about the 8th century.
  • A legend says each of the twelve apostles wrote one line. Historians see it as a summary of the apostles' teaching, not their words.
  • It is used mainly in Western churches: Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran, Methodist, Reformed.
Mission 3Read the source

The Apostles' Creed

"I believe in God the Father Almighty, Maker of heaven and earth:

And in Jesus Christ his only Son our Lord, Who was conceived by the Holy Ghost, Born of the Virgin Mary, Suffered under Pontius Pilate, Was crucified, dead, and buried, He descended into hell; The third day he rose again from the dead, He ascended into heaven, And sitteth on the right hand of God the Father Almighty; From thence he shall come to judge the quick and the dead.

I believe in the Holy Ghost; The holy catholic Church; The Communion of Saints; The Forgiveness of sins; The Resurrection of the body, And the life everlasting. Amen." (Book of Common Prayer, 1662)

  • Old words: Ghost = Spirit; quick = living; sitteth = sits.

Mission 3

What the Creed affirms

  • Father: Almighty Creator of everything.
  • Son: Jesus is God's Son and Lord; born as a human (Incarnation); died, rose, ascended; will return as judge.
  • "Descended into hell": went to the place of the dead, between his death and resurrection.
  • Holy Spirit: God at work now.
  • "Holy catholic Church": catholic means universal: all Christians everywhere, not one denomination.
  • "Communion of saints": all believers, living and dead, united in Christ.
  • Ends with hope: forgiveness, resurrection of the body, life everlasting.

Mission 3

The Nicene Creed

  • Agreed at the Councils of Nicaea (325) and Constantinople (381).
  • Said at the Eucharist or Mass on Sundays (Catholic, Anglican, Lutheran) and at the Orthodox Divine Liturgy.
  • Same three parts, but more detail on who the Son is: "God of God, Light of Light, Very God of very God, Begotten, not made, Being of one substance with the Father".
  • More on the Spirit: "the Lord and giver of life", worshipped and glorified with the Father and the Son.
  • So it states the Trinity more fully: three Persons, each fully God.
  • Both creeds are key sources of wisdom and authority for the Trinity.
Quick check
?Which of these is found in the Nicene Creed but not in the Apostles' Creed?
Mission 3

Creeds in Christian life

  • Baptism and confirmation: candidates (or parents and godparents) answer questions drawn from the Apostles' Creed.
  • Sunday worship: the Nicene Creed at Mass or Eucharist; the Apostles' Creed at Anglican Morning and Evening Prayer.
  • Unity: Christians of different churches can say the same words together.
  • Daily life: "forgiveness of sins" → forgive others; "Maker of heaven and earth" → care for creation; "he shall come to judge" → live responsibly; "resurrection of the body" → hope at funerals.
Mission 3Diversity

Different views on creeds

  • Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican, Lutheran, Methodist: creeds are central, trusted summaries of the faith.
  • Orthodox: use the Nicene Creed only, without the Western words "and the Son" about the Spirit.
  • Many Baptist, Pentecostal and independent evangelical churches: hold the same core beliefs, but stress the Bible itself; many rarely recite creeds.
  • Quakers: no creeds; they stress the inward experience of God and fear set words can replace living faith.
  • Unitarians: no creeds; they reject the Trinity.
  • Some liberal Christians: say the creeds, but read some lines (e.g. the virgin birth) as symbols.

Mission 4

God the Creator

  • Christians believe God made everything that exists: the whole universe.
  • God made it out of nothing (Latin ex nihilo): no material existed before.
  • God made it freely, by speaking: "God said… and there was" (Genesis 1).
  • God saw it was "very good" (Genesis 1:31): creation is good.
  • Paul: God's eternal power and divine nature can be clearly seen in what God has made (Romans 1:20, paraphrase).

Mission 4

Father, Word and Spirit at work

  • Father: the source; the one who creates (Genesis 1:1).
  • Word (the Son): the one through whom all things were made (John 1:3), heard in "God said" (Genesis 1:3).
  • Spirit: God's life-giving power, "hovering" over the waters (Genesis 1:2).
  • John 1 means the Son existed before he was born as Jesus: his pre-existence.

Mission 4

Two accounts, side by side

  • First account (Genesis 1:1–2:3): six days, then God rests on the seventh. God speaks; humans, male and female together, come last, in God's image. God is transcendent.
  • Second account (from Genesis 2:4): no days. "The LORD God" forms the man from dust and breathes life into him, plants a garden, brings the animals, then makes the woman. God is close and personal.
  • Different order, different style, different picture of God.
  • Most Christians read them as two complementary accounts, each teaching something true about God and humans.

Mission 4Diversity

Literal or metaphorical?

  • Literal: some Christians, often evangelical or fundamentalist, read Genesis 1 as six 24-hour days; the earth is thousands of years old (young-earth creationism).
  • Day-age: others read each "day" as a long age; the order still matters.
  • Metaphorical (non-literal): Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican and many Protestant Christians read Genesis as teaching truths about God and humans, not a scientific timetable.
Quick check

Mission 4Evaluate

"Christian beliefs about creation conflict with science."

  • Reasons for: a literal reading gives six days and a young earth; science gives an age of billions of years. In Genesis 1, plants appear before the sun. Some creationists reject evolution.
  • Reasons against: most Christians read Genesis metaphorically: science asks how, Genesis asks who and why. The Big Bang theory was first proposed by Georges Lemaître (1894–1966), a Catholic priest and physicist. A universe with a beginning fits belief in creation out of nothing.
  • Judge it by: Does it depend on how Genesis is read? Can science answer "why is there anything at all?" Do literal readers speak for all Christians?
Mission 4Worked answer

Word and Spirit in creation

Question: Explain two Christian beliefs about the role of the Word and the Spirit in creation. Refer to a source of wisdom and authority.

  • Belief 1: The Word, the Son, was the agent of creation. Source: "All things were made through him." (John 1:3) Development: so Christians believe Jesus existed before creation, and creation bears his mark.
  • Belief 2: The Spirit gave life and order. Source: "God's Spirit was hovering over the surface of the waters" (Genesis 1:2). Development: so Christians believe the same Spirit still gives life and guides believers today.

Mission 5

Made in the image of God

"God created man in his own image. In God's image he created him; male and female he created them." (Genesis 1:27)

  • Latin: imago Dei, "image of God".
  • Not a physical likeness: Christians believe God has no body.
  • It means humans share, in a small way, some of God's qualities: reason, free will, conscience, creativity, and the ability to love and to know God.
  • Male and female are made in God's image equally.

Mission 5

Humans in Genesis 2: made for a purpose

  • Dust: humans belong to the earth. (Hebrew adam, "human", sounds like adamah, "ground".)
  • Breath of God: humans have a spiritual side and a special relationship with God.
  • Work: to "cultivate and keep" the garden. Work and care are good, part of God's plan.
  • Naming the animals (Genesis 2:19–20): understanding and responsibility for other creatures.
  • Partnership: God says it is not good for the man to be alone, and makes the woman (Genesis 2:18–23, paraphrase). Humans are made for relationship.
  • Purpose: to know and love God, to love one another, and to care for creation.

Mission 5

Dominion or stewardship?

  • Dominion: rule or authority over nature (Genesis 1:26, 28).
  • Historian Lynn White (1907–1987) argued in 1967 that reading dominion as a licence to exploit helped cause the ecological crisis.
  • Stewardship: looking after something that belongs to someone else, and answering to them for it.
  • "The earth is the LORD's" (Psalm 24:1): humans are not the owners.
  • Most Christians today read dominion as stewardship: rule as God would, like a good shepherd, not a tyrant.
Quick check
?Which statement best describes the Christian idea of stewardship?
Mission 5Diversity

How Christians see our place in creation

  • Stewardship (Catholic, Anglican, Methodist, many evangelicals): humans care for creation on God's behalf.
  • Human needs first: some conservative Christians stress dominion and human development; environmental policy must not make the poor poorer.
  • Orthodox: humans as "priests of creation", offering the world back to God in thanksgiving. Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew is called the "Green Patriarch".
  • Kinship with creation (Franciscan): St Francis of Assisi (c. 1181–1226) praised "Brother Sun" and "Sister Moon".
  • All agree that creation is good and humans are accountable to God for it.
Mission 5Evaluate

"Being made in God's image means humans matter more than the rest of creation."

  • Reasons for: only humans are made in God's image (Genesis 1:27) and given dominion (1:28). Jesus says people are worth more than many sparrows (Matthew 10:31). Many Christians believe human life is sacred in a way other life is not (the sanctity of life).
  • Reasons against: God called each part of creation "good" before humans existed (Genesis 1:10–25). God's covenant after the Flood is with "every living creature" (Genesis 9:10). Stewardship means more responsibility, not more importance.
  • Judge it by: Does "special" mean "more important" or "more responsible"? Can something matter to God without being in God's image? Who speaks for future generations?
Mission check

"Rule" in Genesis is read by most Christians as care: the earth is God's, and humans answer for it.

Mission 6

The Fall

  • In Eden, God allows the first humans to eat from every tree but one: the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 2:16–17).
  • The serpent says that if they eat, they will be "like God" (Genesis 3:5, paraphrase).
  • The woman eats, and gives some to the man (3:6). They feel shame and hide from God (3:7–8).
  • Each blames another: the man the woman, the woman the serpent.
  • Consequences: pain, hard work, death; they are sent out of the garden (3:16–24).
  • Christians call this the Fall: humans fall away from their close relationship with God.
  • Genesis says "fruit". The apple comes from later art.

Mission 6

What sin is

  • Sin: any thought, word or action that goes against God's will.
  • Sin damages relationships: with God, with others, with oneself, with creation. (In Genesis 3, all four break.)
  • Paul: "all have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23).
  • God's law (the commandments) shows what sin is, but cannot by itself stop people sinning (Romans 3:20, paraphrase).
Mission 6Diversity

Original sin: different views

  • Original sin: the sinful state, or tendency, that all humans inherit from the first sin. Not the same as each person's own sins.
  • Augustine (354–430): through Adam, all humans inherit a fallen nature and share his guilt (Romans 5:12).
  • Catholic: every human is born lacking the original holiness God intended; original sin is "contracted", not committed. Baptism removes it, but a pull towards sin (concupiscence) remains.
  • Protestant Reformers (Luther, Calvin): human nature is corrupted in every part; only God's grace can save.
  • Orthodox: "ancestral sin": humans inherit death and a weakened nature, but not Adam's guilt.
  • Many liberal Christians: the Fall is a symbolic story about every human's tendency to selfishness.

Mission 6

Free will and its limits

  • Christians believe God gave free will so that humans could love God freely, not as robots.
  • The Fall happened through free will: humans chose to disobey.
  • Limits: our bodies, our upbringing and society, and sin itself.
  • Paul says he fails to do the good he wants to do (Romans 7:19, paraphrase).
  • So many Christians believe humans need God's grace to be truly free.
Mission 6

Sin: a barrier to salvation

  • "For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 6:23)
  • Salvation: being saved from sin and restored to friendship with God.
  • Christians believe sin separates people from God, and most say humans cannot remove it by their own efforts.
  • Christians believe the barrier is removed by God's grace through Jesus.

Mission 6Evaluate

"The Fall must be literally true for original sin to make sense."

  • Reasons for: Paul treats Adam as one man through whom sin entered the world (Romans 5:12). Without a first sin, why would every human share a fallen nature? Catholic teaching says Genesis uses figurative language but describes a real event at the start of human history.
  • Reasons against: adam means "human": the story can describe every person. Selfishness can be seen everywhere without a history lesson. Orthodox teaching stresses inherited death, not inherited guilt. Many Christians accept evolution and still believe in original sin.
  • Judge it by: Does a doctrine need a historical event, or only a true picture of human nature? How should Paul's use of Adam be read?

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Christians believe in one God (monotheism), described by many attributes: omnipotent, loving (benevolent), just.
  • Genesis 1–3 shows God as Creator, eternal, transcendent, omnipotent and good.
  • God is also omniscient, personal, forgiving, and Father, Creator and Judge; different traditions stress different attributes.

Mission 2

  • Christians believe in one God in three Persons: Father, Son and Holy Spirit, each fully God, distinct but not separate.
  • At Jesus' baptism (Matthew 3:16–17) all three Persons are present.
  • The Nicene Creed (Nicaea, 325) says the Son is "of one substance" with the Father.
  • Some groups, such as Unitarians and Christadelphians, reject the Trinity.

Mission 3

  • A creed is a short statement of shared belief; the Apostles' Creed grew from baptism questions in Rome and has three parts: Father, Son, Holy Spirit.
  • The Nicene Creed (325, 381) adds that the Son is "of one substance with the Father", stating the Trinity more fully.

Mission 4

  • Christians believe God created everything out of nothing: the Father creates, through the Word (the Son, John 1:1–3), with the Spirit giving life (Genesis 1:1–3).
  • Genesis has two accounts (1:1–2:3 and from 2:4) with different pictures of God; some Christians read them literally, many metaphorically.

Mission 5

  • Humans are made in God's image (imago Dei, Genesis 1:27): this gives every person equal dignity, free will and responsibility.
  • Genesis 1:28 gives dominion; Genesis 2:15 asks humans to "cultivate and keep" the garden. Most Christians read dominion as stewardship.

Mission 6

  • The Fall (Genesis 3): humans disobey God and their relationships with God, each other and creation break.
  • Original sin is the fallen state all humans inherit; Catholic, Protestant, Orthodox and liberal Christians understand it differently.
  • Sin is a barrier to salvation: "the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life" (Romans 6:23).
