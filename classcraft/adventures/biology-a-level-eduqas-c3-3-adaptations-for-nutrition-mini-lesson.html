Mission 1

Mission 1: Some bacteria thrive in total darkness with no food, just dissolved iron and gases. How do they grow?

Make a guess: Some bacteria thrive in total darkness with no food, just dissolved iron and gases. How do they grow?

Mission 1

Make it or take it

  • Every cell needs carbon compounds: sugars, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids.
  • Autotroph: makes its own carbon compounds from simple inorganic substances (carbon dioxide, water, mineral ions).
  • Heterotroph: obtains carbon compounds from other organisms, living or dead.
  • Making carbon compounds needs energy from outside the organism.
  • Say: "autotrophs use an external energy source" — not: "autotrophs make their own energy".
Mission 1

Two sources of energy

  • Photoautotroph: energy from light.
  • Plants, algae, and several groups of photosynthetic prokaryotes (e.g. cyanobacteria).
  • Chemoautotroph: energy from oxidation reactions of inorganic substances.
  • Oxidation releases energy. Example: iron-oxidising bacteria: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻.
  • The energy released drives carbon fixation. No light needed.
  • Say: "chemoautotrophs oxidise inorganic substances" — not: "chemoautotrophs respire food they absorb".
Quick check
?Iron-oxidising bacteria live in mine drainage water in darkness. How do they obtain the energy they need to synthesise carbon compounds?
Mission 1

Heterotrophs: break down, then rebuild

  • Food holds large carbon compounds: proteins, nucleic acids, polysaccharides.
  • Digest: hydrolyse them into small molecules. Either outside the organism (fungi) or inside it (animals).
  • Absorb the small molecules.
  • Assimilate: build them into the heterotroph's own molecules, e.g. its own proteins.
  • Three kinds of heterotroph: saprotrophic (also called saprobiotic), holozoic, parasitic. Next mission.
  • Say: "amino acids are built into its own proteins" — not: "the protein eaten is used as it is".
Quick check
?A heterotroph eats food containing proteins. Which statement describes how it uses the carbon compounds in this food?
Mission check

?At a deep-sea vent, far below the reach of sunlight, bacteria fix carbon dioxide using energy released by oxidising hydrogen sulphide. Giant tubeworms there have no mouth and no gut. The bacteria live inside the tubeworm's tissues, and the worm obtains its carbon compounds from them. Which statement is correct?

Light or chemicals, the question is always the same: does the organism build its carbon compounds from carbon dioxide, or take them from someone else?

Mission 2

Mission 2: A fungus has no mouth and no stomach. So how does it digest a fallen tree?

Make a guess: A fungus has no mouth and no stomach. So how does it digest a fallen tree?

Mission 2

Ways to take carbon compounds

  • Heterotrophs differ in where they digest and what they feed on.
  • Saprotrophic: feeds on dead organic matter; digests it outside the body.
  • Holozoic: takes food into the body; digests it inside.
  • Parasitic: feeds on a living host, at the host's expense.
  • Eduqas also accepts saprobiotic for saprotrophic.
Mission 2

Saprotrophs: digestion outside the body

  • Fungal hyphae grow through the food; a network of hyphae is a mycelium.
  • Hyphae secrete enzymes onto the food: extracellular digestion.
  • Large molecules (cellulose, proteins, starch) → small soluble ones (sugars, amino acids).
  • Products are absorbed across the hyphal walls and membranes.
  • Mycelium: thin and branched, so a large surface area for secretion and absorption.
  • Many bacteria feed this way too.
  • Say: "saprotrophs digest externally, then absorb" — not: "fungi take in bits of food".
Quick check
?Which statement describes saprotrophic nutrition?
Mission 2

Holozoic: four stages

  • Ingestion: food taken into the body, e.g. through a mouth.
  • Digestion: large molecules hydrolysed to small ones inside the body.
  • Absorption: small molecules cross into the body's cells or blood.
  • Assimilation: absorbed molecules built into the animal's own molecules and tissues.
  • Egestion: undigested matter removed.
  • Say: "absorbed, then assimilated" — not: "absorption and assimilation are the same".
Quick check
?A dog eats meat. Proteins in the meat are hydrolysed in the gut, the amino acids pass across the wall of the small intestine into the blood, and later some of them are used in the dog's muscle cells to make new protein. Which term describes the use of the amino acids to make the dog's own protein?
Mission 2

One cell, one meal: Amoeba

  • Amoeba is unicellular: no gut. Digestion happens inside the cell: intracellular digestion.
  • Pseudopodia flow round a food particle: phagocytosis.
  • The particle is enclosed in a food vacuole.
  • Lysosomes fuse with the vacuole and release hydrolytic enzymes.
  • Soluble products are absorbed into the cytoplasm and assimilated.
  • Undigested remains leave at the cell membrane: egestion by exocytosis.
Mission 2

Parasites: Taenia and Pediculus

  • Taenia (tapeworm): endoparasite in the human small intestine.
    • Scolex with suckers (and, in T. solium, hooks) grips the gut wall.
    • No gut: absorbs food the host has already digested, across its whole body surface.
    • Long and flat: large surface area to volume ratio.
    • Thick tegument resists the host's digestive enzymes.
    • Segments (proglottids) each hold male and female organs; huge numbers of eggs.
  • Pediculus (head louse): ectoparasite on the scalp.
    • Claws grip hairs; flattened body.
    • Piercing mouthparts suck the host's blood.
    • Eggs (nits) glued to hairs.
Mission 2Practical

Observing tapeworm specimens and slides

  • Examine a preserved Taenia specimen: note scolex, neck and chain of proglottids.
  • Under low power, view a prepared slide of the scolex: identify suckers and (T. solium) hooks.
  • View a slide of a mature proglottid: identify reproductive organs.
  • Draw with clean single lines; label; state magnification.
  • Calculation: actual size = image size ÷ magnification.
    • Illustrative data: scolex image 30 mm wide at ×40.
    • Actual width = 30 ÷ 40 = 0.75 mm.
Quick check
?Taenia lives in the human small intestine and has no mouth and no gut. Which statement about its nutrition is correct?
Mission check

?In a compost heap, woodlice eat dead leaves and digest them in their gut. A mould grows hyphae over the same leaves, secretes enzymes onto them and absorbs the sugars released. Soil bacteria on the leaves feed in the same way as the mould. Which statement is correct?

Same dead leaf, two ways in: digest it where it lies, or swallow it and digest it inside.

Mission 3

Mission 3: A skull is all that is left of an animal. How can its teeth reveal what it ate?

Make a guess: A skull is all that is left of an animal. How can its teeth reveal what it ate?

Mission 3

One opening or two

  • Hydra: a sac-like gut with one opening.
    • Tentacles push prey through the mouth into the cavity.
    • Gland cells secrete enzymes: digestion starts in the cavity.
    • Gut cells engulf fragments and finish digestion inside the cells.
    • Undigested waste leaves by the same opening.
    • Gut lining undifferentiated: no specialised regions.
  • Tube gut (e.g. mammals): mouth for ingestion, anus for egestion.
    • Food moves one way through specialised regions.
    • Each region has its own enzymes and conditions, e.g. acid stomach, alkaline small intestine.
    • The animal can keep feeding while earlier food is still being digested.
Quick check
?Hydra has a sac-like gut with a single opening. A mammal has a tube gut with separate openings for ingestion and egestion. Which statement explains why a tube gut is more efficient?
Mission 3

Teeth match the work the food needs

  • Four tooth types: incisors, canines, premolars, molars.
  • Meat: cut and sheared. Sharp, blade-like cheek teeth; long pointed canines.
  • Tough plant food: ground for a long time. Broad, flat, ridged molars; thick enamel; big jaw muscles.
  • Mixed diet (omnivore): smaller, less specialised teeth.
  • Read the whole skull: teeth, jaw size, jaw joint, muscle attachments.
  • Say: "reduced canines and grinding molars" — not: "it has canines, so it eats meat".
Mission 3

Dog and sheep skulls

  • Carnivore (dog):
    • Long pointed canines grip and kill prey.
    • Carnassials (upper premolar 4 + lower molar 1) slice like scissors.
    • Tight jaw joint: up-and-down movement only.
    • Large temporalis muscle: powerful closing bite.
  • Herbivore (sheep):
    • No upper incisors or canines: a horny pad.
    • Lower incisors and canines cut grass against the pad.
    • Diastema: a gap where the tongue moves grass.
    • Broad, ridged premolars and molars grind.
    • Loose jaw joint: side-to-side grinding; large masseter muscle.
Mission 3Practical

Reading a skull

  • Examine the skulls and dentition of a herbivore and a carnivore (e.g. sheep and dog).
  • Identify each tooth type; note size, shape and wear.
  • Check the canines, the cheek teeth, the jaw joint and muscle attachment areas (e.g. a sagittal crest).
  • Compare with living mammals of known diet. State a deduction and the evidence for it.
  • Count teeth as a dental formula (one side; upper / lower):
    • Dog: I 3/3, C 1/1, PM 4/4, M 2/3. Total = 2 × (10 + 11) = 42.
    • Sheep: I 0/3, C 0/1, PM 3/3, M 3/3. Total = 2 × (6 + 10) = 32.
Mission 3

Guts built for cellulose or protein

  • Mammals make no cellulase.
  • Ruminants (cows, sheep) house microorganisms in the rumen that digest cellulose.
  • Grass → rumen (microbes ferment cellulose) → regurgitated, re-chewed (cud) → omasum (water absorbed) → abomasum (true stomach; own enzymes digest the microbes).
  • Products of fermentation, mainly fatty acids, are absorbed through the rumen wall.
  • Herbivores: long gut; plant food is slow to digest.
  • Carnivores: short gut; protein and fat are easier to digest.
  • Say: "microbes in the rumen produce cellulase" — not: "the cow digests cellulose with its own enzymes".
Quick check
?Grass is mostly cellulose. Which statement explains how a cow obtains energy from it?
Mission check

?The giant panda belongs to the mammal order Carnivora, like dogs and cats. Imagine only its skull had been found. The skull has large canines, very broad flat premolars and molars with many low cusps, a heavy lower jaw, wide cheekbones and a tall sagittal crest where large chewing muscles attach. What can be deduced about its diet?

Teeth show the work the food needs, whatever family the animal belongs to.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Food in your gut is still outside your body. So when does a meal actually get inside you?

Make a guess: Food in your gut is still outside your body. So when does a meal actually get inside you?

Mission 4

A tube through the body

  • Holozoic nutrition: food is taken in and digested inside the body.
  • Four stages: ingestion → digestion → absorption → assimilation. Undigested waste is egested.
  • The gut is a tube open at both ends. Its lumen is still outside the body's cells.
  • Digestion: enzymes hydrolyse large, insoluble molecules into small, soluble ones.
  • Absorption: those small molecules cross the gut lining into the blood or lymph. Only now are they inside.
  • Assimilation: cells build the absorbed molecules into the animal's own molecules and tissues.
Mission 4

Sac gut to tube gut

  • Hydra: a sac-like gut with one opening. Food goes in and waste comes out the same way.
  • Its gut lining is barely specialised. A new meal must wait until the last one has gone.
  • Tube gut: mouth for ingestion, anus for egestion. Food moves one way.
  • So the tube can have specialised regions, each with its own enzymes and conditions.
  • Food is processed in sequence, and the animal can keep eating while it digests.
Quick check
?A dog eats meat. At which stage are the amino acids from the meat first inside the dog's body?

Mission 4Practical

Drawing the ileum

  • Practical: examine slides of duodenum and ileum.
  • Use the low-power objective to find the whole wall; high power to check a layer.
  • A block diagram shows where tissues are, not individual cells.
  • Draw each layer's outline in proportion. Clear, continuous lines; no shading.
  • Label with ruled lines that do not cross. Add a title and the magnification.
Quick check

Mission check

?A person eats celery. Humans make no enzyme that digests cellulose. One cellulose fibre from the celery passes along the whole gut and leaves in the faeces. Which statement about this fibre is correct?

A meal gets inside you only when its molecules cross the gut lining. Before that, it is still in a tube that runs through you.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Autotrophs make carbon compounds from inorganic substances using external energy: light (photoautotrophs) or oxidation of inorganic substances (chemoautotrophs). Heterotrophs take carbon compounds from other organisms, digest them, absorb and assimilate the products.

Mission 2

  • Saprotrophs secrete enzymes onto dead matter and absorb the products; holozoic animals ingest, digest internally, absorb and assimilate.
  • Amoeba digests food inside a vacuole in its one cell; parasites such as Taenia (inside the host) and Pediculus (outside) feed from a living host.

Mission 3

  • A tube gut with one-way flow and specialised regions digests more efficiently than Hydra's sac gut. Carnivores have pointed canines, carnassials and a short gut; ruminants have grinding molars, a horny pad and a rumen where microbes digest cellulose.
  • Diet is deduced from the whole skull, using a theory built from living mammals.

Mission 4

  • Holozoic nutrition: ingest, digest (extracellular hydrolysis in the lumen), absorb across the gut lining, assimilate.
