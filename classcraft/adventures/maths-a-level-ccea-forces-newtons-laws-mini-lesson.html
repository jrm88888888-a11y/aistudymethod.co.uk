Mechanics · Newton's laws

Newton's laws and the forces you meet

  • First law: a body stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a resultant force acts.
  • Second law: resultant force =ma= ma, in the direction of the acceleration. Forces add as vectors, so F=maF = ma too.
  • Third law: if A pushes B, B pushes A with an equal and opposite force.
  • Forces: weight W=mgW = mg, normal reaction RR, tension in a string, thrust in a rod, resistance. R=0R = 0 means contact is lost.

Mechanics · Newton's laws

Lifts and apparent weight

  • A passenger in a lift has two forces on them: weight mgmg down and the floor's reaction RR up.
  • Taking up as positive: R−mg=maR − mg = ma, so R=m(g+a)R = m(g + a).
  • Bathroom scales measure RR and display gR​R/g as a mass.
  • Accelerating upwards (or slowing on the way down): R>mgR > mg. Accelerating downwards (or slowing on the way up): R<mgR < mg. Constant velocity: R=mgR = mg.
  • For the lift cable, treat lift and passenger as one particle: T−(M+m)g=(M+m)aT − (M + m)g = (M + m)a.

Mechanics · Newton's laws

Connected particles

  • Particles joined by a light inextensible string (or a rigid tow bar) have the same acceleration.
  • A light string over a smooth pulley has the same tension on both sides.
  • Find the acceleration from the whole system: the tension is internal and cancels.
  • Then use one particle on its own to find the tension (or the thrust in a tow bar when braking).

Car and trailer

Question
A car of mass 1,000 kg tows a trailer of mass 400 kg along a straight horizontal road with a light rigid tow bar. The driving force is 2,000 N. Resistances are 300 N on the car and 160 N on the trailer. Find the acceleration and the tension in the tow bar.

Mission check

?On scales in a lift, what do they read as it speeds up going up? A 60 kg person stands on them while the lift accelerates upwards at 0.5 m s−2⁻².

The scales measure the floor's push RR. While the lift speeds up going up, R−mg=maR − mg = ma, so R=m(g+a)R = m(g + a) is more than your weight: a 60 kg person accelerating up at 0.5 m s−2⁻² reads 63.1 kg. At steady speed the reading is normal again.

Mechanics · resolving and friction

Resolving a force

  • A force FF at angle θθ to a direction has component FcosθFcos θ along it and FsinθFsin θ perpendicular to it.
  • On a slope at θθ to the horizontal, resolve parallel and perpendicular to the slope: the weight gives mgsinθmgsin θ down the slope and mgcosθmgcos θ into the slope.
  • A particle is in equilibrium if and only if the resolved parts in every direction sum to zero. Two perpendicular directions are enough.
  • Choose directions that avoid an unknown force where you can.

Mechanics · resolving and friction

The friction model

  • Friction acts along the surface, opposing motion or the tendency to move.
  • On a rough surface, F≤μRF≤μ R, where μμ is the coefficient of friction and RR is the normal reaction.
  • Static friction takes whatever value is needed, up to μRμ R. When it reaches μRμ R the body is in limiting equilibrium: about to slide.
  • When the body is moving, F=μRF=μ R.

Mission check

?A box is about to slide on a rough slope. Why doesn't the box's weight affect the angle?

Down the slope the pull is mgsinθmgsin θ; the most friction can give is μmgcosθμ mgcos θ. Both contain mm, so the box slides once tanθ>μtan θ>μ, whatever its mass. Stack two identical boxes and they slide at the same angle as one.

Recap

Big ideas

  • F=maF = ma uses the resultant force; forces add as vectors.
  • Lifts: R−mg=maR − mg = ma with up positive. Scales read RR, not your mass.
  • Connected particles share one acceleration: use the whole system for aa, then one particle for the tension.
  • Resolve parallel and perpendicular to a slope: weight gives mgsinθmgsin θ along and mgcosθmgcos θ into it; other forces can change RR.
  • Friction obeys F≤μRF≤μ R; F=μRF=μ R when moving or in limiting equilibrium.
  • On the point of sliding with no other forces, μ=tanθμ=tan θ, so mass does not matter.
