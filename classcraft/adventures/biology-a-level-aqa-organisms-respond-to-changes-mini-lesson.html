Mission 1

Mission 1: A plant stem can grow longer, and even bend towards a window, without making new cells. How?

Make a guess: A plant stem can grow longer, and even bend towards a window, without making new cells. How?

Mission 1

Chemical messengers from the tips

  • Plants coordinate growth with chemical signals: plant hormones.
  • AQA calls them plant growth factors.
  • Made in growing regions (shoot and root tips); they move to other tissues and act there.
  • No glands, no blood: they move from cell to cell or in the vascular tissue.
  • The best known: auxin. The main natural auxin is IAA (indoleacetic acid).
  • A tropism is a directional growth response to a directional stimulus: towards it = positive; away from it = negative.
Mission 1

Auxin makes cells longer

  • IAA acts in the region just behind the tip.
  • It promotes cell elongation, not cell division.
  • More elongation on one side than the other → the organ bends.
Quick check
?Which statement about IAA is correct?
Mission 1

Bending towards the light

  • Light from one side (lateral light) is detected at the shoot tip.
  • IAA is redistributed to the shaded side. It is not destroyed by light.
  • Shaded-side cells elongate more than lit-side cells.
  • Unequal growth bends the shoot towards the light: positive phototropism.
  • Takes hours to days: it is growth, not movement.
  • Say: "the shaded side elongates more" — not: "the lit side grows towards the light".
Quick check
?A seedling is grown in a box with a window on its left side only. After two days the shoot has curved towards the window. Which statement explains the curvature?
Mission 1

Roots and shoots respond in opposite ways

  • Root cells are far more sensitive to IAA than shoot cells.
  • A concentration that stimulates shoot cells inhibits root cells.
  • Seedling on its side: IAA collects on the lower side of both shoot and root.
  • Shoot: lower side elongates more → bends up: negative gravitropism.
  • Root: lower side elongation inhibited → upper side grows more → bends down: positive gravitropism.
  • Roots in lateral light: IAA moves to the shaded side, inhibits elongation → root bends away from light: negative phototropism.

Quick check
?A seedling is laid on its side. IAA builds up on the lower side of both its root and its shoot, reaching about 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³. The upper sides have about 10⁻¹⁰ mol dm⁻³. Using the table on the last screen, how will the root and shoot respond?
Mission 1Practical

IAA concentration and root growth (OCR PAG11)

  • AQA suggests this investigation; it is not one of the 12 required practicals.
  • Serial dilution: 1 cm³ of 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ IAA + 9 cm³ distilled water → 10⁻⁴; repeat down to 10⁻⁸.
  • Control: distilled water only.
  • 10 germinated seedlings per dish, on filter paper soaked in one solution. Measure root lengths.
  • Keep in the dark at 25 °C for 48 h (no phototropism). Measure again.
  • Calculate mean change in length, % change compared with control, and standard deviation.
  • Safe and ethical: goggles (IAA is an irritant); use only as many seedlings as needed; dispose of them properly.

Worked example (illustrative data): increase in root length / mm

  • Water: 12, 14, 11, 13, 15 → mean 13.0, SD 1.6
  • 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³: 6, 8, 5, 7, 9 → mean 7.0, SD 1.6
  • % change = (7.0 − 13.0) ÷ 13.0 × 100 = −46%
  • Mean ± 1 SD: water 11.4–14.6 mm; IAA 5.4–8.6 mm. The ranges do not overlap: the difference is likely to be real, not chance.
Mission check

?A thin plastic sheet is pushed down through the tip of a shoot so that it separates the half facing a lamp from the shaded half. The plastic stops any chemical crossing from one half to the other. The shoot is lit from one side for a day. It grows upwards without bending. What is the best explanation?

Bending is just unequal elongation: block the hormone gradient and the bend disappears.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Cut every nerve to a heart and it keeps on beating. So what are those nerves for?

Make a guess: Cut every nerve to a heart and it keeps on beating. So what are those nerves for?

Mission 2

The heart starts its own beat

  • Cardiac muscle is myogenic: it contracts without any nerve impulse.
  • The sinoatrial node (SAN), in the wall of the right atrium, is the pacemaker.
  • It sends out a wave of electrical excitation at regular intervals. Each wave starts one heartbeat.
  • Nerves and hormones only change the rate of the SAN. They do not start the beats.
  • Say: "the SAN initiates each beat" — not: "the brain sends each heartbeat".
Mission 2

Spreading the wave

  • (1) The wave from the SAN spreads across both atria → atrial systole.
  • (2) A layer of non-conducting tissue between atria and ventricles blocks it. The only way through is the atrioventricular node (AVN).
  • (3) The AVN delays the wave (about 0.1 s): the atria finish emptying first.
  • (4) The wave passes down the bundle of His in the septum to the apex, then up through the ventricle walls.
  • Ventricles contract from the apex upwards, squeezing blood up into the arteries.
  • AQA names: SAN, AVN, Purkyne tissue in the bundle of His.
Quick check
?Suppose the AVN passed the wave of excitation on with no delay. What would be the main effect?
Mission 2

Nerves and hormones adjust the rate

  • A centre in the medulla oblongata controls heart rate.
  • Sympathetic nerve to the SAN → SAN fires faster → heart rate up; contractions stronger, so stroke volume up.
  • Parasympathetic nerve (the vagus) to the SAN → SAN fires more slowly → heart rate down.
  • Say: "the medulla changes the rate of the SAN" — not: "the medulla makes the heart beat".
Mission 2

What the medulla senses

  • Baroreceptors (pressure receptors): stretch receptors in the walls of the aorta and carotid arteries. They monitor blood pressure.
  • Chemoreceptors: in the aorta and carotid arteries, and in the medulla. They monitor blood pH, CO2 and O2.
  • Blood pressure high → more baroreceptor impulses → medulla → more vagus impulses → rate falls.
  • Blood pressure low → fewer baroreceptor impulses → medulla → more sympathetic impulses → rate rises.
  • CO2 up, pH down (e.g. exercise) → chemoreceptors → medulla → sympathetic → rate rises → CO2 removed faster.
  • Each response reverses the change: negative feedback.
Quick check
?A person stands up quickly and their blood pressure falls for a few seconds. Which sequence describes the feedback response that restores blood pressure?
Mission check

?In a heart transplant, the nerves linking the donor heart to the medulla are cut, and they do not regrow for many months. Which statement about the transplanted heart is correct?

The heart makes its own beat; the nerves are the volume control, turning the rate up or down to match what the body needs.

Make a guess: Why does a resting nerve cell keep spending energy when it is not sending any signal?

Mission 3

A motor neurone

  • Cell body: nucleus and most of the cytoplasm.
  • Dendrites: many short fibres; carry impulses towards the cell body.
  • Axon: one long fibre; carries impulses away from the cell body.
  • Axon terminals end at an effector (a muscle or gland).
Mission 3

Myelin

  • Many axons are wrapped in a myelin sheath.
  • Made by Schwann cells, which wrap their membrane round and round the axon.
  • Myelin is lipid-rich: an electrical insulator.
  • Gaps between sheath sections: nodes of Ranvier.
  • Non-myelinated axons have no sheath and no nodes.
Quick check
?Which description of the structure of a neurone is correct?
Mission 3

The resting potential

  • At rest, the inside of the axon is negative relative to the outside.
  • Typical value: about −70 mV. This is the resting potential.
  • A membrane with a charge difference across it is polarised.
  • Membrane potential = voltage inside relative to outside.
  • The neurone is not "off": it is held ready to fire.
Mission 3

How the resting potential is made

  • Sodium–potassium pump: uses ATP to move 3 Na⁺ out for every 2 K⁺ in.
  • Result: Na⁺ high outside; K⁺ high inside. Two concentration gradients.
  • The membrane is more permeable to K⁺ than to Na⁺: many K⁺ channels are open; most Na⁺ channels are closed.
  • So K⁺ diffuses out faster than Na⁺ diffuses in → more positive charge outside.
  • Large negatively charged proteins stay inside.
  • Each ion moves down its electrochemical gradient (concentration + charge). K⁺ outflow slows as the inside becomes negative.
  • Say: "Na⁺ is actively transported out" — not: "Na⁺ diffuses out".
Quick check
?Measurements on a resting neurone show that potassium ions are about 30 times more concentrated inside than outside and sodium ions about 10 times more concentrated outside than inside, yet the inside is at −70 mV relative to the outside. Which explanation of the negative resting potential is consistent with these measurements?
Mission check

?A neurone is treated with a chemical that stops it producing ATP. Which prediction about its resting potential is correct?

Leaks never stop, so the pump never can: that is the energy cost of being ready.

Mission 4

From stimulus to response

  • A stimulus is a change in the environment, inside or outside the body.
  • Receptor detects it → sensory neurone → CNS → motor neurone → effector responds.
  • CNS (central nervous system) = brain + spinal cord. It coordinates: it links input to output.
  • Effectors: muscles contract; glands secrete.
Mission 4

The reflex arc

  • A reflex: a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus, with no conscious decision.
  • Frame 1 — Receptor detects the stimulus; sensory neurone carries impulses into the spinal cord.
  • Frame 2 — In the grey matter of the spinal cord, one relay neurone (interneuron) passes impulses on, across synapses.
  • Frame 3 — Motor neurone carries impulses out to the effector: a skeletal muscle, which contracts.
  • Three neurones, two synapses: a three-neurone simple reflex.
Quick check
?A person touches a hot pan and withdraws their hand before feeling any pain. Which sequence describes the reflex arc that produced the withdrawal?
Mission 4

Fast, automatic, protective

  • Fast: a short pathway with only two synapses.
  • Involuntary: the response is produced in the spinal cord. The brain does not decide.
  • Protective: the body part is removed before much damage is done.
  • The brain still finds out: other neurones carry impulses up the cord. Pain is felt after the movement.
  • Say: "the reflex is coordinated in the spinal cord" — not: "the brain decides very quickly".
Quick check
?Which statement about the spinal cord and unconscious processes is correct?
Mission check

?Months after an injury that cut through the spinal cord in the neck, a doctor pricks the sole of a patient's foot. The leg pulls the foot away, but the patient says they felt nothing. Which explanation is correct?

The movement and the feeling travel separate routes. The spinal cord moves you first; the brain finds out later.

Mission 5

The autonomic nervous system

  • Autonomic = involuntary. Motor neurones to cardiac muscle, smooth muscle and glands.
  • Two branches, usually acting on the same organs with opposite effects (antagonistic):
Sympathetic ("fight or flight")Parasympathetic ("rest and digest")
Heart rateincreasesdecreases
Pupildilatesconstricts
Gut activitydecreasesincreases
  • The balance between the two sets the level of activity.
  • Centres in the medulla and hypothalamus control both branches.
Mission 5

Worked example: heart rate

  • The SAN starts every beat by itself: heart muscle is myogenic.
  • Nerves only change the rate. A centre in the medulla oblongata controls them.
  • Sympathetic neurones → SAN fires faster → heart rate up.
  • Parasympathetic neurones (in the vagus nerve) → SAN fires slower → heart rate down.
  • Chemoreceptors (aorta, carotid arteries, medulla): CO2 up, pH down in exercise → medulla → more sympathetic → rate up.
  • Pressure receptors (aorta, carotid arteries): blood pressure high → medulla → more parasympathetic → rate down. Pressure low → more sympathetic → rate up.
  • Say: "the medulla changes the rate" — not: "the medulla makes the heart beat".
Quick check
?In animal experiments, cutting both vagus nerves makes the resting heart rate rise. What does this show?
Quick check
?Atropine is a drug that blocks the effects of parasympathetic neurones on their effectors. What does it do to heart rate and pupil size?
Mission check

?A patient takes a drug that blocks the effect of sympathetic neurones on the heart. What happens to their heart rate when they exercise?

The heart beats on its own; the autonomic system only turns the rate up or down.

Mission 6

Receptors are transducers

  • A receptor responds to one kind of stimulus only: it is specific.
  • It is a transducer: it converts stimulus energy (pressure, light, heat) into an electrical change in a membrane.
  • That change is the generator potential. It is graded: a bigger stimulus gives a bigger generator potential.
  • If the change reaches threshold, action potentials start in a sensory neurone.
  • Below threshold: no impulses at all.
  • Say: "the receptor converts the stimulus into an electrical signal" — not: "the receptor sends the stimulus to the brain".
Mission 6

The Pacinian corpuscle

  • A pressure receptor deep in the skin.
  • Centre: the ending of one sensory neurone.
  • Around it: many lamellae (layers of connective tissue) with gel between them.
  • Its membrane has stretch-mediated sodium ion channels.
  • Pressure deforms the lamellae → membrane stretches → channels open → Na⁺ diffuses in → depolarisation = generator potential.
  • At threshold → action potential in the sensory neurone.

Mission 6

Rods and cones

  • Both are photoreceptors in the retina.
  • Rods: pigment rhodopsin; broken down by dim light. Very sensitive. One pigment, so no colour.
  • Cones: pigment iodopsin, in three types of cone (red-, green-, blue-sensitive). Need bright light. Give colour vision.
  • Fovea: cones only. Rods: more towards the edge of the retina.
Mission 6

Wiring: sensitivity versus acuity

  • Many rods share one bipolar neurone (convergence).
  • Small (below-threshold) responses from several rods summate → threshold reached in dim light → high sensitivity.
  • But the brain cannot tell which rod fired → low visual acuity.
  • Each cone has its own bipolar neurone → separate impulses from neighbouring points → high visual acuity.
  • Cones need more light: no summation → low sensitivity.
  • These bipolar neurones connect to neurones of the optic nerve.
Quick check
?Why does the fovea give the sharpest image in bright light?
Mission check

?A local anaesthetic blocks the voltage-gated sodium channels in the axon of the sensory neurone leading from a Pacinian corpuscle. It does not affect the stretch-mediated channels in the nerve ending. Firm pressure is applied. What is recorded?

Two jobs, two kinds of channel: the receptor transduces; the axon fires.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Plant hormones are made in growing regions and move to other tissues; auxin (IAA) promotes cell elongation, and unequal elongation bends an organ.
  • Phototropism: auxin moves to the shaded side; shaded cells elongate more; the shoot bends towards light.
  • The same IAA concentration that stimulates shoot cells inhibits root cells: shoots grow up, roots grow down.

Mission 2

  • Cardiac muscle is myogenic: the SAN starts each beat; the AVN delays the wave; the bundle of His and Purkyne tissue carry it to the apex.
  • Baroreceptors and chemoreceptors → medulla → sympathetic (faster) or vagus (slower) nerves to the SAN.

Mission 3

  • A neurone: cell body, many short dendrites, one long axon; myelin insulates the axon except at nodes of Ranvier.
  • Myelin is made by Schwann cells.
  • At rest the inside is about −70 mV: the membrane is polarised.
  • The Na⁺/K⁺ pump (3 Na⁺ out, 2 K⁺ in, ATP) builds gradients; K⁺ leaks out faster than Na⁺ leaks in, so the inside stays negative.

Mission 4

  • Receptor → sensory neurone → relay neurone in the spinal cord → motor neurone → effector: a fast, involuntary, protective reflex, with no decision by the brain.

Mission 5

  • The autonomic system is involuntary: sympathetic and parasympathetic branches act antagonistically on the same organs, e.g. heart rate via the SAN.

Mission 6

  • Receptors are specific transducers: a stimulus opens (or closes) ion channels, changing the membrane potential; impulses start only at threshold.
  • Pacinian corpuscle: pressure deforms lamellae, stretch-mediated Na⁺ channels open, graded generator potential.
  • Rods converge (sensitive, low acuity, no colour); cones each have their own bipolar neurone (high acuity, colour).
