How Vivaldi Makes a Violin Shiver

3 missions and a finale. Shivering strings, rain on a roof and a storm of winds. Listen, guess, learn, then check.

Mission 1

Mission 1: How do you make a violin shiver?

Make a guess: How do you make a violin shiver?

Mission 1

Who is playing

  • Winter is a solo concerto: one solo violin against a small string orchestra.
  • The orchestra: first violins, second violins, violas, cellos.
  • Under them, the continuo: a cello on the bass line, plus a keyboard (harpsichord or organ) filling in the chords.
  • When everyone plays together it is called tutti.
Mission 1

The frozen opening

  • Bar 1: the bass (cellos and keyboard) repeats one note, quaver after quaver: a pulsing repeated-note bass. A short pattern repeated over and over like this is often called an ostinato.
  • Bar 2 the violas join on a different note; bar 3 the second violins; bar 4 the first violins and the soloist.
  • The notes pile up into a chord that clashes: a dissonance. The sonnet line printed here says "Aggiacciato tremar trà nevi algenti": to shiver, frozen, amid icy snows.
Quick check
?The bass instruments repeat the same short pattern, bar after bar, while the parts above them change. What is this device called?
Mission 1

Trill or tremolo?

  • A trill (written tr) is a fast wobble between a note and the one above it. From bar 4 the solo violin plays repeated notes with a trill on each one.
  • Tremolo is the same note repeated very fast.
  • At bar 47 the soloist plays rapid repeated chords (two or three strings at once), marked Piano. The caption: "batter i denti", teeth chattering.
  • Listening exam tip: a trill moves between two pitches; tremolo stays on one.
Quick check
?You hear a violin note shaking rapidly between two neighbouring pitches. What is the device?
Mission 1

Wind, stamping feet, and chattering teeth

  • Bar 12 (letter B): the soloist enters with very fast scales and broken chords. Caption: "orrido Vento", horrid wind.
  • Bar 22 (letter C): leaping tutti figures. Caption: "Correr battendo i piedi", running, stamping the feet.
  • Bar 33: the orchestra bursts in with rapid repeated notes, marked "Venti" (winds). Bar 47: soloist's chattering teeth, Piano. Bar 56: tutti, Forte.
  • Dynamics switch suddenly between piano and forte: no crescendo is marked.
Mission check

?How do you make a violin shiver?

Mission 2

Mission 2: How do we know the slow movement is about rain?

Make a guess: How do we know the slow movement is about rain?

Mission 2

Programme music

  • Music that depicts a story or a scene is programme music. Music that depicts nothing outside itself is absolute music.
  • The 1725 printed edition gives each of the four Seasons a sonnet. Cue letters (A, B, C ...) in the parts key each line to a passage.
  • Nobody knows for certain who wrote the sonnets. Vivaldi himself may have.
  • So we know what the rain is because the composer's edition tells us, not because rain has a sound of its own.
Mission 2

Rain on the roof

  • Largo. The violins are marked "Pizziccati forte": pizzicato, plucked with the finger, not bowed.
  • The caption over them: "La Pioggia", the rain. Short dry plucks, steady semiquavers.
  • The viola is marked "con l'arco" (arco, with the bow) and holds long quiet notes.
  • Exam trap: staccato is short bowed notes. Pizzicato is plucked.
Quick check
?In the Largo the violins pluck the strings with a finger instead of bowing. What is the instruction?
Mission 2

Sitting by the fire

  • Over the plucked rain the solo violin plays a long, smooth, singing melody: cantabile.
  • One clear tune on top, chords underneath: the texture is melody-dominated homophony.
  • After F minor, the key is now E flat major: warmer. Bass marked "Sempre Piano".
  • Caption: "Passar al foco i di quieti e contenti", to spend quiet, contented days by the fire.
Quick check
?The Largo has one singing melody on the solo violin, with plucked chords underneath. What is this texture?
Mission 2

Soloist versus the rest

  • In Baroque fast movements the orchestra opens with a passage called the ritornello ("little return").
  • The soloist then plays an episode; the ritornello comes back, often in a new key; and so on.
  • In Winter movement 1 the orchestra's shivering opening returns, for example at bar 39, and the tutti takes over again at bar 56.
  • This alternation, orchestra then soloist, is ritornello form.
Quick check
?In a Baroque concerto, the orchestra's opening passage comes back between the soloist's episodes. What is it called?
Mission check

Mission 3: What makes Winter sound 300 years old?

Make a guess: What makes Winter sound 300 years old?

Mission 3

Baroque fingerprints

  • Continuo: keyboard plus cello on the bass line, with figures telling the player which chords to fill in.
  • Terraced dynamics: sudden steps between Piano and Forte, no crescendo.
  • Ornamentation (trills), ritornello form, a string orchestra, and steady driving semiquavers (motor rhythm).
  • Major and minor keys: F minor for the cold, E flat major for the fire.
Quick check
?At bar 47 the music is marked Piano; at bar 56 it switches straight to Forte. What is this feature called?
Mission 3

Walking on ice

  • Finale, 3/8, back in F minor. Bar 1 (letter F): the soloist alone, over a long held bass note. Caption: "Caminar Sopra'l giaccio", walking on the ice.
  • Bar 40 (letter H): "Sdruzziolar, cader à terra", to slip and fall: a tumbling downward run. Bar 89 (letter L): the ice "Si rompe", breaks: the line splinters into fragments and rests.
  • Bar 101 (letter M), marked Lento: a slow, smooth passage for the Sirocco wind, with the bass silent.
  • Bar 120 (letter N): "Sirocco, Borea, e tutti i Venti in guerra", all the winds at war: the soloist's fast scales, then from bar 124 ("Venti") bursts of fast repeated notes in every part, to the end.
Mission 3

After Vivaldi

  • Vivaldi (1678–1741). Bach (1685–1750): his Brandenburg Concerto No. 5, 3rd movement, is the Edexcel GCSE Baroque set work.
  • Mozart (1756–1791): the Classical concerto. No harpsichord continuo; a larger orchestra; a cadenza for the soloist.
  • Classical melodies sit in balanced phrases, often question-and-answer, with simpler, lighter accompaniment than the Baroque continuo.
Quick check
?Which feature tells you a concerto is Classical (Mozart) rather than Baroque (Vivaldi)?
Mission check

?What makes Winter sound 300 years old?

Continuo, terraced dynamics, ornaments and a returning ritornello.

Finale

Can music mean something without words?

  • Take the sonnet away and the Largo is a tune over plucked chords. Rain? Dripping? Clocks?
  • Vivaldi's generation answered by printing the words with the notes.
  • The question is still open: how much meaning is in the music, and how much do we bring to it?
  • The sonnet's last line: "Quest'è 'l verno, mà tal, che gioja apporte", this is winter, but one that brings joy.
Quick check
?What is "programme music"?
Recap

The big ideas

  • A solo concerto sets one soloist against a string orchestra with continuo.
  • Shivering: repeated notes in the bass, trills, and a dissonant pile-up of entries.
  • Trill moves between two pitches; tremolo repeats one. Pizzicato is plucked; staccato is short but bowed.
  • Programme music: the 1725 sonnets, keyed by letter to the score, tell us what each passage depicts.
  • Ritornello form: the orchestra's passage returns between the soloist's episodes.
  • Baroque fingerprints: continuo, terraced dynamics, ornaments, ritornello. The Classical concerto drops the continuo and adds a cadenza.
