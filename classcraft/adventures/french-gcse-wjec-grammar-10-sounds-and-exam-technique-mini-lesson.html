Mission 1

One word, a whole family

  • Many French words are a base word + an add-on.
  • porter (to carry) → portable (carryable: a laptop, a mobile) → porteur (carrier).
  • préparer (to prepare) → préparation (preparation).
  • possible → impossible.
  • Three steps for a word you don't know:
  • 1: Cover the add-on. 2: Find a word you know. 3: Add the meaning of the add-on.
Mission 1

Prefixes: add-ons at the start

  • in- / im- = un-, in-, "the opposite of". im- before b, m, p.

Mission 1

Suffixes: add-ons at the end

  • A suffix often changes the type of word.
  • Verb stem + -able = adjective, English -able / -ible:
  • utiliser (to use) → utilisable – usable · changer → changeable – changeable.
  • Verb stem + -ion / -ation = noun, English -ion / -ation:
  • préparer → une préparation · réserver → une réservation – a booking · inviter → une invitation.
  • Nouns in -tion are feminine: la réservation.
  • Your turn: what does Cette carte est utilisable dans le bus. mean?
    Show answer

    This card is usable (can be used) on the bus.

Mission 1

People, ways and order

  • Verb stem + -eur / -ateur = the person who does it:
  • jouer → un joueur – a player · vendre → un vendeur – a seller, a shop assistant · organiser → un organisateur – an organiser.
  • Feminine: -euse / -atrice: une vendeuse, une organisatrice.
  • Adjective + -ment = adverb, English -ly: heureuse → heureusement – fortunately · patient → patiemment – patiently (-ent → -emment).
  • Number + -ième = ordinal: deux → deuxième – second.
  • Your turn: un consommateur? (consommer = to consume)
    Show answer

    a consumer.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Why are "tu" and "tout" different words to a French ear, and how do accents tell you what to say?

Make a guess: Why are "tu" and "tout" different words to a French ear, and how do accents tell you what to say?

Mission 2

Pure vowels, even beat

  • English vowels often glide: "day" is really "d-ay-ee".
  • French vowels are pure: one sound, no glide. été – summer.
  • English stresses one syllable hard: CIN-e-ma.
  • French gives every syllable the same weight, with a slight lift on the last one: ci-né-ma.
  • im-por-tant – important · res-tau-rant – restaurant
  • Watch out: don't swallow syllables. restaurant has three clear beats in French.
  • Your turn: tap out chocolat. How many beats, and which is strongest?
    Show answer

    Three: cho-co-lat. The last.

  • tu – you · tout – all

    dur: it has the u sound. jour has the ou sound (tongue back).

Mission 2

Closed é, open è

  • Two "e" sounds with accents. Think of how wide your mouth opens.
GroupSpellingsSoundExamples
closedé, -er, -ezlike "ay" in "day", no glideété, parler, avez, chez
openè, ê, ailike "e" in "bed"très, être, vrai, faire
  • -er and -ez at the end of a verb: the r and z are silent.
  • So mangé, manger, mangez all sound the same.
  • Watch out: parler does not rhyme with "air". The ending is the closed é sound.
  • Watch out: in some short words the r is said: mer, hiver, cher (open sound + r).
  • Your turn: does avez sound like avé or like avair?
    Show answer

    like avé: closed sound, silent z.

Mission 2

o, eu and oi

  • Closed o (like "go", no glide): au, eau, ô (nearly always), and o at the end of a word.
    • autre, beau, tôt, stylo
  • Open o (like "hot"): a plain o with a consonant sounded after it.
    • porte, notre, alors
  • Closed eu at the end of a syllable: say é with rounded lips.
    • deux, peu
  • Open eu / œu before a sounded consonant.
    • leur, sœur, cœur, jeune
  • oi = "wa": moi, mois, voir. oy = "wa" + "y": moyen, envoyer.
  • Rule of thumb for plain o and eu: vowel at the end of the syllable → closed; consonant sounded after it → open.
  • Your turn: nos (our) or notre (our): which has the open o?
    Show answer

    notre: the t is sounded after the o.

Mission 2

The silent final e

  • An e with no accent at the end of a longer word is not said. (Short words like je, le, de keep their "uh".)
    • elle, jeune, chaque, timide
  • But it wakes up the consonant before it.
    • petit – the t is silent · petite – the t is heard
    • vert · verte (green, m · f)
  • So you can hear many feminine adjectives.
  • Watch out: don't add "uh" or "ee" at the end: timide has two beats, ti-mide.
  • Your turn: how many beats in chaque?
    Show answer

    One: chaque.

Mission 3

What a nasal vowel is

  • A nasal vowel: air goes out through your mouth and nose together.
  • The n or m is not said. It is a signal: "make this vowel nasal".
  • bon – good: one sound, no "n" at the end.
  • When? Vowel + n or m at the end of a word, or before another consonant.
    • grand (n + d) · temps (m + p) · fin (end of word)
  • French has three main nasal sounds (plus un).
  • Watch out: don't add "n" or "ng". bon is not "bonn" or "bong".
  • Your turn: pinch your nose and say bon. It should feel blocked.
    Show answer

    If the sound stops, the air was going through your nose: it's nasal.

Mission 3

an, en, am, em

  • One sound, four spellings. Mouth open, like "a" in "father", plus nose.
SpellingExamples

  • Spelling tip: before b or p, write m, not n: jambe, temps, chambre.
  • Watch out: -ent at the end of an ils/elles verb is silent: ils parlent, elles mangent.
  • But -ent in other words is nasal: souvent, comment.
  • Your turn: ils entendent (they hear). How many nasal sounds?
    Show answer

    Two: en-ten-. The final -ent is a silent verb ending.

Mission 3

on, om

  • Lips rounded, like "o" in "go", plus nose.
    • mon, non, nom, combien, tomber
  • on and an are different words:
    • son – his, her · sans – without
    • on – we · en – in
  • Watch out: round your lips for on. Open lips give an, and son becomes sans.
  • Your turn: which word has the on sound: temps or tomber?
    Show answer

    tomber. (temps has the an sound.)

Mission 3

in, ain, aim; un; -ien

  • Mouth fairly spread, like "a" in "bag", plus nose.
SpellingExamples
in, imfin, important
ain, aimpain, faim
einplein
  • So pain – bread, fin – end, faim – hunger all end in the same sound.
  • un (un, lundi): the same vowel with rounded lips. Many speakers in France now say it like in. Both are fine.
  • -ien = "y" + the in sound: bien, rien, chien, canadien.
  • Watch out: -ien is not English "-ian". canadien ends in a nasal, with no "n".
  • Your turn: which two rhyme: bien, sans, rien?
    Show answer

    bien and rien.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Why is the h in "hôpital" silent, and which other French spellings trip up English speakers?

Make a guess: Why is the h in "hôpital" silent, and which other French spellings trip up English speakers?

Mission 4

ch, j and soft g

  • ch = "sh": chose, cher, chercher, marché.
  • j = the "s" in "pleasure" (we'll write it "zh"): je, jouer, jour.
  • g before e, i, y = the same sound as j: manger, collège.
  • g before a, o, u = hard "g": gare, gauche.
  • Watch out: ch is not "tch". cher starts like "share".
  • Watch out: a few words from Greek have ch = "k": technologie.
  • Spelling tip: to keep g soft before a or o, add e: nous mangeons.
  • Your turn: hard or soft g in gauche? In collège?
    Show answer

    gauche: hard (g + a). collège: soft (g + e).

Mission 4

c and ç

  • c before e, i, y = "s": cette, ici, cinéma.
  • c before a, o, u = "k": café, comment.
  • ç (c cédille) keeps the "s" sound before a, o, u:
    • ça, garçon, français, reçu, façon
  • Same trick as mangeons: nous commençons.
  • Watch out: never put ç before e or i. The c is already soft: cinéma, not çinéma.
  • Your turn: which needs a cedilla: garcon or ici?
    Show answer

    garçon (c + o).

Mission 4

qu, gn, th

  • qu = "k", with no "w": qui, quand, question, banque.
  • gn = "ny", like the middle of "onion": gagner, campagne, ligne.
  • th = plain "t": thé, théâtre, bibliothèque.
  • Watch out: question starts with "k", not "kw".
  • Watch out: thé has no English "th" sound.
  • Your turn: how do you say quand?
    Show answer

    "k" + the an nasal; the d is silent.

Quick check

Mission 4

h is always silent

  • French never says the "h" sound.
    • homme, hôpital, heure, hôtel, habiter
  • hôpital sounds "o-pi-tal".
  • Most of these words behave as if they start with a vowel: l'homme, l'hôpital. More in Mission 5.
  • Watch out: an English "h" is one of the most common errors in reading aloud.
  • Your turn: say J'habite à l'hôtel. Where are the h sounds?
    Show answer

    None. Both h's are silent.

Mission 4

French r and the -s- rule

  • r is made at the back of the throat: tongue tip rests behind your bottom teeth; the back of the tongue nearly touches the roof of your mouth. A soft gargle.
    • rue, rien, rester, comprendre
  • Don't roll it with the tip of your tongue, and don't use an English "r".
  • s between two vowels = "z": maison, cuisine, chose, choisir.
  • ss = "s": poisson, aussi.
  • s at the start of a word = "s": salut.
  • Watch out: poisson (fish) has "s"; with a "z" it sounds like poison.
  • Your turn: "s" or "z" in cuisine?
    Show answer

    "z": one s between two vowels.

Mission 5

Mission 5: Why are so many final letters silent in French, and when do they suddenly come back to life?

Make a guess: Why are so many final letters silent in French, and when do they suddenly come back to life?

Mission 5

Silent final consonants

  • Most final consonants are not said: d, p, s, t, x, z.
    • grand, trop, nous, tout, deux, chez
  • Plural -s is silent: le chat / les chats. You hear the plural in le → les.
  • Verb endings are silent: je parle, tu parles, ils parlent sound the same.
  • But think CaReFuL: final c, r, f, l are usually said.
    • avec, sac, bonjour, neuf, il
  • Watch out: -er verbs break CaReFuL: parler, r silent.
  • A few others are said: bus, sept.
  • Your turn: which final letters are heard in trop, avec, neuf, deux?
    Show answer

    The c of avec and the f of neuf.

Mission 5

Must link, never link

  • Always link:
    • little word + noun: les‿enfants, mes‿amis, un‿ami
    • pronoun + verb: nous‿avons, vous‿êtes, on‿a, ils‿ont
    • after très, chez, dans, en: chez‿elle, en‿été
  • Never link:
    • after et: toi et / elle
    • after a singular noun: le chat / est noir
    • before a few h words: en / haut (up)
  • Liaison can change meaning:
    • ils‿ont ("z") – they have · ils sont ("s") – they are
    • nous‿avons – we have · nous savons – we know
  • Your turn: Elles ont faim. "z" or "s"?
    Show answer

    "z": elles‿ont = they have.

Mission 6

Your read aloud task

  • Unit 1 Oracy (30%): the first task is a read aloud and role play.
  • WJEC allocates your card. You have 10 minutes' preparation for the oracy test.
  • No dictionary at any point.
  • The sounds tested are WJEC's sound-spelling list (–).
  • Your teacher has WJEC's exact card format; ask to see a sample.
Mission 6

What the examiner listens for

  • Can a French speaker understand every word?
  • Major error: changes or blocks the meaning.
    • ils sont for ils ont · tout for tu · poison for poisson
  • Minor error: sounds foreign, but the word is clear.
    • a slightly English r · an "air" ending on parler
  • Aim: no major errors, as few minor ones as you can.
  • Watch out: speed does not hide errors. Clear and steady beats fast.
Mission 6

Prepare in four steps

1. Read for meaning. Know what each sentence says: it shapes your rhythm and helps with the questions after.

2. Spot the traps (–):

  • silent endings: -ent, -s, -t, -x
    • links: les‿amis, vous‿avez
    • nasals: an, on, in
    • spellings: ch, qu, gn, -ill-, -tion, h

3. Rehearse.

  • Silently: mouth the hard words.

4. Read at a steady pace, in word groups, with a slight lift on the last syllable of each group.

  • Watch out: don't stop and restart a sentence. Keep going.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Prefix in-/im- = not: inconnu, impossible; also re- (again), dé- (opposite), demi- (half).
  • Suffixes: -able (can be …), -ation (the action/thing), -eur/-ateur (person), -ment (-ly), -ième (order).
  • Cognate swaps: -té = -ty, -eux = -ous, é- = s-, circumflex = hidden s.

Mission 2

  • French vowels are pure; syllables are even, with a light lift on the last.
  • u ≠ ou; closed é / -er / -ez ≠ open è / ê / ai; closed and open o and eu.
  • Accents are spelling: é closed, è open, ê often a lost s, ç soft, ë separate; a/à, ou/où differ in meaning.

Mission 3

  • Vowel + n/m at the end of a word or before a consonant = nasal; the n/m is silent.
  • Three main nasals: an/en/am/em (open) · on/om (rounded) · in/im/ain/aim/ein (spread); plus un; -ien = "y" + in.
  • n/m + vowel, or nn/mm → no nasal: bon/bonne, voisin/voisine; ils parlent: -ent silent.

Mission 4

  • ch = "sh"; j and soft g = "zh"; qu = "k"; gn = "ny"; th = "t".
  • c/ç = "s" before the right vowels; h is always silent; r is made at the back.
  • One s between vowels = "z"; -ill(e) = "y" (not ville, mille, tranquille); -ail = "eye"; -tion = "s-yon".

Mission 5

  • Final consonants are mostly silent; CaReFuL (c, r, f, l) are usually said, but -er is not.
  • Liaison: s/x → "z", t → "t", n → "n" before a vowel or silent h; compulsory after little words and pronouns; never after et. ils‿ont ≠ ils sont.
  • Elision: le, la, je, me, te, se, ne, de, que + vowel → apostrophe; always written.

Mission 6

  • Know your board's task: length, marks and the questions that follow.
  • Prepare in four steps: meaning → traps → rehearse → steady reading.
  • Major errors change the meaning (ils sont / ils ont, tu / tout): hunt those first.
