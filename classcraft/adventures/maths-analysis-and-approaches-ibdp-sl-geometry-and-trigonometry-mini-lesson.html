Geometry · 3-D shapes

Distance and midpoint in 3-D

  • Points in space have three coordinates (x,y,z)(x, y, z).
  • Distance between (x1​,y1​,z1​)(x₁, y₁, z₁) and (x2​,y2​,z2​)(x₂, y₂, z₂):
d=(x1​−x2​)2+(y1​−y2​)2+(z1​−z2​)2​d=√((x₁−x₂)²+(y₁−y₂)²+(z₁−z₂)²)
  • Midpoint: (2x1​+x2​​,2y1​+y2​​,2z1​+z2​​)((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2, (z₁+z₂)/2)
  • In a cuboid a×b×ca × b × c, Pythagoras on the base gives a2+b2​√(a²+b²); Pythagoras again with the height gives the space diagonal a2+b2+c2​√(a²+b²+c²).

Geometry · 3-D shapes

Angles in 3-D

  • The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection on the plane (the "shadow" straight below it).
  • Drop a perpendicular from a point on the line to the plane: this makes a right-angled triangle containing the angle.
  • The angle between two intersecting lines is found in the triangle that both lines lie in.
  • At SL, 3-D angle questions use right-angled trigonometry: sketch the triangle flat, label it, then use SOH CAH TOA.

Geometry · trigonometry

Right-angled trigonometry, elevation and bearings

  • In a right-angled triangle: sinθ=hypopp​sin θ=opp/hyp, cosθ=hypadj​cos θ=adj/hyp, tanθ=adjopp​tan θ=opp/adj. Use sin−1sin⁻¹, cos−1cos⁻¹, tan−1tan⁻¹ to find angles.
  • Angle of elevation: measured up from the horizontal. Angle of depression: measured down from the horizontal.
  • Bearings are measured clockwise from north, written with three figures (e.g. 040°).
  • Always sketch and label a diagram from the words first.

Geometry · trigonometry

Sine rule, cosine rule and area

  • Label the side opposite angle AA as aa, and so on.
  • Sine rule: sinAa​=sinBb​=sinCc​a/(sin A)=b/(sin B)=c/(sin C). Use it with a side and its opposite angle known.
  • Cosine rule: c2=a2+b2−2abcosCc²=a²+b²−2abcos C, or cosC=2aba2+b2−c2​cos C=(a²+b²−c²)/2ab. Use it with two sides and the angle between them, or with all three sides.
  • Area =21​absinC=1/2absin C (CC is the angle between sides aa and bb).
  • Finding an angle with the sine rule can give two possible triangles (the ambiguous case); you meet it in the unit circle mission.

Mission check

?How can you find a mountain's height without climbing it?

Two angles of elevation, measured a known distance apart on level ground, make a triangle with one known side. The sine rule gives the distance to the summit, and a right-angled triangle then gives the height, as in the tower example.

Geometry · circles

Arc length and sector area

  • A sector is a "pizza slice" of a circle; its curved edge is an arc.
  • A sector with angle θ∘θ° is the fraction 360θ​θ/360 of the whole circle, so
arc length=360θ​×2πrsector area=360θ​×πr2arc length=θ/360× 2π r sector area=θ/360× π r²
  • Perimeter of a sector == arc +2r+ 2r (two radii).

Radians

  • One radian is the angle at the centre when the arc length equals the radius.
  • A full turn is 2π2π radians, so π rad=180∘π rad=180°.

  • Learn: 30∘=6π​30°=π/6, 45∘=4π​45°=π/4, 60∘=3π​60°=π/3, 90∘=2π​90°=π/2.
  • With θθ in radians: arc l=rθl=rθ and sector area A=21​r2θA=1/2r²θ. Set your GDC to radian mode.

Trigonometry · unit circle

Sine and cosine on the unit circle

  • The unit circle has centre OO and radius 1. Turn a radius θθ anticlockwise from the positive xx-axis.
  • Its end point is (cosθ,sinθ)(cos θ, sin θ): cosine is the xx-coordinate, sine is the height.
  • tanθ=cosθsinθ​tan θ=(sin θ)/(cos θ), the gradient of the radius; the line is y=xtanθy=xtan θ.
  • This works for any angle, so signs follow the quadrant: in the 2nd quadrant sinθ>0sin θ>0 and cosθ<0cos θ<0.
Trigonometry · unit circle

Angles in different quadrants

  • Reflect in the yy-axis: sin(180∘−θ)=sinθsin (180°−θ)=sin θ, cos(180∘−θ)=−cosθcos (180°−θ)=−cos θ.
  • Reflect in the xx-axis: sin(−θ)=−sinθsin (−θ)=−sin θ, cos(−θ)=cosθcos (−θ)=cos θ.
  • Half a turn: sin(180∘+θ)=−sinθsin (180°+θ)=−sin θ, cos(180∘+θ)=−cosθcos (180°+θ)=−cos θ.
  • In radians, 180∘180° is ππ: for example sin(π−x)=sinxsin (π−x)=sin x.

Mission check

?Why is sin150∘sin 150° exactly equal to sin30∘sin 30°?

Sine is the height of the point on the unit circle. The radii at 30∘30° and 150∘150° are reflections of each other in the yy-axis, so they end at the same height: sin(180∘−θ)=sinθsin (180°−θ)=sin θ. That same fact gives the second triangle in the ambiguous case.

Trigonometry · graphs and equations

The graphs of sine, cosine and tangent

  • y=sinxy=sin x and y=cosxy=cos x repeat every 2π2π (360°): period 2π2π, amplitude 1, range −1≤y≤1−1≤ y≤ 1.
  • y=cosxy=cos x is y=sinxy=sin x translated 2π​π/2 to the left.
  • y=tanxy=tan x has period ππ, no amplitude, and vertical asymptotes at x=2π​,23π​,…x=π/2, 3π/2, …
  • Radians are assumed unless degrees are stated.

Trigonometry · graphs and equations

Solving trig equations in an interval

  • Rearrange to sin(…)=ksin (…)=k, cos(…)=kcos (…)=k or tan(…)=ktan (…)=k. Find the first value with the inverse function.
  • Use the graph or unit circle for the others: sinsin repeats as π−απ−α, coscos as 2π−α2π−α, tantan as α+πα+π, then add periods.
  • For sin(2x)sin (2x), change the interval first: 0≤x≤2π0≤ x≤ 2π means 0≤2x≤4π0≤ 2x≤ 4π.
  • A quadratic in sinxsin x or cosxcos x: use sin2x+cos2x=1sin²x+cos²x=1 if needed, factorise, then reject values outside [−1,1][−1, 1].
  • Graphically: count every intersection of the curve with the line in the interval.

Recap

Big ideas

  • 3-D distance: (x1​−x2​)2+(y1​−y2​)2+(z1​−z2​)2​√((x₁−x₂)²+(y₁−y₂)²+(z₁−z₂)²); a cuboid's space diagonal is a2+b2+c2​√(a²+b²+c²).
  • The angle between a line and a plane is the angle with its projection: find the right-angled triangle and use trigonometry.
  • Combined solids: add volumes; count only the outside surfaces.
  • Right-angled triangles: SOH CAH TOA. Elevation is up from the horizontal; depression is down; bearings are clockwise from north.
  • Sine rule needs a side with its opposite angle; cosine rule needs two sides and the included angle, or three sides. Area =21​absinC=1/2absin C.
  • Break a real problem into triangles and sketch a labelled diagram first.
  • Arc =360θ​×2πr=θ/360× 2π r and sector =360θ​×πr2=θ/360×π r²; perimeter adds two radii.
  • In radians: π=180∘π=180°, arc l=rθl=rθ, sector A=21​r2θA=1/2r²θ.
  • Segment == sector −− triangle, with the triangle 21​r2sinθ1/2r²sin θ.
  • On the unit circle, the point at angle θθ is (cosθ,sinθ)(cos θ, sin θ) and tanθ=cosθsinθ​tan θ=(sin θ)/(cos θ); the quadrant fixes the signs.
  • sin(180∘−θ)=sinθsin (180°−θ)=sin θ, so the sine rule can give two angles: test both.
  • cos2θ+sin2θ=1cos²θ+sin²θ=1, sin2θ=2sinθcosθsin 2θ=2sin θcos θ, cos2θ=2cos2θ−1cos 2θ=2cos²θ−1; learn the exact values.
  • y=asin(b(x+c))+dy=asin (b(x+c))+d: amplitude ∣a∣|a|, period b2π​2π/b, translation cc left, principal axis y=dy=d.
  • Solve in an interval: first value, then the symmetry partner, then add periods; widen the interval for sin(bx)sin (bx).
  • Quadratic trig equations: factorise, then reject values outside [−1,1][−1, 1].
