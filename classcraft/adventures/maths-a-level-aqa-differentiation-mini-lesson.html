Differentiation · first principles

Chords close in on the tangent

  • The gradient of a curve at a point is the gradient of the tangent there.
  • A chord joins P(x,f(x))P(x, f(x)) to a nearby point Q(x+h,f(x+h))Q(x+h, f(x+h)). Its gradient is
hf(x+h)−f(x)​(f(x+h) − f(x))/h
  • As h→0h → 0, QQ slides towards PP and the chord turns into the tangent. The tangent's gradient is the limit of the chord gradients.
  • We write it dxdy​dy/dx or f′(x)f'(x): the rate of change of yy with respect to xx.

The power rule

  • For any rational nn:
dxd​(axn)=anxn−1d/dx(axⁿ) = anxⁿ⁻¹

Differentiation · concavity and inflection

Sketching the gradient function

  • To sketch y=f′(x)y = f'(x) from y=f(x)y = f(x), read off the gradient of ff as you move left to right.
  • Where ff has a stationary point, f′f' crosses or touches the xx-axis.
  • Where ff is increasing, f′f' is above the axis; where ff is decreasing, f′f' is below it.
  • Where ff is steepest, f′f' has a turning point.
  • On a displacement–time graph, the gradient function is the velocity.

Differentiation · concavity and inflection

Convex and concave (A-level only)

  • f′′(x)f''(x) is the rate of change of the gradient.
  • If f′′(x)>0f''(x) > 0 on an interval, the gradient is increasing: the curve is convex there (it bends upwards, like a bowl).
  • If f′′(x)<0f''(x) < 0 on an interval, the gradient is decreasing: the curve is concave there (it bends downwards, like a cap).
  • A curve can rise and be concave at the same time: it is going up, but less and less steeply.
Differentiation · concavity and inflection

Points of inflection (A-level only)

  • A point of inflection is where the curve changes between concave and convex: f′′(x)=0f''(x) = 0 and f′′(x)f''(x) changes sign.
  • f′′(x)=0f''(x) = 0 alone is not enough: y=x4y = x⁴ has f′′(0)=0f''(0) = 0, but f′′(x)=12x2≥0f''(x) = 12x² ≥ 0 on both sides, so (0,0)(0, 0) is a minimum.
  • If f′(x)=0f'(x) = 0 there as well, it is a stationary point of inflection (like y=x3y = x³ at the origin). If f′(x)=0f'(x) ≠ 0, it is a non-stationary point of inflection.

Mission check

?A balloon's radius grows 1 cm/s. Why does its volume grow ever faster?

By the chain rule, dtdV​=drdV​×dtdr​=4πr2×1dV/dt = dV/dr × dr/dt = 4π r² × 1. At r=1r = 1 cm that is about 12.6 cm³/s, but at r=10r = 10 cm it is about 1,257 cm³/s. The radius grows steadily, but each new centimetre wraps a much larger shell around the balloon.
