Si-clauses & reported speech

Two high-value IB Spanish B structures that examiners love: the conditional "si" sentence ("if…, then…") in its three types, and reported speech — turning what someone said into an indirect account, with its trademark tense backshift. In IB Diploma Spanish B, this grammar powers your work across the five prescribed themes — Identities, Experiences, Human ingenuity, Social organization and Sharing the planet — especially when you report on Social organization and Human ingenuity.

Si + tense → matching result tensereported speech: present → imperfect · future → conditional

Conditional sentences · Type 1

The real condition

Type 1 is about a real, likely condition. After si you use the present indicative, and the result clause takes the present, future or imperative:

Si + present indicative → present / future / imperativeSi tengo tiempo, iré — "If I have time, I'll go"
  • Si llueve, me quedo en casa. — "If it rains, I stay at home."
  • Si estudias, aprobarás. — "If you study, you'll pass."

Golden rule: after si you never use the present subjunctive and never the conditional.

Quick check

A real condition

?Complete (Type 1): Si ___ tiempo, iré. ("If I have time, I'll go.") — from tener, yo.
Conditional sentences · Type 2

The hypothetical condition

Type 2 is hypothetical / unlikely ("if I were to…"). After si you use the imperfect subjunctive, and the result clause takes the conditional:

Si + imperfect subjunctive → conditionalSi tuviera tiempo, iría — "If I had time, I would go"
  • Si fuera rico, viajaría. — "If I were rich, I would travel."
  • Si estudiaras más, aprobarías. — "If you studied more, you would pass."

Contrast: Si tengo tiempo, iré (real, likely) vs Si tuviera tiempo, iría (hypothetical). The imperfect subjunctive signals "this probably won't happen".

Conditional sentences · Type 3

The impossible past

Type 3 looks back at what could not now change ("if I had…, I would have…"). After si you use the pluperfect subjunctive, and the result takes the conditional perfect:

Si + pluperfect subjunctive → conditional perfectSi hubiera tenido tiempo, habría ido — "If I had had time, I would have gone"
  • Si hubiera sabido, habría venido. — "If I had known, I would have come."
  • Si hubieras estudiado, habrías aprobado. — "If you had studied, you would have passed."

Reported speech · the backshift

Turning direct speech indirect

When you report what someone said after a past reporting verb (dijo que…), the tense shifts back a step:

direct (present) →reported (past)
present → imperfect (estoy → estaba)
preterite / perfect → pluperfect (fui → había ido)
future → conditional (iré → iría)

In use: "Estoy cansado" → Dijo que estaba cansado. — "He said he was tired."

Reported speech · time & place

The words that shift too

When you backshift, time and place words move with the tenses:

  • hoy → aquel día ("today" → "that day")
  • mañana → al día siguiente ("tomorrow" → "the next day")
  • aquí → allí ("here" → "there")

Full example: "Vendré mañana" → Dijo que vendría al día siguiente. — future shifts to conditional, mañana shifts to al día siguiente.

The three types at a glance

Pair the tenses correctly

  • Type 1 (real): Si llueve, me quedo / me quedaré.
  • Type 2 (hypothetical): Si lloviera, me quedaría.
  • Type 3 (impossible past): Si hubiera llovido, me habría quedado.

Never after si: the present subjunctive or the plain conditional. Match the pair and you're safe.

Recap

Your si-clause & reported-speech toolkit

Type 1 (real): Si + present indicative → present / future / imperative (Si tengo tiempo, iré).

Type 2 (hypothetical): Si + imperfect subjunctive → conditional (Si tuviera tiempo, iría).

Type 3 (impossible past): Si + pluperfect subjunctive → conditional perfect (Si hubiera tenido tiempo, habría ido).

Reported backshift: present → imperfect; preterite/perfect → pluperfect; future → conditional (Dijo que estaba cansado).

Time/place: hoy → aquel día, mañana → al día siguiente, aquí → allí.
