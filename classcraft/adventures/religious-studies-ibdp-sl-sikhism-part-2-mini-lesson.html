Mission 1

The journey of the soul

  • Samsara: the cycle of birth, death and rebirth (Sikh phrase: aavan jaan, coming and going).
  • The soul passes through many life forms: traditionally 8.4 million (chaurasi lakh joon).
  • Human birth is rare and precious: the one chance to meet God.
  • You have been given this human body; this is your turn to meet the Lord of the universe (Guru Granth Sahib 12, paraphrase).
Mission 1

Karma

  • Karma = action and its consequences, carried from life to life.
  • By karma comes the body, this birth (Japji pauri 4, paraphrase).
  • Deeds, good and bad, are "read out" before Dharam, the righteous judge (Japji closing verse, paraphrase).
  • Karma is not fate: present choices matter, and a person can turn from manmukh to gurmukh.
  • Karma works within hukam, God's order.
Mission 1

Grace beyond karma

  • By karma comes the body; by grace (nadar), the gate of liberation (Japji pauri 4, paraphrase).
  • Karma explains where a soul is; it cannot by itself set the soul free.
  • Liberation is God's gift: Gurprasad.
  • So good deeds are not a payment for liberation; they are the life of someone open to grace.

Mission 1

Mukti: liberation

  • Mukti = liberation from the cycle of rebirth.
  • For Sikhs it is union with God: the soul's light merging with the divine light, or dwelling in Sach Khand.
  • Jivan mukti: liberation while still alive: the jivan mukt lives in the world, free of haumai, absorbed in the Name.
  • Heaven and hell are not the goal: many Sikhs see them as states of separation or nearness to God.

Karma explains where the soul is; grace sets it free, and Sikhs believe that freedom can begin in this life.

Mission 2

Nam: the Name

  • Nam (EDQ: Naam) = the Name of God; more than a word: God's presence and revealed nature in all creation.
  • All creatures, worlds and realms are upheld by the Name (Sukhmani, Guru Granth Sahib 284, paraphrase).
  • To remember the Name is to become aware of God everywhere.
  • Forgetting the Name is the root of the human problem.
Mission 2

The names of God

  • Waheguru (Wonderful Lord): the Gurmantar.
  • Satnam (True Name), Akal Purakh (Timeless Being), Nirankar (Formless), Kartar (Creator).
  • The Gurus also use Hari, Ram, Gobind, Allah, Khuda: names from Hindu and Muslim use, for the one God of all.
  • Guru Arjan: the tongue speaks names drawn from God's deeds; Satnam is God's primal name (Guru Granth Sahib 1083, paraphrase).
  • Philosophical point: every name is a window on God, but no name contains God.
Mission 2

Naam Japo and the 5 Shabads

  • Naam Japo: remembering God by repeating and focusing the mind on the Name: simran, kirtan, Nitnem.
  • The 5 Shabads: scripture speaks of five "sounds" (panch shabad) of a heavenly, unstruck (anhad) music heard within when the mind is absorbed in God.
  • Sikhs read this as God as Word: the divine is experienced as sound and Word, not as an image.
  • IB link: this is Sikh religious experience: inward, disciplined, often musical.
Mission 2

Hukam: God's will and order

  • Hukam = command, order, will.
  • Everything comes to be by hukam; no one is outside it (Japji pauri 2, paraphrase).
  • The way to God is to walk in hukam (Japji pauri 1, paraphrase).
  • Understanding hukam ends haumai: you stop seeing yourself as the centre.
  • Accepting God's will (bhana) is not fatalism: Sikhs still work, choose and resist injustice.
Mission 2

Nadar: grace

  • Nadar = God's gracious glance; also called kirpa and, in the Mool Mantar, Gurprasad.
  • By karma comes the body (this birth); by grace comes the gate of liberation (Japji pauri 4, paraphrase).
  • Grace is a gift: it cannot be earned or bought by ritual.
  • But Sikhs are not passive: they meditate, serve and live honestly, and pray for grace.

Mission 2

The five khands

Japji pauris 34–37 (Guru Granth Sahib 7–8, paraphrase): five realms of the soul's progress.

  • Dharam Khand: realm of duty: the earth as a place to act rightly; every deed is judged.
  • Gian Khand: realm of knowledge: awe at the vastness of creation.
  • Saram Khand: realm of spiritual endeavour (or beauty): the mind and understanding are reshaped.
  • Karam Khand: realm of grace: power, filled with God.
  • Sach Khand: realm of truth, where the Formless One dwells.

Mission 3

Nam and simran

  • Nam ("Name"): not just a word for God, but God's presence and nature running through all creation.
  • Simran: remembrance: keeping God constantly in mind.
  • Nam simran: remembering God through the Name; nam japna is its active form: repeating the Name.
  • Aim: to turn the self from haumai (ego) to God, moving from manmukh (self-centred) to gurmukh (God-centred).
  • Nam is also a controlling force: the divine order (hukam) that sustains everything (see Mission 2).
Mission 3

Mantras

  • Gurmantar: Waheguru, given to the initiate at the amrit ceremony; repeated silently or aloud.
  • Mool Mantar ("root mantra"): the opening of the Guru Granth Sahib, from Ik Onkar to Gur Prasad: God is one, Truth is the Name, Creator, without fear or hate, timeless, unborn, self-existent, known by the Guru's grace (paraphrase).
  • Mantra here means a sacred formula that focuses the mind; it is not a spell that controls God.
  • Some Sikhs use a mala (rosary) to keep count; others think counting distracts from love.
  • Many link repetition with the breath: Wahe on the in-breath, Guru on the out-breath.

Mission 3

Practices of nam simran

  • Amrit vela: the "ambrosial hours" before dawn: Japji tells Sikhs to meditate on the True Name then (Japji, pauri 4, Ang 2; paraphrase).
  • Nam japna: personal repetition of Waheguru or the Mool Mantar.
  • Nitnem: the daily banis (morning, evening, night prayers).
  • Kirtan: singing hymns from the Guru Granth Sahib, alone or in the sangat: collective nam simran.
  • Akhand Path: a continuous reading of the whole scripture over about 48 hours.
  • Guru Arjan's Sukhmani ("Jewel of Peace") is a long hymn in praise of simran.
Mission 3

From repetition to experience

  • Gurbani describes stages of deepening:
  • Repetition with the tongue → with the mind → ajapa jaap, "unrecited repetition": the Name repeats itself, without effort.
  • Vismad: awe and wonder at God's greatness.
  • Sahaj: calm, natural poise; the mind at peace in God.
  • Anahad shabad: the "unstruck sound": a divine music heard within. Gurbani speaks of the panch shabad, the "five sounds" (pictured as five instruments), resounding in the attuned soul (e.g. Anand Sahib, Ang 917).
  • All by grace (nadar): effort prepares; God gives.

Mission 4

Three pillars

  • Nam japna: remember God's Name.
  • Kirat karni: earn an honest living by your own work.
  • Vand chhakna: share what you have with others.
  • Rooted in Guru Nanak's community at Kartarpur: prayer, farming and shared meals.
Mission 4

Nam japna: remembering the Name

  • Keeping God in mind all the time, not only at set prayers.
  • Forms: simran (repeating "Waheguru"), Nitnem (daily prayers), kirtan (sung hymns), amrit vela (early-morning meditation).
  • Purpose: to overcome haumai and become gurmukh.
  • The other two pillars come from this remembrance: work and sharing done with God in mind.
Mission 4

Kirat karni: honest work

  • Earn by honest effort: no theft, cheating, bribery or exploiting others.
  • Guru Nanak rejected begging and renunciation as a path: the Sikh is a householder (grihasti).
  • Janamsakhi story: Guru Nanak eats with Bhai Lalo, a poor carpenter, rather than at the feast of Malik Bhago, a rich official. Squeezed, Lalo's bread gives milk; Bhago's gives blood: wealth gained by oppression.
  • Work is not a distraction from God; done honestly, it is worship.
Mission 4Read the source

Earn, give, remember

Guru Granth Sahib (paraphrase):

  • One who works for what he eats, and gives some of what he has: Nanak says, he knows the path (GGS 1245, Guru Nanak).
  • Make the effort and earn, and enjoy it; meditate, and meet God, and anxiety ends (GGS 522, Guru Arjan).

Q1 Which two pillars appear in the first verse?

Show answer

Kirat karni, earning by work; vand chhakna, giving.

Q2 Which pillar does the second verse add to effort?

Show answer

Nam japna: meditating on God.

Mission 4

Vand chhakna: sharing

  • Share wealth, food, time and skill, especially with those in need.
  • Dasvandh: giving a tenth of income (Guru Arjan organised it; the Rehat Maryada commends it).
  • Langar: free food for all, of any faith or caste: sharing made visible.
  • Sarbat da bhala: "the welfare of all", prayed for at the end of every Ardas.
  • Sharing cuts lobh (greed) and moh (attachment), two of the five thieves.

Mission 5

What sewa means

  • Sewa (also spelled seva): selfless service, to God and to others.
  • Sikhs see no line between the two: God (Waheguru) is present in every person, so serving people is serving God.
  • Sewa is done freely, with no wish for reward, praise or return.
  • It belongs with the three pillars (see Mission 4): remember God, work honestly, share.
Mission 5

Sewa in the Guru Granth Sahib

  • Guru Nanak: serve in this world, and you will be given a seat of honour in God's court (Guru Granth Sahib, Ang 26, Sri Rag; paraphrase).
  • Guru Arjan, in the Sukhmani: the one who serves without desire for reward (nishkam) reaches the Lord (Ang 286; paraphrase).
  • So sewa is not a side-activity: it is part of the path to mukti (liberation).
  • But Sikhs hold that liberation comes finally by God's grace (nadar): sewa prepares the self; it does not buy salvation.
Mission 5

Three forms of sewa

  • Tan (body): physical service. Cooking and serving langar, washing dishes, cleaning the gurdwara, caring for the sick, wiping pilgrims' shoes.
  • Man (mind): mental service. Teaching, sharing knowledge, reciting and explaining gurbani, counselling, offering skills.
  • Dhan (wealth): material service. Giving money and goods; many Sikhs aim to give dasvandh, a tenth of their income.
  • Sikh tradition often ranks tan sewa highly: it is personal, cannot be paid for by someone else, and is the most humbling.
Mission 5

The state of mind: sewa against haumai

  • Haumai: the ego, "I-am-ness"; self-centredness that keeps the soul apart from God.
  • A manmukh (self-willed person) serves to be seen. A gurmukh (God-centred person) serves because self has given way to God.
  • So the intention matters more than the act: selfless and desire-less action.
  • Sewa done for status or reputation feeds haumai; done humbly, it loosens it.
  • An IB question may ask you to define haumai: the ego, self-centredness, which separates the soul from God; sewa, nam simran and grace overcome it.

Mission 5

Sarbat da bhala: sewa for everyone

  • Every Ardas (the congregational prayer) ends asking for sarbat da bhala: "the welfare of all".
  • Sewa is the practical side of that prayer: not only for Sikhs.
  • Langar shows it: anyone may eat, sitting together, whatever their religion, caste, gender or wealth.
  • Sewa is a central Sikh ethical practice: it turns belief in one God present in all into moral conduct.

Mission 6

Miri and piri

  • Piri: spiritual authority, from pir (a holy teacher).
  • Miri: temporal (worldly, political) authority, from mir (a ruler).
  • Miri-piri: the two belong together. Faith is not only private prayer; it must also act in the world, defending justice.
Mission 6

Guru Hargobind's two swords

  • 1606: Guru Arjan, the fifth Guru, dies in Mughal custody in Lahore.
  • His son Guru Hargobind (1595–1644) becomes the sixth Guru.
  • At his installation he wears two swords: one of miri, one of piri.
  • He builds the Akal Takht ("Throne of the Timeless One") facing the Harmandir Sahib: a seat of temporal authority beside the seat of worship.
  • He keeps horses and armed followers; the Sikhs fight defensive battles against Mughal forces.
  • Many Sikhs see this as Arjan's own wish: the community must be able to protect itself.
Mission 6

The saint-soldier: Sant Sipahi

  • Guru Gobind Singh (1666–1708), tenth Guru, founds the Khalsa in 1699.
  • Ideal: the sant sipahi, the saint-soldier.
  • Sant duties: nam simran, sewa, honest work, self-discipline, humility.
  • Sipahi duties: courage, readiness to defend the weak and the faith, discipline in arms.
  • The saint comes first: the soldier is only legitimate when controlled by a saintly mind free of anger, greed and ego.

Mission 6Read the source

The Zafarnama

  • 1705: Guru Gobind Singh writes the Zafarnama ("Letter of Victory"), in Persian, to the emperor Aurangzeb, after Mughal commanders broke sworn oaths of safe passage at Anandpur.
  • Verse 22 (paraphrase): when every other means has failed, it is right to take up the sword.

Q1 What condition does the verse set before force may be used?

Show answer

All other means must have failed: force is a last resort.

Q2 How does this fit the sant sipahi ideal?

Show answer

The saint seeks peace first; the soldier acts only when peaceful means are exhausted.

Mission 6

Teg and deg

  • Teg: the sword: protecting the poor and oppressed.
  • Deg: the cooking pot: feeding the hungry (langar).
  • Together: Sikhs meet material and spiritual needs: food for the body, protection for the vulnerable, the Name for the soul.
  • The phrase "Deg Teg Fateh" ("victory to the pot and sword") appears on the seal of Banda Singh Bahadur (from 1710) and on Sikh coins from 1764.
  • Today: langar and Sikh relief work are deg; standing up for others' rights is teg.
Mission 6

The kirpan

  • One of the 5Ks worn by amritdhari (initiated) Sikhs.
  • A common explanation of the word: kirpa (mercy, grace) + aan (honour, dignity).
  • It is a religious article of faith, not a weapon for attack: a reminder of the duty to defend justice and the weak.
  • Sizes vary: from a full sword in ceremonies to a small blade worn under clothes.
  • Many Sikhs treat it with great respect and do not draw it in anger.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • The soul passes through many lives (samsara); human birth is the precious chance to meet God.
  • Karma shapes birth, but liberation (mukti), union with God, comes by grace (nadar), and can begin in this life (jivan mukti).

Mission 2

  • Nam is God's Name and presence; hukam is God's will and order; nadar is grace, God's gift.
  • The five khands of Japji (duty, knowledge, endeavour, grace, truth) map the soul's progress; Saram Khand and Karam Khand pair effort with grace.

Mission 3

  • Nam is God's presence and nature; nam simran is loving remembrance; nam japna (Naam Japo) repeats the Name.
  • Mantras: the Gurmantar Waheguru and the Mool Mantar; practices include amrit vela, Nitnem, kirtan and Akhand Path.
  • Repetition deepens to ajapa jaap, sahaj and the "unstruck sound" (panch shabad); Sikhs differ on methods and on whether simran is mystical union or ethical remembrance.

Mission 4

  • Nam japna (remember the Name), kirat karni (earn honestly) and vand chhakna (share) form one gurmukh way of life, rooted in Guru Nanak's Kartarpur.

Mission 5

  • Sewa (seva) is selfless service to God and to others; Sikhs see no line between the two.
  • Three forms: tan (physical), man (mental), dhan (material); intention matters most: desire-less action that loosens haumai.

Mission 6

  • Miri (temporal) and piri (spiritual) belong together: Guru Hargobind's two swords and the Akal Takht facing the Harmandir Sahib.
  • Guru Gobind Singh's Khalsa lives the ideal of the sant sipahi (saint-soldier): force only for dharam, as a last resort, under saintly control.
