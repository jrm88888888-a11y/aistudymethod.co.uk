Mission 1

Mission 1: Men in Wokingham live about nine years longer than men in Blackpool. What has work got to do with it?

Make a guess: Men in Wokingham live about nine years longer than men in Blackpool. What has work got to do with it?

Specification: what this mission covers

Edexcel A-level Geography (9GE0)

  • 4A.1a classifying economic activity by sector (primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary) and by employment type (part-time/full-time, temporary/permanent, employed/self-employed)
  • 4A.1b differences in economic activity (employment and output data) reflected in health, life expectancy and education
  • 4A.1c pay inequalities across sectors and employment types reflected in quality of life indices
Mission 1

Four economic sectors

  • Primary: extracting raw materials (farming, fishing, forestry, mining).
  • Secondary: making things (manufacturing, construction, energy and water supply).
  • Tertiary: providing services (retail, transport, health, education, hospitality).
  • Quaternary: knowledge-based work (research and development, information and communication, specialist professional services).
  • Official UK statistics use industry sections (SIC). They do not label a "quaternary" sector, so geographers group sections (e.g. information and communication; professional, scientific and technical) as a proxy.
Mission 1

Types of employment

  • Full-time / part-time: hours worked (the census counts 30 hours a week or fewer as part-time).
  • Permanent / temporary: whether the job has a fixed end.
  • Employed / self-employed: working for an employer, or working for yourself.
  • UK, June–August 2026: 34.58 million in employment; 4.44 million self-employed (about 13%); 8.71 million part-time (about 25%).
  • Temporary employees: 1.60 million, 5.4% of all employees.
  • Percentages calculated from the ONS levels (4,439 ÷ 34,577; 8,713 ÷ 34,577).

Mission 1

Two contrasting places: jobs by industry

  • Census 2021, residents in employment, share by industry:
    • Accommodation and food: Blackpool 10.0% · Wokingham 2.9% · England 4.9%
    • Information and communication: 1.7% · 15.0% · 4.7%
    • Professional, scientific and technical: 3.3% · 9.2% · 6.7%
    • Human health and social work: 20.5% · 10.5% · 14.6%
  • Blackpool's work leans to tertiary tourism and care; Wokingham's to quaternary-type knowledge work.
Mission 1

Two places: types of employment

  • Part-time share of residents in employment: Blackpool 33.1% · Wokingham 25.9% · England 29.8%.
  • Of all residents aged 16+:
    • In employment (excluding full-time students): 49.5% · 62.2% · England 55.7%
    • Long-term sick or disabled: 8.2% · 1.7% · England 4.1%
  • Self-employed without employees: 7.1% · 8.7% · England 8.0%. Self-employment is not only a sign of a weak economy: Wokingham's is higher.
Quick check
?Which statement do the Census 2021 data for Blackpool and Wokingham support?
Mission 1

Health, life expectancy and education

  • Life expectancy at birth, 2021–2023: males Blackpool 73.1 · Wokingham 82.5 · England 79.1 years; females 78.9 · 85.6 · 83.1.
  • Bad or very bad health (age-standardised), 2021: 8.8% · 2.8% · England 5.3%.
  • No qualifications (residents 16+): 24.6% · 10.4% · England 18.1%. Level 4 or above (degree level): 22.5% · 46.2% · England 33.9%.
  • The links run both ways: low-paid, insecure work can harm health; poor health and few qualifications also limit the jobs people can get. Data show association, not proof of cause.
Mission 1

Pay differs by sector and by type of job

  • UK median gross hourly pay, full-time employees, 2025:
    • Accommodation and food £14.12 · Wholesale and retail £16.16 · Health and social work £18.78
    • Manufacturing £19.17 · Information and communication £26.77 · Financial and insurance £30.66
  • Part-time employees, all industries: £14.13 an hour, against £19.75 for full-time.
  • So places with many hospitality, retail and part-time jobs have lower typical earnings.
Quick check

Mission 1

Pay in the two places and a quality of life index

  • Median gross weekly pay, full-time, residents, 2025: Blackpool £647.90 · Wokingham £953.70.
  • Part-time hourly pay: Blackpool £12.79 · Wokingham £15.07.
  • Index of Multiple Deprivation 2025 combines income, employment, education, health, crime, barriers to housing and services, and living environment.
    • Blackpool: rank 1 of 296 districts (most deprived) on average score; 40% of its small areas are in England's most deprived 10%.
    • Wokingham: rank 295.
Mission 1

Putting it together

  • Sector mix → pay and security of jobs → household income → health, education and other domains → quality of life index.
  • Feedback: poor health and low qualifications feed back into lower-paid work.
  • Exam move (EDX "Assess" / "Explain"): pair one employment statistic with one social statistic for each place, then say why the link is association, not proof of cause.
Mission check

?A town loses a large car factory. New jobs appear in warehouses and care homes, many of them part-time or temporary. What is the most likely effect on the town's score in a quality of life index such as the IMD?

Work shapes places through what people do (sector) and how they are employed (hours, contract, self-employment). Pay gaps between sectors and job types feed into income, health, education and life expectancy, which quality of life indices such as the IMD combine.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Is a place just a dot on a map, or something more?

Make a guess: Is a place just a dot on a map, or something more?

Specification: what this mission covers

Edexcel A-level Geography (9GE0)

  • 4A.2a places change function (administrative, commercial, retail, industrial) and demographics (gentrification, age, ethnicity)
  • 4A.2b reasons for change: physical factors, accessibility, historical development, local and national planning
  • 4A.2c measuring change: employment, demographic and land-use change, deprivation indices
Mission 2

Space or place?

  • Space: a location or area, described by coordinates, size, distance.
  • Place: space that people have given meaning: memories, feelings, identity.
  • Yi-Fu Tuan (Space and Place, 1977) argued that space becomes place as we get to know it and attach value to it.
  • Places matter to people: belonging, identity, security, and where we choose to live, work and invest.
Mission 2

Two contrasting places

  • Blackpool (Lancashire coast) and Wokingham (Berkshire, west of London), Census 2021:
  • Population change 2011–21: Blackpool −0.7%; Wokingham +15.0%; England +6.6%.
  • Aged 65+: Blackpool 20.7%; Wokingham 17.2%; England 18.4%.
  • Born outside the UK: Blackpool 7.9%; Wokingham 18.9%; England 17.4%.
  • Jobs (residents in work): accommodation and food, Blackpool 10.0% vs Wokingham 2.9%; information and communication, 1.7% vs 15.0%.
  • Deprivation (IMD 2025, rank of average score, 296 districts): Blackpool 1st (most deprived); Wokingham 295th.
Mission 2

Changing function and demographics

  • Function = the main roles a place plays: administrative, commercial, retail, industrial (and leisure).
  • Blackpool, still a resort, is less dominated by it: accommodation and food fell from 12.1% (2011) to 10.0% (2021) of residents in work.
  • Wokingham's information and communication share rose from 12.6% to 15.0%.
  • Age: Blackpool's 65+ share rose from 19.2% to 20.7%. Origin: Wokingham's born-outside-UK share rose from 12.3% to 18.9%.
  • Gentrification (higher-income residents moving into an area, changing housing and services) is another demographic change to look for in your own places.
Mission 2

Why places change

  • Physical factors: Blackpool's flat, sandy coast made it a resort; Wokingham's flat inland land is easy to build on.
  • Accessibility and connectedness: links by rail, road and air decide who can reach a place, and what else they can reach instead.
  • Historical development: a Victorian resort leaves hotels and piers; a market town near a growing city gains commuter estates.
  • Planning: local plans and national housing targets decide where new homes and jobs go.
Mission 2

Measuring change

  • Employment trends: industry shares at each census (Blackpool's tourism jobs, Wokingham's information jobs).
  • Demographic change: population, age structure, country of birth at each census.
  • Land-use change: maps and satellite images of new housing, derelict land, converted hotels.
  • Deprivation: the IMD combines seven weighted domains: income 22.5%, employment 22.5%, education 13.5%, health 13.5%, crime 9.3%, barriers to housing and services 9.3%, living environment 9.3%.
  • Blackpool ranked 1st of 317 districts in 2019 and 1st of 296 in 2025; Wokingham 316th of 317 and 295th of 296.
Quick check
?Blackpool was ranked 1st of 317 districts on IMD 2019 and 1st of 296 on IMD 2025. What can you safely conclude?
Mission 2

Flows and time-space compression

  • Time-space compression (David Harvey, 1989): faster, cheaper transport and communication make distant places feel closer.
  • UK residents' visits abroad rose from 17.5 million in 1980 to 93.1 million in 2019.
  • For a seaside resort, beaches abroad came within easy reach of its visitors: an exogenous change (from outside the place) that resorts such as Blackpool must compete with.
  • For Wokingham, flows of people (more residents born abroad), investment and ideas (information and communication jobs) tied it more closely to global networks.
Quick check
?UK residents made 17.5 million visits abroad in 1980 and 93.1 million in 2019. By what factor did visits rise?
Mission check

?A new housing estate is built on an empty field. A year later, residents say it "has no sense of place yet". Following Tuan, what is most likely to turn this space into a place?

A place is a location plus meaning, shaped by features inside it and flows from outside, and seen differently by different people. Census and deprivation data measure how it changes; people's meanings explain why it matters.

Mission 3: Your street has a story no map tells. How do you uncover what made your own place what it is?

Make a guess: Your street has a story no map tells. How do you uncover what made your own place what it is?

Specification: what this mission covers

Edexcel A-level Geography (9GE0)

  • 4A.3a / 4B.3a global, international, national and regional influences shaping the chosen places
  • 4A.3c / 4B.3c how (demographic and cultural) continuity and change affected people's lives; erosion vs enrichment
  • 4A.6a statistical evidence to judge the need for regeneration in the local place
  • 4B.6a statistical evidence of positive or negative images of the local place
Mission 3

What is a local place study?

  • A locality, neighbourhood or small community, urban or rural, where you live or study.
  • Goal: describe its character, explain how it developed, and show how people's lives changed.
  • Edexcel: your local place plus one contrasting place, studied through your chosen option (Regenerating Places or Diverse Places).
  • This mission models the method with one exemplar: Elephant and Castle, London.
Mission 3

The place profile

Six lenses (a checklist for any place study):

  • Demographic: age, ethnicity, migration.
  • Socio-economic: jobs, income, deprivation, housing tenure.
  • Cultural: languages, faiths, food, festivals, shops.
  • Political: who governs; campaigns; votes.
  • Built: housing types, street pattern, landmarks.
  • Natural: relief, rivers, green space.

Sources: quantitative (census, IMD, employment) and qualitative (photos, oral histories, planning documents, newspapers, social media).

Mission 3

Exemplar: Elephant and Castle

  • A neighbourhood in the London Borough of Southwark.
  • Built, then: the Heygate estate, completed 1974, about 3,000 people; 1,212 homes.
  • Built, now: Elephant Park, approved January 2013: 3,000 new homes; nearly half public space.
  • Cultural: a cluster of Latin American–owned businesses, surveyed by the community group Latin Elephant from July 2012.
  • Political: the shopping centre's demolition application was rejected in January 2018, then approved in January 2019.
Mission 3

The numbers: Southwark, Census 2011 → 2021

  • Population 288,300 → 307,600 (+6.7%; London +7.7%).
  • Households renting socially 43.7% → 39.7%: England's second-largest fall.
  • Ethnic group: White 54.2% → 51.4%; Black 26.9% → 25.1%; Asian 9.4% → 9.9%.
  • Born in Nigeria: 13,600 → 11,200 residents.
  • In work (16+, excluding full-time students): 59.6% → 62.7%; England fell 56.5% → 55.7%.
Quick check
?Southwark's 2011–21 census figures are for the whole borough. What is their main limitation for studying Elephant and Castle?
Mission 3

Lived experience: the qualitative layer

  • Oral and community records: Latin Elephant began in 2012 as a blog by a researcher studying Latin Americans in Elephant and Castle.
  • In 2021 it launched MyElephantStory, a digital archive of the area, on the first anniversary of the shopping centre's closure.
  • Residents' views: some Heygate residents said "releasing the very significant value of the land was the key factor" in demolition.
  • Official accounts: the council reports that 99% of its rehoused Heygate tenants chose to stay in Southwark.
  • Different voices, different experiences of the same change.
Mission 3

Connections and flows

  • People: Latin American traders and residents; Nigeria-born residents fewer than in 2011.
  • Money: the council partnered with Lend Lease (now Lendlease) in 2007 to redevelop the Heygate site; Lendlease lists Elephant Park beside projects in Sydney, Singapore, Malaysia and New York.
  • Ideas: national talk of "so-called sink estates" and a £140 million regeneration fund (2016); local ideas, such as a "Latin Quarter" vision (2014–16).
  • These flows tie one London neighbourhood to the region, the nation and the world.

Quick check
?Which is a flow of money/investment shaping the place?
Mission 3

Continuity and change in people's lives

  • Change: 595 council tenants rehoused; 99% stayed in Southwark; all offered the right to return.
  • Change: shopping centre closed 24 Sep 2020; traders' relocation began the next day, "although not everyone could be relocated".
  • Continuity: Latin American businesses and community groups still organise in the area.
  • Erosion: loss of trading space and long-standing homes. Enrichment: new homes, a large new park; affordable housing in the scheme raised from 11% to 25% after renegotiation.
Mission 3

Statistics: is regeneration needed? Is the image fair?

  • Index of Multiple Deprivation 2025: Southwark 102nd of 296 districts (1 = most deprived), so more deprived than about two-thirds of districts; 0% of its neighbourhoods in England's most deprived 10%.
  • Very good health: 44.9% → 49.4% of residents, 2011–21 (age-standardised).
  • In work: up, while England fell.
  • Regenerating Places: does the data show need? Diverse Places: does it support a positive or a negative image?
  • Method: compare with London and England, over time, and at small-area level.
Quick check
?Southwark ranks 102nd of 296 on IMD 2025, with 0% of neighbourhoods in the most deprived 10%. What does this show?
Mission 3

Your own study, step by step

  • 1. Define the boundary (a ward, an LSOA cluster, a village).
  • 2. Profile it with the six lenses.
  • 3. Collect at least two quantitative and two qualitative sources, past and present.
  • 4. Trace flows and influences at each scale.
  • 5. Record whose lives changed, how, and how you experience it.
  • 6. State your sources' limits (date, scale, author's purpose).
Mission check

?A student's study of their village uses residents' interviews, old photos and the 2021 census for the whole district. Which addition would most improve it?

A strong place study mixes quantitative and qualitative sources, past and present, at the right scale, and traces the flows and influences that made the place.

Mission 4

Two ways to show a place

  • Representation: how a place is portrayed: what is shown, by whom, for whom.
  • Formal (statistical) representation: census tables, maps, geospatial data, deprivation indices. Made by official bodies with published methods.
  • Informal representation: media made with a purpose or a point of view.
  • Edexcel: compare varied representations with formal statistical images.
  • Neither is neutral: both select.
Mission 4

Slough, informally

  • A poem: John Betjeman's "Slough" asks for "friendly bombs" to fall on the town (as recalled in Parliament).
  • TV: the sitcom The Office and its manager David Brent are tied to Slough's image; in 2019 Slough's MP said the town has "a lot more than just David Brent and 'The Office' to offer".
  • 2002, an MP for another area: "I would not be as unkind as Mr. Betjeman in calling for friendly bombs to fall on Slough" — then used Slough as a unit of sprawl: "40 towns the size of Slough".
  • An outsider's image, still being repeated decades later.
Mission 4

Slough, formally

  • Population +13.0%, 2011–21 (England +6.6%).
  • Median age 34 (England 40).
  • 46.7% Asian, Asian British or Asian Welsh; 36.0% White (2021).
  • Index of Multiple Deprivation 2025: 105th of 296 districts (1 = most deprived); 0% of its neighbourhoods in England's most deprived 10%.
  • The formal image: young, growing, diverse; more deprived than about two-thirds of districts, yet with no neighbourhood in the worst 10%. Not the poem's image.
Quick check
?Which of these is a formal representation of Slough?
Mission 4

Insiders answer back

  • 2019, in a debate on a "Town of Culture" award, Slough's MP Tan Dhesi: "Slough is on the up: we have moved on from John Betjeman's poem."
  • "Slough is a fantastic, diverse cultural melting pot … a major business, creative and cultural powerhouse, with Pinewood Studios right on our doorstep."
  • Continuity: the outsider image persists. Change: insiders contest it, here to win a culture award.
  • Contrasting media evidence: the same town framed as a joke and as a "powerhouse".
Mission 4

Who feels they belong?

Community Life Survey, England, 2023/24: adults who feel they belong strongly to their immediate neighbourhood.

  • All adults: 61%.
  • Age: 16–24 54%; 25–34 51%; other age groups 59–73%.
  • Sex: female 63%; male 60%.
  • Sexual orientation: heterosexual 61%; gay or lesbian 49%; bisexual 45%.
  • Religion: Jewish 68%, Muslim 67%, Christian 66%; no religion 56%.
Mission 4

Same park, different place

  • ONS, Great Britain, Feb–Mar 2022: walking alone in a park or open space after dark.
  • Felt very or fairly unsafe: 82% of women, 42% of men.
  • Women aged 16–34 felt the most unsafe of any age and sex group using public transport alone after dark.
  • Perception depends on who you are, not only on the place.
Mission 4

Feelings change behaviour

  • Place attachment: an emotional bond between a person and a place.
  • Fear changes routines: adults who stopped walking in parks or open spaces after dark rose from 32% to 37% (women) and 18% to 24% (men), June 2021 → Feb–Mar 2022.
  • Attachment keeps people: 61% would still like to live in their area in five years; 84% of adults aged 75+.
  • Attachment mobilises people: at Elephant and Castle, London, the community group Latin Elephant objected at the January 2018 planning committee on the shopping centre.
Quick check
?Belonging is 54% among 16–24-year-olds and 59–73% for adults aged 35 and over. Which conclusion is best supported?
Mission 4

Representation and the "need" to regenerate

  • 2016, the Prime Minister: "so-called sink estates … brutal high-rise towers and dark alleyways that are a gift to criminals"; a £140 million fund for estate regeneration.
  • Heygate estate, Southwark (completed 1974, about 3,000 people): a study "concluded that the buildings were structurally sound, but in need of complete refurbishment".
  • The council cited "housing management challenges and regeneration opportunities"; "Some residents therefore say that releasing the very significant value of the land was the key factor".
  • A powerful image can make demolition look necessary; other evidence can question it.
Mission 4

Representing a diverse place

  • 1998: Channel 4's documentary The Peacemaker showed mediation between gangs of young people from the Hindu, Muslim and Sikh communities of Slough and Southall.
  • 2019: Slough's MP calls it "a fantastic, diverse cultural melting pot".
  • Census 2021: 46.7% Asian, 36.0% White.
  • The same diversity can be framed as conflict or as strength; each frame shapes how outsiders see cultural change.
Quick check
?A council says an estate "must be demolished". Which evidence most directly questions that need?
Mission 4

How to judge any representation

  • Who made it? For whom? When? Why?
  • What is left out?
  • Check informal images against formal data. Then check the data: a census is only every 10 years; categories are chosen by officials; averages hide neighbourhoods.
  • Exam move ("Assess"): judge how far a representation matches the statistical image.
Mission check

?A seaside town's tourism video shows sunny beaches and busy cafés. Census data show an old population and high deprivation. A local blog posts photos of closed shops. Which evaluation is strongest?

Representations are made for purposes. Informal and formal images of the same place differ because they are built by different people for different audiences, and they can change how a place is treated.

Mission 5: Santa Clara County, in Silicon Valley, has a median household income about twice the US figure. Could any part of it need regeneration?

Make a guess: Santa Clara County, in Silicon Valley, has a median household income about twice the US figure. Could any part of it need regeneration?

Specification: what this mission covers

Edexcel A-level Geography (9GE0)

  • 4A.4a successful regions (Palo Alto and Silicon Valley) have high employment, inward migration (internal and international), low multiple deprivation, but high property prices and skill shortages in urban and rural areas
  • 4A.4c the perceived need for regeneration varies with economic and social inequalities in urban and rural locations
Mission 5

The region

  • Silicon Valley: all of Santa Clara County plus nearby cities in San Mateo, Alameda and Santa Cruz counties; about 3 million people.
  • Palo Alto sits in Santa Clara County; Stanford University lies beside it.
  • Measured here with the US Census Bureau's American Community Survey (ACS), 2020–2024 averages.
Mission 5

Signs of success

  • Median household income: Santa Clara County $164,281; USA $80,734 (≈ 2.0 ×).
  • Adults with a bachelor's degree or higher: 56.5% v 35.7%.
  • People below the poverty line: 7.1% v 12.5%.
  • Palo Alto: income $231,101; degree 82.3%; poverty 6.0%.
Mission 5

Employment: high, but cyclical

  • San Jose metro unemployment averaged 2.7% in 2019 (USA 3.7%).
  • In 2024 it averaged 4.2% (USA 4.0%) after tech job cuts.
  • Successful regions are not immune to downturns; their advantage is skills and investment.
Mission 5

Inward migration

  • International: 41.5% of Santa Clara County residents were born abroad (USA 14.1%); Silicon Valley's share hit a record 40% in 2022.

  • Change is contested when these groups want different outcomes; rising land value often drives it.
Mission 11

Lagos: Otodo-Gbame

  • Lagos State's planning ministry estimated in 2013 that over 70% of Lagos's population lived in informal settlements (reported by Amnesty International).
  • On 9 October 2016 the State Governor announced that informal settlements along the waterways would be demolished within seven days.
  • Amnesty estimates that over 30,000 residents of the waterfront community of Otodo-Gbame were forcibly evicted, in three operations from November 2016 to April 2017.
  • The estimate deducts the people who remained (about 4,700) from an earlier population estimate.
Mission 11

Lagos: process and outcome

  • All 75 Otodo-Gbame evictees Amnesty interviewed said they had no prior written notice.
  • Amnesty reports that the evictions went ahead in violation of court orders of 7 November 2016 and 26 January 2017.
  • The government gave varying reasons, Amnesty reports, from denying responsibility to "claiming to save the environment from degradation … to preventing crime".
  • A luxury housing estate was being built on part of the land and water area (Amnesty, 2017).
Mission 11

London: the Heygate estate

  • A council estate in Southwark, completed in 1974, originally housing about 3,000 people; 1,212 homes, 1,020 council-owned.
  • A late-1990s survey found the buildings "structurally sound, but in need of complete refurbishment".
  • In a 1998 consultation, 96% of 500 responses favoured major regeneration.
  • The redevelopment "has been the subject of a two-decade long controversy".
Mission 11

Heygate: decisions and players

  • 2005: the 595 council tenants were given top priority to move; 99% chose to stay in Southwark, and all were offered the right to return.
  • 2007: Southwark Council partnered with the developer Lend Lease (now Lendlease).
  • 2010: renegotiated terms raised affordable housing from 11% to 25%, plus a council share of any extra profits.
  • January 2013: plans for 3,000 new homes approved; July 2014: demolition complete. "Nearly half of the new development is public space".
Mission 11

Players in placemaking

  • Government: Southwark Council led the scheme; the Mayor of London sets conditions on GLA funding. In some UK schemes, EU funds also paid for regeneration.
  • Corporate body: Lendlease, the development partner.
  • Not-for-profit organisations: in Lagos, Amnesty International and Justice and Empowerment Initiatives documented the evictions.
  • Community groups: residents' campaigns, such as the Heygate campaign quoted by the London Assembly.

  • Some residents: "releasing the very significant value of the land was the key factor".

Mission 11

Why groups contest rebranding

  • A new name and image (the Heygate site is now marketed as Elephant Park) targets new buyers and investors.
  • Long-standing residents may lose homes, neighbours and shops, and their attachment to the place.
  • Benefits (new homes, a park) may go mainly to newcomers; costs fall on those displaced.
  • So contest comes from those who lose most: here, social tenants and leaseholders on the estate.
Mission 11

NIMBYism

  • NIMBYism ("not in my back yard"): opposing a development near your own home, while perhaps accepting it elsewhere.
  • Typical targets: tall towers, denser housing, roads or waste sites next to existing homes.
  • The Heygate contest was different: opposition came mainly from residents losing their own homes, not neighbours resisting a new scheme.
  • Exam move: say whose interests drive each objection: neighbours (NIMBY) or displaced residents.

Mission 11

Strategies to manage inequality and segregation

  • Resident ballots: from 18 July 2018, London landlords seeking GLA funding for estate regeneration that demolishes any social homes and builds 150 or more homes must win a residents' ballot.
  • No net loss: the Mayor requires "no net loss of affordable homes" and "full rights to return or remain for social tenants".
  • Upgrade, don't clear: the UN calls for upgrading slums with basic services, to prevent displacement.
  • Secure tenure and courts: protections only work if they are respected; Amnesty reports Lagos court orders were not.
Mission 11

Comparing the two neighbourhoods

  • Similar: lower-income homes cleared on valuable land; luxury development on part of the Lagos site; some Heygate residents say land value decided it.
  • Process: London, a council decision with rehousing priority and a right to return; Lagos, operations Amnesty documents as without notice or compensation, despite court orders.
  • Scale: the Heygate originally housed about 3,000 people; Amnesty estimates over 30,000 were evicted from Otodo-Gbame.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Classify activity by sector (primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary) and by employment type (full/part-time, permanent/temporary, employed/self-employed).
  • Blackpool (tourism and care, more part-time work) and Wokingham (knowledge work) differ in pay, health, qualifications and life expectancy (males 73.1 against 82.5 years), and so in the IMD (rank 1 against 295 of 296).

Mission 2

  • Space vs place (Tuan); places as meeting points of flows (Massey); endogenous and exogenous factors.
  • Blackpool vs Wokingham: −0.7% vs +15.0% population (2011–21); IMD 2025 rank 1 vs 295; tourism vs information and communication jobs. Measure change with employment, demography, land use and the IMD. Time-space compression (UK visits abroad 17.5 m in 1980 → 93.1 m in 2019) reshapes function and sense of place.

Mission 3

  • Profile a place with six lenses; use quantitative and qualitative sources; check scale and date.
  • Elephant and Castle: Heygate to Elephant Park, Latin American traders, global developer, national policy, contested change.
  • Influences at global, international, national and regional scales; statistics to judge need and image.

Mission 4

  • Slough: a poem and a sitcom made an outsider image; census and IMD data paint a young, diverse, mid-ranked town; insiders contest the image.
  • Perception and attachment vary with age, sex, sexuality and religion, and they change behaviour (avoiding parks after dark, staying, campaigning).
  • Representations such as "sink estates" can drive regeneration; contrasting evidence can question it.

Mission 5

  • Santa Clara County: income about 2 × the US, 41.5% foreign-born, 7.1% poverty, but median home value about 4.5 × the US and global recruitment of tech staff.
  • Perceived need for regeneration is strongest where inequality is felt: East Palo Alto, renters, rural towns; compare Detroit's region-wide decline.

Mission 6

  • Engagement varies: national turnout 40–75% by constituency (2024); London ward turnout 26–61% within one borough (2022).
  • Attachment and engagement vary with age, ethnicity, gender, length of residence and deprivation; conflicts over regeneration grow from inequality and lack of representation (Aylesbury, Heygate, resident ballots).

Mission 7

  • Infrastructure (HS2: £87.7–102.7bn, London–Birmingham only; Heathrow third runway: decision 2029, up to 40% of claimed benefits outside London and the South East) can redirect growth only if it reaches lagging regions.
  • Planning targets (1.5m homes; 208,600 built in 2024–25), affordability (London 10.6×, North East 5.0×; rural second homes), migration rules and capital-market deregulation (Big Bang 1986; London 62% of non-resident purchase value) shape which regions regenerate.

Mission 8

  • Councils compete for investment through local plans, Enterprise Zones and science/technology parks (Here East). Interest groups contest preservation versus change; London 2012 relocated firms and tenants and raised gentrification concerns.
  • Strategies: sport, leisure and retail-led (Olympic Park, Westfield); rural diversification (Mid Wales Growth Deal in Powys). Rebranding re-images places through media, as Glasgow did with its industrial heritage.

Mission 9

  • Strategies: heritage tourism (visitor centre 2012; 754,000 → 1,039,000 visitors, 2013–18), sport (Open 2019, 2025; £280m+), farm diversification (Broughgammon), outdoor rebranding (kayak trail, Causeway Coast Way).
  • Contested: golf resort approved 2012 for economic benefits; National Trust, World Heritage Committee and IUCN opposed; permission expired 2017. Residents raise congestion, parking, planning limits and house prices.

Mission 10

  • Judge regeneration with economic, social, demographic and environmental variables; separate absolute change (levels) from relative change (ranks).
  • Hull's 2017 rebranding raised participation and pride; data show modest absolute gains, but it remains among England's most deprived districts.

Mission 11

  • Regeneration and rebranding are contested because gains and losses fall on different groups.
  • Players: government, corporate bodies, not-for-profits and community groups; each judges success by its own criteria.
  • NIMBYism is neighbours opposing nearby development; the Heygate contest was about displacement.
  • Strategies: ballots, no net loss of affordable homes, rights to return, upgrading in place.
