Joining ideas

To lift a simple answer into a top-band one, you join your ideas together and compare things. This lesson covers comparatives & superlatives (plus / moins / aussi … que), how to build adverbs in -ment, the everyday prepositions and their contractions (au, du, aux, des), how to say which country you go to (en / au / aux), and the conjunctions that stitch clauses together.

Comparing two things

plus / moins / aussi … que

To compare, wrap your adjective with a word for more / less / as, and finish with que ("than / as"):

plus / moins / aussi + adjective + quemore / less / as … than
  • plus grand que — taller than
  • moins cher que — less expensive than
  • aussi rapide que — as quick as

Remember agreement: the adjective still agrees with the noun it describes — elle est plus grande que moi (she is taller than me).

Superlatives: le/la/les plus…

To say something is the most or the least, add the article le / la / les before plus / moins + adjective:

FrenchEnglish
le plus grandthe tallest (m)
la plus grandethe tallest (f)
les plus grandsthe tallest (pl)
le moins cherthe least expensive

Tip: the article agrees with the noun — a girl is la plus grande, a boy is le plus grand.

Two irregulars to learn

"better": meilleur vs mieux

French has two words for better, and mixing them up is a classic trap. Which one you use depends on whether you are describing a noun or a verb:

meilleur — better (adjective)

From bon (good). Describes a noun: ce livre est meilleur (this book is better).

mieux — better (adverb)

From bien (well). Describes a verb: je chante mieux (I sing better).

Never say plus bon — the comparative of bon is meilleur, and the comparative of bien is mieux.

Describing a verb

Making adverbs with -ment

An adverb tells you how something is done ("slowly", "happily"). To build most French adverbs, take the feminine form of the adjective and add -ment:

adjective (m → f)adverb
lent → lentelentement (slowly)
heureux → heureuseheureusement (happily)
doux → doucedoucement (gently)

Two shortcuts: if the adjective already ends in a vowel, just add -ment (vrai → vraiment). And -ant → -amment, -ent → -emment: récent → récemment (recently).

Irregular adverbs

Three you just memorise

A few very common adverbs don't follow the -ment rule at all — learn them cold:

  • bien — well (from bon)
  • mal — badly (from mauvais)
  • vite — quickly / fast

Careful: rapide is the adjective ("quick"), rapidement and vite are the adverbs ("quickly"). Adjectives describe nouns; adverbs describe verbs.

Preposition + article

au, du, aux, des

You cannot leave à le or de le side by side — they fuse. Learn the four forced contractions:

combinationbecomes
à + leau
à + lesaux
de + ledu
de + lesdes

No fusion with la or l': à la maison and de l'école stay as two words. Only le and les trigger the contraction.

Position & direction

The everyday prepositions

Prepositions tell you where or how. Keep these high-frequency ones ready:

FrenchEnglish
à / deto, at / of, from
en / dansin / inside
sur / souson / under
avec / sanswith / without
chez / pourat (someone's) / for
entre / devant / derrièrebetween / in front of / behind

Handy one: chez means "at the house/place of" — chez moi (at my place), chez le médecin (at the doctor's).

Going to a country

en France, au Portugal, aux États-Unis

"To / in" a country depends on its gender and whether it is plural:

type of countryuse
feminine or starts with a vowelen France, en Espagne
masculineau Portugal, au Canada
pluralaux États-Unis

Most countries ending in -e are feminine and take en: en France, en Italie, en Espagne. le Portugal and le Canada are masculine, so au.

Stitching clauses

Conjunctions & connectives

Conjunctions join two ideas into one sentence — the fastest way to sound more fluent:

FrenchEnglish
et / mais / ouand / but / or
donc / carso / because (for)
parce que / puisquebecause / since
quand / lorsquewhen / when (formal)
si / commeif / as, like
pendant quewhile

Two words for "because": parce que starts an explanation, while car (= "for") links it after a comma. Both push your answers into the higher bands.

Turning up the volume

Intensifiers: très, trop, assez…

Small words in front of an adjective or adverb change the degree:

  • très — very (très grand = very tall)
  • trop — too (trop cher = too expensive)
  • assez — quite / enough
  • beaucoup — a lot (with a verb)
  • un peu — a little

Word order: the intensifier sits before the adjective — assez rapide (quite quick), un peu fatigué (a little tired).

Recap

Your joining-ideas toolkit

Comparative: plus / moins / aussi + adjective + que (plus grand que = taller than).

Superlative: le / la / les plus (moins) + adjective (le plus grand).

Irregulars: bon → meilleur (adjective), bien → mieux (adverb).

Adverbs: feminine adjective + -ment (lentement); vowel just add -ment (vraiment); -ant→-amment, -ent→-emment (récemment); irregular bien, mal, vite.

Contractions: à+le=au, à+les=aux, de+le=du, de+les=des.

Countries: en France (fem/vowel), au Portugal (masc), aux États-Unis (pl); chez moi = at my place.

Conjunctions & intensifiers: et, mais, parce que, quand, si…; très, trop, assez, beaucoup, un peu.
