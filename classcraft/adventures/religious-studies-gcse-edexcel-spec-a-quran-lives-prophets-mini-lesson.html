Mission 1

Mission 1: Why do Muslims in Makkah today retrace the steps of a father, a mother and their son?

Make a guess: Why do Muslims in Makkah today retrace the steps of a father, a mother and their son?

Mission 1

Ibrahim, friend of Allah

  • Ibrahim (Abraham) is one of the greatest messengers (rasul) of Allah in Islam.
  • He is called Khalilullah, "friend of Allah": "Allah did take Abraham for a friend." (Surah 4:125, Yusuf Ali)
  • He is a hanif: one who turns away from idols to the one true God (16:120).
  • He received scrolls (Suhuf Ibrahim), now lost (87:19).
  • He is the father of two prophets: Ismail (with Hajra) and Ishaq (Isaac, with Sarah). Muslims trace the Prophet Muhammad's descent to Ismail.
  • His whole life is a series of tests, which he "fulfilled" (2:124).
Mission 1

Against the idols

  • Ibrahim grew up among people who worshipped idols; his father Azar is named in 6:74.
  • He reasoned from the sky: a star, the moon, the sun each set, so none could be his Lord (6:76–79).
  • He challenged his father and people: "What are these images, to which ye are (so assiduously) devoted?" (Surah 21:52, Yusuf Ali)
  • Their only answer: "We found our fathers worshipping them." (21:53)
  • He broke the idols; the people threw him into a fire, but Allah said: "O Fire! be thou cool, and (a means of) safety for Abraham!" (Surah 21:69, Yusuf Ali)
  • This is preaching tawhid (the oneness of Allah) at great personal risk.
Mission 1Read the source

The broken idols

> "'And by Allah, I have a plan for your idols - after ye go away and turn your backs'.. So he broke them to pieces, (all) but the biggest of them, that they might turn (and address themselves) to it." (Surah 21:57–58, Yusuf Ali)

  • Later the people admit: "Thou knowest full well that these (idols) do not speak!" (21:65)

Q1 Why did Ibrahim leave the biggest idol whole?

Show answer

So the people would turn to it, and see that it could not speak or act.

Q2 What does this teach Muslims about shirk?

Show answer

Worshipping anything besides Allah is worshipping what cannot help or harm: 21:66.

Mission 1

Hajra, Ismail and the valley

  • Ibrahim leaves Hajra (Hagar) and the baby Ismail "in a valley without cultivation, by Thy Sacred House" (Surah 14:37, Yusuf Ali), trusting Allah to provide.
  • A hadith tells how Hajra ran seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwah looking for water, until the spring of Zamzam appeared (Sahih al-Bukhari 3364, paraphrase).
  • Ismail grows up in that valley; a settlement grows round the water.
  • Ibrahim prays: "Praise be to Allah, Who hath granted unto me in old age Isma'il and Isaac" (Surah 14:39, Yusuf Ali).
  • Hajra shows tawakkul (trust in Allah) and effort together: she trusts, and she also runs.
Mission 1Read the source

Raising the Ka'ba

> "And remember Abraham and Isma'il raised the foundations of the House (With this prayer): 'Our Lord! Accept (this service) from us: For Thou art the All-Hearing, the All-knowing.'" (Surah 2:127, Yusuf Ali)

  • 2:128: they pray to be "Muslims, bowing to Thy (Will)", with descendants who submit too.
  • 2:129: they pray for "a Messenger of their own" to teach scripture and wisdom. Muslims believe this was answered in Muhammad.

Q1 Who built the Ka'ba, according to 2:127?

Show answer

Ibrahim and Ismail together.

Q2 What do father and son ask for themselves?

Show answer

That Allah accept their work and make them Muslims, people who submit.

Mission 1

The command to sacrifice

  • Ibrahim sees in a vision that he must sacrifice his son. He tells him: "Now see what is thy view!"
  • The son replies: "O my father! Do as thou art commanded" (Surah 37:102, Yusuf Ali).
  • "So when they had both submitted their wills (to Allah)" (37:103), Allah calls out: the vision is fulfilled.
  • "We ransomed him with a momentous sacrifice" (Surah 37:107, Yusuf Ali); tradition says a ram.
  • "This was obviously a trial" (37:106): Muslims understand that Allah was testing Ibrahim's submission, not seeking the son's death.
  • Characteristics (37:109–111): "Peace and salutation to Abraham!" He "do[es] right" and is "one of our believing Servants".

?According to Surah 37:102, how did Ibrahim's son respond when told of the vision?
Mission 1Diversity

Which son? The Qur'an and the Tawrat

  • The Qur'an does not name the son in 37:102.
  • Most Muslims say Ismail: Ishaq's birth is announced only after the story (37:112), and the rites at Makkah recall Ismail. Ibn Kathir (d. 1373) argued for this.
  • Some early scholars said Ishaq; al-Tabari (d. 923) held this view.
  • The Tawrat (Genesis 22): "take your son, your only son, Isaac" (Genesis 22:2, WEBBE). Isaac is not told; he asks, "where is the lamb for a burnt offering?" (Genesis 22:7, WEBBE). A ram caught in a thicket is offered instead (22:13). Jews call this the Akedah (binding).
  • In Genesis, Hagar and Ishmael are sent away and God promises Ishmael "a great nation" (Genesis 21:18, WEBBE).
  • Muslims believe the Tawrat was revealed to Musa, but many hold that its text changed over time; the Qur'an confirms and "guard[s]" earlier scripture (5:48).
Mission 1

Ismail, a prophet in his own right

  • "He was (strictly) true to what he promised, and he was a messenger (and) a prophet." (Surah 19:54, Yusuf Ali)
  • He "used to enjoin on his people Prayer and Charity" (19:55).
  • He is named among those "of constancy and patience" (Surah 21:85, Yusuf Ali).
  • With his mother: he grew up with Hajra in the valley, the child for whom she searched for water.
  • With his father: he accepts the command to be sacrificed, then builds the Ka'ba with him.
  • He is listed with the prophets to whom revelation came (3:84; 4:163).
Mission 1

Their example in Muslim life today

  • Hajj: Muslims circle the Ka'ba the family built, pray near the Maqam Ibrahim (station of Ibrahim, 2:125), walk between Safa and Marwah (sa'i) and drink Zamzam.
  • At Mina, stoning the pillars recalls, by tradition, Ibrahim and Ismail rejecting Shaytan's temptation.
  • Id ul-Adha: an animal is sacrificed (qurbani) and the meat shared with the poor. "It is not their meat nor their blood, that reaches Allah: it is your piety that reaches Him" (Surah 22:37, Yusuf Ali).
  • Salah: at the end of each prayer Sunni Muslims ask Allah to bless Muhammad as He blessed Ibrahim (the Salat Ibrahimiyya).
  • Daily life: rejecting today's "idols" (money, status), trust in hard times, keeping promises (Ismail).
Mission 1Evaluate

"Ibrahim's willingness to sacrifice his son is the best example of submission for Muslims today."

  • For: the Qur'an praises both father and son for submitting (37:103–111). It was the hardest test. Muslims recall it every year at Id ul-Adha.
  • Against (Muslim): his stand against the idols (21:57) or Hajra's trust in the desert (14:37) are closer to tests Muslims face today. Ibrahim was tested in many ways (2:124).
  • Against (non-religious): many atheists and Humanists argue that a command to kill a child would be wrong whoever gave it; obedience should never override conscience.
  • Muslim response: it was a trial that Allah stopped (37:106–107); "Allah never commands what is shameful" (Surah 7:28, Yusuf Ali), so Muslims do not take it as permission to harm.
  • Judge it by: Which example can Muslims actually follow today? Does the story teach obedience, or trust? Is a test the same as a command?

One family's trust (a father who obeys, a mother who searches, a son who submits) is lived out again every year in Hajj, at Id ul-Adha and in daily life.

Make a guess: Why would the Qur'an praise a man who preached for centuries and convinced almost no one?

Mission 2

Nuh, one of the first messengers

  • Nuh (Noah) is one of the earliest messengers (rasul). In a hadith, people at the Last Day call him "the first of the messengers to the people of the earth" (Sahih al-Bukhari 4712, paraphrase).
  • He is one of the five great messengers with whom Allah made a "solemn covenant" (33:7): Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa and Muhammad.
  • His people worshipped idols, named in 71:23: Wadd, Suwa', Yaguth, Ya'uq and Nasr.
  • He preached among them "a thousand years less fifty" (Surah 29:14, Yusuf Ali).
  • Surah 71 is named after him (Surah Nuh).
Mission 2Read the source

Nuh's preaching (Surah 71)

> "He said: 'O my Lord! I have called to my People night and day: But my call only increases (their) flight (from the Right).'" (Surah 71:5–6, Yusuf Ali)

  • He called "aloud" (71:8), "in public and secretly in private" (71:9).
  • His message (71:10–12): ask forgiveness; Allah will send rain, wealth, children, gardens and rivers.
  • He points to creation as a sign: seven heavens, the moon as light, the sun as a lamp (71:15–16).

Q1 Name two ways Nuh tried to reach his people.

Show answer

Night and day; aloud; in public and in private.

Q2 What did he ask them to do first?

Show answer

Ask Allah's forgiveness and worship Allah alone: 71:3, 71:10.

Mission 2

His challenges

  • His people "thrust their fingers into their ears, covered themselves up with their garments" (Surah 71:7, Yusuf Ali).
  • The leaders mocked him as he built the Ark (11:38).
  • Only "a few believed with him" (11:40).
  • His own son refused to board: "I will betake myself to some mountain" (11:43). His wife is named as an example of disbelief (66:10).
  • Nuh still asks Allah for mercy for his son, then accepts Allah's answer (11:45–47).
  • After Allah revealed that no more would believe (11:36), Nuh prayed against the wrongdoers (71:26).

Mission 2

The Ark and the destruction of his people

  • Allah commands: "construct an Ark under Our eyes and Our inspiration" (Surah 11:37, Yusuf Ali).
  • On board: pairs of each kind, his family (except those who disbelieved) and the believers (11:40).
  • Nuh boards with the words "In the name of Allah, whether it move or be at rest!" (Surah 11:41, Yusuf Ali).
  • The waves rise "like mountains"; his son drowns (11:42–43).
  • The Ark rests on Mount Judi (11:44).
  • "Because of their sins they were drowned" (Surah 71:25, Yusuf Ali): the Qur'an presents the flood as judgement after long warning, and the Ark as "a Sign (for all time)" (54:15).
Mission 2

Thankfulness

  • "O ye that are sprung from those whom We carried (in the Ark) with Noah! Verily he was a devotee most grateful." (Surah 17:3, Yusuf Ali)
  • Pickthall translates it: "he was a grateful slave". The Arabic is 'abdan shakura.
  • He was thankful in every condition: through rejection, loss and rescue.
  • Significance today: shukr (gratitude) to Allah in good times and hard ones; sabr (patience) when others reject you; putting faith before even family ties (11:46).
  • Muslims see Nuh's long effort as encouragement for anyone whose good work seems to fail.
Mission 2

Dawud in the army of Talut

  • Dawud (David) is a messenger and a king.
  • The Children of Israel ask for a king; Allah appoints Talut (Saul) (2:246–247).
  • Talut tests the army at a stream: only those who barely drink may go on (2:249).
  • The few who remain face Jalut (Goliath) and his army.
  • Dawud kills Jalut: "By Allah's will they routed them; and David slew Goliath" (Surah 2:251, Yusuf Ali).
  • Allah then gives him "power and wisdom" (2:251): he becomes king, prophet and judge (38:26).
Mission 2Read the source

A small force, by Allah's will

Those "convinced that they must meet Allah" said:

> "How oft, by Allah's will, Hath a small force vanquished a big one? Allah is with those who steadfastly persevere." (Surah 2:249, Yusuf Ali)

> "Our Lord! Pour out constancy on us and make our steps firm: Help us against those that reject faith." (Surah 2:250, Yusuf Ali)

Q1 Why were the believers not afraid of Jalut's army?

Show answer

They trusted that victory comes "by Allah's will", not by numbers.

Q2 What did they ask Allah for before the battle?

Show answer

Constancy, firm steps and help.

Mission 2

Armour, devotion and the Zabur

  • Armour: "We made the iron soft for him" (34:10). He made coats of mail "to guard you from each other's violence" (21:80).
  • A hadith says Dawud ate only from what he earned with his own hands (Sahih al-Bukhari 2072, paraphrase).
  • Devotion: mountains and birds sang Allah's praises with him (34:10; 38:18–19). He "ever turned (to Allah)" (38:17).
  • A hadith calls his fasting (every other day) and his night prayer the most loved by Allah (Sahih al-Bukhari 1131, paraphrase).
  • The Zabur: "We gave to David (the gift of) the Psalms." (Surah 17:55, Yusuf Ali; also 4:163)
  • Muslims believe the Zabur was a revealed book; the Psalms in the Bible are linked with it, though Muslims hold the original revelation was not preserved unchanged.

Mission 2Evaluate

"Nuh's mission was a failure."

  • For: after some 950 years only "a few believed" (11:40). His son and wife were lost. His people were destroyed.
  • Against: a messenger's duty "is but to proclaim (the message)" (Surah 5:99, Yusuf Ali). Nuh did this faithfully, so most Muslims say he succeeded.
  • Against: the believers were saved, and Allah "made his progeny to endure" (37:77); the Qur'an praises him as "most grateful" (17:3).
  • Non-religious view: many atheists and Humanists read the flood as a myth, and question a God who destroys a whole people. Muslim response: the people had centuries of warning (71:5–9) and chose their path; the Qur'an tells the story as a sign and a warning (54:15).
  • Judge it by: What counts as success for a prophet: numbers or faithfulness? Does the story's purpose (a warning) change the answer?
Mission 2Worked answer

Belief, source, development

Question: Explain two ways the example of Dawud is important for Muslims today. In your answer you must refer to a source of wisdom and authority.

  • Way 1: Dawud trusted Allah rather than numbers. Source: the believers said, "How oft, by Allah's will, Hath a small force vanquished a big one?" (2:249). Development: Muslims facing something that looks impossible pray and persevere instead of giving up.
  • Way 2: Dawud combined work and worship. Development: he made armour with his own hands (34:11) and still fasted and prayed at night, so Muslims see honest work and regular worship as belonging together.
  • (c) tip: two ways, each developed, plus one source of wisdom and authority (here the Qur'an).
Mission check

?For three years Amira has run a stall in her town centre answering questions about Islam. Almost nobody has become Muslim, and a friend says she is wasting her time. Which reply best reflects what most Muslims learn from Nuh's example?

For Nuh and Dawud, faithfulness came first: one kept preaching when few listened, the other trusted Allah when his side was small.

Mission 3: How did a boy thrown down a well by his own brothers end up saving them from famine?

Make a guess: How did a boy thrown down a well by his own brothers end up saving them from famine?

Mission 3

Surah Yusuf

  • Surah 12 tells the whole life of Yusuf (Joseph) in one continuous story, unlike most prophets' stories, which are spread across many surahs.
  • The Qur'an calls it "the most beautiful of stories" (Surah 12:3, Yusuf Ali).
  • Yusuf is a prophet, son of the prophet Ya'qub (Jacob), grandson of Ishaq, great-grandson of Ibrahim (12:6).
  • A hadith calls him the most honourable of people: a prophet, son of a prophet, son of a prophet, son of Allah's friend Ibrahim (Sahih al-Bukhari 3383, paraphrase).
  • The Surah ends: "There is, in their stories, instruction for men endued with understanding." (Surah 12:111, Yusuf Ali)
Mission 3Read the source

The dream

> "Behold! Joseph said to his father: 'O my father! I did see eleven stars and the sun and the moon: I saw them prostrate themselves to me!'" (Surah 12:4, Yusuf Ali)

  • Ya'qub warns him: "relate not thy vision to thy brothers, lest they concoct a plot against thee" (Surah 12:5, Yusuf Ali).
  • He adds that Allah will choose Yusuf and teach him "the interpretation of stories (and events)" (12:6).

Q1 Who do the eleven stars, the sun and the moon turn out to be?

Show answer

His eleven brothers and his parents: 12:100.

Q2 Why does Ya'qub tell Yusuf to keep the dream secret?

Show answer

He fears the brothers' jealousy.

Mission 3

Betrayal

  • The brothers resent that Ya'qub loves Yusuf and his brother (Binyamin) more (12:8).
  • One says: do not kill him, "throw him down to the bottom of the well" (12:10).
  • They bring back his shirt, stained "with false blood" (12:18), and claim "the wolf devoured him" (12:17).
  • Ya'qub does not believe them: "(for me) patience is most fitting" (Surah 12:18, Yusuf Ali). This sabr jamil (beautiful patience) is a model for Muslims.
  • A caravan finds Yusuf; he is sold "for a few dirhams" (12:20).
Quick check

Mission 3

Life in Egypt: the test of integrity

  • An Egyptian official (al-Aziz) buys Yusuf and tells his wife to treat him honourably (12:21).
  • She tries to seduce him. He refuses: "Allah forbid! truly (thy husband) is my lord! he made my sojourn agreeable!" (Surah 12:23, Yusuf Ali)
  • His shirt, torn from behind, shows he was running away (12:25–28).
  • When pressure grows, he prays: "O my Lord! the prison is more to my liking than that to which they invite me" (Surah 12:33, Yusuf Ali).
  • He is imprisoned though innocent.
  • In prison he preaches tawhid: "are many lords differing among themselves better, or the One Allah, Supreme and Irresistible?" (Surah 12:39, Yusuf Ali)
Mission 3

From prison to the storehouses

  • Yusuf explains two prisoners' dreams; one is freed (12:36–42).
  • The king dreams of "seven fat kine, whom seven lean ones devour" and seven green and seven withered ears of corn (12:43).
  • No one can explain it, until the freed prisoner remembers Yusuf.
  • Yusuf's plan: sow for seven years, store the grain in the ear, then survive seven hard years (12:47–49).
  • He insists on clearing his name first (12:50–53), then asks: "Set me over the store-houses of the land" (Surah 12:55, Yusuf Ali).
  • So the betrayed slave becomes the man who feeds Egypt in famine.

Mission 3

Reunion

  • Famine drives the brothers to Egypt for grain. Yusuf knows them; they do not know him (12:58).
  • He keeps Binyamin with him by a plan with a cup (12:70–76).
  • Ya'qub, blind with grief, still says: "never give up hope of Allah's Soothing Mercy" (Surah 12:87, Yusuf Ali).
  • Yusuf reveals himself: "I am Joseph, and this is my brother" (12:90).
  • His shirt restores his father's sight (12:93–96).
  • The family come to Egypt and bow before him. Yusuf: "this is the fulfilment of my vision of old! Allah hath made it come true!" (Surah 12:100, Yusuf Ali)
Mission 3Read the source

No reproach

> "He said: 'This day let no reproach be (cast) on you: Allah will forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of those who show mercy!'" (Surah 12:92, Yusuf Ali)

  • Early biographies of the Prophet report that, when Makkah surrendered in 630 CE, Muhammad used Yusuf's words to forgive the Quraysh who had persecuted him.

Q1 Does Yusuf pretend the brothers did no wrong?

Show answer

No: he names what they did (12:89), then forgives.

Q2 Whom does he say will forgive them?

Show answer

Allah, "the Most Merciful".

Mission 3

Why Yusuf matters to Muslims today

  • Sabr (patience): Yusuf and Ya'qub endure years of loss without despair (12:18, 12:87).
  • Integrity: he chooses prison over wrongdoing (12:33): a model for resisting temptation and pressure.
  • Trust in Allah's plan (qadar): "my Lord understandeth best the mysteries of all that He planneth" (12:100).
  • Forgiveness: no revenge on those who betrayed him (12:92).
  • Serving society: he uses his skills to plan for famine in a land not his own (12:55).
  • Dreams: Muslims believe a true dream can come from Allah; a hadith calls a good dream a small part of prophecy (Sahih al-Bukhari 6989, paraphrase).
Mission 3Diversity

Reading the story

  • 12:24 says she desired him, "and he would have desired her, but that he saw the evidence of his Lord".
  • Some commentators read this as a passing human inclination that Yusuf mastered; others, stressing that prophets are protected from sin ('isma), read it as: he would have, had he not seen the sign, so he did not.
  • Sufi poets turned the story into a picture of divine beauty and love; the wife of al-Aziz is called Zulaikha in later tradition (e.g. Jami's poem Yusuf and Zulaikha, 1483), a name not in the Qur'an.
  • Sunni and Shi'a Muslims tell the story the same way in its main events, and both stress Yusuf's purity.
  • The Torah also tells the story (Genesis 37–50), with differences of detail; the Qur'an calls its version "a confirmation of what went before it" (12:111).
Mission 3Evaluate

"Forgiveness is the most important lesson of Yusuf's life for Muslims today."

  • For: 12:92 forgives the worst betrayal; early biographies report that the Prophet repeated these words at Makkah; the Qur'an praises one who "forgives and makes reconciliation" (Surah 42:40, Yusuf Ali).
  • Against: patience runs through the whole Surah, for Ya'qub as well as Yusuf (12:18, 12:83). Integrity under pressure (12:33) is a test young Muslims face daily. Trust in Allah's plan (12:100) is what holds the story together.
  • Non-religious view: many Humanists also value forgiveness, but argue it should never mean excusing serious harm or abandoning justice. Muslim response: the Qur'an agrees that justice is allowed (42:40); forgiveness is praised as the higher choice, not demanded of victims.
  • Judge it by: Which lesson appears most often in Surah 12? Which can Muslims live out most often? Can the lessons be separated?

Mission 4

Maryam: birth and childhood

  • Maryam (Mary) is the only woman named in the Qur'an; Surah 19 bears her name.
  • Her mother, "a woman of 'Imran", dedicates her unborn child to Allah's service (3:35).
  • A girl is born: "I have named her Mary" (Surah 3:36, Yusuf Ali). Her mother asks Allah to protect her and her offspring from Shaytan.
  • The prophet Zakariyya becomes her guardian. He finds food in her room and asks where it came from: "From Allah" (3:37).
  • The angels say: "Allah hath chosen thee and purified thee- chosen thee above the women of all nations." (Surah 3:42, Yusuf Ali)
  • A hadith names Maryam among the best women of the world (Sahih al-Bukhari 3432, paraphrase).
Mission 4Read the source

The annunciation

> "He said: 'Nay, I am only a messenger from thy Lord, (to announce) to thee the gift of a holy son.' She said: 'How shall I have a son, seeing that no man has touched me, and I am not unchaste?'" (Surah 19:19–20, Yusuf Ali)

  • In the Arabic of 19:17 Allah calls the messenger "Our spirit" (Yusuf Ali: "our angel"); Muslims identify him as the angel Jibril. He appears "as a man in all respects".
  • The reply: "that is easy for Me" (19:21); Allah says "Be", and it is (3:47).

Q1 What is Maryam's first reaction to the visitor?

Show answer

Fear: she seeks refuge in Allah, 19:18.

Q2 What makes this birth different?

Show answer

No father: Isa is created by Allah's command.

Mission 4

The birth of Isa

  • Maryam withdraws to a remote place; birth pains drive her to a palm tree (19:22–23).
  • A voice tells her not to grieve: a stream below her, ripe dates to shake down (19:24–25).
  • Her people accuse her of wrongdoing. She points to the baby (19:27–29).
  • The baby speaks: "I am indeed a servant of Allah: He hath given me revelation and made me a prophet" (Surah 19:30, Yusuf Ali).
  • "It is not befitting to (the majesty of) Allah that He should beget a son." (Surah 19:35, Yusuf Ali)
  • Isa's creation is compared with Adam's: from dust, by the word "Be" (3:59).
Quick check

Mission 4Evaluate

"Jibril's visit to Maryam was not a real event and proves nothing about Allah."

  • Non-religious argument 1: many atheists and Humanists say visions can be explained by the mind (stress, expectation, dreams).
  • Non-religious argument 2: no one else saw this one.
  • Non-religious argument 3: a private experience, even if real, cannot prove God to anyone else.
  • Muslim response 1: Muslims know of the event through the Qur'an, which they accept as Allah's word; its truth rests on revelation, not on the vision proving itself.
  • Muslim response 2: Muslims agree that faith does not rest on one vision; the Qur'an points to many signs, such as creation (71:15–16).
  • Muslim response 3: if Allah exists, such an event is "easy" (19:21): the question is really whether Allah exists.
  • Non-religious reply: these responses assume the Qur'an's account is reliable, which is the point in question.
  • Judge it by: What would count as evidence? Is the claim about the vision, or about the Qur'an? Can private experience ever be evidence for others?
Mission 4

Isa's mission

  • Isa (Jesus) is a messenger to the Children of Israel (3:49) and the Messiah (al-Masih, 3:45).
  • Allah gave him the Injil (Gospel): "therein was guidance and light, and confirmation of the Law" (Surah 5:46, Yusuf Ali).
  • His preaching: worship Allah alone: "It is Allah Who is my Lord and your Lord; then worship Him." (Surah 3:51, Yusuf Ali)
  • He confirmed the Tawrat and made lawful some things once forbidden (3:50).
  • His disciples (hawariyyun) said: "We are Allah's helpers" (3:52).
  • The Qur'an calls him a "Word" from Allah and "a spirit proceeding from Him" (4:171); Muslims understand both as created, never divine.
Mission 4

Miracles and knowledge, by Allah's leave

  • In 3:49 Isa says he makes a bird of clay and breathes into it, "and it becomes a bird by Allah's leave".
  • He heals "those born blind, and the lepers", and raises the dead, "by Allah's leave" (3:49; 5:110).
  • Knowledge: he can tell people "what ye eat, and what ye store in your houses" (3:49). Allah taught him "the Book and Wisdom, the Law and the Gospel" (3:48).
  • He prays for a table of food from heaven for his disciples (5:112–115).
  • The repeated phrase "by Allah's leave" shows the power is Allah's, given to a messenger.
  • He denies telling anyone to worship him or his mother (5:116–117).

Show answer

That Isa was killed; that he was crucified.

Q2 What happened instead, according to 4:158?

Show answer

Mission 4Diversity

Views of Isa's death

  • Most Sunni and Shi'a Muslims: Isa was not killed or crucified; Allah raised him bodily, alive; hadith teach he will return before the Last Day as a just ruler (Sahih al-Bukhari 3448, paraphrase); other reports add that he will then die a natural death.
  • Many commentators explain "made to appear" (4:157) as someone else made to look like Isa and crucified in his place; others say only that the people were mistaken.
  • Some modern scholars read "I will take thee and raise thee to Myself" (3:55) as a natural death later, with "raised" meaning honoured (e.g. Mahmud Shaltut, Egypt, 1940s).
  • Ahmadiyya Muslims teach that Isa survived the cross and died naturally later; most Muslims reject Ahmadi teaching.
  • Christians believe Jesus was crucified, died and rose again: the central contrast with Islam.
Mission 4

Foretelling Muhammad

> "...giving Glad Tidings of a Messenger to come after me, whose name shall be Ahmad." (Surah 61:6, Yusuf Ali)

  • Ahmad comes from the same root as Muhammad: "the Praised One" (Pickthall's rendering of 61:6).
  • So Muslims see Isa as a link in one chain of messengers, ending with Muhammad.
  • Some Muslims connect this with Jesus' promise of "another Counsellor" (John 14:16, WEBBE). Christians identify the Counsellor as "the Holy Spirit" (John 14:26, WEBBE).
Mission 4

Their example for Muslims today

  • Maryam: devotion in worship (3:43), purity, and trust in Allah when wrongly accused; her pain in childbirth (19:23) is honoured, not hidden.
  • Isa: a messenger of mercy (19:21), humble ("not overbearing", 19:32), kind to his mother, and confident that power belongs to Allah.
  • Muslims say "peace be upon him" after Isa's name and honour him, but worship Allah alone (tawhid).
  • Belief in his return gives hope of final justice.
  • Shared respect for Maryam and Isa is a starting point for Muslim–Christian dialogue, alongside honest difference about the cross and the Trinity.

Mission 5

From private to public

  • 610 CE: the first revelation comes to Muhammad on Mount Hira: "Proclaim! (or read!)" (96:1).
  • At first he shares the message privately: Khadijah (his wife), Ali (his young cousin), Abu Bakr (his friend), Zayd. Tradition says this lasted about three years.
  • Then the command comes to go public: "Therefore expound openly what thou art commanded" (Surah 15:94, Yusuf Ali).
  • Surah 26:214 names where to start: his own nearest kinsmen.
  • Muslims see Muhammad as the final messenger (Khatam an-Nabiyyin, seal of the prophets, 33:40), so his preaching is the model for all inviting to Islam.
Mission 5Read the source

The command (26:213–217)

> "So call not on any other god with Allah, or thou wilt be among those under the Penalty. And admonish thy nearest kinsmen, And lower thy wing to the Believers who follow thee. Then if they disobey thee, say: 'I am free (of responsibility) for what ye do!' And put thy trust on the Exalted in Might, the Merciful,-" (Surah 26:213–217, Yusuf Ali)

Q1 What is the core of the message in 26:213?

Show answer

Tawhid: worship no god but Allah.

Q2 What must Muhammad do if people refuse?

Show answer

Declare himself free of responsibility for what they do, and trust in Allah, not force them.

Mission 5

On Mount Safa

  • After 26:214, Muhammad climbs the hill of Safa and calls each clan of Quraysh by name (Sahih al-Bukhari 4770, paraphrase).
  • He asks: if I said an army was behind this hill, would you believe me? They say yes: they have only known him as truthful (al-Amin, "the trustworthy").
  • He says: then I am a warner to you of a severe punishment ahead.
  • His uncle Abu Lahab curses him; Surah 111 is revealed naming Abu Lahab.
  • Reports in early histories (e.g. al-Tabari) describe a meal for his clan, Banu Hashim, where only the young Ali offered support. Shi'a Muslims see this as an early sign of Ali's special role; Sunni scholars discuss how reliable some versions are.

Mission 5

What the verses teach about preaching

  • Start close to home (26:214): those who know you best can judge your honesty.
  • Gentleness (26:215): "lower thy wing" is an image of a bird sheltering its young: humility and care for believers.
  • Freedom (26:216): the messenger delivers; people choose. "Let there be no compulsion in religion" (Surah 2:256, Yusuf Ali).
  • Method: "Invite (all) to the Way of thy Lord with wisdom and beautiful preaching" (Surah 16:125, Yusuf Ali).
  • Trust (26:217): results belong to Allah. "Thou art not one to manage (men's) affairs" (88:22).
Mission 5

Da'wah: the invitation

  • Da'wah means "call" or "invitation": inviting people to Islam, and reminding Muslims of their faith.
  • 3:104: "Let there arise out of you a band of people inviting to all that is good" (Surah 3:104, Yusuf Ali).
  • A hadith: "Convey from me, even if it is one verse" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3461, paraphrase).
  • Divergent views on who must do it: many scholars call da'wah a communal duty (fard kifayah: some must do it for all); others stress that every Muslim must share what they know.
  • Significance today: Muslims follow the Prophet's example (33:21) by inviting with honesty, gentleness and patience.
Mission 5Diversity

Different ways Muslims answer the command

  • By example: many Muslims believe the best invitation is good character (akhlaq): honesty at work, kindness to neighbours. They rarely speak of conversion.
  • Open invitation: street stalls, public talks and debates, free copies of the Qur'an, online videos.
  • Dialogue: mosque open days and interfaith meetings to explain, not persuade; "To you be your Way, and to me mine" (Surah 109:6, Yusuf Ali).
  • Calling Muslims back: some movements (e.g. Tablighi Jamaat) focus da'wah on Muslims who have stopped practising.
  • Limits most share: no pressure, no insulting other faiths (6:108), special care with the young and vulnerable.

Mission 5

"Trying to convert people is wrong"

  • Some atheists and Humanists argue that trying to convert people in today's society is wrong:
  • Belief is private: religion should be a personal matter, not pressed on others.
  • Autonomy: people should reach beliefs by their own reasoning, without being targeted.
  • Vulnerability: converts may be lonely, grieving, young or in prison, and easy to pressure.
  • Social harm: in a diverse society, efforts to convert can seem to say others are wrong, and cause division.
  • Many Humanists also defend free speech, including sharing beliefs; their objection is mainly to pressure and to targeting the vulnerable.
Mission 5Evaluate

"It is wrong to try to convert people in today's society."

  • For (non-religious): the four arguments on the earlier screen: privacy, autonomy, vulnerability, division.
  • Muslim response 1: Islam forbids compulsion (2:256); da'wah is an invitation that people are free to refuse (26:216).
  • Muslim response 2: sharing what you believe is true and good is care, not disrespect; a hadith says none truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself (Sahih al-Bukhari 13, paraphrase).
  • Muslim response 3: freedom of religion in law, e.g. the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (1948, Article 18), includes freedom to teach and to change one's belief.
  • Muslim response 4: many Muslims agree that pressure on the vulnerable is wrong, and see it as against 16:125.
  • Judge it by: Is there a difference between inviting and pressuring? Does free speech include religious persuasion? Who decides what counts as pressure?
Mission 5Worked answer

Building a (d) paragraph

Statement: "Muslims should not try to convert others in modern Britain."

  • Muslim view + teaching: Muslims follow 26:214, where Allah commands Muhammad to warn his kinsmen, so sharing Islam is obedience, not arrogance.
  • Develop: but 26:216 and 2:256 show the invitation must stay free, so most Muslims reject pressure.
  • Non-religious view: a Humanist might argue that targeting the vulnerable disrespects autonomy.
  • Weigh it: both sides oppose pressure; they differ on whether inviting at all is respectful.
  • (d) tip: Muslim teachings, a non-religious view where the question asks, and a justified conclusion.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Ibrahim, friend of Allah, rejected idols and preached tawhid; with Ismail he raised the Ka'ba (2:127–129).
  • Father and son submitted to the command to sacrifice (37:102–111); the Qur'an does not name the son (most Muslims: Ismail; Genesis 22: Isaac).
  • Their example lives on in Hajj, Id ul-Adha, Sunni Salah and daily trust in Allah.

Mission 2

  • Nuh preached night and day for centuries (Surah 71); few believed; the Ark saved the believers and the flood destroyed the rest (11:37–44); he was "most grateful" (17:3).
  • Dawud fought in Talut's army and killed Jalut (2:249–251), made armour (34:10–11), was devoted in prayer and fasting, and received the Zabur (17:55).
  • Muslims take from both: patience, gratitude, trust in Allah over numbers, and work joined with worship.

Mission 3

  • Surah 12 tells Yusuf's life in one story: the dream, betrayal by his brothers, slavery, temptation and prison, the king's dream, rule over the storehouses and reunion.
  • Ya'qub and Yusuf model sabr (patience); Yusuf models integrity, trust in Allah's plan and forgiveness (12:92).
  • Muslims read details differently (e.g. 12:24), but all see Yusuf as a pure prophet and a model for today.

Mission 4

  • Maryam was dedicated before birth, raised under Zakariyya's care, chosen and purified (3:35–42); Jibril announced a son without a father (19:16–21).
  • Isa spoke from the cradle, received the Injil, taught tawhid, had disciples and worked miracles "by Allah's leave" (3:49).
  • Most Muslims believe he was not crucified but raised alive (4:157–158) and will return; he foretold Ahmad (61:6).

Mission 5

  • Muhammad first preached privately; 26:213–217 and 15:94 commanded him to warn his kin openly, as he did from Mount Safa.
  • The verses teach tawhid, gentleness, freedom and trust in Allah; da'wah today takes many forms: example, open invitation, dialogue, reminding Muslims.
  • Some atheists and Humanists say converting others is wrong; Muslims reply that invitation without compulsion (2:256) respects freedom.
