Mission 1

Mission 1: Every point on a spinning wheel turns through the same angle. So why does the rim move fastest?

Make a guess: Every point on a spinning wheel turns through the same angle. So why does the rim move fastest?

Mission 1

Angular displacement

  • Angle in radians: θ = s/r (arc length ÷ radius).
  • 1 revolution = 2π rad = 360°.
  • Angular displacement Δθ = θ − θ0: the angle turned about an axis.
  • Choose one sense as positive, usually counter-clockwise; clockwise is then negative.
  • Rigid system: keeps its shape, but different points move in different directions.
  • If only the center of mass matters (e.g. Earth orbiting the Sun), treat the system as a single object.
Quick check
?A disk on a fixed axle rotates 1.0 revolution counter-clockwise and then 0.25 revolution clockwise. Counter-clockwise is taken as positive. What is the disk's angular displacement?
Mission 1

Angular velocity and angular acceleration

  • Angular velocity ωavg = Δθ/Δt, in rad/s.
  • Angular acceleration αavg = Δω/Δt, in rad/s².
  • Each has a linear twin: θ ↔ x, ω ↔ v, α ↔ a.
  • Graphs work the same way: slope of θ–t = ω; slope of ω–t = α; area under ω–t = Δθ; area under α–t = Δω.
Mission 1

Constant angular acceleration

  • Same equations as for constant linear acceleration, with angular symbols:
  • ω = ω0 + αt
  • θ = θ0 + ω0t + ½αt²
  • ω² = ω0² + 2α(θ − θ0)
  • Example: a wheel speeds up from 5.0 to 25 rad/s in 4.0 s.
  • α = (25 − 5.0) ÷ 4.0 = 5.0 rad/s².
  • Δθ = average ω × t = 15 × 4.0 = 60 rad (about 9.5 revolutions).
Quick check
?Two identical fans, X and Y, start from rest. Each speeds up with constant angular acceleration until it reaches the same final angular velocity. The angular acceleration of fan X is twice that of fan Y. What is the ratio ΔθX/ΔθY of the angles through which the fans turn while speeding up?
Mission 1

Linking angular and linear

  • In a rigid body, every point has the same ω and α.
  • A point at distance r from the axis:
  • arc length Δs = rΔθ
  • speed v = rω
  • tangential acceleration aT = rα
  • So linear quantities grow in proportion to r. The rim moves fastest.
  • θ must be in radians for these to work.
Quick check
?A light string is wrapped around a pulley of radius 0.0600 m that turns on a fixed axle. The string is pulled so that it unwinds without slipping while the pulley turns through 20.0 rad. What length of string unwinds from the pulley?
Mission check

?A turntable speeds up with constant angular acceleration. A student claims that a dot near the edge of the turntable has a greater angular acceleration than a dot near the center, because the edge dot's speed increases by more each second. Which statement correctly evaluates the claim?

Same angle, same ω, same α for every point. But a point at radius r covers arc rΔθ, so its speed is v = rω: the rim is fastest.

Mission 2

Torque

  • Torque τ: the turning effect of a force about an axis. Unit N m.
  • Only the force component perpendicular to the line from the axis (r) turns the object: F⊥ = F sin θ.
  • τ = rF sin θ
  • θ = angle between F and r (r from the axis to where the force acts).
  • θ = 90°: greatest torque. θ = 0° or 180°: line of action passes through the axis, no torque.
  • Torque has a sense: clockwise or counter-clockwise.
Quick check
?A wheel turns on a fixed axle through its center. Four forces, each of 20 N and each in the plane of the wheel, act on it one at a time. Which force exerts no torque about the axle?
Mission 2

The lever arm

  • Two ways to get the same torque:
  • r × (F sin θ): distance × perpendicular component of the force.
  • F × (r sin θ): force × lever arm.
  • Lever arm r⊥ = perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of action of the force.
  • Pick whichever is easier from the diagram.
Mission 2

Worked example

  • A wrench: force 60 N at the end, 0.25 m from the bolt, at 50° to the handle.
  • τ = rF sin θ = 0.25 × 60 × sin 50°
  • τ = 15 × 0.766 = 11 N·m
  • Check with the lever arm: r⊥ = 0.25 × sin 50° = 0.19 m; 60 × 0.19 = 11 N·m.
  • Trap: using cos 50° gives 9.6 N·m. Wrong angle.
Quick check
?A rod of length 0.60 m can rotate about an axle through one end. A string is attached to the rod 0.40 m from the axle and pulls on it with a force of 50 N directed at 37° to the rod. What is the magnitude of the torque exerted on the rod by the string's force about the axle? Use sin 37° = 0.60 and cos 37° = 0.80.
Quick check
?A uniform rod is held horizontal by a hinge at its left end and a vertical string attached to its right end. A student is drawing a force diagram to analyze the torques exerted on the rod about the hinge. Which instruction for drawing the diagram is correct?
Mission 2

Force diagrams

  • A force diagram is like a free-body diagram, but drawn on the extended object.
  • Start each arrow where that force is exerted.
  • Arrow lengths show relative sizes; mark the axis.
  • Include every force on the object, even one at the axis (it has zero torque about that axis, but it still acts).
Mission check

?The diagram shows four forces, A, B, C and D, exerted on a rod that can rotate about a fixed axis through point P, perpendicular to the page. The arrows are drawn to scale and start where each force is exerted. Which force exerts the torque of greatest magnitude about P?

Torque = force × lever arm. At the far edge of a door, the lever arm is largest, so the same torque needs the smallest push.

Make a guess: A hoop and a disk have the same mass and radius. Which is harder to start spinning, and why?

Mission 3

Resistance to turning

  • Rotational inertia I: how strongly an object resists a change in its rotation.
  • The rotational twin of mass. Unit kg m².
  • Depends on the mass and how far that mass is from the axis.
  • Depends on the axis chosen. Does not depend on how fast it spins.
Quick check
?A bicycle wheel can rotate about its axle. Which change would increase the wheel's rotational inertia about the axle?
Mission 3

Point masses

  • One small mass at distance r from the axis: I = mr².
  • Several masses: add them. I = Σmr².
  • r = perpendicular distance from the axis.
  • Example: light rod, axis at one end. 0.40 kg at 0.20 m, 0.30 kg at 0.50 m, 0.20 kg at 0.80 m.
  • I = 0.40 × 0.20² + 0.30 × 0.50² + 0.20 × 0.80²
  • I = 0.016 + 0.075 + 0.128 = 0.22 kg·m²
  • The lightest mass gives the biggest share: r is squared.
Quick check
?A small object of mass m is fastened to a light rod a distance r from the rod's axis of rotation. It is replaced by a small object of mass m/3 fastened a distance 3r from the same axis. The new rotational inertia of the system about the axis is how many times the original rotational inertia?
Mission 3

Shape matters

  • Same mass M, same radius R, axis through the center:
  • Hoop: all mass at R → I = MR².
  • Disk: much of the mass near the axis → I = ½MR².
  • Rod (length L): about its center ML²/12; about one end ML²/3.
  • Mass farther out → larger I → harder to start or stop spinning.
  • Formulas for extended objects are given in the exam; know the reasons qualitatively.
Quick check
?The diagram shows three objects, each of which can rotate about a fixed axis through its center, perpendicular to the page. Each object's type and mass are labeled. Which ranking of their rotational inertias about these axes, I1, I2 and I3, is correct?
Mission 3

Least about the center of mass

  • For axes in a given direction, I is smallest when the axis passes through the center of mass.
  • Move the axis away, and on average the mass is farther from it.
  • Example: 2m and m at the ends of a light rod of length L. Center of mass is L/3 from 2m.
  • About the center of mass: I = 2m(L/3)² + m(2L/3)² = 0.67mL².
  • About the midpoint: I = 2m(L/2)² + m(L/2)² = 0.75mL². Larger.
Mission 3

Parallel-axis theorem

  • I′ = Icm + Md²
  • Icm: about an axis through the center of mass. d: distance to a parallel axis.
  • Md² is never negative, so Icm is the minimum.
  • Example: disk, M = 2.0 kg, R = 0.30 m. Icm = ½MR² = 0.090 kg·m².
  • Axis through a point on the rim: I′ = 0.090 + 2.0 × 0.30² = 0.27 kg·m² (3 times as much).
Quick check
?A uniform rod of mass 2.0 kg and length 1.2 m has a rotational inertia of 0.24 kg·m² about an axis through its center, perpendicular to the rod. What is its rotational inertia about a parallel axis through one end of the rod?
Quick check
?A light rod of length L has a small object of mass 3m fastened at its left end and a small object of mass m fastened at its right end. The system can be made to rotate about any one of the axes A, B or C shown in the diagram, each perpendicular to the page. About which axis is the rotational inertia of the system least, and why?
Mission check

?Two thin rods, X and Y, have the same mass and length. In rod X most of the mass is concentrated equally near the two ends; in rod Y most of the mass is concentrated near the middle. Each rod can rotate about an axis through its midpoint, perpendicular to the rod. Which claim about the rods' rotational inertias about these axes is correct?

I = Σmr² weights mass by distance squared. A hoop puts all its mass at R (I = MR²); a disk keeps much of it near the axis (½MR²). The hoop is twice as hard to spin up.

Mission 4

Mission 4: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Make a guess: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Mission 4

Rotational equilibrium on a see-saw

  • Add torques with signs: counter-clockwise +, clockwise − (or the reverse).
  • Rotational equilibrium: the net torque about any axis is zero: Στ = 0.
  • Full equilibrium also needs ΣF = 0. Then the torques balance about any point.
  • See-saw: child of weight W at 3d balances an adult of weight 3W at d. W × 3d = 3W × d.
  • The pivot pushes up with 4W, so the forces balance too.
Mission 4

Worked example

  • Uniform plank, 3.0 m long, weight 120 N, rests on a pivot 1.0 m from end A.
  • Its weight acts at its center of mass, the midpoint: 1.5 m from A, so 0.50 m beyond the pivot.
  • Torque of the plank's weight about the pivot = 120 × 0.50 = 60 N·m.
  • Weight W hung at A balances it: W × 1.0 = 60, so W = 60 N.
  • Net force also zero: pivot pushes up with 120 + 60 = 180 N.
  • Tip: take torques about the point where an unknown force acts. Its torque there is zero.
Quick check
?A uniform board of mass 3.0 kg and length 2.0 m rests horizontally on a pivot 0.60 m from its left end. What mass must be placed at the left end of the board for it to remain balanced?
Mission 4

Balanced does not mean still

  • Zero net torque means no angular acceleration.
  • The object is at rest, or turns at constant angular velocity.
  • A turntable turning steadily: the motor's torque balances the friction torque at the bearing.
  • Rotational and translational equilibrium are independent:
  • Two equal, opposite forces at opposite ends of a rod: net force zero, net torque not zero. It starts to spin.
  • Two equal forces in the same direction at the ends: net torque zero, net force not zero. It accelerates without turning.
Quick check
?An electric fan is switched on, and after a few seconds its blades turn at a constant angular velocity. Which statement about the torques exerted on the blades while they turn at this constant angular velocity is correct?
Mission check

?A uniform board of weight W rests horizontally on two supports: support P at its left end and support Q one-quarter of the board's length from its right end. The diagram shows a student's force diagram for the board: FP and FQ are the forces exerted on the board by the supports, and W is drawn at the board's center. Which statement about the student's diagram is correct?

The support farther from the board's midpoint pushes up with the smaller force. On the see-saw, the child sits three times as far from the pivot: W × 3d balances 3W × d.

Mission 5

Mission 5: A bucket falls down a well, unwinding rope from a heavy drum. Why does it fall with less than g?

Make a guess: A bucket falls down a well, unwinding rope from a heavy drum. Why does it fall with less than g?

Quick check
?A rigid wheel turns on a fixed axle. The net torque exerted on the wheel is not zero. Which statement about the wheel must be true?
Mission 5

Unbalanced torque

  • Net torque zero → angular velocity constant (perhaps zero).
  • Net torque not zero → angular velocity changes.
  • Torque in the same sense as the rotation: speeds up.
  • Torque opposite to the rotation: slows down (and may reverse).
  • No torque is needed to keep something spinning, only to change its spin.
Mission 5

Newton's second law for rotation

  • α = τnet / I
  • Twin of F = ma: torque ↔ force, I ↔ mass, α ↔ a.
  • α is in the same sense as the net torque.
  • Same torque, double I → half the angular acceleration.
Mission 5

Worked example

  • A flywheel, I = 0.80 kg·m², starts from rest.
  • A rope pulls tangentially on its rim: 30 N at r = 0.20 m. Friction torque at the axle: 1.2 N·m.
  • Driving torque = 30 × 0.20 = 6.0 N·m.
  • Net torque = 6.0 − 1.2 = 4.8 N·m.
  • α = 4.8 ÷ 0.80 = 6.0 rad/s².
  • After 3.0 s: ω = 0 + 6.0 × 3.0 = 18 rad/s.
Quick check
?A solid disk of radius 0.50 m and rotational inertia 0.25 kg·m² turns on a fixed axle. A string wrapped around its rim is pulled with a constant force of 2.0 N, tangent to the rim, in the sense in which the disk is turning. Friction at the axle exerts a constant torque of 0.25 N·m that opposes the rotation. What is the magnitude of the disk's angular acceleration?
Mission 5

Linear and rotational together

  • Bucket (mass m) on a rope wound around a drum that turns on a frictionless axle (I, radius R).
  • Bucket, linear: mg − T = ma
  • Drum, rotational: TR = Iα
  • Rope does not slip: a = Rα
  • Solve: a = mg / (m + I/R²). Less than g.
  • The rope must pull up on the bucket (T > 0) to spin the drum up.
Mission 5

Worked numbers

  • m = 2.0 kg, drum I = 0.090 kg·m², R = 0.15 m. So I/R² = 4.0 kg.
  • a = 2.0 × 10 ÷ (2.0 + 4.0) = 3.3 m/s²
  • T = m(g − a) = 2.0 × (10 − 3.33) = 13 N
  • T < mg: the bucket accelerates down. T > 0: the drum spins up.
Quick check
?Two identical uniform pucks rest on a horizontal surface with negligible friction. A string is tied to a peg at the center of puck 1, and a string is wrapped many times around the rim of puck 2. Each string is pulled horizontally with the same constant force F; puck 2's string unwinds as puck 2 spins. How do the accelerations of the pucks' centers of mass compare while the strings are pulled?
Mission check

?A block on a level, frictionless table is pulled by a string that passes over a pulley at the table's edge to a hanging mass. The light pulley is then replaced by a uniform solid pulley of the same radius but large mass. The string does not slip on the pulley. The system is again released from rest. Which statement is correct?

The drum resists spinning up (τ = Iα), so the rope must pull up on the bucket. Net force mg − T < mg, so a < g.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Angles in radians; every point of a rigid body shares θ, ω and α.
  • Constant α: the constant-acceleration equations with θ, ω, α in place of x, v, a.
  • Δs = rΔθ, v = rω, aT = rα: linear quantities grow with distance from the axis.

Mission 2

  • τ = rF sin θ: only the perpendicular component turns; or force × lever arm.

Mission 3

  • Rotational inertia I = Σmr²: mass far from the axis counts most; the hoop (MR²) beats the disk (½MR²).
  • I is least for an axis through the center of mass.
  • Parallel-axis theorem: I′ = Icm + Md².

Mission 4

  • Rotational equilibrium: net torque = 0, so the object is at rest or turns at constant angular velocity; translational equilibrium (ΣF = 0) is a separate condition.

Mission 5

  • α = τnet/I: a nonzero net torque changes ω; zero net torque keeps ω constant.
  • Coupled systems: write F = ma for each mass, τ = Iα for each wheel, and link them with a = Rα.
