Mission 1

Mission 1: Why do ammonia plants squeeze their gases to about 200 times atmospheric pressure?

Make a guess: Why do ammonia plants squeeze their gases to about 200 times atmospheric pressure?

Mission 1

Le Châtelier's principle

  • Disturb a system at equilibrium and the position shifts to oppose the change (partly).
  • Position of equilibrium: the composition of the equilibrium mixture.
  • Concentration and pressure change the position, not K.
  • Temperature changes the position and K.
  • A catalyst changes neither.
Mission 1

Changing a concentration

  • N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g).
  • Add N2: the position shifts right: more NH3.
  • Remove NH3: the position shifts right, replacing some of it.
  • The shift only partly uses up the added N2.
  • K is unchanged.
  • Why: adding N2 makes the concentration ratio smaller than Kc. Net forward reaction raises it until it equals Kc again.
Quick check
?The following system is at equilibrium in a sealed container: H2(g) + I2(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). More hydrogen is injected, and the temperature and volume are kept constant. Which statement about the new equilibrium mixture, compared with the original one, is correct?
Mission 1

Changing the pressure

  • Raise the pressure by reducing the volume: the position shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas.
  • N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3: 4 mol of gas → 2 mol, so it shifts right.
  • Equal moles of gas on each side: no shift.
  • Only gases count. K is unchanged.
  • Why: squeezing changes the concentration ratio away from Kc; the mixture reacts until the ratio equals the same Kc.
Quick check
?CH4(g) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H2(g). The pressure is increased, by reducing the volume, at constant temperature. What happens?
Mission 1

Changing the temperature

  • Raise T: the position shifts in the endothermic direction.
  • Forward exothermic (ΔH < 0): heating shifts it left; K decreases.
  • Forward endothermic (ΔH > 0): heating shifts it right; K increases.
  • Temperature is the only change that alters K.
  • The same holds for Kp.
  • Order of events: the temperature change alters K first; the mixture then shifts until its ratio fits the new K.
Quick check
?The forward reaction in the following equilibrium is exothermic: 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g). The temperature of the equilibrium mixture is increased at constant pressure. How do the amount of SO3 and the value of K at the new equilibrium compare with those at the original equilibrium?
Mission 1

Catalysts, and the summary

  • A catalyst speeds up forward and backward reactions equally.
  • Equilibrium is reached sooner: same composition, same K.

Mission 1

Seeing a shift in the lab

  • Coloured equilibria show the shift directly.
  • 2CrO4²⁻(aq) (yellow) + 2H⁺(aq) ⇌ Cr2O7²⁻(aq) (orange) + H2O(l).
  • Add acid: more H⁺, shifts right: orange. Add alkali: OH⁻ removes H⁺, shifts left: yellow.
  • [Co(H2O)6]²⁺ (pink) + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl4]²⁻ (blue) + 6H2O. Forward endothermic.
  • Warm it: bluer. Cool it in ice: pinker. Add concentrated HCl: bluer.
  • Method: equal volumes in test tubes; change one factor; compare with an untouched control tube.
Quick check
?A test tube holds orange potassium dichromate(VI) solution: 2CrO4²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) ⇌ Cr2O7²⁻(aq) + H2O(l). Sodium hydroxide solution is added drop by drop. What is seen, and why?
Mission 1

The Haber process: a compromise

  • N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g), ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • Pressure about 200 atm: shifts right (4 mol gas → 2) and speeds it up. Higher still costs more in energy, plant and safety.
  • Temperature about 450 °C: lower T gives a higher yield (exothermic) but is too slow. 450 °C: lower yield, much faster.
  • Catalyst: iron. Faster; no change to the yield.
  • Only about 15% is converted per pass. NH3 is removed by cooling (it liquefies); unreacted N2 and H2 are recycled.
Quick check
?Percentage of NH3 in the equilibrium mixture at 200 atm (illustrative values): 350 °C, 57% · 450 °C, 36% · 550 °C, 22%. Why do ammonia plants run at about 450 °C rather than 350 °C?
Mission check

?Methanol is made from CO(g) + 2H2(g) ⇌ CH3OH(g). The forward reaction is exothermic. Plants run it at high pressure. Why does raising the pressure, by reducing the volume, increase the equilibrium yield of methanol?

High pressure shifts the position towards fewer moles of gas. Ammonia is the same story: N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 goes from 4 mol of gas to 2, so about 200 atm pushes the position towards ammonia. High pressure also speeds up the reaction, but that is a separate rate effect.

Even higher pressure would raise the yield further, but costs more than it earns: about 200 atm is a compromise.

Mission 2

Mission 2: A sealed flask looks unchanging. Can you tell which way it will react?

Make a guess: A sealed flask looks unchanging. Can you tell which way it will react?

Mission 2

Which way will it react?

  • Beyond your spec, but it answers the video: put the concentrations right now into the Kc expression. That ratio is called Q.
  • Q < K: too little product. Net forward reaction until Q = K.
  • Q > K: too much product. Net backward reaction.
  • Q = K: at equilibrium; no net change.
Mission 2

ICE tables (worked example)

A(g) + B(g) ⇌ 2C(g). Start: [A] = [B] = 0.50, no C. At equilibrium, [C] = 0.60.

Concentration[A][B][C]
Initial0.500.500
Change−0.30−0.30+0.60
Equilibrium0.200.200.60
  • All values in mol dm⁻³.
  • Changes follow the coefficients: 0.60 of C needs 0.30 each of A and B.
  • Kc = [C]² ÷ ([A][B]) = 0.36 ÷ 0.040 = 9.0.
Mission 2

Units of Kc

  • Put units into the expression and cancel.
  • N2O4 ⇌ 2NO2: Kc = [NO2]²/[N2O4] → (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ mol dm⁻³ = mol dm⁻³.
  • N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3: (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ (mol dm⁻³)⁴ = dm⁶ mol⁻².
  • A + B ⇌ 2C: (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ (mol dm⁻³)² → no units.
  • Shortcut check: power on top minus power below gives the power of mol dm⁻³.
Mission 2

Starting from moles (worked example)

  • PCl5(g) ⇌ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g).
  • 0.500 mol PCl5 is sealed in a 2.00 dm³ flask. At equilibrium it holds 0.200 mol Cl2.
  • Moles at equilibrium: Cl2 0.200, PCl3 0.200, PCl5 0.500 − 0.200 = 0.300.
  • Divide by the volume: [PCl5] = 0.150, [PCl3] = [Cl2] = 0.100.
  • Kc = (0.100 × 0.100) ÷ 0.150 = 0.0667 mol dm⁻³.
  • Two terms on top, one below: the volume does not cancel. Moles alone give 0.133, twice too big.
Quick check
?N2O4(g) ⇌ 2NO2(g). N2O4 is placed in a sealed vessel at an initial concentration of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ and the mixture is allowed to reach equilibrium at constant temperature. At equilibrium, [NO2] = 0.080 mol dm⁻³. What is the value of Kc at this temperature?
Mission 2

Finding equilibrium amounts in the lab

  • CH3COOH(l) + C2H5OH(l) ⇌ CH3COOC2H5(l) + H2O(l): one liquid phase, so all four go in Kc.
  • Mix known amounts with a little HCl(aq) catalyst. Stopper; keep at constant temperature for about a week.
  • Titrate with standard NaOH(aq). Subtract the titre for the HCl alone → moles of ethanoic acid at equilibrium.
  • Example result: start 0.100 mol acid, 0.100 mol ethanol, 0.050 mol water (from the catalyst solution). Equilibrium: 0.040 mol acid.
  • So 0.060 mol reacted: ethanol 0.040, ester 0.060, water 0.110 mol.
  • Kc = (0.060 × 0.110) ÷ (0.040 × 0.040) = 4.1. No units: the volume cancels.
  • Count the water added with the catalyst. Leaving it out gives 2.25: too low.
Quick check
?0.400 mol SO2 and 0.200 mol O2 are sealed in a 2.00 dm³ vessel. At equilibrium the vessel contains 0.300 mol SO3. What is Kc for 2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g)?
Mission 2

When K is very small

  • Background: not named in your spec, but a neat shortcut.
  • Very little reactant reacts, so [reactant] at equilibrium ≈ [reactant] at the start.
  • Then solve for the product directly. No quadratic needed.
  • Z2(g) ⇌ 2Z(g), Kc = 4.0 × 10⁻⁸ mol dm⁻³, [Z2] at the start = 0.10 mol dm⁻³.
  • [Z]² = K × [Z2] = 4.0 × 10⁻⁸ × 0.10 = 4.0 × 10⁻⁹.
  • So [Z] = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.
  • Check: only 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ of the 0.10 of Z2 reacted. The approximation holds.
Mission 2

K and Gibbs energy

  • The link: ΔG = −RT ln K (ΔG in J mol⁻¹, T in K, R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹).
  • ΔG negative ↔ K > 1: products favoured. ΔG positive ↔ K < 1.
  • K = 1000 at 298 K: ΔG = −8.31 × 298 × ln 1000 = −17.1 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • On your spec: use it to show that feasible reactions (negative ΔG) have large K, and vice versa.
  • K itself changes with temperature: down for exothermic, up for endothermic (see Mission 1).
Mission check

?Back to the sealed flask: H2(g) + I2(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). It was filled with 0.500 mol H2 and 0.500 mol I2 and no HI, in a 2.00 dm³ flask. It has now reached equilibrium and holds 0.800 mol HI. What is Kc at this temperature?

Kc = 64 here. So can you tell which way a flask will react? Yes: put its concentrations into the Kc expression. Bigger than Kc: net backward; smaller: net forward; equal: at equilibrium.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Deep underwater, a diver breathes ordinary air. Why can its oxygen become poisonous?

Make a guess: Deep underwater, a diver breathes ordinary air. Why can its oxygen become poisonous?

Mission 3

Mole fraction and partial pressure

  • Mole fraction of gas A: x(A) = moles of A ÷ total moles of gas.
  • Mole fractions add up to 1.
  • Partial pressure: the pressure one gas in a mixture would exert on its own in the same volume.
  • p(A) = x(A) × total pressure.
  • Partial pressures add up to the total pressure.
Mission 3

The diver

  • Air is about 21% O2 by moles: x(O2) ≈ 0.21.
  • About every 10 m of seawater adds about 1 atm. At 40 m the total pressure is about 5 atm.
  • p(O2) = 0.21 × 5 atm ≈ 1.05 atm: five times the oxygen pressure at the surface.
  • Above about 1.4–1.6 atm of O2, the risk of oxygen toxicity rises sharply: it can cause seizures. Breathing air, that point comes at roughly 55–65 m.
  • Same mole fraction, bigger total pressure → bigger partial pressure.
  • 1 atm = 101 kPa.
Quick check
?A gas mixture contains 2.0 mol N2, 6.0 mol H2 and 2.0 mol NH3. The total pressure is 200 kPa. What is the partial pressure of H2?
Mission 3

The Kp expression

  • For gas equilibria, use equilibrium partial pressures instead of concentrations.
  • N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g): Kp = p(NH3)² / (p(N2) × p(H2)³).
  • Products on top, coefficients as powers: as for Kc.
  • Write p(X) with round brackets. Square brackets mean concentration.
Mission 3

Units of Kp

  • Cancel the pressure units, as for Kc.
  • In atm: Kp for NH3 → atm² ÷ atm⁴ = atm⁻².
  • Same total power top and bottom: no units.
Mission 3

Heterogeneous equilibria

  • Only gases go into Kp. Solids (and pure liquids) are left out.
  • CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g): Kp = p(CO2).
  • So at a fixed temperature, the CO2 pressure over hot limestone is fixed, however much solid there is.
  • C(s) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + H2(g): Kp = p(CO) × p(H2) / p(H2O).
  • Units for Kp = p(CO2): atm.
Quick check
?3Fe(s) + 4H2O(g) ⇌ Fe3O4(s) + 4H2(g). Which is the Kp expression?
Mission 3

Calculating Kp (worked example)

  • N2O4(g) ⇌ 2NO2(g). Equilibrium: 0.60 mol N2O4 and 0.80 mol NO2. Total 1.40 mol.
  • Step 1, mole fractions: x(N2O4) = 0.60 ÷ 1.40 = 0.429; x(NO2) = 0.80 ÷ 1.40 = 0.571.
  • Step 2, partial pressures (× total), then Step 3, Kp:
  • Total 1.40 atm: p(N2O4) = 0.600 atm, p(NO2) = 0.800 atm. Kp = 0.800² ÷ 0.600 = 1.07 atm.
  • Keep unrounded mole fractions until the end.
Quick check
?PCl5(g) ⇌ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g). An equilibrium mixture contains 0.80 mol PCl5, 0.60 mol PCl3 and 0.60 mol Cl2. What is Kp?
  • The total pressure is 2.50 atm.
Mission check

?2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g). An equilibrium mixture contains 0.20 mol SO2, 0.10 mol O2 and 1.70 mol SO3. What is Kp?
  • The total pressure is 4.00 atm.

Same rule as the diver's oxygen: partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure. SO3 is 0.85 of the gas, so it supplies 0.85 of the pressure.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Le Châtelier: the position shifts to partly oppose a change. Only temperature changes K; a catalyst changes neither position nor K.
  • Industry balances yield, rate and cost: about 200 atm and 450 °C for ammonia.

Mission 2

  • ICE tables: changes follow the coefficients; divide moles by volume unless the volume cancels.
  • Kc units come from cancelling mol dm⁻³; same total power top and bottom means no units.

Mission 3

  • Partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure; partial pressures add up to the total.
  • Kp: partial pressures in place of concentrations; only gases appear.
  • Kp units come from cancelling the pressure units; only temperature changes Kp.
