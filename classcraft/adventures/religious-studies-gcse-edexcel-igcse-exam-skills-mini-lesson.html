Mission 1

Paper 1 at a glance

  • Beliefs and values: 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, 60% of your International GCSE.
  • Four sections: the universe, creation and the place of human beings · life and death · peace and conflict · rights, equality and social justice.
  • Each section has two questions. You choose one: four questions in all.
  • Each question: 25 marks, in four parts: 3 · 4 · 6 · 12.
  • Answer from your chosen religion, with non-religious beliefs and values where the topic includes them.
  • About 25 minutes per question, nearly half of it on the 12-mark part.
Mission 1

Four command words

  • Identify (3, AO1): recall facts.
  • Outline (4, AO1): recall facts and develop them.
  • Explain (6, AO1 + AO2): a developed understanding, with reasons.
  • Evaluate (12, AO1 + AO2): weigh a statement; different points of view; a justified conclusion.
  • Paper 1 is half AO1, half AO2. The last two parts carry 18 of every 25 marks.
Mission 1

Choosing your question

  • Read all four parts of both questions before writing.
  • Decide on the 12-mark part: it is worth almost half the question.
  • Check you can do the 6-mark part too.
  • Do not choose on the 3-mark part alone; it is the smallest.
  • Once you start, stay with your choice. Switching wastes time; answering both does not earn extra.

?In Section 2 you know the 3- and 4-mark parts of Question 3 well, but not its 12-mark part. Question 4's 12-mark part is on a topic you know well. What is the best choice?
Mission 1

Identify and Outline

  • Identify (3): three separate, accurate facts. One line each is enough.
  • Outline (4): facts with development: say a little more about each, such as what it means or an example.
  • E.g. Identify: "Identify three Christian beliefs about the nature of human beings." → made in God's image; given a soul; able to sin.
  • Use correct terms (e.g. Imago Dei: "image of God").
  • No evaluation needed. Keep these short to save time for the long parts.
Mission 1

Explain (6 marks)

  • Give reasons and develop them: point → explanation → example or teaching.
  • Use a source where you can: scripture, a religious leader, a Church or community teaching.
  • Show different attitudes within your religion where they exist.
  • Two or three developed points beat six bare ones.
  • Linking words show understanding: "because", "this means", "so".

What Evaluate means

Your specification defines Evaluate (12 marks, AO1 and AO2):

> "Analyse the statement provided, considering different points of view and creating logical chains of reasoning that are underpinned by understanding of religion and belief. Reasoned arguments and judgements should be made, supported by the appraisal of arguments and leading to a justified conclusion."

Q1 What must your reasoning be "underpinned by"?

Show answer

Understanding of religion and belief: accurate knowledge.

Q2 What must the answer lead to?

Show answer

A justified conclusion.

Mission 1

The synoptic 12-mark part

  • Synoptic: you draw together knowledge from different parts of the course.
  • E.g. a peace question can use beliefs from creation (Section 1) or sanctity of life (Section 2).
  • Shape: views for, with reasons and sources → views against, with reasons and sources → appraise (which is stronger, and why?) → justified conclusion.
  • Include different views within your religion, and non-religious views where relevant.
  • A conclusion that follows from your reasoning can go either way and still score highly.
Mission 1Worked answer

One synoptic paragraph

Statement: "Christians should always be pacifists." Evaluate this statement.

  • For: Jesus taught: "love your enemies, bless those who curse you, do good to those who hate you" (Matthew 5:44). Many Quakers are pacifists for this reason.
  • Synoptic link: if every person is made in the image of God (Genesis 1:27; Section 1), killing destroys what God made sacred (sanctity of life, Section 2).
  • Against: Just War theory (Augustine; Aquinas) holds that war can be right under strict conditions, e.g. a just cause, as a last resort, to protect the innocent.
  • Appraise: pacifists stress Jesus' words and example; Just War thinkers stress the duty to protect the weak and the ruler's role (Romans 13:4). Which is stronger, and why?
  • Non-religious view: some Humanists accept defensive war; others reject all war on grounds of human welfare.
  • Conclusion: decide which reasoning is stronger, and say why.
