Mission 1

Mission 1: Christ isn't Jesus' surname. So what were his followers claiming when they called him Christ?

Make a guess: Christ isn't Jesus' surname. So what were his followers claiming when they called him Christ?

Mission 1

Why titles matter

  • A title says who someone is and what they are for: "Doctor", "Captain", "King".
  • Jesus' first followers were Jews. They used titles from the Jewish scriptures (the Christian Old Testament) to say who Jesus was.
  • Main titles: Messiah (Christ), Son of God, Son of Man.
  • Each title is a claim: Christians say Jesus fulfils it; others disagree.
Mission 1

Messiah, Christ, Son of David

  • Messiah (Hebrew mashiach) = "anointed one". Kings of Israel were anointed with oil.
  • Christ (Greek Christos) means the same. "Jesus Christ" = "Jesus the Messiah".
  • Many Jews hoped for a Messiah from King David's family who would free Israel and bring God's peace.
  • Christians believe Jesus is the Messiah, but a servant who suffers, not a warrior king. Jews do not accept Jesus as the Messiah: many look for a Messiah, or a messianic age, still to come, bringing peace to the whole world.
Mission 1

Son of God, Son of Man

  • Son of God: the title says Jesus has a unique relationship with God the Father. Christians believe he is the eternal Son, the second Person of the Trinity.
  • Son of Man: Jesus' favourite way of speaking about himself in the Gospels.
  • It stresses his humanity, and that he will suffer (Mark 8:31).
  • It also recalls Daniel 7:13–14: "one like a son of man" who comes with the clouds and is given everlasting authority.

Mission check

?Early Christians used a fish symbol. The Greek word for fish, ΙΧΘΥΣ (ichthys), was read as initials: "Jesus Christ, God's Son, Saviour". What were they claiming by the word "Christ"?

Every title is a claim about who Jesus is: Christ (Messiah), Son of God, Son of Man, Lord, Saviour.

Mission 2

Mission 2: In less than a day Jesus went from supper with his friends to a cross. What happened, and why does it matter?

Make a guess: In less than a day Jesus went from supper with his friends to a cross. What happened, and why does it matter?

Mission 2

The last days

  • It is Passover in Jerusalem: the Jewish festival remembering freedom from slavery in Egypt.
  • Thursday evening: the Last Supper with the Twelve; then prayer in Gethsemane; Jesus is arrested after Judas betrays him.
  • Night and early morning: questioned by the Jewish council (Sanhedrin), then sent to Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor.
  • Friday: sentenced, crucified at Golgotha outside the city, dies in the afternoon, and is buried before the Sabbath.
  • Christians remember these days in Holy Week: Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, then Easter.
Mission 2Read the source

The crucifixion (Mark 15)

  • Simon of Cyrene is forced to carry the cross to Golgotha, "the place of a skull" (15:21–22).
  • The charge above him reads "THE KING OF THE JEWS". He is crucified between two robbers, and mocked: "He saved others. He can't save himself." (15:26–31)
  • Darkness from noon to three. Jesus cries: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (15:34, from Psalm 22:1)
  • He dies; the Temple curtain is torn in two from top to bottom (15:38).
  • The Roman centurion: "Truly this man was the Son of God!" (15:39)

Show answer

A Roman soldier: an outsider.

Mission 2

Why the cross matters

  • Christians believe the crucifixion was not a defeat but the centre of God's plan.
  • Salvation: Jesus' death brings forgiveness and restores the relationship with God (atonement; full in Mission 5).
  • Who Jesus is: innocent, obedient, forgiving; the King whose throne is a cross.
  • The Son of God (centurion).
  • God shares suffering: Christians believe God knows human pain from the inside.
  • Example: love that gives itself for others (John 15:13).
Quick check
?Why do Christians call the day of Jesus' crucifixion "Good Friday"?
Mission 2Evaluate

"The crucifixion was the most important event of Holy Week."

  • Reasons for: Christians believe salvation comes through Jesus' death (atonement). The cross is the central Christian symbol. Paul: "we preach Christ crucified" (1 Corinthians 1:23).
  • Reasons against: without the resurrection, the cross would be a defeat (1 Corinthians 15:14). The Last Supper gave the Eucharist, the centre of most Christian worship. Gethsemane shows the obedience that makes the cross meaningful.
  • Another view: the events form one story, the Paschal Mystery (see Mission 3): none can be ranked alone.
  • Judge it by: Which event do the others depend on? Which shapes worship most? What do the Gospels give most space to?
Mission check

?A hospital chaplain sits with a dying patient who says, "Does God even know what this is like?" The chaplain points to a cross on the wall. What belief about the crucifixion is the chaplain relying on?

Christians see the cross both as the act that reconciles people to God and as God's presence in human suffering.

Mission 3

What "resurrection" means

  • Christians believe that on the third day after his death God raised Jesus from the dead.
  • Not a ghost: in the Gospels the risen Jesus can be touched and eats food (Luke 24:39–43).
  • Not a resuscitation: Lazarus came back to ordinary life and would die again. Jesus was raised to a new, transformed life that cannot die (Romans 6:9).
  • The same Jesus, still bearing the wounds of the cross, but not always recognised at first.
  • Easter Sunday, the most important Christian festival, celebrates it.
Mission 3Read the source

The empty tomb (Matthew 28)

  • At dawn Mary Magdalene and "the other Mary" go to the tomb. There is an earthquake; an angel rolls back the stone; the guards shake with fear (28:1–4).
  • The angel: "Don't be afraid… He is not here, for he has risen, just like he said." (28:5–6)
  • On their way to tell the disciples, Jesus meets them. They hold his feet and worship him (28:9).
  • "Don't be afraid. Go tell my brothers that they should go into Galilee, and there they will see me." (28:10)

Q1 What is the women's first task? (To tell the disciples.) Q2: How does the women's touch matter?

Show answer

It shows Jesus is bodily risen, not a vision.

Mission 3

The appearances

  • On the road to Emmaus: two disciples walk with a stranger; they recognise Jesus when he breaks bread (Luke 24:13–35).
  • To the disciples in Jerusalem: "See my hands and my feet… a spirit doesn't have flesh and bones" (Luke 24:39). He eats fish.
  • Thomas doubts until he sees the wounds: "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28)
  • In Galilee: Jesus sends the eleven out to the world (Matthew 28:16–20).
  • Paul lists appearances to Peter, the Twelve, more than 500 at once, James, and finally himself (1 Corinthians 15:5–8).
Mission 3Read the source

Paul: "If Christ has not been raised"

"For I delivered to you first of all that which I also received: that Christ died for our sins according to the Scriptures, that he was buried, that he was raised on the third day according to the Scriptures" (1 Corinthians 15:3–4)

  • "If Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain and your faith also is in vain." (1 Corinthians 15:14)
  • Written about 20–25 years after Jesus' death: one of the earliest written records of the resurrection.

Q1 What three events does Paul hand on as "first of all"?

Show answer

Christ died for our sins, was buried, was raised.

Q2 What would follow if Christ had not been raised?

Show answer

Christian preaching and faith would be empty.

Mission 3Read the source

The ascension (Acts 1)

  • Forty days after Easter (Acts 1:3), Jesus is taken up and a cloud hides him from their sight (1:9).
  • In the Old Testament a cloud often marks God's presence, as when a pillar of cloud led Israel out of Egypt (Exodus 13:21).
  • Two men in white say: "You men of Galilee, why do you stand looking into the sky? This Jesus, who was received up from you into the sky, will come back in the same way as you saw him going into the sky." (Acts 1:11)

Q1 What do the two men promise? (Jesus will return.) Q2: What does the cloud recall from the Old Testament?

Show answer

God's presence, as in the Exodus.

Mission 3

What the ascension means

  • Jesus' earthly appearances end; he returns to the Father.
  • The Creed: he "sitteth on the right hand of God the Father Almighty": a picture of authority, not a place in the sky.
  • His human nature is taken into heaven: Christians see a pledge that humans can be with God.
  • He sends the Holy Spirit, so he is present everywhere, not in one place (John 16:7).
  • He will come again in glory (Acts 1:11).
  • Many Christians read "up" and "cloud" as signs of entering God's presence; others take the account more literally. Ascension Day is the Thursday 40 days after Easter.
Quick check
?Christians say Jesus "sits at the right hand of the Father". What do most Christians mean by this?
Mission 3

Why it matters

  • Who Jesus is: Christians believe the resurrection shows God's approval of Jesus: he is "declared to be the Son of God with power" (Romans 1:4).
  • Victory: Christians believe sin and death are defeated.
  • Hope: Christ is the "first fruit": believers will also be raised (1 Corinthians 15:20).
  • Worship: Christians meet on Sunday, the day of resurrection; Easter is the high point of the year.
  • Life: courage, forgiveness and joy; the first disciples went from hiding to preaching.
Mission 3Evaluate

"Without the resurrection, Christianity would have no meaning."

  • Reasons for: Paul says faith would be "in vain" (1 Corinthians 15:14). The first Christian preaching centres on it (Acts 2:32). Christian hope of eternal life depends on it.
  • Reasons against: Jesus' teaching on love has value in itself; many non-Christians admire it. The Incarnation and the cross also carry meaning. Some liberal Christians, who believe Jesus is alive with God without an empty tomb, find deep meaning in a spiritual reading.
  • Another view: the resurrection gives meaning to the rest, but only together with Jesus' life and death.
  • Judge it by: What did the first Christians say mattered most? Can "Christianity" survive without a risen Jesus? Does "meaning" here mean truth, hope or ethics?
Mission check

?A friend says, "Easter is when Christians believe Jesus' ghost appeared to his followers." Which correction would most Christians (Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant) make?

Not a ghost and not a return to old life: Christians believe Jesus was raised bodily to a life beyond death, and then ascended to the Father.

Mission 4

Mission 4: One bite of fruit in a garden: why do many Christians say it affects every human being since?

Make a guess: One bite of fruit in a garden: why do many Christians say it affects every human being since?

Mission 4

The Fall

  • In Eden, God allows the first humans to eat from every tree but one: the tree of the knowledge of good and evil (Genesis 2:16–17).
  • The serpent says that if they eat, they will be "like God" (Genesis 3:5, paraphrase).
  • The woman eats, and gives some to the man (3:6). They feel shame and hide from God (3:7–8).
  • Each blames another: the man the woman, the woman the serpent.
  • Consequences: pain, hard work, death; they are sent out of the garden (3:16–24).
  • Christians call this the Fall: humans fall away from their close relationship with God.
  • Genesis says "fruit". The apple comes from later art.
Mission 4Read the source

Genesis 3

"When the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be desired to make one wise, she took some of its fruit, and ate. Then she gave some to her husband with her, and he ate it, too." (Genesis 3:6)

"You will eat bread by the sweat of your face until you return to the ground, for you were taken out of it. For you are dust, and you shall return to dust." (Genesis 3:19)

Show answer

Mission 4

What sin is

  • Sin: any thought, word or action that goes against God's will.
  • Sin damages relationships: with God, with others, with oneself, with creation. (In Genesis 3, all four break.)
  • Paul: "all have sinned, and fall short of the glory of God" (Romans 3:23).
Mission 4Diversity

Original sin: different views

  • Original sin: the sinful state, or tendency, that all humans inherit from the first sin. Not the same as each person's own sins.
  • Augustine (354–430): through Adam, all humans inherit a fallen nature and share his guilt (Romans 5:12).
  • Catholic: every human is born lacking the original holiness God intended; original sin is "contracted", not committed. Baptism removes it, but a pull towards sin (concupiscence) remains.
  • Protestant Reformers (Luther, Calvin): human nature is corrupted in every part; only God's grace can save.
  • Orthodox: "ancestral sin": humans inherit death and a weakened nature, but not Adam's guilt.
  • Many liberal Christians: the Fall is a symbolic story about every human's tendency to selfishness.

Mission 4

Sin: a barrier to salvation

  • "For the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord." (Romans 6:23)
  • Salvation: being saved from sin and restored to friendship with God.
  • Christians believe sin separates people from God, and most say humans cannot remove it by their own efforts.
  • Christians believe the barrier is removed by God's grace through Jesus (see Mission 5).
Quick check
?In Romans 6:23, Paul contrasts "wages" with a "free gift". What point is he making?
Mission 4Evaluate

"The Fall must be literally true for original sin to make sense."

  • Reasons for: Paul treats Adam as one man through whom sin entered the world (Romans 5:12). Without a first sin, why would every human share a fallen nature? Catholic teaching says Genesis uses figurative language but describes a real event at the start of human history.
  • Reasons against: adam means "human": the story can describe every person. Selfishness can be seen everywhere without a history lesson. Orthodox teaching stresses inherited death, not inherited guilt. Many Christians accept evolution and still believe in original sin.
  • Judge it by: Does a doctrine need a historical event, or only a true picture of human nature? How should Paul's use of Adam be read?

The Fall explains, for many Christians, why selfishness comes so naturally: a wounded nature passed to every human.

Mission 5

Mission 5: Christians say Jesus' death puts people right with God. How can one death do that?

Make a guess: Christians say Jesus' death puts people right with God. How can one death do that?

Mission 5

Saved from what?

  • Salvation: being saved from sin and its effects, to live with God for ever (eternal life).
  • Sin is anything that goes against God's will. It damages the relationship with God (see Mission 4).
Mission 5

Law, grace and Spirit

  • Law: God's commands, such as the Ten Commandments (Exodus 20). Keeping them is part of a holy life.
  • But no one keeps the law perfectly, so most Christians believe law alone cannot save.
  • Grace: God's free, undeserved love and help. It cannot be earned.
  • "For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, that no one would boast." (Ephesians 2:8–9)
  • Spirit: Christians believe the Holy Spirit lives in believers, giving grace and helping them grow more like Jesus (see Mission 6).
Mission 5

Atonement: making "at one"

  • Atonement: sin is dealt with and the broken relationship is repaired: people are made "at one" with God.
  • Christians believe Jesus, fully God and fully human, did this by his death on the cross.
  • Forgiveness: Christians believe that through Jesus, God forgives the sins of all who repent (turn back).
Quick check
?Which statement correctly describes grace, as Christians use the word?
Mission 5

The whole story saves

  • Christians (Catholics call it the Paschal Mystery) see Jesus' life, death, resurrection and ascension as one saving work: redemption (being bought back, set free).
  • Life: in the Incarnation God shares human life; Jesus lives in perfect obedience and shows God's love.
  • Death: Jesus gives his life for others; Christians believe it takes away sin.
  • Resurrection: Jesus defeats death; believers are promised new life (see Mission 3).
  • Ascension: Jesus returns to the Father, and the Spirit is sent to the Church.
Mission 5Read the source

Jesus and Nicodemus (John 3:10–21)

  • Context: Nicodemus, a Jewish teacher, visits Jesus by night. Jesus says he must be "born again" (from above) by the Spirit (John 3:3–8).
  • Jesus says the Son of Man must be "lifted up", like the bronze snake Moses lifted up to heal the people (John 3:14; Numbers 21:9).
  • "For God so loved the world, that he gave his only born Son, that whoever believes in him should not perish, but have eternal life." (John 3:16)
  • "For God didn't send his Son into the world to judge the world, but that the world should be saved through him." (John 3:17)
  • Those who refuse to believe have chosen darkness instead of light (John 3:18–21, paraphrase).

Q1 According to John 3:16, what must a person do to receive eternal life?

Show answer

Believe in God's Son.

Q2 According to John 3:17, why did God send his Son?

Show answer

To save the world, not to condemn it.

Mission 5

Pictures of the cross

  • No single picture says it all. Christians use several theories of atonement, side by side.
  • Sacrifice: as animals were offered in the Temple for sin, Jesus offers himself, the Lamb of God (John 1:29), once for all.
  • Substitution: Jesus takes the punishment sinners deserve, in their place. Stressed by many evangelical Protestants.
  • Ransom: "For the Son of Man also came not to be served, but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many." (Mark 10:45)
  • Reconciliation / example: the cross shows God's love so fully that it wins people back and inspires them to love.
  • Most Christians hold several of these together.
Quick check
?A preacher says: "Jesus paid the price to set us free, as you might pay to free a hostage." Which picture of atonement is she using?
Mission 5Diversity

Saved when? Saved how?

  • Many evangelical Protestants: saved by faith alone (Luther, 1517 onwards). A moment of conversion ("born again") brings assurance. Good works are the fruit of faith, not its cause.
  • Catholics: saved by grace, received in faith and lived out in love and good works; grace comes especially through the sacraments (baptism, Mass, reconciliation). "Faith, if it has no works, is dead in itself." (James 2:17)
  • Orthodox: salvation is a lifelong journey of becoming more like God (theosis).
Quick check
?Which statement best matches Catholic teaching on how a person is saved?
Mission 5Evaluate

"Faith in Jesus is all a person needs to be saved."

  • Reasons for: "By grace you have been saved through faith … not of works" (Ephesians 2:8–9). The criminal crucified beside Jesus was promised paradise with no time for good works (Luke 23:43). The 39 Articles (XI) teach justification "by Faith, and not for our own works". Grace that had to be earned would not be grace.
  • Reasons against: "Faith, if it has no works, is dead" (James 2:17). In Matthew 25:31–46 people are judged by how they treated the hungry and the stranger. Catholics stress the sacraments, especially baptism (John 3:5). Faith that does not change how someone lives may not be real faith.
  • Another view: many Christians say the question is badly posed: real faith always shows itself in love, so faith and works cannot be separated.
  • Judge it by: What does "faith" mean: belief, or trust that changes a life? Which texts carry most weight, and why? Can a person earn what is a gift?
Mission check

?A firm has broken its contract with a supplier. The boss of the supplier pays the losses herself and invites the firm to sign a new agreement. Most Christians would say this pictures which Christian belief?

Atonement means the one who was wronged pays the price and opens the way back. Christians believe God did this in Jesus.

Mission 6

Who is the Holy Spirit?

  • Christians believe the Holy Spirit is the third Person of the Trinity: fully God, with the Father and the Son.
  • Christians see the Spirit at work in the Old Testament: "God's Spirit" hovers over the waters at creation (Genesis 1:2) and inspires prophets.
  • For Christians, the Spirit is how God's saving grace reaches believers and changes them from the inside.
Mission 6Read the source

Pentecost (Acts 2:1–8)

  • Fifty days after Passover, the apostles are together in one house in Jerusalem (Acts 2:1).
  • "Suddenly there came from the sky a sound like the rushing of a mighty wind, and it filled all the house where they were sitting." (Acts 2:2)
  • "Tongues like fire appeared and were distributed to them, and one sat on each of them." (Acts 2:3)
  • They are filled with the Holy Spirit and begin to speak in other languages (Acts 2:4).
  • Jews from many nations, in Jerusalem for the festival, each hear them in their own language, and are amazed (Acts 2:5–8, paraphrase).

Q1 What two signs come before the apostles speak?

Show answer

A sound like a mighty wind; tongues like fire.

Q2 Why are the crowds amazed?

Show answer

Each hears the apostles speaking in his own native language.

Mission 6

God's power given to believers

  • Christians believe the Spirit lives in every believer, not only in the apostles.
  • The Spirit guides into truth (John 16:13), helps in prayer (Romans 8:26) and gives courage to witness.
  • The Spirit makes believers holy over time: grace at work in a life (see Mission 5).
  • Significance: Christians pray "Come, Holy Spirit" before decisions, worship and study.
Mission 6Diversity

The Spirit in worship

  • Pentecostal and charismatic Evangelical churches: lively, free worship; raised hands; spontaneous prayer; speaking in tongues, prophecy and prayer for healing. Modern Pentecostalism grew from the Azusa Street revival (Los Angeles, 1906).
  • Some other Protestants (cessationists) believe the sign-gifts such as tongues ended with the apostles. Paul: "Let all things be done decently and in order" (1 Corinthians 14:40).
  • Catholic, Orthodox and Anglican liturgy: the Spirit is invoked in set prayers, for example over the bread and wine. The Catholic Charismatic Renewal (from 1967) brought charismatic worship into some Catholic parishes.
  • Quakers: sit in silence until the Spirit moves someone to speak.

Mission check

?After a terrible fire, Sam, a shy Christian, finds the courage to organise help, speak at a public meeting and comfort strangers. Which belief about the Holy Spirit would most Christians use to explain this?

Pentecost turned frightened followers into bold witnesses. Christians believe the same Spirit gives power, gifts and a changed character today.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Christ = Messiah = "anointed one": the king Jews hoped for from David's family.
  • Son of God expresses Jesus' unique relationship with the Father; Son of Man his humanity, suffering and future glory.

Mission 2

  • Last Supper (bread and cup, new covenant) → Gethsemane → arrest → trials (council, Pilate) → crucifixion → burial.
  • Christians believe Jesus' death was the centre of God's plan: forgiveness, a new covenant, God sharing human suffering.

Mission 3

  • Christians believe God raised Jesus bodily on the third day: the empty tomb and the appearances.
  • Christians believe the resurrection shows who Jesus is and gives hope that death is defeated (1 Corinthians 15).
  • At the ascension Jesus returns to the Father, promises the Spirit and will come again.

Mission 4

  • The Fall (Genesis 3): humans disobey God and their relationships with God, each other and creation break.
  • Original sin is the fallen state all humans inherit; Catholic, Protestant, Orthodox and liberal Christians understand it differently.
  • Sin is a barrier to salvation: "the wages of sin is death, but the free gift of God is eternal life" (Romans 6:23).

Mission 5

  • For Christians, salvation is being saved from sin to eternal life with God; atonement is the repairing of the relationship through Jesus.
  • Christians believe law shows sin, grace is God's free, undeserved love, and the Spirit helps believers live it out.
  • Christians picture the cross as sacrifice, ransom and reconciliation; Catholics and many Protestants differ on how faith and works relate.

Mission 6

  • Christians believe the Holy Spirit is the third Person of the Trinity, God present and active on Earth, promised by Jesus.
  • Acts 2 tells how, at Pentecost, wind, fire and many languages marked the Spirit's coming and the start of the Church's mission.
  • Christians differ on how the Spirit shows itself: tongues and gifts in Pentecostal worship, sacraments and liturgy in Catholic and Orthodox worship, silence for Quakers.
