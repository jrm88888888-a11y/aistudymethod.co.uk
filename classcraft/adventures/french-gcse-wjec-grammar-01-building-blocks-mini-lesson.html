Mission 1

Every noun has a gender

  • English has one word for "the": the boy, the girl, the table.
  • French splits every noun into masculine or feminine. Even things.
  • The gender shows in the little word in front:
masculinefeminine
thele garçon – the boyla fille – the girl
aun livre – a bookune table – a table
  • The gender is grammar, not meaning: a table isn't "female". It's just a feminine word.
  • So learn every noun with un or une: un livre, une maison.
  • Your turn: "a house"?
    Show answer

    une maison

Mission 1

Endings that give the gender away

  • You can't always know, but the ending is a strong clue.
Usually masculineUsually feminine
-age: le fromage – cheese-tion: la solution – solution
-eau: le gâteau – cake-té: la santé – health
-ment: le bâtiment – building-ure: la voiture – car
-isme: le tourisme – tourism-ette: la baguette – French stick
  • Exceptions to know: la plage – beach, l'image (f) – picture, l'eau (f) – water, l'été (m) – summer, le côté – side.
  • Watch out: a final -e does not prove feminine: le collège, le musée, le lycée are masculine.
  • Watch out: l' hides the gender. l'école is feminine (une école); l'hôtel is masculine (un hôtel).

Mission 1

Plurals

  • Main rule: add -s, and le/la/l' become les: le livre → les livres; la table → les tables.
  • un/une become des: un chien → des chiens – (some) dogs.
  • -eau and -eu add -x: le gâteau → les gâteaux; le jeu → les jeux.
  • Already ends in -s or -x: no change: le bus → les bus; le prix → les prix.
  • Most -al nouns → -aux: l'animal → les animaux; le journal → les journaux.
  • Watch out: le festival just adds -s: les festivals.
  • Learn alone: l'œil → les yeux – eyes; monsieur → messieurs; madame → mesdames.
  • The plural -s is silent. You hear the plural in the article: le vs les.
  • Before a vowel, les links with a z sound: les amis sounds like "lay-zamee".
  • Your turn: "the boats" (le bateau)?
    Show answer

    les bateaux

Mission 1

Verbs as nouns: the infinitive

  • English uses -ing as a noun: Swimming is fun. I like reading.
  • French uses the infinitive: the -er/-ir/-re form.
  • Lire est important. – Reading is important.
  • Voyager, c'est génial. – Travelling is great.
  • Manger trop de sucre est mauvais pour la santé. – Eating too much sugar is bad for your health.
  • After a verb of liking: J'aime lire. – I like reading.
  • Watch out: don't hunt for an "-ing" form. Lire est important, not Lisant est important.
  • Your turn: "Travelling is expensive."

Mission 2

le or l'?

  • Before a vowel or a silent h, le and la shorten to l': l'ami, l'école, l'hôtel, l'histoire.
  • les never shortens. It links with a z sound: les amis, les hôtels.
  • A few words start with an "h" that blocks this (h aspiré): le héros – the hero; les héros, with no z sound.
  • l'hôtel, les hôtels (z link) vs le héros, les héros (no link).
  • Watch out: l' hides the gender. Check it with un/une: une école (f), un hôtel (m).
  • Your turn: "the friend (f)"?
    Show answer

    l'amie

Mission 2

"The" where English says nothing

  • English drops "the" when talking about things in general. French keeps it.
  • Le chocolat est bon. – Chocolate is good.
  • La santé est importante. – Health is important.
  • After aimer, adorer, préférer, détester: always le/la/l'/les.
  • J'aime les chiens. – I like dogs. Je déteste les devoirs. – I hate homework.
  • Countries and languages: J'adore la France. J'apprends le français.
  • But after parler, the article is usually dropped: Je parle français.
  • Watch out: J'aime chocolat → J'aime le chocolat.
  • Your turn: "I prefer cats."
    Show answer

    Je préfère les chats.

Mission 2

Days, looks and jobs

  • Days: samedi = this Saturday; le samedi = every Saturday.
  • Samedi, je travaille. – On Saturday (this one), I'm working.
  • Le samedi, je travaille. – On Saturdays, I work.
  • No word for "on": not sur samedi.
  • Looks: Elle a les yeux bleus. – She has blue eyes. J'ai les cheveux longs. – I have long hair.
  • Jobs after être: no article. Mon père est médecin. – My dad is a doctor.
  • With c'est, keep it: C'est un médecin. – He's a doctor.
  • Your turn: "My mum is a teacher."
    Show answer

    Ma mère est prof.

Mission 2

Making nouns with le / la

  • le or la + adjective = a noun: seul – only → le seul, la seule – the only one.
  • Languages are masculine, with no capital: l'anglais – English; le français – French.
  • People get a capital: l'Anglaise – the Englishwoman; les Français – the French.
  • The adjective stays small: un film français; Il est anglais.
  • One sentence, both: Les Français parlent français. – The French speak French.
  • Your turn: "the English (people)"?

Mission 3

A whole one, or some of it?

  • English: "I'm eating bread." No article needed.
  • French must say "some": Je mange du pain.
  • un gâteau – a (whole) cake → du gâteau – some cake.
  • une pizza – a (whole) pizza → de la pizza – some pizza.
before a consonantbefore a vowel or silent h
masculinedu painde l'argent – money
femininede la pizzade l'omelette
pluraldes fritesdes œufs – eggs
  • du = de + le (never de le).
  • Watch out: Je mange pain → Je mange du pain.
  • Your turn: "I'm eating (some) chips."
    Show answer

    Je mange des frites.

Mission 3

jouer du, faire de la

  • Instruments: jouer de + the instrument.
  • Je joue du piano. – I play the piano. Elle joue de la guitare. – She plays the guitar.
  • Sports and activities: faire de + the activity.
  • Je fais du sport. – I do sport. Je fais du vélo. – I go cycling.
  • Il fait de la danse. – He does dance. Elle fait de la natation. – She goes swimming.
  • Watch out: games and team sports use jouer à: Je joue au foot. (au = à + le; see a later lesson.)
  • Your turn: "We do dance." (nous)
    Show answer

    Nous faisons de la danse.

Mission 3

Not any: pas de

  • After a negative, un, une, du, de la, des all become de (d' before a vowel).
  • J'ai un chien. → Je n'ai pas de chien. – I don't have a dog.
  • Il y a des frites. → Il n'y a pas de frites. – There aren't any chips.
  • Je mange de la viande. → Je ne mange jamais de viande. – I never eat meat.
  • J'ai de l'argent. → Je n'ai plus d'argent. – I've got no money left.
  • Two things don't change:
  • after être: C'est un chien. → Ce n'est pas un chien.
  • le/la/les after liking verbs: J'aime le fromage. → Je n'aime pas le fromage.
  • Your turn: J'ai des frères. → negative?
    Show answer

    Je n'ai pas de frères.

Mission 3

A lot of, too much: de

  • After a quantity, just de (d' before a vowel). No du, de la, des.
  • beaucoup de devoirs – a lot of homework
  • trop de bruit – too much noise
  • assez d'argent – enough money
  • un peu de fromage – a little cheese
  • Tu as combien de frères ? – How many brothers do you have?
  • Containers and weights work the same: une bouteille d'eau – a bottle of water; un kilo de pommes – a kilo of apples.
  • Watch out: "a lot of friends" is beaucoup d'amis, not beaucoup des amis and not beaucoup de amis.
  • Your turn: "too much homework"?
    Show answer

    trop de devoirs

Mission 3

Some milk, some luck

  • Some nouns are never "a whole one": liquids and ideas. They take the partitive too.
  • Je bois du lait. – I drink milk. Tu veux de l'eau ? – Do you want some water?
  • Il faut du temps. – It takes time.
  • Tu as de la chance ! – You're lucky! (literally "you have some luck")
  • Il y a du bruit. – There's noise.
  • Compare: J'aime le café (coffee in general), mais je bois du thé (some tea, now).
  • Your turn: "There's (some) water."
    Show answer

    Il y a de l'eau.

  • English: small never changes. a small boy, small girls.
  • French: the adjective matches its noun's gender and number.

Mission 4

Hear the difference

  • The final consonant of petit is silent.
  • Add -e and you hear it: petit → petite ("puh-tee" → "puh-teet").
  • petit, petite. grand, grande. vert, verte. français, française.
  • Plural -s stays silent: petits sounds like petit.
  • So in speaking, the feminine shows; the plural mostly doesn't.
  • Your turn: does grande end with a d sound?
    Show answer

    Yes: the -e makes you say the d.

Plurals and mixed groups

After être, and colours that never change

Mission 5

After the noun: the default

  • English: adjective before the noun. a red car.
  • French: adjective usually after the noun. une voiture rouge.
  • un film intéressant – an interesting film
  • un garçon français – a French boy
  • une maison moderne – a modern house
  • Colours and nationalities always go after.

Mission 6

not a single one: aucun

  • aucun = no, not any, not a single.
  • It agrees: aucun (m), aucune (f). Use it in the singular.
  • It goes with ne before the verb:
  • Je n'ai aucune idée. – I have no idea.
  • Il n'y a aucun problème. – There's no problem at all.
  • Stronger than pas de: "not even one".
  • Your turn: what does Elle n'a aucun ami ici mean?
    Show answer

    She has no friends here (not a single one).

Mission 7

0 to 69: the patterns

  • 0 = zéro. 1–16 have their own words: un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq, six, sept, huit, neuf, dix, onze, douze, treize, quatorze, quinze, seize.
  • 17–19 = ten + seven / eight / nine: dix-sept, dix-huit, dix-neuf.
  • Tens: vingt, trente, quarante, cinquante, soixante.
  • 21, 31, 41, 51, 61 use et: vingt et un, trente et un.
  • The rest use a hyphen: vingt-deux, quarante-sept, soixante-neuf.
  • six, dix, huit alone: say the last letter. Before a consonant: silent (six livres). Before a vowel: linked (six‿euros, dix‿ans, huit‿heures).
  • vingt: silent t alone; t sounded in 21–29 (vingt-deux).
  • Your turn: 47? 61?
    Show answer

    quarante-sept, soixante et un.

Mission 7

70 to 99: counting in twenties

  • After 69, French counts in twenties.
  • 70 = 60 + 10: soixante-dix. 71 = soixante et onze. 72 = soixante-douze … 79 = soixante-dix-neuf.
  • 80 = 4 × 20: quatre-vingts.
  • 81 = quatre-vingt-un (no et, no -s). 85 = quatre-vingt-cinq.
  • 90 = 4 × 20 + 10: quatre-vingt-dix. 91 = quatre-vingt-onze … 99 = quatre-vingt-dix-neuf.
  • Watch out: quatre-vingts keeps -s only when no other number follows: quatre-vingts ans, but quatre-vingt-deux ans.
  • In Belgium and Switzerland people say septante (70) and nonante (90). You may hear them; in France, use soixante-dix, quatre-vingt-dix.
  • Your turn: 75? 98?
    Show answer

    soixante-quinze, quatre-vingt-dix-huit.

Mission 7

100 and beyond: prices and years

  • 100 = cent (no un). 101 = cent un.
  • 200 = deux cents; 250 = deux cent cinquante (-s only when no other number follows: deux cents euros).
  • 1000 = mille (no un, never -s): deux mille.
  • A million = un million; with a noun, add de: un million d'habitants.
  • Years: en 2026 = en deux mille vingt-six. 1998 = mille neuf cent quatre-vingt-dix-huit.
  • Prices: French writes a comma: 3,50 € = trois euros cinquante.
  • Big numbers: a space, not a comma: 1 500 = mille cinq cents.
  • Your turn: €4.20? the year 2010?
    Show answer

    quatre euros vingt, deux mille dix.

Mission 7

Dates: just the number

  • English: "the 14th of July". French: le quatorze juillet – "the fourteen July".
  • Pattern: le + number + month. No "of", no "-th".
  • Exception: the 1st = le premier: le premier mai.
  • Months and days take no capital: le samedi 3 mars.
  • "in May" = en mai; "in 2026" = en 2026.
  • Ask: Quelle est la date aujourd'hui ? → C'est le dix octobre.
  • Ordinals (2nd, 3rd…) are for other things: number + -ième. deuxième, troisième, quatre → quatrième, cinq → cinquième, neuf → neuvième. au deuxième étage – on the second floor.
  • Your turn: "on 1st April"? "on 2nd June"?
    Show answer

    le premier avril, le deux juin.

Mission 7

Telling the time

  • Ask: Quelle heure est-il ? – What time is it? Answer: Il est … heure(s).
  • Il est une heure. (une: heure is feminine) · Il est deux heures.
  • Minutes past: just add them: Il est deux heures dix.
  • et quart (quarter past) · et demie (half past) · moins le quart (quarter to) · moins dix (ten to).
  • Il est neuf heures moins le quart. – It's quarter to nine (8.45).
  • midi (noon), minuit (midnight): Il est midi et demi.
  • "At" = à: à sept heures du matin (a.m.), à trois heures de l'après-midi, à huit heures du soir.
  • Watch out: don't drop heures. Il est trois. → Il est trois heures.
  • Your turn: "at 7.30 in the evening"?
    Show answer

    à sept heures et demie du soir.

Mission 7

The 24-hour clock

  • Timetables, TV, school, cinema: French uses the 24-hour clock a lot.
  • Say the hour, then the minutes: 14 h 30 = quatorze heures trente.
  • 20 h 15 = vingt heures quinze. 12 h 45 = douze heures quarante-cinq.
  • No et quart, et demie, moins with the 24-hour clock: just numbers.
  • Written 14 h 30 or 14h30.
  • Your turn: 17:50?
    Show answer

    dix-sept heures cinquante.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Every French noun is masculine or feminine: learn it with un/une. Endings help (-tion, -té feminine; -age, -eau, -ment masculine).
  • Plurals: usually add a silent -s; -eau/-eu add -x; -al → -aux; -s/-x don't change.
  • People: -e, no change, -euse, -trice, -enne.

Mission 2

  • le/la/l'/les and un/une/des agree with the noun; le/la → l' before a vowel or silent h.
  • French uses le/la/les for things in general, after aimer/détester, and for "every Saturday"; jobs after être take no article.

Mission 3

  • French always says "some": du, de la, de l', des, including jouer du (instruments) and faire de la (activities).
  • After a negative or a quantity, the article becomes de/d'. Not after être, and not le/la/les after liking verbs.

Mission 4

  • Adjectives agree with their noun: feminine + e, plural + s.
  • Patterns: -x → -se, -f → -ve, -er → -ère, -el → -elle, -en → -enne; -al → -aux in the masculine plural.
  • Mixed group = masculine plural; marron and orange never change.

Mission 5

  • Most adjectives go after the noun; colours and nationalities always do.
  • BAGS (beauty, age, good/bad, size) go before; bel, nouvel, vieil before a masculine vowel or silent h.
  • ancien, cher, propre, pauvre, dernier, seul, grand change meaning with position.

Mission 6

  • mon/ma/mes, ton/ta/tes, son/sa/ses agree with the noun; mon, ton, son before a feminine vowel.
  • ce / cet / cette / ces and quel / quelle / quels / quelles: same idea, check the noun's gender and number.
  • chaque + singular; plusieurs, quelques + plural; tout has four forms; aucun(e) + ne = not a single.

Mission 7

  • 70 = soixante-dix, 80 = quatre-vingts, 90 = quatre-vingt-dix; et only in 21–71 (vingt et un … soixante et onze).
  • Dates: le + plain number + month, lower case; the 1st = le premier.
  • Time: Il est … heure(s); et quart, et demie, moins le quart; 24-hour clock = hour + minutes.
