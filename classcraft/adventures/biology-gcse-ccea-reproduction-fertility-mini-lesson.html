Reproduction & Fertility

This mini-lesson covers reproduction, fertility and contraception from CCEA GCSE Biology (Unit 2): sexual vs asexual reproduction, the human male and female reproductive systems, sex hormones, the menstrual cycle, fertility treatment (IVF) and methods of contraception.

Reproduction · sexual vs asexual

Sexual and asexual reproduction

  • Sexual reproduction — needs two parents and the fusion of gametes (sperm + egg). It mixes genes, so the offspring are genetically different — this creates variation.
  • Asexual reproduction — needs one parent and no gametes (e.g. bacteria dividing, a strawberry plant sending out runners). Offspring are genetically identical clones.

Key difference: sexual reproduction uses meiosis to make gametes and gives variation; asexual reproduction uses mitosis only and gives identical clones.

Reproduction · human systems

Human reproductive systems

You need the main parts of each system and what they do:

  • Male: testes make sperm and testosterone · scrotum holds the testes · sperm tube carries sperm · prostate gland adds fluid · urethra carries sperm out through the penis.
  • Female: ovaries release eggs and make oestrogen · oviducts (where fertilisation happens) carry the egg to the uterus (womb) · the cervix is the neck of the uterus, leading to the vagina.

Where fertilisation happens: the sperm meets the egg in the oviduct (fallopian tube), not in the uterus. The fertilised egg then travels to the uterus to implant.

Reproduction · sex hormones

Sex hormones

  • Testosterone — made by the testes; the main male sex hormone. It causes male secondary sexual characteristics (e.g. deeper voice, facial hair, sperm production).
  • Oestrogen — made by the ovaries; the main female sex hormone. It causes female secondary sexual characteristics (e.g. breast development, wider hips) and controls the menstrual cycle.

Match hormone to organ: testosterone → testes; oestrogen → ovaries. Both are made during puberty and drive the changes into adulthood.

Reproduction · menstrual cycle

The menstrual cycle

The menstrual cycle is roughly 28 days long. The key events are:

  • Menstruation (days ~1–5) — the uterus lining breaks down and is lost (a period).
  • The lining then rebuilds, controlled by oestrogen.
  • Ovulation (around day 14) — an egg is released from an ovary. Fertilisation is most likely around this time.
  • After ovulation, progesterone maintains the thick uterus lining, ready for a fertilised egg to implant.

Two key hormones here: oestrogen rebuilds the lining before ovulation; progesterone maintains it afterwards.

Fertility · treatment

Infertility & fertility treatment

Infertility is difficulty conceiving a baby. CCEA fertility treatments include:

  • Fertility hormones — given to stimulate the ovaries to release several eggs (ova) at once.
  • In vitro fertilisation (IVF) — eggs are collected and fertilised by sperm outside the body ("in vitro" = in glass), in a lab.
  • Several embryos are grown and then transferred into the uterus.

"In vitro" clue: IVF means fertilisation happens in a lab dish, not inside the oviduct. The best embryos are then placed in the uterus.

Fertility · contraception

Contraception

Contraception prevents pregnancy. CCEA groups methods as:

  • Mechanical (barrier): the condom physically blocks sperm — and also helps stop STIs such as HIV/AIDS and chlamydia.
  • Chemical (hormonal): the contraceptive pill and implants change hormone levels and stop the ovum developing/being released.
  • Surgical: sterilisation cuts or blocks the sperm tubes (male) or oviducts (female).

Only barriers stop STIs: the condom is the only method here that also protects against sexually transmitted infections; the pill and sterilisation do not.

The big ideas to know

Sexual vs asexual: sexual = two parents, gametes, variation (meiosis); asexual = one parent, identical clones (mitosis)

Male system: testes, scrotum, sperm tube, prostate, urethra, penis

Female system: ovaries, oviducts (fertilisation site), uterus, cervix, vagina

Hormones: testosterone (testes) · oestrogen (ovaries) · progesterone maintains the lining

Menstrual cycle: menstruation (days 1-5); ovulation ~day 14 (most fertile)

Fertility & contraception: IVF & fertility hormones; barrier (condom, stops STIs) / hormonal (pill, implant) / surgical (sterilisation)
