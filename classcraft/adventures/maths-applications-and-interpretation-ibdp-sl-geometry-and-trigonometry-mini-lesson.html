Geometry · 3-D shapes

Distance and midpoint in 3-D

  • Points in space have three coordinates (x,y,z)(x, y, z).
  • Distance between (x1​,y1​,z1​)(x₁, y₁, z₁) and (x2​,y2​,z2​)(x₂, y₂, z₂):
d=(x1​−x2​)2+(y1​−y2​)2+(z1​−z2​)2​d=√((x₁−x₂)²+(y₁−y₂)²+(z₁−z₂)²)
  • Midpoint: (2x1​+x2​​,2y1​+y2​​,2z1​+z2​​)((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2, (z₁+z₂)/2)
  • In a cuboid a×b×ca × b × c, Pythagoras on the base gives a2+b2​√(a²+b²); Pythagoras again with the height gives the space diagonal a2+b2+c2​√(a²+b²+c²).

Geometry · 3-D shapes

Angles in 3-D

  • The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection on the plane (the "shadow" straight below it).
  • Drop a perpendicular from a point on the line to the plane: this makes a right-angled triangle containing the angle.
  • The angle between two intersecting lines is found in the triangle that both lines lie in.
  • At SL, 3-D angle questions use right-angled trigonometry: sketch the triangle flat, label it, then use SOH CAH TOA.

Geometry · trigonometry

Right-angled trigonometry, elevation and bearings

  • In a right-angled triangle: sinθ=hypopp​sin θ=opp/hyp, cosθ=hypadj​cos θ=adj/hyp, tanθ=adjopp​tan θ=opp/adj. Use sin−1sin⁻¹, cos−1cos⁻¹, tan−1tan⁻¹ to find angles.
  • Angle of elevation: measured up from the horizontal. Angle of depression: measured down from the horizontal.
  • Bearings are measured clockwise from north, written with three figures (e.g. 040°).
  • Always sketch and label a diagram from the words first.
Quick check
?From the top of a vertical cliff 40 m high, the angle of depression of a boat is 25°. How far is the boat from the foot of the cliff, to 3 significant figures?
Geometry · trigonometry

Sine rule, cosine rule and area

  • Label the side opposite angle AA as aa, and so on.
  • Sine rule: sinAa​=sinBb​=sinCc​a/(sin A)=b/(sin B)=c/(sin C). Use it with a side and its opposite angle known.
  • Cosine rule: c2=a2+b2−2abcosCc²=a²+b²−2abcos C, or cosC=2aba2+b2−c2​cos C=(a²+b²−c²)/2ab. Use it with two sides and the angle between them, or with all three sides.
  • Area =21​absinC=1/2absin C (CC is the angle between sides aa and bb).

Mission check

?How can you find a mountain's height without climbing it?

Two angles of elevation, measured a known distance apart on level ground, make a triangle with one known side. The sine rule gives the distance to the summit, and a right-angled triangle then gives the height, as in the tower example.

Geometry · circles

Arc length and sector area

  • A sector is a "pizza slice" of a circle; its curved edge is an arc.
  • A sector with angle θ∘θ° is the fraction 360θ​θ/360 of the whole circle, so
arc length=360θ​×2πrsector area=360θ​×πr2arc length=θ/360× 2π r sector area=θ/360× π r²
  • Perimeter of a sector == arc +2r+ 2r (two radii).

Geometry · circles

Segments and compound shapes

  • A segment is the region between a chord and its arc.
  • Segment area == sector area −− triangle area, with the triangle area 21​r2sinθ1/2r²sin θ (two radii and the angle between them).
  • For a shape made of sectors, rectangles and triangles: find each piece, then add or subtract.
  • Work with unrounded values and round only the final answer to 3 significant figures.

Geometry · Voronoi diagrams

Perpendicular bisectors

  • The perpendicular bisector of ABAB is the set of points equally distant from AA and BB.
  • It passes through the midpoint (2x1​+x2​​,2y1​+y2​​)((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2).
  • Its gradient is the negative reciprocal of the gradient of ABAB: if ABAB has gradient mm, the bisector has gradient −m1​−1/m.
  • Then use y−y1​=m(x−x1​)y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) through the midpoint. If ABAB is horizontal, the bisector is vertical (x=x = constant), and vice versa.
Worked example Geometry · Voronoi diagrams

The boundary between two masts

Question
Masts are at A(1,1)A(1, 1) and D(8,8)D(8, 8), in km. Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of ADAD.
  1. 1
    Midpoint: (21+8​,21+8​)=(4.5,4.5)((1 + 8)/2, (1 + 8)/2) = (4.5, 4.5)
  2. 2
    Gradient of ADAD: 8−18−1​=1(8 − 1)/(8 − 1) = 1
  3. 3
    Perpendicular gradient: −11​=−1−1/1 = −1
  4. 4
    Line through the midpoint: y−4.5=−1(x−4.5)y − 4.5 = −1(x − 4.5)
Answer
y=−x+9y = −x + 9
⚠️ Watch out
the bisector goes through the midpoint, not through AA or DD. Using (1,1)(1, 1) gives y=−x+2y = −x + 2, which is wrong.

Geometry · Voronoi diagrams

Voronoi diagrams

  • A Voronoi diagram splits a region into cells, one around each site. Every point in a cell is nearer its own site than any other.
  • Edges are parts of perpendicular bisectors between pairs of sites. A vertex is where three edges meet: it is equidistant from three sites.
  • Nearest neighbour interpolation: every point in a cell is given the same value as its site (for example, the rainfall measured there).
  • Exams give the site coordinates; you will not construct bisectors.
Quick check
?Rain gauges at the four sites A(1,1)A(1, 1), B(11,1)B(11, 1), C(1,7)C(1, 7) and D(8,8)D(8, 8) record 12 mm, 20 mm, 16 mm and 9 mm. Use nearest neighbour interpolation to estimate the rainfall at H(4,6)H(4, 6).
Geometry · Voronoi diagrams

Adding a site; the toxic waste dump

  • Adding a site: draw the bisectors between the new site and its neighbours; the new cell is carved from theirs.
  • Toxic waste dump problem: find the point in the region as far as possible from every site (a dump, or the weakest signal).
  • Within the region, the farthest point from every site is at a Voronoi vertex or on the region's boundary; exams put it at a vertex. Find each vertex's distance to one of its sites: the largest wins.

Where is the weakest signal?

Question

  2. 2

  3. 3
    Distance from PP to AA (one of AA, CC, DD): 42+32​=5√(4² + 3²) = 5 km
  4. 4
    Compare: 5.39>55.39 > 5

⚠️ Watch out
measure from the vertex to one of its own three sites. QQ to CC is 6.40 km, but AA, BB and DD are nearer.

Mission check

?Four phone masts: where in town is the signal weakest?

Inside a cell you can always move farther from its mast until you reach an edge (or the edge of town), and along an edge the distance to its two masts is greatest at one end. So the farthest point is at a Voronoi vertex, where three cells meet, or on the town's boundary. Here no boundary point does better: compare the vertices' distances to their own masts, and the largest one, vertex Q(6,3)Q(6, 3) at 5.39 km, is where the signal is weakest and where a fifth mast would help most.

Recap

Big ideas

  • 3-D distance: (x1​−x2​)2+(y1​−y2​)2+(z1​−z2​)2​√((x₁−x₂)²+(y₁−y₂)²+(z₁−z₂)²); a cuboid's space diagonal is a2+b2+c2​√(a²+b²+c²).
  • The angle between a line and a plane is the angle with its projection: find the right-angled triangle and use trigonometry.
  • Combined solids: add volumes; count only the outside surfaces.
  • Right-angled triangles: SOH CAH TOA. Elevation is up from the horizontal; depression is down; bearings are clockwise from north.
  • Sine rule needs a side with its opposite angle; cosine rule needs two sides and the included angle, or three sides. Area =21​absinC=1/2absin C.
  • Break a real problem into triangles and sketch a labelled diagram first.
  • Arc =360θ​×2πr=θ/360× 2π r and sector =360θ​×πr2=θ/360×π r²; perimeter adds two radii.
  • Segment == sector −− triangle, with the triangle 21​r2sinθ1/2r²sin θ.
  • The perpendicular bisector of ABAB passes through the midpoint with gradient −m1​−1/m; every point on it is equidistant from AA and BB.
  • Voronoi cells hold the points nearest each site; edges lie on bisectors; vertices are equidistant from three sites. Nearest neighbour interpolation gives a cell its site's value.
  • Toxic waste dump: test the vertices and choose the one farthest from its three sites.
