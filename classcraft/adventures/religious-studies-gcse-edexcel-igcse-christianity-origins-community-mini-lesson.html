Mission 1

Mission 1: How can a baby born in Bethlehem be, as Christians believe, God himself?

Make a guess: How can a baby born in Bethlehem be, as Christians believe, God himself?

Mission 1

What "Incarnation" means

  • Incarnation comes from Latin in carne: "in flesh".
  • Christians believe God the Son, the second Person of the Trinity, became a human being: Jesus of Nazareth.
  • Jesus is fully God and fully human: not half and half, not God in disguise, not a man who became God.
  • One person, two natures (divine and human): agreed at the Council of Chalcedon (451).
  • The Nicene Creed: he "was incarnate by the Holy Ghost of the Virgin Mary, and was made man".

Mission 1

The virgin birth

  • Christians believe Jesus was conceived by the Holy Spirit, without a human father, and born of the Virgin Mary (Apostles' Creed).
  • It shows Jesus is from God (divine) and born of a woman (human).
  • Matthew and Luke both tell it; Christians see it as God's initiative, not human achievement.
  • Catholic and Orthodox Christians honour Mary as Theotokos, "God-bearer" (Council of Ephesus, 431).
  • Most Christians accept the virgin birth as a historical event; some liberal Protestants read it as a symbol of Jesus' unique link with God.
Quick check
?What do Christians mean by the virgin birth?
Mission 1Diversity

Why it matters, and how Christians differ

  • For all mainstream Christians: God is not distant; Jesus shows what God is like; God understands human life from the inside.
  • Catholic: Mary as Mother of God; the Angelus prayer recalls the Incarnation three times a day; Christmas Midnight Mass.
  • Orthodox: God became human so humans can share God's life (theosis). Athanasius: "He was made man that we might be made God" (On the Incarnation 54).
  • Protestant: often stress the cross more; the Incarnation makes the saving death possible.
  • Impact: worship at Christmas; respect for human bodies and lives; serving the poor, in whom Jesus is met (Matthew 25:40).
Mission 1Evaluate

"The Incarnation is the most important Christian belief."

  • Reasons for: without it, Jesus' death is the death of a good man, not God's saving act. It shows God's love (John 3:16). Only God become human can reveal God fully (John 1:18).
  • Reasons against: Paul says faith depends on the resurrection (1 Corinthians 15:14). Many Protestants see the cross as the centre. The Trinity is the foundation all else rests on.
  • Another view: the beliefs depend on each other: Incarnation, cross and resurrection form one story.
  • Judge it by: Which belief do the others depend on? What do Christians celebrate most (Christmas or Easter)? Which makes most difference to daily life?
Mission 1Worked answer

Influence on Christians today

Question: Explain two ways belief in the Incarnation influences Christians today.

  • Way 1: Christians celebrate it at Christmas. Development: worship centres on God coming to Earth as a baby, "the Word became flesh" (John 1:14), so many attend Midnight Mass or carol services.
  • Way 2: Christians follow Jesus' example. Development: if God showed himself in a human life, then Jesus' way of loving and serving is how God wants people to live.

In flesh, not in disguise: Christians believe Jesus is one person, fully God and fully human.

Mission 2

Why titles matter

  • A title says who someone is and what they are for: "Doctor", "Captain", "King".
  • Jesus' first followers were Jews. They used titles from the Jewish scriptures (the Christian Old Testament) to say who Jesus was.
  • Main titles: Messiah (Christ), Son of God, Son of Man.
  • Each title is a claim: Christians say Jesus fulfils it; others disagree.
Mission 2

Messiah, Christ, Son of David

  • Messiah (Hebrew mashiach) = "anointed one". Kings of Israel were anointed with oil.
  • Christ (Greek Christos) means the same. "Jesus Christ" = "Jesus the Messiah".
  • Many Jews hoped for a Messiah from King David's family who would free Israel and bring God's peace.
  • Christians believe Jesus is the Messiah, but a servant who suffers, not a warrior king. Jews do not accept Jesus as the Messiah: many look for a Messiah, or a messianic age, still to come, bringing peace to the whole world.
Mission 2

Son of God, Son of Man

  • Son of God: the title says Jesus has a unique relationship with God the Father. Christians believe he is the eternal Son, the second Person of the Trinity.
  • Son of Man: Jesus' favourite way of speaking about himself in the Gospels.
  • It stresses his humanity, and that he will suffer (Mark 8:31).
  • It also recalls Daniel 7:13–14: "one like a son of man" who comes with the clouds and is given everlasting authority.

The baptism of Jesus

  • The Spirit of God comes down "as a dove", and a voice from heaven says:

Mission 2

The temptations

  • After 40 days fasting in the wilderness, Jesus is hungry (Matthew 4:2). The devil tests him three times:
  • 1. "If you are the Son of God", turn stones into bread. Reply: "Man shall not live by bread alone, but by every word that proceeds out of God's mouth." (4:4, from Deuteronomy 8:3)
  • 2. Throw yourself from the Temple, so angels catch you. Reply: do not test God (4:7, Deuteronomy 6:16).
  • 3. Worship the devil to gain all the world's kingdoms. Reply: worship and serve God only (4:10, Deuteronomy 6:13).
  • Significance: Jesus will not be a Messiah of spectacle or worldly power. He was truly tempted, as humans are, yet without sin (Hebrews 4:15).

Mission 2Evaluate

"Son of God is the most important title for Jesus."

  • Reasons for: it expresses his divinity, central to the Trinity and the creeds.
  • The Father's voice uses it at the baptism.
  • Reasons against: "Son of Man" is the title Jesus himself uses most. "Christ" became his name for all Christians.
  • For many, "Saviour" (one who rescues) says most about what he does.
  • Another view: each title needs the others: one says who he is, another what he does.
  • Judge it by: Which title did Jesus use? Which do the creeds stress? Which matters most to Christians today?

Every title is a claim about who Jesus is: Christ (Messiah), Son of God, Son of Man, Lord, Saviour.

Mission 3

What a disciple is

  • Disciple = learner, follower. Jesus called twelve: "Come after me, and I will make you fishers for men." (Matthew 4:19)
  • A disciple learns from Jesus and lives like him.
  • The cost: disciples put God first, even above family, wealth and safety (Luke 14:26–27, paraphrase).
  • Christians today see all baptised believers as disciples, not only the Twelve.
  • This mission: what the Gospels (mainly Matthew) say a disciple's life looks like.
Mission 3Read the source

Salt and light

"You are the salt of the earth, but if the salt has lost its flavour, with what will it be salted?" (Matthew 5:13)

"You are the light of the world. A city located on a hill can't be hidden." (Matthew 5:14)

"Even so, let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father who is in heaven." (Matthew 5:16)

  • Salt preserved food and gave flavour: disciples keep the world from going bad and make life better.
  • Light shows the way: disciples' lives point others to God.

Q1 Why should others see a disciple's good works?

Show answer

So they give glory to God, not to the disciple.

Q2 What would "salt that has lost its flavour" be?

Show answer

A disciple who no longer makes a difference.

Mission 3

How to pray (Matthew 6:5–15)

  • Do not give to be praised: give in secret (6:1–4). The same goes for prayer.
  • Do not pray to be seen by others: pray in private to "your Father who is in secret" (6:6).
  • Do not heap up empty words: God knows what you need (6:7–8).
  • The Lord's Prayer (6:9–13): God's name kept holy · God's Kingdom and will · daily bread · forgiveness "as we also forgive" · protection from temptation and evil.
  • Forgiveness is built in: if you forgive others, God will forgive you (6:14–15).
  • Today: the Lord's Prayer is said in almost every Christian tradition, at baptisms, weddings, funerals and daily prayer.

Mission 3

The other cheek (Matthew 5:38–48)

  • The Law said "eye for eye, tooth for tooth" (Exodus 21:24): a limit on revenge in courts.
  • Jesus goes further: do not hit back; if struck on the cheek, "turn to him the other also" (5:39). Give more than is demanded; go the extra mile (5:40–41).
  • "Love your enemies… and pray for those who mistreat you and persecute you" (5:44).
  • God sends sun and rain on good and bad alike; disciples love like God (5:45–48).
  • Divergent readings: some Christians (e.g. many Quakers, Mennonites) are pacifists; most churches read it as rejecting revenge, while allowing self-defence.
Mission 3Read the source

The unforgiving servant (Matthew 18:21–35)

Peter asks: "Lord, how often shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? Until seven times?" Jesus: "I don't tell you until seven times, but, until seventy times seven." (Matthew 18:21–22)

  • Parable: a king cancels a servant's vast debt (10,000 talents: more than a lifetime of wages).
  • The same servant chokes a fellow servant over a small debt (100 denarii: about 100 days' pay) and has him jailed.
  • The king hands the first servant over to punishment. So God will treat those who do not forgive "from your hearts" (18:35, paraphrase).

Q1 Does "seventy times seven" mean exactly 490?

Show answer

No: it means without limit.

Q2 Why should disciples forgive?

Show answer

Because God has forgiven them far more.

Mission 3

Wealth and poverty

  • "No one can serve two masters… You can't serve both God and Mammon." (Matthew 6:24) Mammon = money and possessions.
  • Do not worry about food or clothes: God feeds the birds and clothes the lilies (6:25–32). "But seek first God's Kingdom and his righteousness; and all these things will be given to you as well." (6:33)
  • A rich young man keeps the commandments. Jesus: "go, sell what you have, and give to the poor… and come, follow me." He leaves sad (19:21–22).
  • "It is easier for a camel to go through a needle's eye than for a rich man to enter into God's Kingdom." (19:24) But "with God all things are possible" (19:26).
  • Readings: literal (St Francis; monks and nuns take vows of poverty) · attitude (wealth must not come first) · stewardship (use money for God and others; tithing).
Mission 3

The parable of the sower

  • A farmer scatters seed; it lands on four kinds of ground (Matthew 13:1–9). Jesus explains (13:18–23):
  • Path: hears but does not understand; the evil one snatches the word away.
  • Rocky ground: receives it with joy, but has no root; gives up when trouble or persecution comes.
  • Thorns: hears, but worries and the lure of riches choke it.
  • Good soil: hears, understands and bears fruit: a hundred, sixty or thirty times.
  • The seed is "the word of the Kingdom": the parable is about how people respond to Jesus' teaching.
Quick check
?Jo joins a church youth group with great excitement, but stops coming when friends at school start mocking her faith. Which ground in the parable of the sower is she most like?
Mission 3Diversity

Living the teaching today

  • Salt and light: church foodbanks, debt advice (e.g. Christians Against Poverty), street pastors.
  • Prayer: daily prayer, private and public; the Lord's Prayer at every Mass and most services.
  • Forgiveness: the Corrymeela Community in Northern Ireland (founded 1965) works for reconciliation. Gordon Wilson, whose daughter Marie died in the Enniskillen bombing (1987), publicly said he bore no ill will.
  • Wealth: tithing (giving a tenth); Catholic religious vows of poverty; Christian Aid Week.
  • Christians differ on how literally to take each teaching (pacifism; selling all possessions).
Mission 3Evaluate

"Jesus' teaching is too demanding to live by today."

  • Reasons for: loving enemies and turning the other cheek seem unrealistic against violence. "Sell what you have" would leave families in need. Forgiving without limit may let abusers off.
  • Reasons against: many Christians have lived it (Gordon Wilson; Corrymeela). Jesus also teaches grace: God forgives those who fail (the Lord's Prayer). Forgiving does not mean excusing wrong or giving up on justice.
  • Another view: the teaching sets a direction to grow towards, not a test passed or failed in a day.
  • Judge it by: Is it meant literally? Does it depend on God's help? What would society be like if more people tried?
Mission check

?Amira, a Christian, runs a small business. She gives a large part of her profits to charity without telling anyone, prays alone each morning, and has forgiven a partner who cheated her. Which statement best links her actions to Jesus' teaching?

Jesus' teaching is about the heart: God before praise and money, and mercy because mercy has been received.

Mission 4: Why did Jesus touch lepers and eat with cheats when respectable people kept away?

Mission 4

Agape

  • Agape (Greek): selfless, unconditional love that seeks the good of others, even at a cost.
  • Asked for the greatest commandment, Jesus answers with two (Matthew 22:37–39):
  • "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind." (from Deuteronomy 6:5)
  • "You shall love your neighbour as yourself." (from Leviticus 19:18)
  • Jesus also ate with tax collectors (seen as cheats and traitors for working for Rome) and others whom people called sinners (Mark 2:15–17).
  • Christians believe Jesus not only taught agape: he lived it, especially towards people others shut out.
Mission 4

Healing as agape

  • A leper (Matthew 8:1–3): "Lord, if you want to, you can make me clean." Jesus "stretched out his hand and touched him, saying, 'I want to. Be made clean.'"
  • People with leprosy (a range of skin diseases) were excluded: they lived apart and called out "Unclean!" (Leviticus 13:45–46). Jesus touches him before he is clean.
  • Mark 5:21–42: a woman who has bled for twelve years, also treated as unclean, touches Jesus' clothes and is healed: "Daughter, your faith has made you well. Go in peace" (5:34).
  • Then Jairus' twelve-year-old daughter, already dead: "Talitha cumi!", "Girl, I tell you, get up!" (5:41). She gets up.
  • Significance: healing restores people to community, shows God's power, and is a sign of God's Kingdom.
Quick check
?In Mark 5, Jesus tells the woman healed of bleeding, "Daughter, your faith has made you well." What is most significant about how he treats her?
Mission 4Read the source

Ten lepers (Luke 17:12–19)

  • Ten men with leprosy stand at a distance and call out, "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us!" (17:13)
  • People with leprosy were excluded from towns and worship (Leviticus 13:45–46).
  • Jesus sends them to the priests (who could declare them clean). On the way, all ten are healed.
  • One, a Samaritan, turns back, praises God and thanks Jesus. "Weren't the ten cleansed? But where are the nine?" (17:17)
  • "Get up, and go your way. Your faith has healed you." (17:19)

Show answer

To give thanks to God.

Mission 4Diversity

Following the example today

  • Healthcare: Christians founded many hospitals and the modern hospice movement (Cicely Saunders, St Christopher's Hospice, 1967). The Leprosy Mission (founded 1874) still works today.
  • Healing ministry: Pentecostal and charismatic churches pray for miraculous healing; Catholics, Orthodox and Anglicans anoint the sick.
  • Outcasts: prison chaplaincy, work with homeless people and refugees; eating with those others avoid.
  • Christians differ: some expect miracles today; others see God healing mainly through medicine and care.
Mission 4Evaluate

"Jesus' example of welcoming outcasts is the most important thing for Christians to follow today."

  • Reasons for: agape is the greatest commandment (Matthew 22:37–39). The healings, like the leper Jesus touched, show it in action.
  • The woman healed in Mark 5 is restored to her community, not only cured.
  • Reasons against: worship and love of God come first in the commandments. Some say faith in Jesus matters more than imitating him.
  • Another view: welcoming people and calling them to change belong together.
  • Judge it by: What did Jesus say was greatest? Do welcome and holiness pull against each other? What do Christians actually do?
Mission check

?A church nurse runs a free clinic at a night shelter, treating people whom many others avoid. Which reason would most Christians give, based on Jesus' life?

Jesus' encounters and healings show agape in action: love that crosses boundaries and restores people to community.

Mission 5

Mission 5: In less than a day Jesus went from supper with his friends to a cross. What happened, and why does it matter?

Make a guess: In less than a day Jesus went from supper with his friends to a cross. What happened, and why does it matter?

Mission 5

The last days

  • It is Passover in Jerusalem: the Jewish festival remembering freedom from slavery in Egypt.
  • Thursday evening: the Last Supper with the Twelve; then prayer in Gethsemane; Jesus is arrested after Judas betrays him.
  • Night and early morning: questioned by the Jewish council (Sanhedrin), then sent to Pontius Pilate, the Roman governor.
  • Friday: sentenced, crucified at Golgotha outside the city, dies in the afternoon, and is buried before the Sabbath.
  • Christians remember these days in Holy Week: Maundy Thursday, Good Friday, then Easter.
Mission 5

The trials

  • Before the Jewish council (led by the high priest Caiaphas): false witnesses; the high priest asks whether he is the Christ, the Son of God. His answer is judged blasphemy (Matthew 26:57–68).
  • Meanwhile Peter denies knowing Jesus three times, then weeps (26:69–75).
  • Before Pilate: the charge becomes political: claiming to be "King of the Jews", a threat to Rome.
  • Pilate offers to release a prisoner; the crowd chooses Barabbas. Pilate hands Jesus over to be crucified.
  • Churches today teach that the blame cannot be laid on Jews as a people, then or now (Catholic Church, Nostra Aetate, 1965).
Mission 5Read the source

The crucifixion (Mark 15)

  • Simon of Cyrene is forced to carry the cross to Golgotha, "the place of a skull" (15:21–22).
  • The charge above him reads "THE KING OF THE JEWS". He is crucified between two robbers, and mocked: "He saved others. He can't save himself." (15:26–31)
  • Darkness from noon to three. Jesus cries: "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" (15:34, from Psalm 22:1)
  • He dies; the Temple curtain is torn in two from top to bottom (15:38).
  • The Roman centurion: "Truly this man was the Son of God!" (15:39)

Mission 5

Why the cross matters

  • Christians believe the crucifixion was not a defeat but the centre of God's plan.
  • Salvation: Jesus' death brings forgiveness and restores the relationship with God (atonement; full in an earlier lesson).
  • Who Jesus is: innocent, obedient, forgiving; the King whose throne is a cross.
  • The Son of God (centurion).
  • God shares suffering: Christians believe God knows human pain from the inside.
  • Example: love that gives itself for others (John 15:13).
Quick check
?Why do Christians call the day of Jesus' crucifixion "Good Friday"?
Mission 5Evaluate

"The crucifixion was the most important event of Holy Week."

  • Reasons for: Christians believe salvation comes through Jesus' death (atonement). The cross is the central Christian symbol. Paul: "we preach Christ crucified" (1 Corinthians 1:23).
  • Reasons against: without the resurrection, the cross would be a defeat (1 Corinthians 15:14). The Last Supper gave the Eucharist, the centre of most Christian worship. Gethsemane shows the obedience that makes the cross meaningful.
  • Another view: the events form one story, the Paschal Mystery (see Mission 6): none can be ranked alone.
  • Judge it by: Which event do the others depend on? Which shapes worship most? What do the Gospels give most space to?
Mission check

?A hospital chaplain sits with a dying patient who says, "Does God even know what this is like?" The chaplain points to a cross on the wall. What belief about the crucifixion is the chaplain relying on?

Christians see the cross both as the act that reconciles people to God and as God's presence in human suffering.

Mission 6

What "resurrection" means

  • Christians believe that on the third day after his death God raised Jesus from the dead.
  • Not a ghost: in the Gospels the risen Jesus can be touched and eats food (Luke 24:39–43).
  • Not a resuscitation: Lazarus came back to ordinary life and would die again. Jesus was raised to a new, transformed life that cannot die (Romans 6:9).
  • The same Jesus, still bearing the wounds of the cross, but not always recognised at first.
  • Easter Sunday, the most important Christian festival, celebrates it.
Mission 6Read the source

The empty tomb (Matthew 28)

  • At dawn Mary Magdalene and "the other Mary" go to the tomb. There is an earthquake; an angel rolls back the stone; the guards shake with fear (28:1–4).
  • The angel: "Don't be afraid… He is not here, for he has risen, just like he said." (28:5–6)
  • On their way to tell the disciples, Jesus meets them. They hold his feet and worship him (28:9).
  • "Don't be afraid. Go tell my brothers that they should go into Galilee, and there they will see me." (28:10)

Q1 What is the women's first task? (To tell the disciples.) Q2: How does the women's touch matter?

Show answer

It shows Jesus is bodily risen, not a vision.

Mission 6

The appearances

  • On the road to Emmaus: two disciples walk with a stranger; they recognise Jesus when he breaks bread (Luke 24:13–35).
  • To the disciples in Jerusalem: "See my hands and my feet… a spirit doesn't have flesh and bones" (Luke 24:39). He eats fish.
  • Thomas doubts until he sees the wounds: "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:28)
  • In Galilee: Jesus sends the eleven out to the world (Matthew 28:16–20).
  • Paul lists appearances to Peter, the Twelve, more than 500 at once, James, and finally himself (1 Corinthians 15:5–8).
Quick check
?How does the resurrection of Jesus differ from the raising of Lazarus, according to Christian belief?
Mission 6

Why it matters

  • Who Jesus is: Christians believe the resurrection shows God's approval of Jesus: he is "declared to be the Son of God with power" (Romans 1:4).
  • Victory: Christians believe sin and death are defeated.
  • Hope: Christ is the "first fruit": believers will also be raised (1 Corinthians 15:20).
  • Worship: Christians meet on Sunday, the day of resurrection; Easter is the high point of the year.
  • Life: courage, forgiveness and joy; the first disciples went from hiding to preaching.
Mission 6Diversity

Different understandings

  • Catholic, Orthodox and most Protestant Christians: a real, bodily resurrection and an empty tomb.
  • Some liberal Christians: the resurrection is spiritual: the disciples truly experienced Jesus alive with God; the empty tomb is less central.
  • Orthodox Easter greeting: "Christ is risen!" "Truly he is risen!" Easter Vigil worship in many churches begins in darkness with a new fire and the Paschal candle.
  • Non-religious explanations: the body was stolen (a rumour Matthew 28:11–15 already reports), Jesus did not really die, or visions. Christian replies: Roman executions were thorough; there were many witnesses; disciples suffered for the claim.
Mission 6Evaluate

"Without the resurrection, Christianity would have no meaning."

  • Reasons for: Paul says faith would be "in vain" (1 Corinthians 15:14). The first Christian preaching centres on it (Acts 2:32). Christian hope of eternal life depends on it.
  • Reasons against: Jesus' teaching on love has value in itself; many non-Christians admire it. The Incarnation and the cross also carry meaning. Some liberal Christians, who believe Jesus is alive with God without an empty tomb, find deep meaning in a spiritual reading.
  • Another view: the resurrection gives meaning to the rest, but only together with Jesus' life and death.
  • Judge it by: What did the first Christians say mattered most? Can "Christianity" survive without a risen Jesus? Does "meaning" here mean truth, hope or ethics?
Mission check

?A friend says, "Easter is when Christians believe Jesus' ghost appeared to his followers." Which correction would most Christians (Catholic, Orthodox and Protestant) make?

Not a ghost and not a return to old life: Christians believe Jesus was raised bodily to a life beyond death, and then ascended to the Father.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Christians believe in the Incarnation: God the Son became human in Jesus, fully God and fully human (John 1:14).
  • He was conceived by the Holy Spirit and born of the Virgin Mary (Luke 1:35).

Mission 2

  • Christ = Messiah = "anointed one": the king Jews hoped for from David's family.
  • Son of God expresses Jesus' unique relationship with the Father; Son of Man his humanity, suffering and future glory.
  • Christians see in the baptism and temptations what kind of Son of God he is: obedient, trusting, not seeking power.

Mission 3

  • Jesus calls disciples salt and light: their lives should point others to God (Matthew 5:13–16).
  • Jesus teaches: give and pray humbly and in secret; forgive without limit, because God forgives (Matthew 6, 18).
  • He teaches putting God before wealth; the sower shows that how people respond to his teaching matters.

Mission 4

  • Agape: selfless love of God and neighbour, the two greatest commandments (Matthew 22:37–39).
  • Jesus touched and healed the excluded and welcomed sinners, restoring them to community.

Mission 5

  • Last Supper (bread and cup, new covenant) → Gethsemane → arrest → trials (council, Pilate) → crucifixion → burial.
  • Christians believe Jesus' death was the centre of God's plan: forgiveness, a new covenant, God sharing human suffering.

Mission 6

  • Christians believe God raised Jesus bodily on the third day: the empty tomb and the appearances.
  • Christians believe the resurrection shows who Jesus is and gives hope that death is defeated (1 Corinthians 15).
