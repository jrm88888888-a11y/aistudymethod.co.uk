Mission 1

Mission 1: When does 3 + 4 = 5 in physics?

Make a guess: When does 3 + 4 = 5 in physics?

Mission 1

Scalars and vectors

  • Scalar: magnitude (size) only. Vector: magnitude and direction.
  • Scalars: distance, speed, mass, time, energy, temperature.
  • Vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum.
  • Draw a vector as an arrow: length ∝ magnitude, pointing in its direction.
  • Printed in bold, v; handwritten with an arrow or an underline.
Quick check
?A student records four facts about a school bus trip. Which fact describes a vector quantity?
Mission 1

Adding along a line

  • Choose a positive direction. The opposite direction is negative.
  • 3 m east, then 8 m west. East positive: +3 + (−8) = −5 m, i.e. 5 m west.
  • The sign gives the direction, not the size: −5 m is 5 m long.
  • Two 5 N forces: same direction → 10 N. Opposite directions → 0 N.
Quick check
?A hiker walks 5 m east and then 9 m west along a straight path. Student 1 takes east as the positive direction; Student 2 takes west as the positive direction. Each finds the hiker's resultant displacement as a signed component. Which statement correctly compares their results?
Mission 1

Adding at right angles

  • Draw the vectors tip to tail. The resultant runs from the first tail to the last tip.
  • 3.0 N east + 4.0 N north:
  • Size: √(3.0² + 4.0²) = 5.0 N.
  • Direction: tan θ = 4.0 ÷ 3.0 → θ = 53° north of east.
  • So 3 + 4 = 5, but only for vectors at right angles.
  • Calculate only for perpendicular vectors. Other angles: scale drawing.
Mission 1

Any angle: scale drawing

  • Choose a scale, e.g. 1 cm = 1 N.
  • Draw the first vector. From its tip, draw the second at the given angle.
  • Measure the resultant's length (convert with the scale) and its angle with a protractor.
  • Subtracting: A − B = A + (−B). Reverse B, then add tip to tail.
  • Example: change in velocity, Δv = v − u.
Mission 1

Resolving into components

  • Any vector = two perpendicular components.
  • F at angle θ to the horizontal:
  • Horizontal: F cos θ (side adjacent to θ).
  • Vertical: F sin θ (side opposite θ).
  • 20 N at 30°: 20 cos 30° = 17.3 N; 20 sin 30° = 10.0 N.
  • The two components together replace the original vector.
Mission 1

Components on a slope

  • Choose axes along and perpendicular to the slope.
  • Slope at angle θ: the angle between the weight and the perpendicular to the slope is also θ.
  • Weight W = mg. Along the slope: mg sin θ. Into the slope: mg cos θ.
Quick check
?The diagram shows the velocity of a ball just after it is kicked, drawn with its tail at the origin of an x-y coordinate system with x horizontal and y vertical. The velocity has magnitude v and makes an angle θ with the vertical y-axis. Which expression gives the horizontal component vx of the velocity?
Quick check
?A small block moves up a straight ramp inclined at 30° to the horizontal. Student 1 resolves the block's velocity into horizontal and vertical components. Student 2 resolves the same velocity using an x-axis along the ramp and a y-axis perpendicular to the ramp. Which statement about their results is correct?
Mission check

?A robot vacuum moves 1.1 m east and then 1.6 m north, each along a straight line. What is the magnitude of its displacement from its starting point?

At right angles, sizes do not simply add: √(1.1² + 1.6²) = 1.9 m, not 1.1 + 1.6.

Pythagoras combines them. That is why 3 + 4 can make 5.

Mission 2: With air resistance ignored, which forces still act on a ball once it has left your foot?

Make a guess: With air resistance ignored, which forces still act on a ball once it has left your foot?

Mission 2

A force is an interaction

  • A force is a push or pull by one object on another. Unit: the newton, N.
  • Every force has a source: name it as "force on X by Y".
  • Force is a vector: size and direction.
  • No object exerts a net force on itself. No source, no force.
Mission 2

Weight

  • Weight: the gravitational force on an object by Earth (or another planet or moon).
  • W = mg
  • Mass (kg): how much matter. Same everywhere. Weight (N): changes with g.
  • g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹ near Earth's surface: the gravitational field strength, the force on each kilogram.
  • Weight acts at the centre of gravity. It needs no contact: a field force.
Quick check
?A space probe weighs 4905 N on Earth, where g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹. It lands on Mars, where g = 3.7 N kg⁻¹. What is the probe's mass on Mars?
Mission 2

Normal force, friction, tension

  • Normal contact force: perpendicular to the surface.
  • Friction: parallel to the surface. Opposes sliding, or the tendency to slide, between the surfaces.
  • Tension: the pull of a string, rope or cable, along it, away from the object.
  • Ideal string (light, does not stretch): the same tension all along it, and on both sides of an ideal pulley.
Mission 2

Drawing a free-body diagram

  • One object only. Show every force on it, by something else.
  • Label each arrow with what exerts it. Arrow length shows size.
  • Never draw a "force of motion" or the object's own push on something else.
  • For a particle, start each arrow on a dot.
  • For an extended body, start each arrow where the force acts (weight at the centre of gravity). This matters for moments.
Mission 2

From diagram to resultant

  • Add all the forces as vectors: the resultant (net) force.
  • Resolve each force along two perpendicular axes, then add components.
  • A sledge is pulled by a rope: tension 50 N at 30° above the horizontal; friction 30 N.
  • Horizontal: 50 cos 30° − 30 = 43.3 − 30 = 13.3 N forwards.
  • Vertical: the rope's 50 sin 30° = 25 N upwards, plus the normal force, balances the weight.
Quick check
?A crate is given a push and then slides down a rough ramp on its own. Which list gives all the forces acting on the crate while it slides, and no others?
Mission check

?A hockey puck is hit by a stick and then slides across level ice. Friction and air resistance are negligible. Which forces are exerted on the puck while it slides, after it has left the stick?

Same for the kicked ball: once the foot has gone, only Earth pulls on it (its weight). Nothing pushes it along its path.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Can something be moving fast when all the forces on it cancel out?

Make a guess: Can something be moving fast when all the forces on it cancel out?

Mission 3

Newton's first law

  • A body stays at rest or moves with constant velocity unless a resultant force acts on it.
  • Moving things do not stop "naturally". Friction and drag stop them.
  • No resultant force needed to keep moving; one is needed to change velocity.
Quick check
?A space probe is so far from any star or planet that the forces exerted on it are negligible. Its engines fire briefly and then shut off. Which statement describes the probe's motion after the engines have shut off?
Mission 3

Equilibrium

  • Resultant force: the vector sum of all the forces on a body.
  • Translational equilibrium: resultant force zero → at rest or at constant velocity.
  • A skydiver whose drag equals her weight falls at constant velocity: her terminal velocity. She is in equilibrium.
Mission 3

On a slope

  • Take axes along the slope and perpendicular to it.
  • Weight resolves into mg sin θ down the slope and mg cos θ into the slope.
  • A 5.00 kg block rests on a rough slope at 30.0°. Equilibrium: friction = mg sin θ, normal force = mg cos θ.
  • g = 9.81 m s⁻²: friction = 24.5 N up the slope · normal force = 42.5 N
Quick check
?A block is held at rest on a ramp by a light string that is parallel to the ramp and tied to a post at the top, as shown in the diagram. The ramp is inclined at 37° above the horizontal, friction between the block and the ramp is negligible, and the gravitational force on the block is 50 N. Use sin 37° = 0.60 and cos 37° = 0.80. What is the tension in the string?
Mission 3

Three forces: the closed triangle

  • Three coplanar forces in equilibrium, drawn tip to tail, form a closed triangle.
  • A 50 N lamp is pulled sideways by a horizontal 20 N force; the cord holds it still.
  • Triangle: 50 N down, 20 N sideways, cord tension closes it.
  • Tension = √(50² + 20²) = 53.9 N, at tan⁻¹(20/50) = 21.8° to the vertical.
  • Use a scale drawing, or Pythagoras and trigonometry for a right-angled triangle.
Quick check
?Three forces act at a point. Which set of magnitudes could keep the point in equilibrium?
Quick check
?A hanging basket of mass 3.0 kg is held at rest by two ropes: one horizontal, and one at 55° above the horizontal. Take g = 9.81 m s⁻². What is the tension in the rope at 55°?
Mission check

?Which of the following objects is in translational equilibrium?

Yes. Moving fast or slow, an object with constant velocity has zero net force on it.

A skydiver at terminal velocity falls very fast, typically around 50 m s⁻¹, with zero resultant force: drag balances weight. Zero resultant force means constant velocity, not zero velocity.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Can an object's balance point be in empty space, where there is no material at all?

Make a guess: Can an object's balance point be in empty space, where there is no material at all?

Mission 4

Centre of mass

  • Centre of mass: the single point where the whole mass can be treated as concentrated.
  • A mass-weighted average position of all the bits of the object.
  • Uniform and regular (sphere, cylinder, cube, uniform beam): at the geometric centre.
  • If the mass is spread symmetrically, it lies on every line of symmetry.
  • Uniform ring: at the centre of the hole. No material there.
Quick check
?A cylindrical rod is made of a 0.50 m aluminium section joined end to end to a 0.50 m steel section of the same diameter. Each section is uniform, and the steel section has the greater mass. How does the location of the rod's centre of mass compare with the rod's midpoint?
Mission 4

Centre of gravity

  • Centre of gravity: the point where the whole weight of the body can be taken to act.
  • In a uniform gravitational field it is at the centre of mass.
  • Uniform beam: draw its weight at the middle. This is how you take moments for an extended body.
Mission check

?A high jumper clears the bar by arching her back, so that her body is draped over the bar in an upside-down U shape, with her head and arms hanging on one side and her legs on the other. Which statement about her centre of mass, at the instant her hips are directly above the bar, can be correct?

Yes. The centre of mass is an average position of the mass. For a ring, a horseshoe or an arched jumper, that average lies in empty space.

Mission 5

Mission 5: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Make a guess: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Mission 5

The turning effect of a force

  • Moment of a force about a point = force × perpendicular distance from the point to the force's line of action. Moment = Fd.
  • Unit: N m.
  • Same force, longer perpendicular distance: bigger turning effect.
  • A force whose line of action passes through the pivot has zero turning effect.
  • Each moment is clockwise or anticlockwise.
Quick check
?A mechanic pulls with a 20 N force on the end of a tool handle, 0.25 m from the centre of a nut. The force is in the plane of the handle's turn, but at 30° to the handle instead of at 90°. What is the turning effect of the force about the centre of the nut?
Mission 5

Balanced turning effects

  • Principle of moments: for a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point = the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.
  • Full equilibrium needs both: resultant force = 0 and resultant moment = 0.
  • See-saw: child of weight W at 3d balances an adult of weight 3W at d. W × 3d = 3W × d.
Mission 5

Worked example

  • Uniform plank, 3.0 m long, weight 120 N, rests on a pivot 1.0 m from end A.
  • Its weight acts at its centre of gravity, the midpoint: 1.5 m from A, so 0.50 m beyond the pivot.
  • Moment of the plank's weight about the pivot = 120 × 0.50 = 60 N m.
  • Weight W hung at A balances it: W × 1.0 = 60, so W = 60 N.
  • Resultant force also zero: pivot pushes up with 120 + 60 = 180 N.
  • Tip: take moments about the point where an unknown force acts. Its moment there is zero.
Quick check
?A uniform beam of length 5.0 m and weight 400 N rests horizontally on two supports, one at each end, P and Q. A person of weight 600 N stands on the beam 1.0 m from Q. What is the upward force from support P?
Mission 5

Couples

  • A couple: two equal, opposite, parallel forces whose lines of action do not coincide.
  • Resultant force = zero, but they turn the body.
  • Moment of a couple = one force × perpendicular distance between the lines of action.
  • The same about any point.
  • Steering wheel, diameter 0.36 m, 25 N from each hand: 25 × 0.36 = 9.0 N m.
Quick check
?A tap handle is turned by two equal and opposite forces of 12 N, applied at the two ends of a handle 0.050 m long, each perpendicular to the handle. What is the turning effect of this couple?
Mission check

?A uniform board of weight W rests horizontally on two supports: support P at its left end and support Q one-quarter of the board's length from its right end. The diagram shows a student's force diagram for the board: FP and FQ are the forces exerted on the board by the supports, and W is drawn at the board's centre. Which statement about the student's diagram is correct?

The support farther from the board's midpoint pushes up with the smaller force. On the see-saw, the child sits three times as far from the pivot: W × 3d balances 3W × d.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Vectors have direction: in a line, signs show it; at right angles, combine by Pythagoras and tan θ.
  • Resolve: F cos θ along the side adjacent to θ, F sin θ along the opposite side.
  • Non-perpendicular vectors: scale drawing.

Mission 2

  • Every force is exerted on one object by another; a free-body diagram shows only the forces on the chosen object, each with its source.
  • Weight = mg (Earth's pull); normal force ⟂ surface; friction ∥ surface; tension along the string.

Mission 3

  • Zero resultant force ⇔ constant velocity (including at rest): translational equilibrium.
  • On a slope, resolve along and perpendicular to the surface: mg sin θ and mg cos θ.
  • Three forces in equilibrium form a closed triangle.

Mission 4

  • Centre of mass: the mass-weighted average position; at the centre of a uniform regular object, on every line of symmetry, and possibly outside the material.
  • Centre of gravity: where the whole weight acts.

Mission 5

  • Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action. In equilibrium: Σ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments, and resultant force = 0.
  • A couple: equal, opposite, parallel forces; its moment = one force × the perpendicular distance between them.
