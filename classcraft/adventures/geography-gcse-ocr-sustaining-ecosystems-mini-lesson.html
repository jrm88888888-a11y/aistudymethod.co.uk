Ecosystems

What is an ecosystem?

An ecosystem is the way living things (plants, animals) and non-living things (climate, soil, water) are linked and depend on each other — their interdependence.

Large-scale ecosystems are called biomes. Their global distribution follows climate:

  • Tropical rainforests — around the Equator (hot, wet all year).
  • Hot deserts — ~30° N/S (sinking air, dry).
  • Grasslands — tropical (savanna) and temperate.
  • Temperate forests — mid-latitudes (e.g. UK).
  • Polar regions — high latitudes (very cold).

Key term — interdependence: if one part changes (e.g. trees removed), the others are affected (soil erodes, animals lose habitat).

Tropical rainforest

Inside a tropical rainforest

Tropical rainforests are the most biodiverse land ecosystem. Their key features:

  • Climate: hot (~27 °C) and wet (over 2,000 mm rain) all year.
  • Structure: layered — emergents, canopy, under-canopy, shrub layer.
  • Nutrient cycle: fast — dead leaves decompose quickly in the heat, so most nutrients are stored in the biomass (living things), not the soil.
  • Soil: thin and infertile once trees are removed; heavy rain leaches nutrients.
  • Adaptations: tall trunks, drip-tip leaves, buttress roots, lianas.

Misconception: rainforest soils are not very fertile. The nutrients are locked in the plants, so when the forest is cleared the soil quickly loses fertility.

Threats

Why are rainforests exploited?

Rainforests provide goods (timber, foods, medicines) and services (storing carbon, regulating climate, biodiversity). But human activity threatens them:

  • Logging — for hardwood timber.
  • Agriculture — clearing for cattle ranching and crops (e.g. soy, palm oil).
  • Mineral extraction — mining for gold, iron ore, oil.
  • Tourism — can damage habitats if poorly managed.

Deforestation reduces biodiversity, releases stored carbon and degrades soil.

Sustainable management

Managing rainforests sustainably

OCR wants a case study of sustainable rainforest management (local/regional). Widely-taught: Amazonia (Brazil). Strategies include:

  • Ecotourism — small-scale, low-impact tourism that funds conservation and gives locals income.
  • Selective logging and sustainable forestry — only some trees felled; replanting.
  • Community programmes and biosphere reserves — protected zones managed with local people.

Sustainable means meeting today's needs without stopping future generations meeting theirs — using the forest while conserving it.

Polar environments

Polar and tundra environments

OCR contrasts rainforests with cold polar environments (Antarctica and the Arctic):

  • Climate: extremely cold, dry, with long dark winters and 24-hour daylight in summer.
  • Antarctica: a frozen continent, no permanent human population, home to penguins, seals and krill.
  • Arctic: a frozen ocean surrounded by land; polar bears, and indigenous peoples (e.g. the Inuit).
  • Fragile & interdependent: short food chains mean damage spreads quickly; slow growth means slow recovery.
Threats & management

Threats to polar environments

Human activity threatens polar regions:

  • Climate change — melting sea ice threatens polar bears; the Arctic is warming fast.
  • Fishing, whaling, mineral & oil exploitation — disturb wildlife and habitats.
  • Tourism and scientific research — can cause pollution and disturbance.

Management includes sustainable tourism, conservation, and global agreements — the Antarctic Treaty (1959) reserves Antarctica for peaceful science and bans mining, and Earth Summits coordinate global action.

✎Explain how ecotourism can help manage a rainforest sustainably. Use the words income, local people and conservation.
Model answer
  • Ecotourism is small-scale, low-impact tourism that brings visitors to see the forest without damaging it.
  • It gives local people income from guiding, lodges and crafts, so they value the living forest more than clearing it.
  • Some money funds conservation, and rules limit numbers, so the ecosystem is protected while still being used — a sustainable balance.

Recap

The key ideas to know

Ecosystem: interdependence of climate, soil, water, plants, animals; biomes follow climate.

Rainforest: hot & wet; nutrients stored in biomass; soil thin; high biodiversity.

Threats: logging, agriculture, mining, tourism → deforestation.

Sustainable management: ecotourism, selective logging, biosphere reserves (e.g. Amazonia).

Polar: cold, fragile, interdependent; threats from climate change, fishing, mining.

Global action: the Antarctic Treaty (1959) and Earth Summits.
