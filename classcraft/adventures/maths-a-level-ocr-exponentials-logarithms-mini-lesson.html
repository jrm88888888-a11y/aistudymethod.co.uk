Exponentials and logarithms · exponential functions and e

The graph of y=axy = a^x

  • For a>0a > 0, y=axy = a^x is an exponential function: the variable is in the power.
  • Every graph passes through (0,1)(0, 1), because a0=1a⁰ = 1.
  • y>0y > 0 for all xx. The xx-axis, y=0y = 0, is an asymptote.
  • a>1a > 1: increasing (growth). 0<a<10 < a < 1: decreasing (decay).
  • y=a−x=(a1​)xy = a^(−x) = (1/a)^x is the reflection of y=axy = a^x in the yy-axis.

Compounding more often

Question
£1 earns 100% interest a year. Find its value after one year if the interest is paid once, twice, 4 times and 12 times, each time at an equal share of the rate.
  1. 1
    Paid nn times, each payment multiplies by (1+n1​)(1 + 1/n), so £1 becomes (1+n1​)n(1 + 1/n)ⁿ
  2. 2
    Once: (1+1)1=2(1 + 1)¹ = 2. Twice: 1.52=2.251.5² = 2.25
  3. 3
    4 times: 1.254=2.441…1.25⁴ = 2.441…. 12 times: (1+121​)12=2.613…(1 + 1/12)¹² = 2.613…

Exponentials and logarithms · exponential functions and e

The number e

  • As nn grows, (1+n1​)n(1 + 1/n)ⁿ gets closer to a fixed number, e =2.71828…= 2.71828… (irrational, like ππ).
  • Interest added continuously at rate rr per year turns PP into PertPe^rt after tt years: continuous compound interest.
  • y=exy = e^x has the shape of every axa^x with a>1a > 1: through (0,1)(0, 1), asymptote y=0y = 0.
  • y=eax+b+cy = e^(ax+b) + c has asymptote y=cy = c and meets the yy-axis at eb+ce^b + c.
Exponentials and logarithms · exponential functions and e

Why e is special: its gradient

  • The gradient of y=exy = e^x at any point equals its yy-value. At (1,e)(1, e) the gradient is e.
  • More generally:
dxd​(ekx)=kekxd/dx(e^kx) = ke^kx
  • So for y=Aekxy = Ae^kx, the rate of change is kyky: proportional to yy.
  • That is why exponential models suit populations, money, radioactive decay and cooling: the bigger the amount, the faster it changes.

Exponentials and logarithms · logarithms and their laws

A logarithm is a power

  • loga​blog_a b is the power you raise aa to in order to get bb:
ax=b⟺x=loga​b(a>0, a=1, b>0)a^x = b ⇔ x = log_a b (a > 0, a ≠ 1, b > 0)
  • Example: 25=322⁵ = 32, so log2​32=5log₂ 32 = 5.
  • Always: loga​a=1log_a a = 1 and loga​1=0log_a 1 = 0.
  • The natural logarithm lnx=loge​xln x = log_e x. So lnex=xln e^x = x and elnx=xe^(ln x) = x: they are inverse functions.
  • y=lnxy = ln x is the reflection of y=exy = e^x in y=xy = x: through (1,0)(1, 0), asymptote x=0x = 0, defined only for x>0x > 0.

Exponentials and logarithms · logarithms and their laws

The laws of logarithms

  • Because logs are powers, the index laws become:

loga​x+loga​y=loga​(xy)loga​x−loga​y=loga​(yx​)log_a x + log_a y = log_a (xy) log_a x − log_a y = log_a(x/y) kloga​x=loga​(xk), e.g. −loga​x=loga​x1​, −21​loga​x=loga​x​1​klog_a x = log_a(x^k), e.g. −log_a x = log_a 1/x, −1/2log_a x = log_a 1/(√x)

  • Proof of the first: let x=apx = a^p, y=aqy = a^q. Then xy=ap+qxy = a^(p+q), so loga​(xy)=p+qlog_a(xy) = p + q.
  • Power law: xk=(ap)k=akpx^k = (a^p)^k = a^kp, so loga​(xk)=kp=kloga​xlog_a(x^k) = kp = klog_a x.
  • There is no law for loga​(x+y)log_a(x + y).

Solving ax=ba^x = b by taking logs

  • Take lnln (or log10​log₁₀) of both sides; the power law brings xx down:
ax=b⟹xlna=lnb⟹x=lnalnb​a^x = b ⇒ xln a = ln b ⇒ x = (ln b)/(ln a)
  • For eax+b=pe^(ax+b) = p, take lnln: ax+b=lnpax + b = ln p. For ln(ax+b)=qln (ax + b) = q, use e: ax+b=eqax + b = e^q.
  • Inequalities: if 0<a<10 < a < 1 then lna<0ln a < 0, so dividing by lnaln a reverses the inequality sign.
  • For OCR, solving inequalities like this (for example in compound-interest and other sequence models) is A-level only content, not AS.

Mission check

?A sheet of paper is 0.1 mm thick and each fold doubles its thickness. The Moon is on average 384,400 km away. How many folds until the stack reaches the Moon?

You need 0.1×2n≥3.844×10110.1 × 2ⁿ ≥ 3.844 × 10¹¹ mm, so 2n≥3.844×10122ⁿ ≥ 3.844 × 10¹². Taking logs, n≥ln2ln(3.844×1012)​=41.8n ≥ (ln (3.844 × 10¹²))/(ln 2) = 41.8. So 42 folds: doubling grows so fast that a logarithm turns an astronomical distance into a small number.

Exponentials and logarithms · modelling with exponentials and logs

Exponential growth and decay models

  • y=Aekty = Ae^kt: growth if k>0k > 0, decay if k<0k < 0. Equivalently y=Abty = Ab^t.
  • AA is the initial value, the value when t=0t = 0.
  • The rate of change is kyky: proportional to the amount present.
  • To find the constants: put t=0t = 0 to get AA, then use a second reading and take ln to get kk.

Models that level off

  • Some quantities decay towards a value other than 0. Cooling tea approaches room temperature.
  • Newton's law of cooling (a model): the excess temperature above the room decays exponentially. In a 20 °C room:
θ=20+Ae−ktθ = 20 + Ae^(−kt)
  • As t→∞t → ∞, e−kt→0e^(−kt) → 0, so θ→20θ → 20: a horizontal asymptote. Initially θ=20+Aθ = 20 + A.
  • Limitations: unlimited growth is impossible (food, space); a constant room temperature is an assumption. Refine the model when its predictions disagree with data.

Turning curves into straight lines with logs

  • y=axny = axⁿ: take logs, logy=nlogx+logalog y = nlog x + log a. Plot logylog y against logxlog x: a straight line, gradient nn, intercept logalog a.
  • y=kbxy = kb^x: take logs, logy=xlogb+logklog y = xlog b + log k. Plot logylog y against xx: a straight line, gradient logblog b, intercept logklog k.
  • A straight line on the log graph tells you which model fits. Undo the logs to recover the constants: a=10intercepta = 10^intercept (or eintercepte^intercept for ln).

Mission check

?Tea cools from 90 °C to 60 °C in 10 minutes in a 20 °C room. Using θ=20+70e−ktθ = 20 + 70e^(−kt), when will it be 40 °C?

The excess over the room drops from 70 to 40 in 10 minutes, so e−10k=74​e^(−10k) = 4/7 and k=101​ln1.75=0.0560k = 1/10ln 1.75 = 0.0560. For 40 °C the excess is 20: e−kt=72​e^(−kt) = 2/7, so t=kln3.5​=22.4t = (ln 3.5)/k = 22.4 minutes. Cooling slows down, so the second 20 degrees take longer than the first 30.

Recap

Big ideas

  • y=axy = a^x (a>0a > 0) passes through (0,1)(0, 1), stays above the asymptote y=0y = 0, grows if a>1a > 1 and decays if 0<a<10 < a < 1.
  • (1+n1​)n→e=2.718…(1 + 1/n)ⁿ → e = 2.718…; continuous compounding gives PertPe^rt.
  • The gradient of ekxe^kx is kekxke^kx: rate of change proportional to size, the signature of exponential growth and decay.
  • ax=b⟺x=loga​ba^x = b ⇔ x = log_a b; lnxln x is loge​xlog_e x, the inverse of exe^x.
  • loga​x+loga​y=loga​xylog_a x + log_a y = log_a xy, loga​x−loga​y=loga​yx​log_a x − log_a y = log_a x/y, kloga​x=loga​xkklog_a x = log_a x^k; check that every log in your answer is defined.
  • To solve ax=ba^x = b, take logs of both sides: x=lnalnb​x = (ln b)/(ln a); dividing by a negative log reverses an inequality.
  • y=Aekty = Ae^kt: AA is the initial value; find kk from a second reading by taking ln. Models like θ=20+Ae−ktθ = 20 + Ae^(−kt) level off at 20.
  • Every model has limits: check long-term predictions and refine when they become unrealistic.
  • y=axny = axⁿ gives a straight line of logylog y against logxlog x (gradient nn); y=kbxy = kb^x gives a straight line of logylog y against xx (gradient logblog b).
