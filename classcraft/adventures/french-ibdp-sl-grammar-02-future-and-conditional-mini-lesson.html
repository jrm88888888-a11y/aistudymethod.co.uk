IB French B Grammar

The future & conditional

These two tenses share one stem and split by their endings. The future (le futur simple) says what will happen; the conditional (le conditionnel) says what would happen. Master the stem, the two sets of endings, and the irregulars, and you unlock both at once — plus their compound "perfect" partners.

future = "will" · conditional = "would"same stem — different endings

Formation · the future

Le futur simple

The future is built on the infinitive (for -re verbs, drop the final -e) plus one set of endings: -ai / -as / -a / -ons / -ez / -ont:

parler → stem parler-
je parlerai
tu parleras
il parlera
nous parlerons
vous parlerez
ils parleront
finir → stem finir-
je finirai
tu finiras
il finira
nous finirons
vous finirez
ils finiront
vendre → stem vendr-
je vendrai
tu vendras
il vendra
nous vendrons
vous vendrez
ils vendront

Handy check: the future endings echo the present tense of avoir (ai, as, a, — , — , ont) — a memory hook that never fails.

Formation · the conditional

Le conditionnel

The conditional keeps the same stem as the future but takes the imperfect endings: -ais / -ais / -ait / -ions / -iez / -aient:

parler → conditional
je parlerais
tu parlerais
il parlerait
nous parlerions
vous parleriez
ils parleraient

The one-letter difference: future je parlerai vs conditional je parlerais — the -s is the whole clue. Use the conditional for "would", for politeness (je voudrais), and in the "if" sentence after an imperfect si-clause.

Compound · one step ahead

The future perfect (futur antérieur)

To say something will have happened — finished before another future point — use the future of the auxiliary plus the past participle:

avoir / être (future) + past participle= "will have done"
  • Lundi, j'aurai fini le projet. — By Monday I will have finished.
  • Quand tu arriveras, elle sera partie. — When you arrive, she will have left.

Same participle rules: être verbs and reflexives agree (elle sera partie), just as in the passé composé.

Compound · the other partner

The conditional perfect (conditionnel passé)

To say what would have happened, use the conditional of the auxiliary plus the past participle:

avoir / être (conditional) + past participle= "would have done"
  • J'aurais fait mieux. — I would have done better.
  • Elle serait venue, mais… — She would have come, but… (être → venue)

Exam gold: the pair si + pluperfect → conditional perfect — Si j'avais su, je serais venu ("If I had known, I would have come") — is a reliable IB French B marker.

Where they show up

Two "if" sentences

The future and conditional each belong to a classic si-construction:

  • Real / likely: Si + présent → futur. Si j'ai le temps, je viendrai. — "If I have time, I will come."
  • Hypothetical: Si + imparfait → conditionnel. Si j'avais le temps, je viendrais. — "If I had time, I would come."
  • Past hypothetical: Si + plus-que-parfait → conditionnel passé. Si j'avais eu le temps, je serais venu.

Trap: never put the future or conditional directly after si in an "if"-clause — the si-clause takes present, imperfect or pluperfect.

Recap

Your future & conditional toolkit

Future: infinitive (−e for -re verbs) + -ai/-as/-a/-ons/-ez/-ont = "will".

Conditional: same stem + -ais/-ais/-ait/-ions/-iez/-aient = "would".

Irregular stems: ser-, aur-, ir-, fer-, viendr-, pourr-, voudr-, devr-, verr-, saur-, faudr-, enverr-.

Compounds: future of aux. + participle = future perfect (j'aurai fini); conditional of aux. + participle = conditional perfect (j'aurais fait).
