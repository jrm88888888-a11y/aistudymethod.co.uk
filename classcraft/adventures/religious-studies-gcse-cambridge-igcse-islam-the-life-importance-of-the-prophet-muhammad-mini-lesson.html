Islam: The Life & Importance of the Prophet Muhammad

This mini-lesson follows the 0490 syllabus on Prophet Muhammad (pbuh): his early life and trustworthy character, the first revelation in the cave of Hira, the reasons for his persecution in Makkah, the hijrah, and his significance as God's final messenger and the Seal of the Prophets.

Work through each screen, answer the questions as you go and collect ⭐ stars. Religious Studies asks you to know beliefs and teachings accurately and to evaluate them fairly, so watch for the screens that set out a range of views. Press Start when you're ready.

Respect first

How Muslims speak of the Prophet

Muslims add peace be upon him (often written pbuh, or in Arabic sallallahu alayhi wa sallam) after the Prophet's name, as a mark of respect. The Cambridge syllabus itself writes 'Prophet Muhammad (pbuh)'.

Two things to get right from the start: Muslims do not worship Muhammad (pbuh) — worship belongs to Allah alone — and Islam is not 'Muhammadanism'. He is the messenger, not the message.

Life · Makkah

Early life and character

Muhammad (pbuh) was born in Makkah, orphaned young, and raised first by his grandfather and then his uncle Abu Talib. He worked as a merchant and married Khadijah, a successful businesswoman.

  • The syllabus highlights his trustworthy character: he was known as al-Amin — 'the trustworthy'.
  • Makkah at the time was a trading city whose Kaʿbah housed many idols.

Why examiners care: his reputation for honesty before any revelation is used by Muslims as evidence that he was not an impostor.

The first revelation

2Where and how did the first revelation come to Muhammad (pbuh)?
Revelation

The cave of Hira

Muhammad (pbuh) used to withdraw to meditate in a cave on Mount Hira. There, aged about forty, he received the first revelation through the angel Jibril (Gabriel), beginning with the command 'Iqra' — 'Read!' or 'Recite!' (Surah 96:1–5).

  • The experience terrified him; Khadijah comforted him and became the first Muslim.
  • Revelations continued for around twenty-three years and together make up the Qurʾan.

Significance: Muslims believe the Qurʾan is the direct word of Allah, not Muhammad's composition. That is why its Arabic wording is treated with such care.

Life · persecution

Why Makkah turned against him

When Muhammad (pbuh) began preaching, the leaders of Makkah opposed him. The syllabus asks for the reasons for his persecution:

  • He preached tawhid — that Allah is one — which threatened the idols in the Kaʿbah and the pilgrim trade that came with them.
  • He taught the equality of rich and poor, and of enslaved and free, before God — which threatened the social order.
  • He warned of a Day of Judgement, challenging the powerful.

Early Muslims, especially those without tribal protection, suffered boycott, abuse and violence.

Life · the hijrah

The hijrah to Madinah

In 622 CE the Prophet and his followers migrated to Yathrib, afterwards called Madinah — 'the city of the Prophet'. This migration is the hijrah.

  • The Muslim (Hijri) calendar counts its years from the hijrah — not from the Prophet's birth.
  • In Madinah the first Muslim community was established and the first mosque built.

Its importance for Islam today: the hijrah shows that faith may require leaving what is comfortable, and it marks the moment Islam became a community with a shared life, not just a private belief.

Beliefs

The Seal of the Prophets

Muslims believe Allah sent many prophets — including Adam, Ibrahim (Abraham), Musa (Moses) and ʿIsa (Jesus) — with the same essential message. Muhammad (pbuh) is the last: the Seal of the Prophets (Surah 33:40).

  • The Qurʾan is therefore Allah's final revelation, needing no successor.
  • Belief in the prophets — risalah — is one of the Six Articles of Faith.

Respectful accuracy: Muslims honour Jesus (ʿIsa) as a great prophet, but do not accept that he was the son of God. Say this precisely; it is a common exam question.

Sources of authority

Sunnah and Hadith

Because Muslims believe the Prophet lived the Qurʾan, his example is itself a source of guidance.

  • Sunnah — the Prophet's way of life: his practice, his decisions, his manner.
  • Hadith — the recorded reports of what he said and did, gathered and sifted for reliability by later scholars.

The Qurʾan describes the Messenger as an excellent example for those who hope in Allah (Surah 33:21). Together, Qurʾan and Sunnah are the two main sources of Shariʿah.

Impact

The Prophet in daily Muslim life

The syllabus wants the impact of belief, not just the belief. Muslims follow the Prophet's example in ordinary things:

  • Prayer (salah) follows the pattern he taught.
  • Manners — greeting, eating, kindness to neighbours, treatment of animals — are shaped by Hadith.
  • Muslims say 'peace be upon him' whenever his name is spoken.
  • Because of the emphasis on Allah's oneness, Islamic art avoids images of the Prophet and of living beings, using calligraphy and pattern instead.

Evaluation

How Muslims discuss his importance

Evaluation questions here often ask: 'Muhammad (pbuh) is the most important figure in Islam.' Discuss.

  • Agree — without him there is no Qurʾan in human hands, no Sunnah, no ummah. The shahadah names him alongside Allah.
  • Disagree — Muslims would say the greatest is Allah; to elevate the Prophet too far edges towards shirk (associating partners with God), the gravest sin in Islam.
  • Sunni and Shiʿah Muslims also differ over what happened to leadership after his death — the subject of a separate lesson.

The best answers hold both: supremely important as messenger, but never worshipped.

Recap

The big ideas to know

Character: Al-Amin, the trustworthy, before any revelation

First revelation: Cave of Hira; angel Jibril; Iqra (Surah 96:1–5)

Persecution: Tawhid threatened the idols, the pilgrim trade and the social order

Hijrah: 622 CE, Makkah to Madinah; start of the Hijri calendar

Final messenger: Seal of the Prophets (Surah 33:40); Qurʾan is the final revelation

Authority: Qurʾan and Sunnah; Hadith record his words and deeds
