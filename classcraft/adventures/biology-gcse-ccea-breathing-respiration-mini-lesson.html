Respiration · what it is

Respiration is not breathing

Respiration is the chemical process that releases energy from glucose. It happens in all living cells, all the time — the aerobic form takes place mainly in the mitochondria.

Breathing (ventilation) is completely different: it is the movement of air in and out of the lungs. Breathing supplies the oxygen and removes the carbon dioxide that respiration uses and produces — but breathing itself releases no energy.

Watch out: respiration releases energy — it does not "make" or "create" energy. And it happens in every living cell, not just in the lungs.

Respiration · aerobic

Aerobic respiration

Aerobic respiration uses oxygen to fully break down glucose. It releases a large amount of energy per glucose molecule.

glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water(energy is released)  ·  symbol: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
  • Reactants: glucose and oxygen.
  • Products: carbon dioxide and water.
  • Site: mainly the mitochondria of the cell.
  • Energy: a lot — glucose is completely broken down.

Balanced symbol equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O. Notice there are 6 carbons, 12 hydrogens and 18 oxygens on each side — it balances.

Respiration · anaerobic

Anaerobic respiration

Anaerobic respiration is respiration without oxygen. Glucose is only partly broken down, so it releases much less energy per glucose than aerobic respiration. CCEA needs both of these word equations:

glucose → lactic acidin mammalian muscle during hard exercise (a little energy released)
glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxidein yeast — called fermentation (a little energy released)
  • In muscle: during vigorous exercise, oxygen can't reach the muscles fast enough, so they respire anaerobically and make lactic acid.
  • The lactic acid builds up, causing an oxygen debt and muscle fatigue. Extra oxygen is needed afterwards to break it down.
  • In yeast: yeast respires anaerobically to make ethanol + carbon dioxide — used in brewing (alcohol) and baking (the CO₂ makes bread rise).

Key difference: anaerobic respiration releases far less energy per glucose than aerobic, because the glucose is not fully broken down.

Breathing · the respiratory system

The breathing system

Air travels from the nasal cavity down the trachea, which splits into two bronchi, then into smaller bronchioles, ending in tiny air sacs called alveoli — where gas exchange happens.

Also learn: the lungs sit inside the ribs and are protected by pleural membranes with a thin layer of pleural fluid between them, which reduces friction as the lungs move.

Breathing · ventilation

How we breathe in and out

Breathing works by changing the volume of the thorax, which changes the pressure inside. Air always moves from high pressure to low pressure.

Breathing IN (inhalation)

Intercostal muscles contract → ribs move up and out.

Diaphragm contracts and flattens (moves down).

Thorax volume increases → pressure inside decreases → air moves IN.

Breathing OUT (exhalation)

Intercostal muscles relax → ribs move down and in.

Diaphragm relaxes and domes up.

Thorax volume decreases → pressure inside increases → air is pushed OUT.

Remember the chain: muscles → volume → pressure → air movement. Breathing in and breathing out are the exact opposites of each other.

Breathing · gas exchange

Gas exchange at the alveoli

At the alveoli, gases move by diffusion down their concentration gradients:

  • Oxygen diffuses from the alveolus into the blood.
  • Carbon dioxide diffuses from the blood into the alveolus to be breathed out.

Exchange surfaces are adapted to make diffusion fast: a large surface area, thin walls (short diffusion distance), moist, permeable, a good blood supply, and a steep concentration gradient kept up by breathing.

The big ideas to know

Respiration ≠ breathing: respiration releases energy in all cells; breathing moves air in and out of the lungs

Aerobic: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (lots of energy) · C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O

Anaerobic (muscle): glucose → lactic acid (oxygen debt) · (yeast): glucose → ethanol + CO₂ — much less energy

Breathing in: intercostals + diaphragm contract → thorax bigger → pressure lower → air in (out = the opposite)

Gas exchange: O₂ into blood, CO₂ out, by diffusion; exhaled air = less O₂, more CO₂ & water vapour
