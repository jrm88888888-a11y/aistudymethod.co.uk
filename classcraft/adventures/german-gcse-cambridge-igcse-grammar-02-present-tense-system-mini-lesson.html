The present-tense system

The present tense is the engine of German — you'll use it across all five of Cambridge's topic areas, every paper. This lesson builds it: the regular endings, the strong vowel-change verbs (fährt, gibt, liest, spricht), the two must-know irregulars sein and haben, and the separable verbs that split in two (ich stehe … auf).

What the present tense does

One tense, three English ideas

A single German present-tense verb can be translated three ways in English. There is no separate word for "am/do":

  • Ich spiele Tennis. → I play tennis.
  • Ich spiele Tennis. → I am playing tennis.
  • Ja, ich spiele Tennis. → I do play tennis.

Why this matters: don't try to build a continuous tense with an extra verb. German has no "am ...-ing" — the plain present already carries all three meanings.

Regular verbs

The regular endings

Take the infinitive (e.g. spielen, to play), remove -en to get the stem spiel-, and add the endings:

Same pattern for wohnen (to live), machen (to do/make), hören (to listen), kaufen (to buy). Sie with a capital S is the polite "you" and takes the -en form, just like "they".

Small spelling tweaks

When the stem needs an extra -e-

Two neat rules keep verbs pronounceable:

  • Stem ends in -t or -d → add an extra -e- before st and t: arbeiten → du arbeitest, er arbeitet (to work); likewise finden → du findest.
  • Stem ends in -s, -ß or -z → the du form adds only -t (the s is already there): heißen → du heißt (to be called), tanzen → du tanzt.

Careful with ß: du heißt keeps its ß — never write heisst or heißst.

Strong (vowel-change) verbs

The vowel flips in du and er

Many common verbs change their stem vowel — but only in the du and er/sie/es forms. Everywhere else they're regular:

a → ä · fahren (to go/drive)
ich fahre
du fährst
er fährt
wir fahren
ihr fahrt
sie fahren
e → i · sprechen (to speak)
ich spreche
du sprichst
er spricht
wir sprechen
ihr sprecht
sie sprechen

Two vowel shifts to know: a → ä (fahren, schlafen, tragen) and e → i / ie (geben → gibt, lesen → liest, sehen → sieht). The wir and ihr forms never change.

The two must-know irregulars

sein and haben

These two are so common — and so irregular — that Cambridge expects the full present tense of each. Learn them by heart:

sein — to be
ich bin
du bist
er ist
wir sind
ihr seid
sie/Sie sind
haben — to have
ich habe
du hast
er hat
wir haben
ihr habt
sie/Sie haben

Age uses sein: German says Ich bin 15 Jahre alt ("I am 15") — with sein, not haben. haben is for possessions: Ich habe einen Hund.

Verbs that split

Separable verbs

Some verbs have a prefix that breaks off in a main clause. The verb is conjugated in second position, and the prefix jumps to the very end:

aufstehen → ich stehe um 7 Uhr aufthe prefix "auf" waits at the end of the clause
  • fernsehen → Ich sehe abends fern. — I watch TV in the evening.
  • einkaufen → Wir kaufen im Supermarkt ein. — We shop in the supermarket.
  • mitkommen → Kommst du mit? — Are you coming along?

Spot them by the prefix: auf-, ein-, mit-, an-, aus-, fern-, zu-. In the dictionary the verb is written joined (aufstehen), but in a sentence it splits.

Two present-tense jobs

The present does more than "now"

German uses the plain present in two places where English uses something else:

  • The near future. With a time word, the present covers "will/going to": Morgen spiele ich Tennis. — Tomorrow I'll play tennis.
  • "Since / for" with seit. An action still going on stays in the present: Ich lerne seit zwei Jahren Deutsch. — I have been learning German for two years.

Trap: "I have been learning" feels like a past tense in English, but German keeps it present because you are still learning now. seit also triggers the dative (more in cluster 4).

Recap

Your present-tense toolkit

Regular endings: -e, -st, -t, -en, -t, -en (ich, du, er, wir, ihr, sie/Sie).

Spelling: stem in -t/-d adds -e- (du arbeitest); stem in -s/-ß/-z → du form just -t (du heißt).

Strong verbs: vowel changes only in du & er — a → ä (fährt), e → i/ie (gibt, liest, spricht).

sein: bin, bist, ist, sind, seid, sind · haben: habe, hast, hat, haben, habt, haben. Age uses sein.

Separable verbs: prefix jumps to the end — Ich stehe … auf.

Extra jobs: present covers the near future, and stays present after seit.
