Learning and cognition

The Learning and cognition context asks how we learn and how the mind processes information — conditioning, social learning, memory, attention, perception and thinking. You study it through the biological, cognitive and sociocultural approaches and the key concepts.

Context · how it works

Studying learning and cognition through three approaches

  • A context is explored through the three approaches and the key concepts (measurement, bias, causality…).
  • Learning and thinking have biological (neural), cognitive (mental models) and sociocultural (observational, cultural) explanations.
  • Cognitive research relies heavily on controlled experiments — powerful for causation but sometimes low in ecological validity.

Key idea: memory and thinking are active and reconstructive, not passive recordings.

Thinking & learning · conditioning

Learning by conditioning and observation

  • Classical conditioning (Pavlov): a neutral stimulus paired with an unconditioned stimulus comes to trigger a conditioned response.
  • Operant conditioning (Skinner): behaviour is shaped by reinforcement and punishment.
  • Social (observational) learning (Bandura): we learn by watching and imitating models, especially when the model is rewarded (vicarious reinforcement).

Concept — causality: conditioning studies manipulate stimuli/consequences, supporting causal claims about learning.

Thinking & learning · schemas & biases

Schemas, dual processing and cognitive biases

  • Schema theory: prior knowledge structures (schemas) guide how we interpret and remember information (e.g. Bartlett).
  • Dual processing (Kahneman): fast, automatic System 1 versus slow, effortful System 2 thinking.
  • Cognitive biases such as confirmation bias and anchoring arise largely from System 1 shortcuts.

Link: schemas make thinking efficient but can introduce systematic errors and biases.

Cognitive processes · memory

Models of memory

  • The multi-store model (Atkinson & Shiffrin) distinguishes sensory, short-term and long-term memory.
  • The working memory model (Baddeley & Hitch) replaces a single STM with a central executive, phonological loop and visuospatial sketchpad.
  • Memory is reconstructive: Loftus showed leading questions and misinformation can distort recall.

Implication: eyewitness testimony can be unreliable because memory is rebuilt, not replayed.

Cognitive processes · attention, perception, language

Attention, perception and language

  • Attention is selective — we filter information (e.g. the cocktail-party effect).
  • Perception combines bottom-up sensory data with top-down expectations, so it can be shaped by context and schemas.
  • Language interacts with thought; debates continue over how far language shapes cognition.

Concept — measurement: cognitive processes are inferred from performance (accuracy, reaction time), not observed directly.

Concepts & research

Studying cognition rigorously

  • Lab experiments give strong control (good for causation) but can have low ecological validity.
  • Bias: demand characteristics can distort results; reconstructive memory research must handle ethics of implanting false memories.
  • Measurement: reaction time and accuracy are common objective measures of cognitive processing.

Triangulating lab and real-world evidence gives a fuller picture of learning and cognition.

HL extension — culture, motivation and technology in cognition

  • Culture: culture shapes cognition — e.g. cross-cultural differences in memory and perception.
  • Motivation: attention and learning depend on motivation and goals.
