Mission 1

Three ways to be a couple

  • Le mariage: a civil ceremony at the town hall (la mairie). Only the civil marriage is legally valid; a religious wedding may follow it, never replace it.
  • Le PACS (pacte civil de solidarité, since 1999): a contract between two adults, of the same or different sex. Simpler to sign and to end than a marriage.
  • L'union libre (also le concubinage): living together with no legal tie.
  • 2025: 251,000 marriages, of which about 7,000 between two people of the same sex.
  • 2024: 197,200 PACS (about 10,400 same-sex; latest year available). Marriage is still more common.
  • New trends: living together before marrying is now the norm; some couples stay together without living together (les couples non cohabitants).
  • se marier (avec) – to get married (to) · se pacser – to sign a PACS · vivre en couple – to live as a couple
Mission 1

Laws that changed the family

  • 1975: divorce by mutual consent (le divorce par consentement mutuel) becomes possible.
  • 1999: the PACS is created.
  • 2013: le mariage pour tous – same-sex couples can marry and adopt (law of 17 May 2013). The bill was fiercely debated, with large demonstrations both for and against.
  • 2017: a divorce by mutual consent normally no longer needs a judge: the spouses sign an agreement drawn up by their lawyers.
  • 2021: medically assisted reproduction (la PMA) is opened to female couples and single women (bioethics law of 2 August 2021).
  • Pattern: the law has followed changes in how people live, and each change was debated.

Mission 1

New family shapes

  • INSEE (France's statistics office), 2020, families with children under 18:
  • One in four is a single-parent family (une famille monoparentale): 25%, double the 1990 share (12%). About four in five are headed by the mother.
  • 9% are blended families (des familles recomposées): a couple living with at least one child from a previous relationship.
  • Children under 18: 67% live with both parents, 22% in a single-parent family, 11% in a blended family.
  • Une famille homoparentale: a family with two parents of the same sex.
  • un beau-père / une belle-mère – a stepfather / stepmother (also father-/mother-in-law)
  • un demi-frère / une demi-sœur – a half-brother / half-sister (everyday French also uses it for a stepbrother or stepsister)
  • Watch out: the adjectives agree with famille: une famille monoparentale / recomposée, des familles monoparentales / recomposées. Une famille nombreuse = a large family (3+ children), not a numerous one.
Mission 1

Fewer babies, born later

  • 2025: 645,000 births and 651,000 deaths. For the first time since the Second World War, France had more deaths than births.
  • Fertility: 1.56 children per woman (1.61 in 2024). The generations are no longer fully replaced (about 2.1 needed).
  • Women have children later: average age at childbirth 31.2.
  • Marriage and children are no longer linked: about six births in ten (59.7% in 2024) are to unmarried parents.
  • Across the French-speaking world: in Quebec, over four couples in ten (42.7% in 2021) live in union libre, against 16.9% in the rest of Canada.

Mission 1

Grandparents, parents and children

  • People live longer: life expectancy in 2025, 85.9 for women and 80.3 for men. Many children know their great-grandparents.
  • Grandparents often look after grandchildren (garder les petits-enfants) and help adult children with money or housing.
  • La génération pivot (sandwich generation): adults in their 50s and 60s who support their children and also care for ageing parents. Un(e) aidant(e) – an unpaid carer.
  • Un EHPAD – a care home for dependent older people. Whether to move a parent there is a common family dilemma.
  • Concerns: le fossé des générations (the generation gap) over screens, values and work; young adults who stay longer at home because of the cost of housing; conflict after separation (custody, la garde alternée – shared custody).
  • s'entendre bien avec – to get on well with · se disputer – to argue · soutenir – to support

Mission 1

La famille traditionnelle est-elle en crise ? Argue both sides

  • Arguments made by those who see a crisis or a loss:
  • Les séparations fragilisent les enfants. – Separations make children more vulnerable.
  • Le mariage protégeait mieux les conjoints et les enfants. – Marriage protected spouses and children better.
  • La baisse de la natalité fait craindre pour l'avenir des retraites. – The falling birth rate raises fears about future pensions.
  • Arguments made by those who see change, not crisis:
  • La famille n'a pas disparu, elle s'est diversifiée. – The family has not disappeared; it has diversified.
  • Ce qui compte, c'est l'amour et la stabilité, pas la forme de la famille. – What matters is love and stability, not the shape of the family.
  • Les liens entre générations restent très forts. – Ties between generations remain very strong.
  • Balance: Plutôt qu'une crise, on assiste à une transformation. – Rather than a crisis, we are seeing a transformation.

Mission 2

Enrichment: what the mix brings

  • La mixité – people of different origins living, learning and working together. (Who came and when: see another lesson.)
  • Language: everyday French borrows from Arabic (algèbre, magasin; slang toubib – doctor, kif-kif – the same), from English (le week-end) and more.
  • Food: couscous, nems and kebabs are part of ordinary French menus.
  • Music: raï from Algeria; zouk from the Antilles; French rap mixes many origins.
  • Sport: the 1998 World Cup winners were nicknamed « black-blanc-beur »; Zinedine Zidane is the son of Algerian immigrants.
  • Paris 2024: at the Olympic opening ceremony, Aya Nakamura (born in Mali) sang with the band of the Garde républicaine.
  • Your turn: "French has borrowed many words from Arabic."
    Show answer

    Le français a emprunté beaucoup de mots à l'arabe. (emprunter quelque chose à – to borrow from)

Mission 2

Tolerance and respect: what the data say

  • The CNCDH (France's national human rights commission) measures tolerance each year: l'indice longitudinal de tolérance.
  • 2025: 64 out of 100, one of the best scores since 1990. The long-term trend is upwards.
  • But in 2025 police and gendarmerie recorded 9,737 crimes and offences with a racist, xenophobic or anti-religious motive, 5% more than in 2024.
  • The law: since 1972 (loi Pleven), inciting racial hatred, discrimination or violence is an offence.
  • Watch out: la tolérance – tolerance; tolérer – to put up with. Le respect goes further: treating others as equals.
Mission 2

Diversity: a lesson for life

  • At school: civic and moral education (l'enseignement moral et civique); a yearly week of action against racism and antisemitism around 21 March.
  • Exchanges: Erasmus+; the Franco-German Youth Office (OFAJ, created 1963).
  • Le service civique (since 2010): paid voluntary missions for 16–25-year-olds, mixing young people of all backgrounds.
  • At work: la Charte de la diversité (2004): employers sign up to promote diversity in hiring.
  • In the neighbourhood: sports clubs, associations, la Fête des voisins.
  • Model: C'est en côtoyant des gens différents qu'on apprend à dépasser ses préjugés. – It is by mixing with different people that you learn to overcome prejudice.

Mission 2

Benefits and challenges

  • Benefits often cited: new ideas and creativity, languages, links with other countries, workers in sectors short of staff.
  • Challenges often cited:
  • segregation in housing and schools: some areas concentrate poverty and recent immigrants;
  • religion in public life: laws ban conspicuous religious symbols in state schools (2004) and face coverings in public spaces (2010);
  • identity: how to be both French and attached to another culture.
  • France's model: the Republic recognises citizens, not communities (see Mission 3). Critics of this model say it ignores real differences; its defenders say it guarantees equality.
  • Watch out: le communautarisme is a term used in French debates for groups living apart; it is contested, so attribute it: ce que certains appellent le communautarisme.
Mission 2

La diversité, une richesse ou un défi ? Argue both sides

  • A strength (arguments supporters make):
  • La diversité stimule la créativité, comme le montrent la musique et la cuisine françaises. – Diversity drives creativity, as French music and food show.
  • Grandir avec des cultures différentes prépare à un monde ouvert. – Growing up with different cultures prepares you for an open world.
  • A challenge (arguments supporters make):
  • Sans valeurs communes, une société risque de se fragmenter. – Without shared values, a society risks splitting.
  • La mixité ne se décrète pas : certains quartiers restent séparés. – Mixing cannot be ordered by decree: some areas remain separate.
  • Linkers: Loin d'être …, … · Encore faut-il que … (+ subjunctive) · En définitive, …
  • Your turn: "Far from being a threat, diversity is an asset."
    Show answer

    Loin d'être une menace, la diversité est un atout.

Mission 3

Three models of living together

  • Assimilation (l'assimilation): newcomers drop their culture and adopt the host culture completely.
  • Integration (l'intégration): newcomers join a shared public life (language, laws, values) and may keep their culture in private life.
  • Multiculturalism (le multiculturalisme): the state recognises communities and their differences (e.g. the UK, Canada).
  • France's official model: republican integration. The Republic sees citizens, not communities.
  • In French debate, le communautarisme is a critical word: people living apart, by community.
  • Watch out: s'intégrer (reflexive) = to integrate oneself, fit in. Il s'est bien intégré. / La société intègre les nouveaux arrivants.
  • Your turn: "She has integrated well." → Elle s'est bien intégrée. (être + agreement)
Mission 3

The republican integration path

  • Newcomers who want to stay long-term sign the contrat d'intégration républicaine (CIR) (since 2016).
  • It includes civic training (values of the Republic: liberty, equality, fraternity, secularism) and French classes if needed.
  • Law of 26 January 2024, applied from 1 January 2026:
  • a civic exam (40 multiple-choice questions, pass mark 32) for a multi-year residence card, a 10-year card and naturalisation;
  • higher French levels: B1 for a 10-year card (carte de résident), B2 for naturalisation.
  • Supporters: language and shared values are the keys to a job and to social life.
  • Critics: harder tests may keep out people who work and pay taxes but had little schooling.
  • maîtriser la langue – to master the language; un titre de séjour – a residence permit

Mission 3

What makes integration easier or harder?

  • Easier (les facteurs qui facilitent l'intégration):
  • maîtriser le français – speaking French
  • trouver un emploi stable – a stable job
  • l'école – school, which mixes children of all origins
  • les associations, le sport, le bénévolat – clubs, sport, volunteering
  • Harder (les obstacles):
  • la barrière de la langue – the language barrier
  • la non-reconnaissance des diplômes – foreign qualifications not recognised
  • la concentration dans certains quartiers – living in concentrated, poorer areas (la ghettoïsation in critics' terms)
  • les discriminations – discrimination at work, in housing, in police checks
  • Your turn: one sentence with plus … plus.
    Show answer

    Plus on parle français, plus il est facile de trouver un emploi.

Mission 3

Discrimination: the law

  • Discriminer: to treat someone less well because of a protected characteristic.
  • The Penal Code (art. 225-1) lists more than 25 criteria, including origin, religion, sex, disability, address, surname.
  • Penalty: up to 3 years in prison and a €45,000 fine.
  • The Défenseur des droits (since 2011) is an independent authority that receives complaints and investigates.
  • Forms: la discrimination à l'embauche (hiring) · au logement (housing) · le contrôle au faciès (identity checks based on appearance).
  • In 2016 France's highest court (la Cour de cassation) found the State liable for discriminatory identity checks.
  • Vocabulary: un préjugé – a prejudice · un stéréotype · le racisme · subir une discrimination – to suffer discrimination · porter plainte – to make a complaint
Mission 3

The evidence: testing

  • Testing (le testing): researchers send matched fake applications that differ in one detail only, here the name.
  • Study for the Ministry of Labour (DARES), 2019–2021: 9,600 applications to 2,400 real job offers.
  • Call-back rate: 33.3% for French-sounding names, 22.8% for North African-sounding names.
  • = 31.5% fewer call-backs, with identical CVs.
  • Survey Trajectoires et Origines 2 (Ined/INSEE, 2019–2020): 37% of men who immigrated from the Maghreb said they had been unfairly refused a job in the previous five years, against 15% of men with no migration background.
  • Phrase: À CV égal, un candidat au nom maghrébin a moins de chances d'être rappelé. – With the same CV, a candidate with a North African name is less likely to be called back.

Mission 3

Ethnic statistics: should France count? Argue both sides

  • France does not, in principle, record people's ethnic or racial origin in official data.
  • In 2007 the Constitutional Council ruled that studies may not be based on ethnic or racial origin; they may use country of birth, nationality and parents' origin.
  • For ethnic statistics (arguments supporters make):
  • On ne peut pas combattre ce qu'on ne mesure pas. – You cannot fight what you do not measure.
  • D'autres pays, comme le Royaume-Uni, les utilisent. – Other countries use them.
  • Against (arguments opponents make):
  • Classer les citoyens par origine contredit l'égalité républicaine. – Sorting citizens by origin contradicts republican equality.
  • Cela risque de figer les identités. – It risks fixing identities.
  • Linker: Si certains estiment que …, d'autres soutiennent que …
Mission 3

Community life and alienation

  • Community activities (la vie associative): cultural associations, sports clubs, homework help, places of worship, neighbourhood festivals. They build links and help newcomers.
  • Alienation (l'aliénation, le sentiment d'exclusion): some descendants of immigrants say they feel ni d'ici ni de là-bas – neither from here nor from there.
  • Urban unrest has brought these issues to the front:
  • 2005: after two teenagers died in an electricity substation in Clichy-sous-Bois while fleeing a police check, riots spread for three weeks; a state of emergency was declared.
  • 2023: after the death of Nahel, 17, shot by a police officer during a traffic stop in Nanterre, riots broke out in many towns.
  • Views differ: some stress discrimination and poverty in these areas; others stress the need to restore order and the authority of the State.
  • les quartiers prioritaires – priority neighbourhoods; la politique de la ville – urban policy
Mission 3

Model opinions to reuse

  • Il est inadmissible qu'un candidat soit écarté à cause de son nom. – It is unacceptable that a candidate be rejected because of their name. (il est inadmissible que + subjunctive)
  • L'intégration est un processus à double sens : elle exige des efforts de la part des immigrés comme de la société d'accueil. – Integration is a two-way process.
  • Loin d'être un obstacle, la diversité est une richesse, à condition que chacun respecte les valeurs communes. – Far from being an obstacle, diversity is an asset, provided that everyone respects shared values. (à condition que + subjunctive)
  • Watch out: la discrimination is feminine; les discriminations (plural) is common in French for "discrimination" in general.

Mission 4

Measuring poverty

  • France, like the EU, uses relative poverty: living on less than 60% of the median standard of living (le niveau de vie médian).
  • 2024 threshold for a single person: €1,337 a month.
  • 15.4% of people in metropolitan France were below it: about 9.8 million.
  • This is the highest rate since the series began in 1996 (INSEE, July 2026).
  • Groups most at risk: unemployed people (about 36%), single-parent families (34%), children (about 22%). Pensioners: about 10%.
  • le seuil de pauvreté – poverty line · le taux de pauvreté – poverty rate · le niveau de vie – standard of living · les inégalités (f)
Mission 4

Causes and consequences

  • Causes often cited:
  • unemployment and insecure jobs (la précarité, les contrats courts);
  • high housing costs;
  • family breakdown (one income instead of two);
  • low qualifications.
  • Consequences:
  • poor housing (le mal-logement), homelessness (les sans-abri, les SDF);
  • health problems, giving up care;
  • children's school results; isolation.
  • Social mobility: an OECD study (2018) estimated it could take six generations for a child from a low-income family in France to reach average income.
  • l'ascenseur social – the "social lift" (social mobility) · être en panne – to have broken down

Mission 4

What helps: the State and associations

  • The State:
  • the RSA (revenu de solidarité active, 2009), a minimum income;
  • housing benefit (les APL), the prime d'activité for low-paid workers, family benefits;
  • since 2025, RSA claimants are registered with France Travail and expected to do about 15 hours of activity a week (training, placements).
  • Associations:
  • les Restos du Cœur (founded 1985 by the comedian Coluche): free meals;
  • le Secours populaire, le Secours catholique, Emmaüs, ATD Quart Monde.
  • une association caritative – charity · un(e) bénévole – volunteer · une aide sociale – benefit · l'exclusion (f)
Mission 4

Gender equality at work

  • INSEE (2024 data): in the private sector, women earn on average about 22% less than men.
  • At equal working time: about 14% less (women more often work part-time).
  • Same job, same employer: about 4% less.
  • Laws:
  • 2000: la parité, equal numbers of men and women on many electoral lists;
  • 2011: at least 40% women on the boards of large companies;
  • 2019: an equality index that companies must publish.
  • l'écart de salaire – pay gap · la parité · le plafond de verre – glass ceiling

Mission 4

Poverty in the world

  • 2015: the UN adopts 17 Sustainable Development Goals (objectifs de développement durable); Goal 1: end extreme poverty by 2030.
  • The World Bank's extreme poverty line: $3.00 a day (updated 2025).
  • Many of the poorest countries are French-speaking, e.g. in the Sahel and Haiti.
  • French-born NGOs: Médecins Sans Frontières (1971, Nobel Peace Prize 1999), Action contre la faim.
  • Fair trade (le commerce équitable): a guaranteed minimum price for producers.
  • l'aide humanitaire · l'aide au développement · une ONG – NGO · la malnutrition
Mission 4

Argue both sides

  • Should the State or associations fight poverty?
  • C'est à l'État de garantir à chacun un minimum pour vivre dignement. – It is the State's job to guarantee everyone enough to live with dignity.
  • Les associations, plus proches du terrain, aident souvent mieux les personnes en difficulté. – Associations, closer to the ground, often help people in difficulty better.
  • Should benefits come with obligations (the RSA reform)?
  • Supporters say: Une activité régulière aide à retrouver un emploi. – Regular activity helps people find work again.
  • Opponents say: Imposer des heures d'activité risque de stigmatiser les plus pauvres. – Imposing hours risks stigmatising the poorest.
  • Linkers: Les uns soutiennent que … tandis que d'autres … · Force est de constater que … – One has to recognise that …

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Couples: mariage (251,000 in 2025), PACS (since 1999; 197,200 in 2024), union libre. Key laws: 1975, 1999, 2013, 2017, 2021.
  • Family shapes (INSEE 2020): 25% single-parent, 9% blended; six births in ten outside marriage; fertility 1.56 in 2025.
  • The family has diversified: argue "crisis" vs "transformation" with evidence.

Mission 2

  • Enrichment: language (Arabic, English borrowings), food, music (raï, zouk, rap), sport (1998, Zidane), Paris 2024.
  • Tolerance: CNCDH index 64/100 in 2025 (one of the best since 1990), but 9,737 recorded racist offences (+5%); loi Pleven 1972.
  • Learning diversity: school, OFAJ, service civique (2010), Charte de la diversité (2004); challenges: segregation, religion in public life, identity.

Mission 3

  • Models: assimilation, republican integration (France), multiculturalism; s'intégrer is reflexive.
  • Integration path: CIR; from 2026 a civic exam and higher French levels (B1, B2).
  • Discrimination: illegal (Penal Code art. 225-1), measured by testing (33.3% vs 22.8% call-backs) and TeO2; debate on ethnic statistics.

Mission 4

  • Relative poverty: below 60% of the median (€1,337/month); 15.4%, 9.8 million in 2024, highest since 1996.
  • Measures: RSA (activity requirement since 2025), benefits, associations (Restos du Cœur, 1985).
  • Equality: pay gap 22% overall, 14% equal hours, 4% same job; parity laws 2000, 2011, 2019. World: SDGs 2015, MSF.
