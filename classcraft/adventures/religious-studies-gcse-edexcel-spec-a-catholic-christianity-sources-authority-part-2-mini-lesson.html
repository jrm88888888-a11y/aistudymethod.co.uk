Mission 1

Mission 1: Catholics say the Church is holy. How can that be true when its members are sinners?

Make a guess: Catholics say the Church is holy. How can that be true when its members are sinners?

Mission 1Read the source

Four words in the Creed

  • At Sunday Mass Catholics profess: "I believe in one, holy, catholic and apostolic Church" (Nicene Creed, Roman Missal).
  • These four words are the marks of the Church: signs by which, Catholics believe, the true Church can be recognised.
  • The words come from the First Council of Constantinople (381), which completed the creed of Nicaea (325). So the full creed is called the Nicene (or Niceno-Constantinopolitan) Creed.
  • Shared by Catholic, Orthodox, Anglican and many Protestant Churches.
  • Q: Which of the four words do you think is hardest to see in a real parish? (No wrong answer: hold on to it.)
Mission 1

One and holy

  • One: one Lord, one faith, one baptism (Ephesians 4:4–5). Catholics are united in the same faith, the same sacraments, and communion with the Pope and bishops.
  • Jesus prayed "that they may all be one" (John 17:21): unity is a gift and a task.
  • Holy: Catholics believe the Church is holy because Christ is holy and gives it the Holy Spirit and the sacraments.
  • Its members are sinners being made holy; it is "always in need of being purified" (LG 8, paraphrase).
  • So "holy" is not a claim that Catholics are better people. It names where holiness comes from and what the Church is called to.
  • The saints show what holiness looks like.
Mission 1

Catholic and apostolic

  • Catholic means universal (Greek katholikos, "according to the whole").
  • Two senses: Catholics believe the Church has the whole faith and all the means of salvation; and it is sent to the whole world (Matthew 28:19).
  • Apostolic: built "on the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the chief cornerstone" (Ephesians 2:20).
  • It keeps the apostles' teaching, and its bishops are the apostles' successors.
  • Importance today: Mass in any country is the same Mass; a Catholic belongs to a worldwide family linked to the first Christians.
Quick check
?In the Creed, what does the word "catholic" mean when Catholics say they believe in a "catholic Church"?
Mission 1

Apostolic succession and the Magisterium

  • Apostolic succession: the apostles appointed leaders, who appointed others; Catholics believe that, by ordination, bishops today stand in an unbroken line from the apostles.
  • Peter was given a special role: "on this rock I will build my assembly" (Matthew 16:18). Catholics believe the Pope, Bishop of Rome, is Peter's successor.
  • So the bishops, in communion with the Pope, inherit the apostles' task of teaching with Christ's authority.
  • This is the Magisterium: the Church's living teaching office.
Mission 1Diversity

Same words, different meanings

  • Most Churches accept the Nicene Creed, and many say it in worship. They read the marks differently.
  • Catholic: the one Church of Christ, with its four marks, "subsists in" the Catholic Church (LG 8, paraphrase), though elements of truth and holiness are found in other Churches.
  • Orthodox: the Orthodox Church is the one, holy, catholic and apostolic Church; apostolic succession through bishops, but without the Pope's authority over all.
  • Anglican: keeps bishops in historic succession; sees itself as part of the one Church.
  • Many Protestants: "catholic" = the universal Church of all true believers, across denominations; "apostolic" = faithful to the apostles' teaching in the Bible, not a line of bishops.
  • So for many Christians, unity is spiritual and visible unity is a goal (the ecumenical movement).
Mission 1

Body of Christ, People of God

  • Recap from an earlier lesson. Paul: "we, who are many, are one body in Christ, and individually members of one another" (Romans 12:5); each has different gifts (12:4–6).
  • "Now you are the body of Christ, and members individually" (1 Corinthians 12:27).
  • Body of Christ stresses unity, many gifts, and Christ as the head: links to one and holy.
  • People of God (1 Peter 2:9–10; Lumen Gentium ch. 2) stresses a people on a journey, all the baptised sharing in Christ's mission: links to catholic and apostolic.
  • Importance today: every Catholic has a role; when one suffers, all suffer (1 Corinthians 12:26).
Mission 1Evaluate

"Being one is the most important mark of the Church."

  • Reasons for: Jesus prayed "that they may all be one … that the world may believe" (John 17:21): unity makes the Church's message believable. Without unity, the other marks are disputed between Churches.
  • Reasons against: holiness matters more: unity around something unholy would be worthless. Apostolic faith is the test of truth: unity without the apostles' teaching would not be the Church. Catholicity: the universal mission to the whole world is the reason the Church exists (Matthew 28:19).
  • Another view: the four marks belong together; each depends on the others, as they all depend on Christ.
  • Judge it by: Which mark could the Church lose and still be the Church? Which mark do Christians of different traditions disagree about most? Which mark most affects daily Catholic life?
Mission check

?A news story reports that a parish priest has been convicted of fraud. At Sunday Mass a Catholic student still professes "one, holy, catholic and apostolic Church". Which explanation best fits Catholic teaching about the mark "holy"?

The four marks are gifts Catholics believe come from Christ and tasks the Church is still growing into, as a sign of the Kingdom.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Catholics call Mary "Mother of God". What does that title claim, and what doesn't it?

Make a guess: Catholics call Mary "Mother of God". What does that title claim, and what doesn't it?

Mission 2

Who Mary is

  • Mary of Nazareth: the mother of Jesus. Catholics and Orthodox honour her above all other saints.
  • Annunciation: the angel Gabriel tells her she will bear God's Son; she agrees (Luke 1:26–38).
  • Cana: she tells the servants, "Whatever he says to you, do it." (John 2:5)
  • The cross: she stands by Jesus as he dies (John 19:25); he entrusts her to the beloved disciple: "Behold, your mother!" (John 19:27)
  • Pentecost: she prays with the apostles, waiting for the Spirit (Acts 1:14).
  • She "kept all these sayings, pondering them in her heart" (Luke 2:19): a woman of faith and prayer.
Mission 2

Mother of God

  • Mother of God (Greek Theotokos, "God-bearer"): defined at the Council of Ephesus (431).
  • It is first a claim about Jesus: the child Mary bore is one person, truly God and truly human. So his mother is the Mother of God.
  • It does not mean Mary is divine, existed before God, or is mother of the Father or the Trinity. She is a human being, a creature.
  • Catholics and Orthodox use the title; many Anglicans and Lutherans accept it too.
  • Other Catholic teachings about Mary:
  • Immaculate Conception (defined 1854): Mary was kept free from original sin from the first moment of her own life.
  • Assumption (defined 1950): at the end of her life Mary was taken body and soul into heaven.
Quick check
?Catholics believe in the Immaculate Conception. What does this teaching say?
Mission 2Read the source

The Annunciation (Luke 1:26–39)

  • Gabriel: "Rejoice, you highly favoured one! The Lord is with you." (Luke 1:28)
  • "You will conceive in your womb and give birth to a son, and shall name him 'Jesus.'" (Luke 1:31)
  • Mary asks how this can be. "The Holy Spirit will come on you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you." (Luke 1:35)
  • "For nothing spoken by God is impossible." (Luke 1:37)
  • Mary: "Behold, the servant of the Lord; let it be done to me according to your word." (Luke 1:38)
  • Then she sets out "with haste" to help her relative Elizabeth (Luke 1:39).

Q1 Which verse shows Mary's free consent?

Show answer

Luke 1:38.

Q2 What does Mary do straight after the Annunciation?

Show answer

She hurries to visit and help Elizabeth: faith turns into charity.

Mission 2Read the source

Mary, model of the Church (CCC 963–975)

Catechism of the Catholic Church 963–975 (paraphrase):

  • Mary is the Mother of Christ and Mother of the Church (963).
  • She is joined with Christ in the work of salvation, from her "yes" at the Annunciation to the cross (964).
  • By her faith, hope and charity she is the Church's model ("type") of faith and charity (967).
  • She is the first and best disciple: she hears God's word and keeps it.
  • Her Assumption shows what the whole Church hopes for: resurrection in glory (966, 972).
  • Her prayers for the Church do not compete with Christ; they depend on him (970).
  • Catholics give her special honour, which is different in kind from the worship given to God alone (971).
Quick check
?What does Catholic teaching mean by calling Mary a "model of the Church"?
Mission 2

Mary as guide and role model

  • Model of faith: she says "yes" to God without knowing what it will cost (Luke 1:38).
  • Model of discipleship: "blessed are those who hear the word of God, and keep it" (Luke 11:28).
  • Model of charity: she hurries to help Elizabeth; at Cana she notices others' needs.
  • Model of courage in suffering: she stays at the cross.
  • In prayer: the Hail Mary, the Rosary, the Angelus (prayed at 6 a.m., noon and 6 p.m.), May devotions, Marian feasts.
  • Pilgrimage: shrines such as Lourdes (France) and Knock (Ireland).
  • Catholics ask Mary to pray for them ("pray for us sinners"); they do not worship her.
Mission 2Diversity

Different views of Mary

  • Orthodox: deep love for the Theotokos; icons of her in every church; celebrate her Dormition ("falling asleep") and taking up into heaven, but do not accept the Immaculate Conception as defined by Rome.
  • Catholics: Mother of God, Mother of the Church, model and intercessor; Immaculate Conception and Assumption are dogmas.
  • Most Protestants: honour Mary as the faithful mother of Jesus and a model of faith; do not pray to her or to the saints: "there is one God and one mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus" (1 Timothy 2:5).
  • They reject the Immaculate Conception and Assumption as not taught in the Bible.
  • Anglicans vary: some keep Marian feasts and shrines (e.g. Walsingham); others share the Protestant view.
  • Catholic reply: Mary's prayers depend on Christ's mediation and point to it (CCC 970).
Mission 2Evaluate

"Mary is the most important example for Catholics today."

  • Claim: Mary is the best model of Christian life.
  • For: she says "yes" to God (Luke 1:38); she is the first disciple, faithful at the cross; the Catechism calls her the model of faith and charity (CCC 967); the Magnificat shows care for justice.
  • Against: Jesus is the perfect example: "I have given you an example" (John 13:15). Some find Mary's sinlessness makes her hard to imitate; modern saints may feel closer to everyday lives. Protestants note the Bible says little about her.
  • Judge it by: example of what? for whom? Is a model someone you copy, or someone who points you to Christ?
Mission check

?A Christmas card says "Holy Mary, Mother of God". A classmate says: "So Catholics think Mary is a goddess who existed before God." Which reply corrects this according to Catholic teaching?

"Mother of God" is above all a claim about Jesus: Catholics honour Mary as the human mother of the one who is God.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Jesus said he came to fulfil the law, then made it harder. What did he expect of his followers?

Make a guess: Jesus said he came to fulfil the law, then made it harder. What did he expect of his followers?

Mission 3

Why Jesus has moral authority

  • Christians believe Jesus is the Son of God and the Word of God made flesh (John 1:14): his teaching is God's teaching.
  • The crowds noticed: "he taught them with authority, and not like the scribes" (Matthew 7:29).
  • He teaches by words (sermons, parables) and by example (how he lived and died).
  • Role model: Christians try to copy his love, forgiveness, servanthood, reconciliation and work for justice.
Mission 3Read the source

Fulfilling the law (Matthew 5:17–24)

  • "Don't think that I came to destroy the law or the prophets. I didn't come to destroy, but to fulfil." (Matthew 5:17)
  • Fulfil: to bring the law to its full meaning, and to complete what it pointed towards.
  • Then Jesus goes deeper (5:21–22, paraphrase): the law says "Do not murder"; Jesus says even anger and insults put a person in danger of judgement.
  • Before offering a gift at the altar, first go and make peace with your brother (5:23–24, paraphrase).

Q1 Did Jesus come to abolish the law?

Show answer

No: to fulfil it.

Q2 How does Jesus go beyond "Do not murder"?

Show answer

He forbids the anger that leads to it, and asks for reconciliation first.

Mission 3

The Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5–7)

  • Beatitudes (5:3–12): who is truly blessed.
  • Salt and light (5:13–16): followers should make a difference.
  • Go deeper than the law (5:17–48): anger, adultery, oaths, revenge; "love your enemies" (5:44).
  • Real religion, not show (6:1–18): give, pray (the Lord's Prayer) and fast in secret.
  • Trust, not wealth (6:19–34): "You can't serve both God and Mammon (money)" (6:24).
  • Do not judge; the Golden Rule (7:1–12): treat others as you want to be treated (7:12).
  • Wise and foolish builders (7:24–27): hear these words and do them.
Quick check
?What did Jesus mean when he said he came to fulfil the law (Matthew 5:17)?
Mission 3

Agape and the Two Great Commandments

  • Asked which commandment is greatest, Jesus answers: "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, with all your soul, and with all your mind." (Matthew 22:37)
  • "A second likewise is this, 'You shall love your neighbour as yourself.'" (Matthew 22:39)
  • Agape: selfless, unconditional love that seeks the good of the other, even an enemy.
Mission 3

Jesus' example

  • Taking up the cross: "Whoever wants to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me." (Mark 8:34)
  • Servanthood: Jesus washes his disciples' feet (John 13:1–15).
  • Forgiveness and reconciliation: he forgives those who crucify him (Luke 23:34).
  • Kingdom of God: God's rule of justice and love, begun in Jesus; Christians work for it now (e.g. social justice).
Mission 3Diversity

How literally should Christians follow it?

  • Literal ideal for all: some (e.g. Quakers, Mennonites) refuse oaths and violence because of Matthew 5:34 and 5:39.
  • Catholic: the commands bind every Christian; some (monks, nuns) also follow "counsels of perfection": poverty, chastity, obedience.
  • Lutheran tradition: the Sermon shows how far everyone falls short, so all need God's grace; it is still a guide for living.
  • Liberal Protestant: principles of love, not fixed rules; apply agape to each situation.
  • Divergent implications: some stress personal forgiveness and service; others work for social justice and structural change.
Quick check
?A Quaker refuses to swear an oath in court and instead makes a solemn promise to tell the truth. Which part of Jesus' teaching is she following?
Mission 3Evaluate

"Jesus' moral teaching is too demanding to follow today."

  • Reasons for: Jesus' commands in the Sermon on the Mount, "love your enemies" (Matthew 5:44) and "don't resist him who is evil" (5:39), seem impossible in a violent world. Even anger is condemned (5:22). Lutheran Christians say no one keeps it perfectly.
  • Reasons against: many Christians do live it: e.g. forgiving a family member's killer, or giving up wealth. Jesus promises help: the Spirit and grace (–). The Beatitudes describe God's Kingdom, which Christians grow towards. Ordinary acts of love, such as caring for a stranger in need, count too.
  • Another view: it is a demanding ideal to aim at, not a set of rules to tick off; failure is met with forgiveness.
  • Judge it by: Is it meant as rules or ideals? Does "too demanding" mean impossible or just hard? Do real examples show it can be lived?
Mission check

?A company's code says: "Don't steal from the firm." A new manager tells staff: "Not stealing isn't enough. I want you to want the firm to do well." How does this mirror Jesus' approach to the law?

Christians believe Jesus fulfils the law by taking it to the heart. Christians follow both his words and his example of agape.

Mission 4

Mission 4: If the Bible says nothing about a choice, how do Christians decide what is right?

Make a guess: If the Bible says nothing about a choice, how do Christians decide what is right?

Mission 4

Many sources, one decision

  • Christians make moral decisions using several sources together:
  • The Bible, especially Jesus' teaching and example (see Mission 3).
  • Church teaching: for Catholics, Tradition and the Magisterium (the Church's teaching office).
  • Reason: thinking carefully about what is good for people.
  • Conscience: the inner sense of right and wrong.
  • The individual: in the end each person must decide and take responsibility.
  • Christians differ on which source carries most weight when they seem to clash.
Mission 4

Natural law

  • Thomas Aquinas (c. 1225–1274), Summa Theologiae (1265–1274): God made the world with a purpose and order.
  • Humans can discover that order by reason: this is natural law.
  • The first principle: "good is to be done and pursued, and evil is to be avoided" (ST I-II, q. 94, a. 2).
  • Primary precepts (basic goods): preserve life; reproduce; educate the young; live in an ordered society; know and worship God.
  • Paul: people who never heard God's law still "show the work of the law written in their hearts" (Romans 2:15).
Mission 4

Natural law and the sanctity of life

  • Sanctity of life: human life is holy, because it comes from God and is made in God's image (Genesis 1:27).
  • Preserving life is a primary precept, so natural law reasons that it is always wrong to directly take innocent human life.
  • Catholics hold that natural law can be known by everyone, religious or not, so the Church argues from reason in public debate.
  • Divergent views: many Protestants put less weight on natural law, since sin damages reason; some Christians argue from love and consequences in each case instead.
Quick check
?According to Aquinas' natural law, how can people know what is morally good?
Mission 4

Conscience

  • Conscience: the inner judgement that tells a person what is right or wrong in a particular situation.
  • Catholics call it the voice of God within: a law people did not make for themselves, which calls them to love good and avoid evil (Gaudium et Spes 16).
  • Catholic teaching also calls conscience a judgement of reason (CCC 1778).
  • Primacy of conscience: a person must follow a certain conscience, even when it is mistaken.
  • Catholic teaching adds that conscience must be formed well: by scripture, Church teaching, prayer and advice.
  • John Henry Newman (1801–1890): conscience is "the aboriginal Vicar of Christ" (Letter to the Duke of Norfolk, 1875): Christ's first representative within each person.
Mission 4Read the source

Gaudium et Spes 16 and CCC 1777–1781

  • Gaudium et Spes 16 (Second Vatican Council, 1965, paraphrase): deep within, people find a law they did not make, which they must obey. Conscience is a person's "most secret core and sanctuary", where they are alone with God.
  • CCC 1777 (paraphrase): conscience urges a person, at the right moment, to do good and avoid evil; it judges particular choices.
  • CCC 1778: conscience is a judgement of reason, by which a person recognises the moral quality of an act.
  • CCC 1779–1780: people must listen to their conscience; it needs reflection and self-examination; it is part of human dignity.
  • CCC 1781: conscience lets a person take responsibility and admit wrong done; it can lead to repentance and hope in God's mercy.

Q1 According to Gaudium et Spes 16, who speaks to a person in conscience?

Show answer

God.

Q2 According to CCC 1778, is conscience a feeling or a judgement?

Show answer

A judgement of reason.

Mission 4

Virtue, and growing through suffering

  • Virtue: a good habit of character, built by practice.
  • Cardinal virtues: prudence (wisdom in choosing), justice, fortitude (courage), temperance (self-control).
  • Theological virtues: faith, hope and love: "The greatest of these is love." (1 Corinthians 13:13)
  • Virtue ethics asks: "What would a good person do?": with Jesus as the model (see Mission 3).
Mission 4Diversity

When Bible, Church and conscience seem to clash

  • Catholic: conscience must be followed, but formed by scripture, Tradition and the Magisterium; a conscience that ignores them may be in error.
  • Many evangelical Protestants: the Bible is the final authority; conscience must be "captive to the Word of God" (Luther at Worms, 1521, paraphrase). Some warn that conscience is affected by sin (Jeremiah 17:9).
  • Liberal Protestants: reason and conscience may lead a Christian to read some Bible passages as limited by their time.
  • Quakers: the Inner Light (God's Spirit in each person) guides decisions, tested together in the meeting.
  • On natural law: Catholic teaching gives it real authority; many Protestants trust it less, since reason is affected by sin.
Quick check
?Martin Luther said his conscience was "captive to the Word of God". What does this suggest about how many Protestants see conscience?
Mission 4Evaluate

"A Christian should always follow their conscience."

  • Reasons for: Gaudium et Spes 16: conscience is where God's voice is heard. Catholic teaching: a certain conscience must be obeyed. Newman would toast conscience first and the pope afterwards. Acting against conscience damages a person's integrity.
  • Reasons against: conscience can be wrong or badly formed (CCC 1790–1791); people have done terrible things "in conscience". Many Protestants say the Bible must judge conscience. Church teaching and community advice protect against self-deception.
  • Another view: follow conscience, but only after forming it carefully: the two belong together.
  • Judge it by: What happens when conscience is mistaken? Who checks a person's conscience? Is "following conscience" the same as doing what one wants?
Mission 4Worked answer

Two sources for a decision

Question: Explain two sources Christians use when making moral decisions. Refer to a source of wisdom and authority.

  • Source 1: the Bible. Develop: Christians look to Jesus' teaching, e.g. the Two Great Commandments, love God and love your neighbour (Matthew 22:37–39), to find the principle of love.
  • Source 2: conscience. Source: CCC 1778 calls conscience a judgement of reason. Develop: so a Catholic thinks through the situation and follows a well-formed conscience, even when the Bible gives no direct rule.
  • 5-mark (c) tip: two developed points, plus one source of wisdom and authority.
Mission check

?A Catholic doctor is asked to give a treatment that would deliberately end a patient's life. No Bible verse mentions this treatment. She says: "I have prayed and studied what my Church teaches, and I cannot do it." What is she mainly relying on?

Christians weigh the Bible, Church teaching, reason and conscience together; Catholics hold that a well-formed conscience must be followed.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • The Church is professed as one, holy, catholic (universal) and apostolic (Nicene Creed).
  • These words on the Church come from the First Council of Constantinople (381).
  • Catholics believe apostolic succession links today's bishops, with the Pope, to the apostles; this is the root of the Magisterium.

Mission 2

  • "Mother of God" (Theotokos, Ephesus 431) means the child Mary bore is truly God; Mary herself is human.
  • Catholics see Mary as model of the Church: of faith ("let it be done to me", Luke 1:38), discipleship and charity, and pray with her and to the saints for their prayers.

Mission 3

  • Christians see Jesus as the Word of God: his teaching and example carry God's authority.
  • In Matthew 5:17–24 Jesus says he came to fulfil the law, and he goes deeper, to attitudes such as anger.
  • The Sermon on the Mount (Matthew 5–7) sets out the way of life he expected; Christians differ on how literally to follow it.
  • Agape and the Two Great Commandments: love God, and love your neighbour as yourself.

Mission 4

  • Natural law (Aquinas): reason discovers the purposes God built into a good creation; preserving life is basic, so Catholics uphold the sanctity of life.
  • Conscience is a judgement of reason where Catholics believe God's voice is heard (GS 16; CCC 1777–1781); it must be formed and followed.
  • Christians differ on the weight of Bible, Church, reason and conscience.
  • Virtue grows by practice; many Christians believe God can use even suffering to grow it.
