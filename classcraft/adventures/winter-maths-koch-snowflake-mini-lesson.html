The Snowflake with an Infinite Edge

3 missions and a finale. Build a snowflake whose edge never stops growing. Guess, learn, then check.

Mission 1

Mission 1: How can a shape have an infinite perimeter?

Make a guess: How can a shape have an infinite perimeter?

Mission 1

Start with a triangle

  • Draw an equilateral triangle: three equal sides, three equal angles.
  • Each side is 9 cm.
  • Perimeter is the distance all the way round: 3 × 9 = 27 cm.
Mission 1

The rule

  • Take an edge. Cut it into three equal parts.
  • Remove the middle third. In its place, build the other two sides of a smaller equilateral triangle, pointing outwards.
  • One edge becomes 4 edges, each one third as long.
Quick check
?An edge is 9 cm long. You apply the rule once. How long is the new path along it?
Mission 1

Do it to every edge, again and again

  • Stage 0: a triangle, 3 edges. Apply the rule to every edge.
  • Stage 1: a six-pointed star, 12 edges. Stage 2: 48 edges. Stage 3: 192 edges.
  • Edges: × 4 each stage. Edge length: 9 → 3 → 1 → 1/3 cm, × 1/3 each stage.
Mission 1

Perimeter = number of edges × edge length

  • Stage 0: 3 × 9 = 27 cm. Stage 1: 12 × 3 = 36 cm.
  • Stage 2: 48 × 1 = 48 cm. Stage 3: 192 × 1/3 = 64 cm.
  • Each stage: × 4 edges, × 1/3 length. Together: perimeter × 4/3.
Quick check
?The stage-3 perimeter is 64 cm. What is the stage-4 perimeter?
Mission 1

It never stops

  • Perimeters: 27, 36, 48, 64, 85⅓, … The term-to-term rule is × 4/3.
  • 4/3 is more than 1, so every stage is longer than the last. No stage is the last one.
  • Keep applying the rule for ever and the perimeter grows without bound. That is what "infinite perimeter" means here.
Mission check

?How can a shape have an infinite perimeter?

Each step makes the edge 4/3 as long, and the steps never stop.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Can a shape with an infinite edge still fit on a page?

Make a guess: Can a shape with an infinite edge still fit on a page?

Mission 2

What step 1 adds

  • Call the area of the first triangle 1 unit.
  • Step 1 adds 3 new triangles, one on each edge. Each has sides 1/3 as long as the original.
  • Lengths × 1/3 means area × 1/9 (a third as wide and a third as tall).
  • Area added at step 1: 3 × 1/9 = 1/3 of a unit.
Quick check
?A triangle's sides are all made 1/3 as long. What happens to its area?
Mission 2

Step 2 adds less

  • Step 2 adds 12 triangles, one on each of the 12 edges. Each is 1/9 of a step-1 triangle: 1/81 of a unit.
  • Area added at step 2: 12 × 1/81 = 12/81 = 4/27 of a unit.
  • Compare: step 1 added 1/3 = 9/27. Step 2 added 4/27. The amount added is × 4/9 each step.
  • Four times as many triangles, each a ninth of the size: 4 × 1/9 = 4/9.
Quick check
?Step 2 adds 4/27 of a unit of area. How much does step 3 add?
Mission 2

Fenced in

  • Draw the circle through the three corners of the first triangle.
  • Every new triangle, at every step, stays inside that circle.
  • So the snowflake's area can never be more than the circle's area. It is bounded.
Mission 2

The paradox, stated carefully

  • After any number of steps, the shape has a finite number of edges, a finite perimeter and a finite area.
  • Perimeters go × 4/3 each step: they grow without bound. Added areas go × 4/9 each step: they shrink towards nothing.
  • Keep going for ever: the edge becomes longer than any ruler, but the shape still fits inside the circle.
  • Adding up every step exactly is A-level maths. The answer is 8/5 of the first triangle: not infinite.
Quick check
?You apply the rule 100 times. Which statement about the stage-100 perimeter is true?
Mission check

?Can a shape with an infinite edge still fit on a page?

Yes. The new triangles shrink faster than they multiply, so it stays inside one circle.

Mission 3

Mission 3: You start with a triangle. So why six arms?

Make a guess: You start with a triangle. So why six arms?

Mission 3

Two kinds of symmetry

  • Line symmetry: fold along the line and the two halves match. The line is a mirror line.
  • Rotational symmetry: turn the shape about its centre and it looks the same before a full turn.
  • The order of rotational symmetry is how many times it matches in one full turn.
  • An equilateral triangle: 3 lines of symmetry, rotational symmetry of order 3.
Quick check
?A parallelogram (not a rectangle or a rhombus) has no lines of symmetry. What is its order of rotational symmetry?
Mission 3

Step 1 makes a star

  • Step 1 adds a point to each of the three edges: the triangle becomes a six-pointed star.
  • The star has 6 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 6.
  • Every later step treats every edge the same way, so nothing breaks that symmetry. The snowflake keeps 6 and order 6 at every stage.
Quick check
?You turn a stage-2 snowflake about its centre. What is the smallest turn that makes it look exactly as it did?
Mission 3

Zoom in and see it again

  • Zoom in on one edge of the snowflake and you see the same jagged shape again. Zoom again: the same again.
  • This is called self-similarity. Shapes like this are called fractals.
  • The word was coined by Benoit Mandelbrot in 1975, from the Latin fractus, "broken".
Mission 3

Where it came from

  • The Swedish mathematician Helge von Koch published the curve in 1904.
  • He wasn't modelling snow. His paper's title says why he built it: a continuous curve with no tangent anywhere, made by an elementary construction.
  • A real snow crystal also has six-fold symmetry, from the way its water molecules are arranged in ice. The winter science lesson has that story.
Mission check

?You start with a triangle. So why six arms?

Step 1 turns it into a six-pointed star; later steps keep that symmetry.

Finale

Two sequences, two fates

  • Perimeters: 27, 36, 48, 64, … Rule × 4/3. The multiplier is more than 1, so the terms grow without bound.
  • Areas added: 1/3, 4/27, 16/243, … Rule × 4/9. The multiplier is less than 1, so the terms shrink towards zero.
  • Both are geometric sequences: each term is the one before multiplied by the same number.
Quick check
?The perimeters 27, 36, 48, 64, … cm form a sequence. What is its term-to-term rule?
Recap

The big ideas

  • The rule: cut each edge in thirds, replace the middle third with two sides of a smaller equilateral triangle.
  • Edges × 4, length × 1/3, so the perimeter × 4/3 each step: 27, 36, 48, 64 cm. It grows without bound.
  • New triangles × 4, each area × 1/9, so the area added × 4/9 each step: 1/3, 4/27, 16/243. The shape never leaves one circle.
  • At every stage the shape is finite. "Infinite" describes the never-ending process, not any one stage.
  • Six lines of symmetry and order 6, from the stage-1 star onwards. Zoom in: the same shape again, a fractal.
