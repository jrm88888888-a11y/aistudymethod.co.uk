Mission 1

The paper at a glance

  • Religion, philosophy and ethics in the modern world from the perspective of one religion: 06 Christian, 07 Muslim, 08 Jewish, 09 Buddhist, 10 Hindu.
  • 2 hours, 126 marks: 120 for answers + 6 for SPaG.
  • Four compulsory questions, one per theme: relationships and families · the existence of God, gods and the ultimate reality · religion, peace and conflict · dialogue between religious and non-religious beliefs and attitudes.
  • Each question: (a) 3 · (b) 6 · (c) 6 · (d) 15 = 30 marks.
  • Roughly a mark a minute: about 30 minutes per question, half of it on (d).
  • Half of your whole GCSE: AO1 20%, AO2 30%. This paper leans towards argument.
Mission 1

Command words decide the task

  • OCR puts every command word in one of four categories.
  • 1. Name, State, Give (3 marks, AO1): recall facts.
  • 2. Describe, Outline (3 or 6 marks, AO1): knowledge and understanding, developed.
  • 3. Explain, Compare (6 marks, AO2): analyse and evaluate; build chains of reasoning.
  • 4. "Statement." Discuss. (15 marks, AO1 + AO2): knowledge and argument, ending in a judgement.
  • Read the command word first. It tells you the kind of answer, not just the topic.
Mission 1

Part (a): three marks, three facts

  • Usually "Outline three…", "State three…" or "Give three…".
  • One mark per separate, accurate point. No development needed.
  • Example: "Outline three Christian beliefs about the purposes of marriage."
  • A lifelong union of love; a setting for having and raising children; a covenant made before God.
  • Three ways of saying the same thing earn one mark. Make each point different.
  • Spend about 3 minutes. Save time for (d).

Mission 1

Part (b): describe, with understanding

  • 6 marks, AO1. Command: Describe or Outline.
  • Aim for two or three points, each developed: what it is → detail or example → a source.
  • Show you understand: use correct specialist terms and say what they mean.
  • Show different views within your religion where they exist (e.g. Catholic and Protestant; Sunni and Shi'a; Orthodox and Reform).
  • No evaluation needed. "This is a good view" earns nothing here.
Mission 1

Part (c): explain is analysis

  • 6 marks, AO2. Command: Explain or Compare.
  • Explain: give reasons, each linked to a belief, teaching or source: "because… this means… so…".
  • Compare: set two views side by side, say how they are alike or differ, and why (different sources, different starting beliefs).
  • Comparisons are often between your religion and non-religious views, or between groups within your religion.
  • A list of facts, however accurate, is description. Turn each fact into a reason.
Mission 1Read the source

What OCR asks for

The specification's command-word table defines category 3 (Explain, Compare) as:

> "Demonstrate the ability to analyse and evaluate aspects of religion and belief. Deconstructing information and/or issues to find connections and provide logical chains of reasoning."

Category 4 (Discuss) adds an emphasis on coherent, reasoned responses supported by evidence (paraphrase).

Q1 Which phrase tells you a list of facts will not score well?

Show answer

"logical chains of reasoning"; also "find connections".

Q2 What does Discuss need that Explain may not?

Show answer

Knowledge and understanding (AO1) as well as analysis, built into one coherent, reasoned answer supported by evidence.

Mission 1

Part (d): the 15-mark discussion

  • A stimulus statement, then "Discuss."
  • Knowledge (AO1): beliefs, teachings, sources, with accurate terms.
  • Argument (AO2): reasons that support the statement and reasons against it.
  • Different perspectives: views within your religion; other religious, philosophical or non-religious views where they help.
  • Weigh: which reasons are stronger, and why?
  • Conclude: a judgement that follows from your reasoning. Either side can reach full marks.
  • SPaG: 6 marks, given in the first two extended answers on the paper (the paper shows which). Spell specialist terms correctly.
Mission 1Worked answer

One developed (d) paragraph

Statement: "Marriage should be for life." Discuss. (Christian perspective)

  • View and reason: many Christians agree, because marriage is a lifelong covenant made before God.
  • Source: Jesus says: "What therefore God has joined together, don't let man tear apart." (Matthew 19:6)
  • Diversity: the Catholic Church teaches that a valid sacramental marriage cannot be dissolved; it may grant an annulment (a ruling that no valid marriage existed). Many Protestant Churches allow divorce and remarriage, for example after adultery (Matthew 5:31–32) or the breakdown of a marriage.
  • Weigh: Catholics point to Jesus' teaching that marriage is permanent (Mark 10:9–12); many Protestants point to the exception in Matthew 5:32 and to forgiveness and fresh starts. Which reading is stronger, and why?
  • Non-religious view: Humanists value commitment but see ending a marriage that has failed as sometimes the kinder choice.
  • Link to judgement: "So the strength of the statement depends on whether…" Then decide, and say why.

Mission 1

Sources, spellings, names

  • Your spec lists suggested sources of wisdom and authority. You get credit for any appropriate source, not only those listed.
  • For Buddhism and Hinduism, OCR treats listed texts as examples of where core ideas appear; teachings come from many sources and traditions.
  • Transliterated words: OCR uses one spelling; other common spellings are not penalised.
  • The Jewish paper (08) writes G-d, a form many Jews use out of respect.
  • The Buddhist and Hindu papers speak of Ultimate Reality in the God theme.
  • Keep to your religion's perspective, but use other views where the question or argument needs them.

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Each question: (a) 3 recall · (b) 6 describe (AO1) · (c) 6 explain or compare (AO2) · (d) 15 discuss (AO1 + AO2).
  • At OCR, Explain is AO2: give linked reasons, not description.
  • (d): views for and against, diversity, weigh, conclude; SPaG on the first two (d) answers.
