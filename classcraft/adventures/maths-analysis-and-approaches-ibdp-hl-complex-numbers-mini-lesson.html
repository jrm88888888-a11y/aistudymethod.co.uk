Mission 1

x2=−1x² = −1 has no real solution. Is inventing one just cheating?

Make a guess: x2=−1x² = −1 has no real solution. Is inventing one just cheating?

Number and algebra · complex numbers

The number ii and Cartesian form

  • Define ii by i2=−1i² = −1. Then square roots of negatives exist: −16​=16​i=4i√(−16) = √16 i = 4i.
  • A complex number in Cartesian form is z=a+biz = a + bi with a,b∈Ra, b ∈ ℝ.
  • aa is the real part, Re(z)=aRe(z) = a. bb is the imaginary part, Im(z)=bIm(z) = b: a real number, with no ii.
  • Real numbers have b=0b = 0; purely imaginary numbers have a=0a = 0.
  • Two complex numbers are equal only if their real parts are equal and their imaginary parts are equal.

Number and algebra · complex numbers

Arithmetic and the conjugate

  • Add or subtract: combine real parts and imaginary parts separately.
  • Multiply: expand the brackets as usual, then replace i2i² with −1−1.
  • The complex conjugate of z=a+biz = a + bi is z∗=a−biz^(*) = a − bi. Then zz∗=a2+b2zz^(*) = a² + b², which is real.
  • Divide: multiply the top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator, so the denominator becomes real.

The Argand diagram and modulus

  • On an Argand diagram (the complex plane), z=a+biz = a + bi is the point (a,b)(a, b): real axis across, imaginary axis up.
  • The modulus ∣z∣=a2+b2​|z| = √(a² + b²) is the distance from the origin to the point. For example, ∣3+4i∣=5|3 + 4i| = 5.
  • The argument argzarg z is the angle from the positive real axis to the point (used in the next mission).
  • z∗z^(*) is the reflection of zz in the real axis.
  • Adding complex numbers adds them like vectors, so ∣z1​−z2​∣|z₁ − z₂| is the distance between the points z1​z₁ and z2​z₂.

Mission check

?x2=−1x² = −1 has no real solution. Is inventing one just cheating?

It is not cheating. Defining i2=−1i² = −1 gives a consistent number system: the usual rules of algebra still work, and complex roots check out exactly when you substitute them back. Real numbers are just the complex numbers with imaginary part 0.

Mission check

?Why does multiplying by ii rotate a point by 90°?

In Euler form i=e2π​ii = e^(π/2 i), with modulus 1 and argument 2π​π/2. Multiplying multiplies moduli (by 1, so no stretch) and adds arguments (adds 2π​π/2), so every point turns a quarter-turn anticlockwise about the origin.

Mission 3

A real cubic has root 2+i2 + i. What other root is guaranteed?

Number and algebra · complex numbers

Conjugate roots of real polynomials

  • If p(z)p(z) has real coefficients and p(w)=0p(w) = 0, then p(w∗)=0p(w^(*)) = 0: non-real roots come in conjugate pairs.
  • Why: the conjugate of a sum is the sum of conjugates, and of a product the product of conjugates. Real coefficients are unchanged by conjugating, so (p(w))∗=p(w∗)(p(w))^(*) = p(w^(*)), and 0∗=00^(*) = 0.
  • The pair w=a±biw = a ± bi gives a real quadratic factor: (z−w)(z−w∗)=z2−2az+(a2+b2)(z − w)(z − w^(*)) = z² − 2az + (a² + b²).
  • So a real cubic always has at least one real root.

Mission check

?A cubic with real coefficients has root 2+i2 + i. What other root is guaranteed?

2−i2 − i must be a root. Conjugating p(2+i)=0p(2 + i) = 0 leaves the real coefficients unchanged, so p(2−i)=0p(2 − i) = 0 too. Together they give the real factor z2−4z+5z² − 4z + 5, and the third root is real.

Recap

Big ideas

  • z=a+biz = a + bi with i2=−1i² = −1: Re(z)=aRe(z) = a, Im(z)=bIm(z) = b, conjugate z∗=a−biz^(*) = a − bi, modulus ∣z∣=a2+b2​|z| = √(a² + b²).
  • Multiply by expanding and using i2=−1i² = −1; divide by multiplying top and bottom by the conjugate of the denominator.
  • On the Argand diagram zz is the point (a,b)(a, b); a real quadratic with b2−4ac<0b² − 4ac < 0 has a conjugate pair of complex roots.
  • z=r(cosθ+isinθ)=rcisθ=reiθz = r(cos θ + isin θ) = r cis θ = re^iθ: find rr by Pythagoras and θθ from a sketch, with −π<θ≤π−π < θ ≤ π.
  • Multiply moduli and add arguments; divide moduli and subtract arguments; for powers, (reiθ)n=rneinθ(re^iθ)ⁿ = rⁿe^inθ. Multiplying by ww is a stretch by ∣w∣|w| and a rotation by argwarg w.
  • The nn roots of zn=wzⁿ = w have modulus rn1​r^(1/n) and arguments nθ+2kπ​(θ + 2kπ)/n: a regular nn-gon.
  • A polynomial with real coefficients has its non-real roots in conjugate pairs; a±bia ± bi gives the real factor z2−2az+a2+b2z² − 2az + a² + b².
  • De Moivre's theorem, (cosθ+isinθ)n=cosnθ+isinnθ(cos θ + isin θ)ⁿ = cos nθ + isin nθ, is proved for positive integers by induction using compound angle identities.
