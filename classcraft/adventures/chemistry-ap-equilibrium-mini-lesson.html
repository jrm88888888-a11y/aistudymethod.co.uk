Mission 1

Mission 1: Air is mostly nitrogen and oxygen, which can react to form NO. Why isn't the air turning into NO?

Make a guess: Air is mostly nitrogen and oxygen, which can react to form NO. Why isn't the air turning into NO?

Mission 1

Dynamic equilibrium

  • A reversible reaction can go both ways: ⇌.
  • Examples: evaporation and condensation, dissolving and precipitation, proton transfer (acid–base), electron transfer (redox).
  • In a closed system, start with reactants only.
  • Forward rate falls as reactants are used up. Backward rate rises as products form.
  • At dynamic equilibrium the two rates are equal, and not zero.
  • Concentrations are then constant, but not usually equal.
  • On graphs: concentrations (or partial pressures) level off; the forward and reverse rate lines meet and run level together.
Mission 1

Signs of an equilibrium

  • Observable properties (color, pressure, density) stay constant.
  • Reactants and products are both present.
  • It needs a closed system: nothing enters or leaves.
  • Physical changes reach equilibrium too: Br2(l) ⇌ Br2(g) in a stoppered flask.
  • The same equilibrium can be reached from either direction (same T, same overall amounts).
  • Before equilibrium, the faster direction decides the net change. Equal rates: no net change.
Quick check
?A sealed flask at constant temperature contains a mixture of brown NO2(g) and colorless N2O4(g), which interconvert by the reaction N2O4(g) ⇌ 2 NO2(g). The intensity of the brown color has not changed for an hour. Which of the following best explains why the color stays the same?
Mission 1

The equilibrium constant expression

  • For aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD:
  • Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / ([A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ), using equilibrium concentrations in M. For gases, partial pressures give Kp (more in Mission 3).
  • Products on top. Each coefficient becomes a power. Terms are multiplied.
  • Worked: CH4(g) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H2(g) gives Kc = [CO][H2]³ / ([CH4][H2O]).
  • Steam, H2O(g), is a species like any other.
  • The reaction quotient Q has the same form, using concentrations at any moment. At equilibrium, Qc = Kc (and Qp = Kp).
Mission 1

Homogeneous and heterogeneous

  • Homogeneous equilibrium: all species in the same phase.
  • Heterogeneous equilibrium: species in more than one phase.
  • Solids and pure liquids are left out of K: their concentrations are constant.
  • CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g): Kc = [CO2].
  • C(s) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + H2(g): Kc = [CO][H2] / [H2O]. Steam is a gas here, so it stays in.
Quick check
?Which is the equilibrium constant expression for 4NH3(g) + 5O2(g) ⇌ 4NO(g) + 6H2O(g)?
Mission 1

K from a particle diagram

  • A rigid box at equilibrium for A(g) ⇌ B(g) shows 4 A and 12 B.
  • Same volume for both, so concentrations are in the ratio of the particle counts.
  • K = [B]/[A] = 12 ÷ 4 = 3.0. K has no units in AP Chemistry.
  • When powers differ (e.g. 2 A ⇌ B), you need what each particle stands for (e.g. 0.010 M).
  • A diagram with B/A below 3.0 has Q < K: more B will form.
Quick check
?For the hypothetical reaction D(g) ⇌ E(g), Kc = 3.0 at a certain temperature. A particulate diagram of a mixture of the two gases in a rigid container at this temperature shows 4 particles of D and 8 particles of E. Which claim about the mixture is correct?
Mission 1

What the size of K tells you

KAt equilibrium
K ≫ 1almost all products: reaction nearly complete
K > 1products favored
K ≈ 1significant amounts of both
K < 1reactants favored
K ≪ 1almost all reactants: hardly any reaction
  • K tells you how far a reaction goes, not how fast.
  • Air: N2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) has K ≈ 4 × 10⁻³¹ at 298 K. At equilibrium, air holds hardly any NO.
Quick check
?A student plans to mix equal volumes of 0.20 M X(aq) and 0.10 M Y(aq) at 25°C, where they react according to the hypothetical equation X(aq) + Y(aq) ⇌ Z(aq), Kc = 5 × 10¹¹ at 25°C. After the mixture reaches equilibrium, the student will measure the concentrations of the three species. Which prediction about the equilibrium [Y] is correct?
Mission check

?A mixture of hydrogen and chlorine can be kept in the dark for days with no visible change. Yet for H2(g) + Cl2(g) ⇌ 2HCl(g), K ≈ 2 × 10³³ at 298 K. What do these facts tell you?

K tells you how far, not how fast. Air is the opposite case: for N2 + O2 ⇌ 2NO, K ≈ 4 × 10⁻³¹ at 298 K, so air at equilibrium holds hardly any NO. The reaction is endothermic, so K is larger at high temperature: hot car engines do make some NO.

Mission 2

Mission 2: A sealed flask looks unchanging. Can you tell which way it will react?

Make a guess: A sealed flask looks unchanging. Can you tell which way it will react?

Mission 2

Which way will it react?

  • The reaction quotient Q has the same form as K, using concentrations (or pressures) at any moment.
  • Q < K: too little product. Net forward reaction until Q = K.
  • Q > K: too much product. Net backward reaction.
  • Q = K: at equilibrium; no net change.
  • A disturbance makes Q ≠ K. Concentration stresses change Q; a temperature change alters K.
Quick check
?For the hypothetical reaction A(g) + B(g) ⇌ C(g), Kc = 4.0 at a certain temperature. At one moment, a reaction mixture at this temperature has Qc = 25. Which statement correctly describes the mixture at that moment?
Mission 2

ICE tables (worked example)

A(g) + B(g) ⇌ 2C(g). Start: [A] = [B] = 0.50, no C. At equilibrium, [C] = 0.60.

Concentration[A][B][C]
Initial0.500.500

  • All values in M.
  • Changes follow the coefficients: 0.60 of C needs 0.30 each of A and B.
  • Kc = [C]² ÷ ([A][B]) = 0.36 ÷ 0.040 = 9.0.
Mission 2

Starting from moles (worked example)

  • PCl5(g) ⇌ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g).
  • 0.500 mol PCl5 is sealed in a 2.00 L flask. At equilibrium it holds 0.200 mol Cl2.
  • Moles at equilibrium: Cl2 0.200, PCl3 0.200, PCl5 0.500 − 0.200 = 0.300.
  • Divide by the volume: [PCl5] = 0.150, [PCl3] = [Cl2] = 0.100.
  • K = (0.100)(0.100)/0.150 = 0.0667.
  • Two terms on top, one below: the volume does not cancel. Moles alone give 0.133, twice too big.
Quick check
?A sample of the hypothetical gas X2 is placed in an evacuated rigid container at an initial concentration of 0.80 M, and the reaction X2(g) ⇌ 2 X(g) occurs at constant temperature. When the concentrations have stopped changing, [X] = 0.40 M. What is the value of Kc for the reaction at this temperature?
Mission 2

Changing the equation changes K

  • Reverse the equation: K becomes 1/K.
  • Multiply every coefficient by n: K becomes Kⁿ. Halving gives √K.
  • Add two equations: multiply their K values.
  • Q follows exactly the same rules.
  • Worked: A ⇌ 2B, K = 0.25. Then 2B ⇌ A: K = 1/0.25 = 4.0. ½A ⇌ B: K = √0.25 = 0.50.
  • Compare ΔH (Hess's law): reverse → change sign; multiply → multiply; add → add. K: invert, raise to a power, multiply.
Quick check
?At a certain temperature, reaction 1, 2 SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2 SO3(g), has equilibrium constant K1, and reaction 2, 2 NO(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2 NO2(g), has equilibrium constant K2. Which expression gives the equilibrium constant at the same temperature for the reaction SO2(g) + NO2(g) ⇌ SO3(g) + NO(g)?
Mission 2

When K is very small

  • Very little reactant reacts, so [reactant] at equilibrium ≈ [reactant] at the start.
  • Then solve for the product directly. No quadratic needed.
  • Z2(g) ⇌ 2 Z(g), K = 4.0 × 10⁻⁸, [Z2] at the start = 0.10 M.
  • [Z]² = K × [Z2] = 4.0 × 10⁻⁸ × 0.10 = 4.0 × 10⁻⁹.
  • So [Z] = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ M.
  • Check: only 3.2 × 10⁻⁵ of the 0.10 of Z2 reacted. The approximation holds.
Quick check
?For the hypothetical reaction X2(g) ⇌ 2 X(g), Kc = 3.6 × 10⁻⁶ at a certain temperature. A rigid container at this temperature is filled with X2 to an initial concentration of 0.10 M, with no X present. What is the equilibrium concentration of X?
Mission 2

K and Gibbs free energy

  • The link: ΔG° = −RT ln K (R = 8.314 J/(mol·K)). You'll use it in Unit 9.
  • ΔG° negative ↔ K > 1: products favored. ΔG° positive ↔ K < 1.
  • K = 1000 at 298 K: ΔG° = −(8.314)(298) ln 1000 = −17.1 kJ/molrxn.
Mission check

?H2(g) + I2(g) ⇌ 2 HI(g), K = 54 at 700 K. A sealed flask at 700 K holds [H2] = 0.20 M, [I2] = 0.050 M and [HI] = 0.80 M. It looks unchanging. Calculate Q. Will it react, and which way?

Yes, you can tell. Calculate Q and compare it with K: Q > K, net reverse; Q < K, net forward.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Deep underwater, a diver breathes ordinary air. Why can its oxygen become poisonous?

Make a guess: Deep underwater, a diver breathes ordinary air. Why can its oxygen become poisonous?

Mission 3

Mole fraction and partial pressure

  • Mole fraction of gas A: x(A) = moles of A ÷ total moles of gas.
  • Mole fractions add up to 1.
  • Partial pressure: the pressure one gas in a mixture would exert on its own in the same volume.
  • PA = XA × Ptotal. Each gas's partial pressure is independent of the other gases.
  • Partial pressures add up to the total pressure.
Mission 3

The diver

  • Air is about 21% O2 by moles: x(O2) ≈ 0.21.
  • About every 10 m of seawater adds about 1 atm. At 40 m the total pressure is about 5 atm.
  • p(O2) = 0.21 × 5 atm ≈ 1.05 atm: five times the oxygen pressure at the surface.
  • Above about 1.4–1.6 atm of O2, the risk of oxygen toxicity rises sharply: it can cause seizures. Breathing air, that point comes at roughly 55–65 m.
  • Same mole fraction, bigger total pressure → bigger partial pressure.
Quick check
?A rigid flask contains N2(g). Some Ar(g) is then added to the flask while the temperature is held constant. Which statement about the partial pressure of N2 after the Ar is added is correct?
Mission 3

The Kp expression

  • For gas equilibria, use equilibrium partial pressures instead of concentrations.
  • N2(g) + 3 H2(g) ⇌ 2 NH3(g): Kp = (PNH3)² / (PN2 × (PH2)³). Qp has the same form at any moment.
  • Products on top, coefficients as powers: as for Kc.
  • Kp has no units. Use partial pressures in atm.
Mission 3

Heterogeneous equilibria

  • Only gases go into Kp. Solids (and pure liquids) are left out.
  • CaCO3(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO2(g): Kp = PCO2.
  • So at a fixed temperature, the CO2 pressure over hot limestone is fixed, however much solid there is.
Quick check
?3 Fe(s) + 4 H2O(g) ⇌ Fe3O4(s) + 4 H2(g). Which is the expression for Kp?
Mission 3

Calculating Kp (worked example)

  • N2O4(g) ⇌ 2NO2(g). Equilibrium: 0.60 mol N2O4 and 0.80 mol NO2. Total 1.40 mol.
  • Step 1, mole fractions: x(N2O4) = 0.60 ÷ 1.40 = 0.429; x(NO2) = 0.80 ÷ 1.40 = 0.571.
  • Step 2, partial pressures (× total), then Step 3, Kp:
  • Total 1.40 atm: PN2O4 = 0.600 atm, PNO2 = 0.800 atm. Kp = (0.800)²/0.600 = 1.07.
  • Keep unrounded mole fractions until the end.
Quick check
?PCl5(g) ⇌ PCl3(g) + Cl2(g). An equilibrium mixture contains 0.80 mol PCl5, 0.60 mol PCl3 and 0.60 mol Cl2. What is Kp?
  • The total pressure is 2.50 atm.
Mission check

?2SO2(g) + O2(g) ⇌ 2SO3(g). An equilibrium mixture contains 0.20 mol SO2, 0.10 mol O2 and 1.70 mol SO3. What is Kp?
  • The total pressure is 4.00 atm.

Same rule as the diver's oxygen: partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure. SO3 is 0.85 of the gas, so it supplies 0.85 of the pressure.

Mission 4: Why do ammonia plants squeeze their gases to about 200 times atmospheric pressure?

Make a guess: Why do ammonia plants squeeze their gases to about 200 times atmospheric pressure?

Mission 4

Le Châtelier's principle

  • Disturb a system at equilibrium and the position shifts to oppose the change (partly).
  • Position of equilibrium: the composition of the equilibrium mixture.
  • Concentration and pressure change the position, not K.
  • Temperature changes the position and K.
  • A catalyst changes neither.
  • Why it works: a disturbance makes Q ≠ K. The net reaction then runs until Q = K again.
Mission 4

Changing a concentration

  • N2(g) + 3H2(g) ⇌ 2NH3(g).
  • Add N2: the position shifts right: more NH3.
  • Remove NH3: the position shifts right, replacing some of it.
  • The shift only partly uses up the added N2.
  • K is unchanged.
  • Why: adding N2 makes Q < K, so there is net forward reaction until Q = K.
Quick check
?The hypothetical reaction D(g) + E(g) ⇌ G(g) is at equilibrium in a rigid container with [D] = 0.20 M. More D(g) is then injected, which raises [D] to 0.50 M at the moment of injection. Which statement describes [D] once equilibrium is re-established at the same temperature?
Mission 4

Changing the pressure

  • Raise the pressure by reducing the volume: the position shifts to the side with fewer moles of gas.
  • N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3: 4 mol of gas → 2 mol, so it shifts right.
  • Equal moles of gas on each side: no shift.
  • Only gases count. K is unchanged.
  • Dilution of a solution shifts toward the side with more dissolved particles.
Quick check
?CH4(g) + H2O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H2(g). The pressure is increased, by reducing the volume, at constant temperature. What happens?
Mission 4

Changing the temperature

  • Raise T: the position shifts in the endothermic direction.
  • Forward exothermic (ΔH < 0): heating shifts it left; K decreases.
  • Forward endothermic (ΔH > 0): heating shifts it right; K increases.
  • Temperature is the only change that alters K.
  • A temperature change alters K, not Q. A concentration change alters Q, not K.
Quick check
?A sealed glass flask contains an equilibrium mixture of brown NO2(g) and colorless N2O4(g): 2 NO2(g) ⇌ N2O4(g). The flask is moved from a water bath at 25°C to an ice-water bath at 0°C. The brown color becomes paler and then stays the same. Which claim about K = [N2O4]/[NO2]² is supported by this observation?
Mission 4

Catalysts, and the summary

  • A catalyst speeds up forward and backward reactions equally.
  • Equilibrium is reached sooner: same composition, same K.

  • Concentration, volume and dilution stresses change Q; temperature changes K.
Mission 4

Seeing a shift in the lab

  • Colored equilibria show the shift directly.
  • 2CrO4²⁻(aq) (yellow) + 2H⁺(aq) ⇌ Cr2O7²⁻(aq) (orange) + H2O(l).
  • Add acid: more H⁺, shifts right: orange. Add alkali: OH⁻ removes H⁺, shifts left: yellow.
  • [Co(H2O)6]²⁺ (pink) + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl4]²⁻ (blue) + 6H2O. Forward endothermic.
  • Warm it: bluer. Cool it in ice: pinker. Add concentrated HCl: bluer.
  • Method: equal volumes in test tubes; change one factor; compare with an untouched control tube.
  • pH: NH3(aq) + H2O(l) ⇌ NH4⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq). Add NH4Cl: shift left, [OH⁻] falls, pH falls.
  • Temperature: in an insulated vessel, a shift in the exothermic direction warms the mixture.
Quick check
?A test tube holds orange potassium dichromate(VI) solution: 2CrO4²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) ⇌ Cr2O7²⁻(aq) + H2O(l). Sodium hydroxide solution is added drop by drop. What is seen, and why?
Mission check

?Methanol is made from CO(g) + 2H2(g) ⇌ CH3OH(g). The forward reaction is exothermic. Plants run it at high pressure. Why does raising the pressure, by reducing the volume, increase the equilibrium yield of methanol?

High pressure shifts the position toward fewer moles of gas. Ammonia is the same story: N2 + 3H2 ⇌ 2NH3 goes from 4 mol of gas to 2, so about 200 atm pushes the position toward ammonia. High pressure also speeds up the reaction, but that is a separate rate effect.

Mission 5

Mission 5: Many barium compounds are poisonous, yet patients drink a barium mixture before X-rays. Why is it safe?

Make a guess: Many barium compounds are poisonous, yet patients drink a barium mixture before X-rays. Why is it safe?

Mission 5

Dissolving is an equilibrium

  • Stir excess solid with water. Ions leave the solid and rejoin it.
  • Saturated solution: the two rates are equal. Dynamic equilibrium.
  • BaSO4(s) ⇌ Ba²⁺(aq) + SO4²⁻(aq)
  • Ksp = [Ba²⁺][SO4²⁻]: the solubility product. The solid is left out.
  • At 25 °C, Ksp(BaSO4) = 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰.
Mission 5

From Ksp to molar solubility

  • Molar solubility, s: moles of solid that dissolve per liter of saturated solution.
  • BaSO4: [Ba²⁺] = [SO4²⁻] = s, so Ksp = s².
  • s = √(1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ M.
  • That is only about 1.4 mg of Ba²⁺ per liter.
  • Soluble barium salts, such as BaCl2, release far more Ba²⁺: those are the poisonous ones.
Mission 5

Salts that give more ions

  • MX2(s) ⇌ M²⁺(aq) + 2 X⁻(aq): [M²⁺] = s, [X⁻] = 2s.
  • Ksp = s × (2s)² = 4s³.
  • Other way round: saturated PbI2 has [Pb²⁺] = 1.3 × 10⁻³ M, so [I⁻] = 2.6 × 10⁻³ M.
  • Ksp = (1.3 × 10⁻³)(2.6 × 10⁻³)² = 8.8 × 10⁻⁹.
  • Comparing salts? Compare s, not Ksp, unless they give the same number of ions.
Quick check
?The hypothetical salt MX2 dissolves according to the equation MX2(s) ⇌ M²⁺(aq) + 2 X⁻(aq). At 25°C, Ksp for MX2 is 4.0 × 10⁻⁹. What is the molar solubility of MX2 in water at 25°C?
Mission 5

Large Ksp, soluble salt

  • Ksp > 1: the salt counts as soluble.
  • Ksp ≪ 1: slightly soluble, like BaSO4.
  • Even a soluble salt stops dissolving once its ion product reaches Ksp.
  • Always soluble: salts of Na⁺, K⁺, NH4⁺, and all nitrates.
Quick check
?The hypothetical salt AB dissolves according to the equation AB(s) ⇌ A⁺(aq) + B⁻(aq). At 25°C, Ksp for AB is 25. Which statement about AB at 25°C is correct?
Mission 5

Will a precipitate form?

  • Work out Q = the ion product, using concentrations just after mixing.
  • Q > Ksp: a precipitate forms until Q = Ksp.
  • Q ≤ Ksp: no precipitate.
  • Mix equal volumes of 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ M BaCl2 and 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ M Na2SO4. Mixing halves each: 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ M.
  • Q = (1.0 × 10⁻⁴)(1.0 × 10⁻⁴) = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ > 1.1 × 10⁻¹⁰: BaSO4 precipitates.
Mission 5

The common-ion effect

  • Dissolve BaSO4 in 0.010 M Na2SO4: SO4²⁻ is already there.
  • Le Châtelier: extra SO4²⁻ shifts BaSO4(s) ⇌ Ba²⁺ + SO4²⁻ left. Less dissolves.
  • Calculation: [SO4²⁻] ≈ 0.010 M, since s from BaSO4 is tiny.
  • s = [Ba²⁺] = Ksp ÷ 0.010 = 1.1 × 10⁻⁸ M: about 1000 times less than in pure water.
  • Ksp is unchanged; only the solubility falls.
Quick check
?The hypothetical salt MX2 dissolves according to the equation MX2(s) ⇌ M²⁺(aq) + 2 X⁻(aq), with Ksp = 3.2 × 10⁻¹¹ at 25°C. What is the molar solubility of MX2 at 25°C in a 0.020 M solution of NaX, a soluble salt?
Mission 5

Acid can dissolve some salts

  • If the anion is a base, acid removes it: F⁻, CH3COO⁻, CO3²⁻ (conjugate bases of weak acids) and OH⁻.
  • CaF2(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺(aq) + 2 F⁻(aq). Add acid: H3O⁺ + F⁻ → HF + H2O.
  • Less F⁻: the equilibrium shifts right. More CaF2 dissolves.
  • Hydroxides: Mg(OH)2 dissolves more at low pH; added OH⁻ makes it less soluble.
  • Anions of strong acids (Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻): solubility barely changes with pH.
  • Predict the direction only: no solubility-versus-pH calculations.
Quick check
?Solid CaF2 is in equilibrium with its saturated solution: CaF2(s) ⇌ Ca²⁺(aq) + 2F⁻(aq). HF is a weak acid. A small amount of HNO3(aq) is added at constant temperature, and solid CaF2 remains. Which statement correctly describes [Ca²⁺] after equilibrium is re-established, and why?
Mission check

?Silver chloride has Ksp = 1.8 × 10⁻¹⁰ at 25 °C. What is its molar solubility in 0.10 M NaCl(aq) at 25 °C?

A tiny Ksp keeps the dissolved ion concentration tiny, so a BaSO4 drink releases only a trace of Ba²⁺. A common ion, as with AgCl here, lowers the solubility further.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Dynamic equilibrium: equal forward and backward rates, constant concentrations, closed system.
  • K = products over reactants, coefficients as powers. K ≫ 1: mostly products; K ≪ 1: mostly reactants.
  • Q has the same form as K at any moment; solids and pure liquids are left out.

Mission 2

  • ICE tables: changes follow the coefficients; divide moles by volume unless the volume cancels.
  • Q < K: net forward; Q > K: net backward. Reverse → 1/K; × n → Kⁿ; add → multiply.

Mission 3

  • Partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure; partial pressures add up to the total.
  • Kp: partial pressures in place of concentrations; only gases appear.
  • Kp has no units; pressures in atm.

Mission 4

  • Le Châtelier: the position shifts to partly oppose a change. Only temperature changes K; a catalyst changes neither position nor K.
  • Concentration and volume stresses change Q; temperature changes K.

Mission 5

  • Ksp is K for a dissolving salt; the solid is left out. Find s from Ksp (s², 4s³ …) and back.
  • Q > Ksp: precipitate. A common ion lowers solubility; acid raises the solubility of salts with basic anions.
