Decision Making in Geography

This mini-lesson covers A2 3 - Decision Making in Geography: how to approach the compulsory decision-making exercise - adopting a role, analysing a resource booklet, processing quantitative data, examining conflicting values, weighing alternatives and making a justified recommendation.

This unit is a synoptic decision-making exercise. Answer each question to unlock the next screen. Press Start.

The exercise

What the A2 3 exam involves

A2 3 is a compulsory decision-making exercise based on a real-world case study. You:

  • Adopt and maintain a particular role throughout.
  • Review and analyse a resource booklet - maps, statistics/data, reports, infographics, diagrams and photographs.
  • Write your answer as a structured report using the headings and subheadings provided.

It is synoptic: you draw together knowledge and skills from across the whole course.

Working with resources

Processing and interpreting data

You must process and present quantitative data using appropriate techniques and interpret and evaluate all the information. Resources come in two broad forms:

  • Quantitative - statistics, data tables, choropleth maps, graphs.
  • Qualitative - reports, interview quotes, photographs, opinions.

Strong answers cross-reference resources and judge their reliability rather than taking each at face value.

Making the decision

Alternatives and justification

To reach a decision you consider alternative choices, then make and justify a recommendation on the basis of greater overall benefits.

A convincing justification:

  • refers directly to the evidence in the resources;
  • weighs benefits against costs and drawbacks;
  • acknowledges the rejected alternatives and why they were weaker;
  • stays consistent with your adopted role.
