Sequences and series · binomial expansion

Pascal's triangle and (a+b)n(a+b)ⁿ

  • Each row of Pascal's triangle starts and ends with 1; every other entry is the sum of the two above it.
  • Row nn gives the coefficients of (a+b)n(a+b)ⁿ. Row 4 is 1, 4, 6, 4, 1:
(a+b)4=a4+4a3b+6a2b2+4ab3+b4(a+b)⁴=a⁴+4a³b+6a²b²+4ab³+b⁴
  • In each term the power of aa falls by 1 as the power of bb rises by 1; the powers always add to nn.

Sequences and series · binomial expansion

Using an expansion to approximate

  • Write the number as a bracket with a small part: 2.01=2+0.012.01=2+0.01, 0.97=1−3(0.01)0.97=1−3(0.01).
  • Expand in powers of xx, then substitute the small value. Each extra power of xx makes the terms much smaller, so the first few terms give a good estimate.
  • Keep adding terms until the next one is too small to change the digits you need.

Arithmetic sequences (A2 only)

  • An arithmetic sequence (or arithmetic progression, AP) goes up or down by the same common difference dd each time.
  • With first term aa, the nnth term is
un​=a+(n−1)duₙ=a+(n−1)d
  • Example: 7,11,15,…7, 11, 15, … has a=7a=7, d=4d=4, so un​=7+4(n−1)=4n+3uₙ=7+4(n−1)=4n+3.
  • The nnth term has only n−1n−1 steps of dd added to aa.

The sum to nn terms, and its proof

  • Write the series forwards and backwards, then add:

Sn​=a+(a+d)+⋯+(l−d)+lSₙ=a+(a+d)+…+(l−d)+l Sn​=l+(l−d)+⋯+(a+d)+aSₙ=l+(l−d)+…+(a+d)+a

  • Every column adds to a+la+l, and there are nn columns: 2Sn​=n(a+l)2Sₙ=n(a+l).
  • So Sn​=2n​(a+l)Sₙ=n/2(a+l). With l=a+(n−1)dl=a+(n−1)d: Sn​=2n​(2a+(n−1)d)Sₙ=n/2(2a+(n−1)d).
  • With a=1a=1, d=1d=1: 1+2+⋯+n=2n(n+1)​1+2+…+n=(n(n+1))/2.

  • A geometric sequence multiplies by the same common ratio rr each time: r=un​un+1​​r = (uₙ₊₁)/uₙ.

Sequences and series · recurrence and sigma

Two ways to define a sequence (A2 only)

  • Formula for the nth term: un​=3n+1uₙ = 3n + 1 gives 4, 7, 10, … and jumps straight to any term.
  • Recurrence relation: a rule un+1​=f(un​)uₙ₊₁ = f(uₙ) plus a first term. un+1​=2un​−1uₙ₊₁ = 2uₙ − 1 with u1​=3u₁ = 3 gives 3, 5, 9, 17, …
  • The first term matters: the same rule with u1​=1u₁ = 1 gives 1, 1, 1, …
  • A sequence is a list of terms; a series is a sum of terms. A sequence can be finite or infinite.

Increasing, decreasing and periodic sequences (A2 only)

  • Increasing: un+1​>un​uₙ₊₁ > uₙ for all nn. Decreasing: un+1​<un​uₙ₊₁ < uₙ for all nn.
  • Periodic: the terms repeat in a cycle. If un+k​=un​uₙ₊ₖ = uₙ for all nn, with kk the smallest such number, the sequence has order kk.
  • un+1​=un​1​uₙ₊₁ = 1/uₙ, u1​=3u₁ = 3 gives 3,31​,3,31​,…3, 1/3, 3, 1/3, …: periodic, order 2.
  • Long-term behaviour: a sequence may converge to a limit, diverge, or oscillate.
