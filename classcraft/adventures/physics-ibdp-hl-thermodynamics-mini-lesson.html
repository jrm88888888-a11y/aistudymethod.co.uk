Mission 1

Mission 1: Squash a gas and it warms up, though nothing hot touched it. Where did the energy come from?

Make a guess: Squash a gas and it warms up, though nothing hot touched it. Where did the energy come from?

Mission 1

Two ways to change internal energy

  • Internal energy U: random kinetic + potential energy of the particles. A property of the gas's state.
  • Heating: energy crosses the boundary because of a temperature difference.
  • Work: energy crosses the boundary because a force moves it (a piston).
  • Heat and work are energy in transit. A gas does not "contain" heat or work; it contains internal energy.
  • Closed system: no mass in or out; energy can cross as heat or work.
Mission 1

The first law

  • Conservation of energy for a gas: change in U = energy in by heating + work done on it.
  • Q = ΔU + W. Q: thermal energy supplied to the gas. W: work done by the gas.
Quick check
?The working gas of a heat engine is taken round one complete cycle, returning to its initial state. Which statement about the cycle is correct?
Mission 1

Work done by a gas

  • Gas at constant pressure pushes a piston of area A a distance Δx.
  • Work = force × distance = PA × Δx = PΔV (P = pressure).
  • W = PΔV. Expansion: W > 0. Compression: W < 0.
  • Units: Pa × m³ = N m⁻² × m³ = J.
Mission 1

Work as an area

  • On a pressure–volume graph, the work done = area under the graph, between the start and end volumes.
  • Constant pressure: a rectangle, pressure × ΔV.
  • Changing pressure: count squares, or use a trapezium for a straight line.
  • Expansion (moving right): work done by the gas. Compression (moving left): work done on the gas.
Mission 1

Worked example: heating at constant pressure

  • A monatomic ideal gas at 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa expands from 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 2.5 × 10⁻³ m³. Pressure stays constant.
  • PΔV = 2.0 × 10⁵ × 1.5 × 10⁻³ = 300 J of work done by the gas.
  • nRΔT = Δ(PV) = PΔV = 300 J, so ΔU = (3/2)nRΔT = 450 J.
  • Q = ΔU + W = 450 + 300 = 750 J.
Quick check
?A fixed mass of an ideal gas is taken from state X to state Y. On a P–V diagram the process is a straight line from X (1.0 × 10⁻³ m³, 4.0 × 10⁵ Pa) to Y (4.0 × 10⁻³ m³, 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa), where each state is given as (volume, pressure). What is the work W, as it appears in Q = ΔU + W, for this process?
Mission 1

Reading the signs

  • Negative Q: the gas loses energy by heating its surroundings.
  • Negative ΔU: internal energy falls, so the temperature of an ideal gas falls.
  • Negative W: work is done on the gas (it is compressed).
  • Constant volume: no work, so all the heat supplied goes into U.
Quick check
?A sample of 8.0 g of helium, a monatomic ideal gas of molar mass 4.0 g mol⁻¹, is heated at constant pressure from 20 °C to 80 °C. The thermal energy supplied is 2.49 × 10³ J. R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. What is the change in internal energy ΔU of the helium?
Mission check

?An ideal gas is in a cylinder whose walls and piston are perfectly insulated. The gas expands, pushing the piston outwards. What happens to the temperature of the gas?

Energy in = energy out + change stored. Push a piston in and you do work on the gas: its internal energy, and so its temperature, rises. Let it push out with no heating, and it cools.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Let a gas expand slowly or suddenly to the same volume. Which ends up colder, and why?

Make a guess: Let a gas expand slowly or suddenly to the same volume. Which ends up colder, and why?

Mission 2

Four special processes

  • Keep one thing fixed:
  • Isovolumetric: volume constant. A vertical line on a P–V diagram.
  • Isobaric: pressure constant. A horizontal line.
  • Isothermal: temperature constant. A curve, PV = constant (an isotherm). Needs a slow change with good thermal contact.
  • Adiabatic: no energy transferred by heating (Q = 0). Perfect insulation, or a change so fast that heating has no time to happen.
Quick check
?Which statement about a thermodynamic process of a fixed mass of monatomic ideal gas is correct?
Mission 2

The first law in each process

  • Recall: Q = ΔU + W, W done by the gas = PΔV.
  • Isovolumetric: W = 0, so all heating changes U.
  • Isobaric: work and a change in U together.
  • Isothermal (ideal gas): T fixed, so ΔU = 0.
  • Isothermal: Q = W. Heat in = work done by the gas.
  • Adiabatic: Q = 0.
  • Adiabatic: ΔU = −W. Compression (W < 0): U and T rise. Expansion: T falls.
Quick check
?The pressure–volume diagram shows two processes in which a sample of ideal gas expands from the same initial state i to the same final volume Vf. Process 1 is isothermal and process 2 is adiabatic. Which claim is supported by the diagram and the first law of thermodynamics?
Mission 2

Isotherms and adiabats

  • Isotherms: PV = constant curves. Hotter isotherms lie further from the origin.
  • An adiabat through the same point is steeper than the isotherm.
  • Adiabatic expansion: P falls faster, because T falls too. The gas ends on a cooler isotherm.
  • Adiabatic compression: the gas ends on a hotter isotherm.
  • Work = area under the curve. From the same start to the same final volume, the isothermal expansion does more work (bigger area).
Quick check
?The pressure–volume diagram shows a sample of ideal gas taken from state X to state Y along the path X → Z → Y. The dashed curve is an isotherm. Which claim about the temperatures TX and TY, with its evidence, is correct?
Mission 2

Adiabatic equation: PV5/3 = constant

  • Monatomic ideal gas, adiabatic change: PV5/3 = constant.
  • Combine with PV/T = constant to find T.
  • Example: helium at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 300 K, compressed adiabatically to half its volume.
  • P2 = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 25/3 = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 3.17 = 3.2 × 10⁵ Pa. (Isothermal would give only 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa.)
  • T2 = T1 × (P2V2)/(P1V1) = 300 × 3.17 × ½ = 4.8 × 10² K.
Quick check
?Helium, a monatomic ideal gas, has a pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and a volume of 3.0 × 10⁻³ m³. It is compressed adiabatically to a volume of 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³. For this process PV5/3 = constant. What is the final pressure of the helium?
Mission 2

Heat engines

  • A heat engine takes a working gas round a cycle of processes, back to its starting state.
  • Over one cycle ΔU = 0, so net Q = net W.
  • Net work by the gas = area enclosed by the loop (clockwise on a P–V diagram).
  • The gas takes in Qh from a hot reservoir, does work W, and gives out Qc to a cold reservoir: W = Qh − Qc.
  • Efficiency η = useful work ÷ input energy = W/Qh.
Mission 2

The Carnot limit

  • No engine working between two reservoirs can beat the Carnot efficiency: ηCarnot = 1 − Tc/Th (T in kelvin).
  • Qc can never be zero: some energy must always be given to the cold reservoir.
  • Example: steam at 800 K, cooling water at 300 K. ηCarnot = 1 − 300/800 = 0.625 (62.5%).
  • Real power stations reach well below this (friction, heat losses, processes that are not reversible).
  • Higher efficiency: make Th hotter or Tc colder.
Quick check
?A Carnot engine works between a hot reservoir at 227 °C and a cold reservoir at 27 °C. In each cycle it absorbs 1200 J of thermal energy from the hot reservoir. How much work does it do in each cycle?
Mission check

?Two identical samples of ideal gas start in the same state. Each is compressed to half its volume: one slowly, in good thermal contact with a water bath (isothermal); the other quickly, in an insulated cylinder (adiabatic). Which statement is correct?

In an adiabatic change no energy is exchanged by heating, so the work changes U. A sudden expansion is nearly adiabatic: the work done by the gas comes only from U, so it cools. A slow one is topped up by heating (isothermal).

Mission 3

Mission 3: Water freezes into a neat crystal. How is that allowed if disorder can never decrease overall?

Make a guess: Water freezes into a neat crystal. How is that allowed if disorder can never decrease overall?

Mission 3

What entropy measures

  • Entropy, S: relates to the degree of disorder of the particles in a system.
  • More ways to arrange the particles and share out their energy → higher entropy.
  • Solid → liquid → gas: entropy rises.
  • Localised energy tends to spread out: a hot spot in a metal bar warms its surroundings, never the reverse.
  • Unit: J K⁻¹. Entropy is not conserved.
Quick check
?In which of these changes does the entropy of the named substance increase?
Mission 3

Counting microstates

  • Toss 4 coins. Microstate: the exact sequence, e.g. HTHT. Macrostate: the number of heads.
  • 2⁴ = 16 microstates, all equally probable.
  • Microstates per macrostate (0, 1, 2, 3, 4 heads): 1, 4, 6, 4, 1.
  • "2 heads" is most likely: it has the most microstates (6 of 16).
  • S = kB ln Ω. Ω = number of microstates of the macrostate; kB = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹.
  • With 100 coins: P(all heads) ≈ 8 × 10⁻³¹. Ordered macrostates are possible but vanishingly rare.
Quick check
?Six fair coins change from the macrostate 'five heads' (6 microstates) to the macrostate 'three heads' (20 microstates). Using S = kB ln Ω, with kB = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹, what is the change in entropy?
Mission 3

Entropy from heat: ΔS = ΔQ/T

  • ΔS = ΔQ/T: thermal energy ΔQ transferred reversibly at constant absolute temperature T.
  • ΔQ into the system: ΔS positive. ΔQ out: ΔS negative.
  • Worked example: melt 0.50 kg of ice at 0 °C. Lf = 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹.
  • ΔQ = mL = 0.50 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 1.67 × 10⁵ J (into the ice).
  • T = 273 K (never 0 °C!).
  • ΔS = 1.67 × 10⁵ ÷ 273 = +612 J K⁻¹.
Mission 3

The second law

  • Isolated system: no matter and no energy cross its boundary.
  • Second law: the total entropy of an isolated system never decreases.
  • Reversible process (an ideal limit): entropy stays constant.
  • Irreversible process (every real one): entropy increases.
  • An isolated system moves towards thermodynamic equilibrium, where its entropy is a maximum.
  • Why heat flows hot → cold: 100 J leaves a body at 400 K and enters one at 300 K. ΔS = −100/400 + 100/300 = +0.083 J K⁻¹. The reverse would give −0.083 J K⁻¹: forbidden.
Mission 3

Clausius and Kelvin forms

  • Clausius form: thermal energy cannot flow spontaneously from a colder body to a hotter body.
  • A refrigerator does move energy cold → hot, but only because work is done on it.
  • Kelvin form: no device working in a cycle can take thermal energy from a single reservoir and convert it completely into work.
  • A heat engine must reject some energy to a colder reservoir.
  • Both forms say the same thing as "the entropy of an isolated system never decreases".
Quick check
?Helium, a monatomic ideal gas, expands reversibly and isothermally at 27 °C, doing 600 J of work on its surroundings. What is the entropy change of the helium?
Quick check
?Water placed in the freezer compartment of a refrigerator freezes, and the entropy of the water decreases. The refrigerator stands in a kitchen. Which statement explains why this is consistent with the second law of thermodynamics?
Mission 3

Local decreases are allowed

  • A system that exchanges energy with its surroundings is not isolated. Its entropy can decrease.
  • Freezing water: energy leaves the water, so the water's entropy falls.
  • The freezer passes that energy, plus the work done by its motor, to the kitchen.
  • The kitchen's entropy rises by at least as much as the water's falls. Total: never down.
  • Freeze 0.50 kg of water at 273 K: ΔSwater = −1.67 × 10⁵ ÷ 273 = −612 J K⁻¹.
  • Kitchen at 293 K must gain ≥ 612 J K⁻¹, so it must receive ≥ 293 × 612 = 1.79 × 10⁵ J: more than was removed. The extra (≥ 1.2 × 10⁴ J) is the motor's work.
  • Living things build ordered molecules in the same way: they pass energy and entropy to their surroundings.
Mission check

?A gas in a cylinder is in good thermal contact with a large water bath. The gas is compressed very slowly, so its temperature stays constant. Which statement about the entropy changes is correct?

Only an isolated system's entropy can never fall. Freezing water gives energy to its surroundings, and their entropy rises by at least as much as the water's falls.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Heat and work are energy in transit; the first law is conservation of energy.
  • Q = ΔU + W, W done by the gas; W = PΔV or the area under the P–V graph; ΔU = (3/2)nRΔT.

Mission 2

  • Isovolumetric (V fixed), isobaric (P fixed), isothermal (T fixed, ΔU = 0), adiabatic (Q = 0).
  • Adiabatic compression warms a gas; adiabatic expansion cools it. Adiabats are steeper than isotherms.
  • Monatomic adiabatic: PV5/3 = constant.
  • Engine: η = W/Qh; Carnot limit η = 1 − Tc/Th.

Mission 3

  • Entropy measures disorder: S = kB ln Ω (microstates) and ΔS = ΔQ/T (T in kelvin).
  • The entropy of an isolated system never decreases: constant if reversible, rising if irreversible. A system that exchanges energy can lose entropy if its surroundings gain at least as much.
  • Clausius: no spontaneous heat flow cold → hot. Kelvin: no cycle turns heat from one reservoir wholly into work.
