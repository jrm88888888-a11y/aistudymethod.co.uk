Cell division · cell cycle

The cell cycle & mitosis

Body cells divide by mitosis during a sequence called the cell cycle:

  • Stage 1 (interphase): the cell grows, makes more sub-cellular structures and copies its DNA so each chromosome becomes two identical copies.
  • Stage 2 (mitosis): chromosomes line up and are pulled apart into two new nuclei.
  • Stage 3 (cytokinesis): the cytoplasm and membrane split, giving two identical cells.

Result: mitosis makes two genetically identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number — used for growth, repair and asexual reproduction.

Stem cells

Stem cells & their uses

A stem cell is an undifferentiated cell that can keep dividing and can differentiate into other cell types.

  • Embryonic stem cells — from early embryos; can become almost any cell type.
  • Adult stem cells — e.g. bone marrow; form a limited range (mostly blood cells).
  • Plant meristems — in root and shoot tips; make any plant cell throughout life, so plants can be cloned.

Evaluate: stem cells could treat diabetes or paralysis, but there are ethical concerns about using embryos and a risk of viral contamination.

Transport · in & out of cells

Diffusion, osmosis & active transport

  • Diffusion — net movement of particles from high to low concentration. No energy needed.
  • Osmosis — movement of water across a partially permeable membrane, dilute → concentrated (high → low water potential). No energy needed.
  • Active transport — moves substances against the gradient, so it needs energy from respiration.

Rate of transport increases with a steeper concentration gradient, higher temperature and a larger surface area. A single-celled organism can rely on diffusion across its surface alone. As organisms get bigger their surface area : volume ratio falls, so they need specialised exchange surfaces and a transport system to reach every cell.

Osmosis practical: potato cylinders in different sugar concentrations — measure the % change in mass. Gains mass in dilute solutions, loses mass in concentrated ones.

Circulatory system · double circulation

The heart & circulatory system

Humans have a double circulatory system: the heart pumps blood twice per circuit — once to the lungs and once to the rest of the body.

  • Arteries — carry blood away from the heart; thick, muscular, elastic walls; high pressure.
  • Veins — carry blood back to the heart; thinner walls, wide lumen and valves to stop backflow.
  • Capillaries — one-cell-thick walls for fast exchange of substances with cells.

Blood: red blood cells (carry oxygen as oxyhaemoglobin), white blood cells (defence), platelets (clotting) and plasma (carries CO₂, glucose, urea).

Plant transport · xylem & phloem

Transport in plants

  • Xylem — dead, hollow tubes that carry water and mineral ions from roots to leaves in the transpiration stream (one direction, up).
  • Phloem — living tubes that carry dissolved sugars (made in the leaves) around the plant — this is translocation (both directions).
  • Root hair cells have a large surface area to absorb water (osmosis) and mineral ions (active transport).

Transpiration — loss of water vapour from leaves through stomata. It is faster in higher temperature, more air movement, lower humidity and brighter light.

Xylem = water up; phloem = food both ways. Guard cells open and close the stomata to balance water loss and gas exchange.

Recap

The big ideas to know

Cell cycle: copy DNA → mitosis → 2 identical cells (growth & repair)

Stem cells: embryonic · adult · plant meristem; can differentiate

Transport: diffusion · osmosis (water) · active transport (needs energy); % mass change

Circulation: double system; arteries (away, high P) · veins (back, valves) · capillaries (exchange)
