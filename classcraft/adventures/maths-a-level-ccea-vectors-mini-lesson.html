Vectors · two dimensions

What a vector is

  • A vector has size (magnitude) and direction; a scalar has size only. Velocity is a vector; speed is a scalar.
  • Write a vector as a column vector (32​)( 3 ; 2 ) or with unit vectors 3i+2j3i + 2j, where ii is one unit in the xx-direction and jj one unit in the yy-direction.
  • Print uses bold aa; by hand, underline it: a​a.
  • The movement from AA to BB is ABAB→. In the diagram, AB=3i+2jAB→ = 3i + 2j.
Vectors · two dimensions

Magnitude and direction

  • The magnitude of a=xi+yja = xi + yj is its length, by Pythagoras:
∣a∣=x2+y2​|a| = √(x² + y²)
  • Its direction comes from tanθ=xy​tan θ = y/x. Sketch the vector first so you put the angle in the right quadrant.
  • Give the direction as an angle with ii (or a bearing in context), saying which way it is measured.
  • Example: 3i+4j3i + 4j has magnitude 5 and makes 53.1∘53.1° with ii.

Vectors · two dimensions

Adding vectors and position vectors

  • Add or subtract component by component; a scalar multiplies every component. Geometrically, vectors add tip to tail (triangle law).
  • aa and bb are parallel if b=kab = ka for some scalar kk.
  • The position vector of AA is OA=aOA→ = a, from the origin OO.
AB=b−aAB→ = b − a
  • The distance ABAB is the magnitude ∣b−a∣|b − a|.
Worked example Vectors · two dimensions

Distance and the fourth corner of a parallelogram

Question
AA, BB and CC have position vectors 2i−j2i − j, 7i+11j7i + 11j and 10i+4j10i + 4j. Find the distance ABAB, and the position vector of DD such that ABCDABCD is a parallelogram.
  1. 1
    AB=b−a=5i+12jAB→ = b − a = 5i + 12j, so AB=52+122​=13AB = √(5² + 12²) = 13
  2. 2
    In parallelogram ABCDABCD, AD=BCAD→ = BC→
  3. 3
    BC=c−b=3i−7jBC→ = c − b = 3i − 7j
  4. 4
    d=a+BC=(2i−j)+(3i−7j)d = a + BC→ = (2i − j) + (3i − 7j)
Answer
AB=13AB = 13 and d=5i−8jd = 5i − 8j

the letters go round in order. ADAD→ equals BCBC→, not CBCB→.

Vectors · two dimensions

Unit vectors and vectors in mechanics

  • Forces, velocities and displacements are vectors. The resultant of several forces is their vector sum.
  • Two velocities combine the same way: a plane's velocity through the air plus the wind's velocity gives its velocity over the ground.
  • In a bearing problem, take ii as east and jj as north.

Mission check

?A plane heads north at 200 km/h in a 50 km/h wind blowing towards the east. How does it actually move?

Add the velocities as vectors: 50i+200j50i + 200j km/h, with ii east and jj north. Its magnitude is 502+2002​≈206√(50² + 200²) ≈ 206 km/h and it points 14.0∘14.0° east of north, so the plane travels at about 206 km/h on a bearing of 014∘014°.

Recap

Big ideas

  • A 2-D vector is xi+yjxi + yj or (xy​)( x ; y ); its magnitude is x2+y2​√(x² + y²) and its direction comes from tanθ=xy​tan θ = y/x with a sketch.
  • Add vectors component by component (tip to tail); AB=b−aAB→ = b − a and the distance AB=∣b−a∣AB = |b − a|.
  • Forces and velocities are vectors: their resultant is the vector sum.
