Mission 1

Mission 1: You walk away from a small light source. How quickly does the light reaching you fade?

Make a guess: You walk away from a small light source. How quickly does the light reaching you fade?

Mission 1

Intensity

  • Intensity: power arriving per unit area, on an area perpendicular to the wave's direction.
  • I = P/A. Unit: W m⁻².
  • Example: sound of intensity 5.0 × 10⁻³ W m⁻² arriving face-on at a 0.50 m² window delivers 2.5 × 10⁻³ W.
Quick check
?Sunlight of intensity 1.0 × 10³ W m⁻² falls perpendicularly on a flat solar cell of area 150 cm². At what rate does the sunlight deliver energy to the cell?
Mission 1

Spreading out

  • A small (point) source sends power P equally in all directions.
  • At distance r, that power is spread over a sphere of area 4πr².
  • I = P/(4πr²): double r → area × 4 → intensity ÷ 4. Triple r → ÷ 9.
  • This is the inverse-square law: I ∝ 1/r².
  • Assumes: point source, no absorption, no reflections.
Mission 1

Worked example

  • A small lamp radiates 3.0 W of light evenly in all directions.
  • At 2.0 m: I = 3.0 ÷ (4π × 2.0²) = 6.0 × 10⁻² W m⁻².
  • At 4.0 m: I = 3.0 ÷ (4π × 4.0²) = 1.5 × 10⁻² W m⁻²: a quarter.
  • At 6.0 m: 6.6 × 10⁻³ W m⁻²: a ninth of the value at 2.0 m.
Mission check

?A small source sends out sound equally in all directions. At 2.0 m from the source the intensity is 6.0 × 10⁻³ W m⁻². Ignoring reflections and absorption, what is the intensity 3.0 m from the source?

The same power spreads over a sphere of area 4πr². Intensity falls as 1/r²: double the distance → a quarter; 1.5 times the distance → 1/2.25.

Mission 2

Mission 2: Why does light bend as it enters water, but not when it hits the surface head-on?

Make a guess: Why does light bend as it enters water, but not when it hits the surface head-on?

Quick check
?Circular ripples spread out across a ripple tank from a vibrating dipper. A student sketches a wavefront–ray diagram of the waves. Which statement correctly describes the rays on the diagram?
Mission 2

Wavefronts and rays

  • Wavefront: a line (or surface) joining points that are in phase, e.g. every crest.
  • Neighbouring wavefronts are one wavelength apart.
  • Ray: a line perpendicular to the wavefronts, showing the direction the wave's energy travels.
  • Point source: circular wavefronts, rays spreading out.
  • Distant source or laser: straight (plane) wavefronts, parallel rays.
Mission 2

Reflection

  • The normal: a line at 90° to the surface where the ray hits it.
  • Measure every angle from the normal, not from the surface.
  • Law of reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection.
  • Incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.
  • Reflection changes direction only: speed, frequency and wavelength stay the same.
Mission 2

Why refraction happens

  • At a boundary, part of a wave is reflected and part is transmitted.
  • The transmitted wave changes speed. Its frequency stays the same (set by the source), so its wavelength changes too: wavelength = speed ÷ frequency.
  • Hitting at an angle, one end of each wavefront reaches the boundary first and slows first. The wavefront turns, so the ray changes direction: refraction.
  • Into a slower medium: wavefronts closer together, ray bends towards the normal.
  • Into a faster medium: ray bends away from the normal.
  • Along the normal: the whole wavefront slows at once. Speed and wavelength change; the direction does not.
  • Water waves travel more slowly in shallower water. In a ripple tank, a shallow region refracts waves as glass refracts light.
Mission 2

Refractive index

  • n = c/v: speed of light in a vacuum ÷ speed in the material.
  • No unit. Vacuum: n = 1 exactly. Air: n ≈ 1.00 (1.0003).
  • Water 1.33 · typical glass about 1.5 · diamond 2.42.
  • Larger n, slower light.
Quick check
?The refractive index of a certain plastic is 1.5. Which statement correctly describes what this value means?
Mission 2

Snell's law

  • n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2, with both angles measured from the normal.
  • Into larger n: smaller angle (towards the normal). Into smaller n: larger angle (away).
  • θ1 = 0 gives θ2 = 0: no bending along the normal.
Mission 2

Worked example

  • Light of wavelength 589 nm in air enters water (n = 1.33) at 60.0° to the normal.
  • 1.00 × sin 60.0° = 1.33 × sin θ2 → sin θ2 = 0.651 → θ2 = 40.6°.
  • Speed in water = c/n = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.33 = 2.26 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.
  • Same frequency, so the wavelength falls by the same factor: 589 ÷ 1.33 = 443 nm.
  • Check: 40.6° < 60.0°, so the ray bent towards the normal, as it should entering a slower medium.
Quick check
?A ray of light passes from air into a transparent liquid. The incident ray makes an angle of 40.0° with the surface of the liquid and the refracted ray makes an angle of 55.0° with the surface. The speed of light in air is 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. What is the speed of light in the liquid?
Mission 2

Measuring n with a ray box

  • Rectangular glass block on paper; draw round it. Narrow ray from a ray box into one face.
  • Mark the ray in and out with crosses; join them through the block. Draw the normal.
  • Measure i (in air) and r (in glass) with a protractor for 5–6 angles from about 10° to 70°.
  • Plot sin i (y-axis) against sin r (x-axis): a straight line through the origin confirms Snell's law. Gradient = n.
  • Dim room, narrow ray, sharp pencil: they reduce the uncertainty in each angle.
Quick check
?Another student does the same experiment but plots her axes the other way round: sin r on the y-axis against sin i on the x-axis. Her points lie on a straight line through the origin with a gradient of 0.66. What is the refractive index of the glass?
Mission check

?The diagram shows straight water waves in a ripple tank crossing the boundary between region X and region Y. The wavefronts, a ray and the normal at the point where the ray crosses the boundary are drawn. Which statement is correct?

Refraction happens because the speed changes at the boundary. Head-on, the whole wavefront slows at once, so the direction does not change.

Mission 3

Mission 3: How can light stay trapped inside a thin glass strand, even when the strand bends?

Make a guess: How can light stay trapped inside a thin glass strand, even when the strand bends?

Mission 3

Heading into a lower n

  • Light going from glass into air (larger n to smaller n) bends away from the normal.
  • Increase the angle of incidence: the refracted ray swings towards the surface.
  • At the critical angle, the refracted ray runs along the boundary (angle of refraction 90°).
  • Beyond the critical angle no light leaves: all of it reflects back. This is total internal reflection (TIR).
  • Below the critical angle there is also a partial reflection, growing stronger as the angle nears the critical angle.
  • TIR obeys the law of reflection.
Mission 3

The critical angle

  • Put θ2 = 90° (sin 90° = 1) into Snell's law.
  • Boundary with air (n ≈ 1): sin C = 1/n.
  • Glass (1.50) to air: 41.8°. Water (1.33) to air: 48.8°.
  • TIR needs both: light heading into a medium of lower n, and angle of incidence greater than the critical angle.
Quick check
?Light can cross a flat boundary between water (n = 1.33) and glass (n = 1.50) in either direction. Which statement about total internal reflection at this boundary is correct?
Mission 3

Worked example: will it reflect?

  • A ray in water (n = 1.33) meets the water surface. Critical angle: sin⁻¹(1/1.33) = 48.8°.
  • At 50.0° to the normal: 50.0° > 48.8°, so TIR. No light leaves.
  • At 45.0°: 45.0° < 48.8°, so it refracts out. 1.33 × sin 45.0° = 1.00 × sin θ2 → sin θ2 = 0.940 → θ2 = 70.1°.
  • Always compare the angle from the normal with the critical angle.
  • An optical fibre uses this: light meets the wall at more than the critical angle at every bounce, even round gentle bends.
Quick check
?A glass block of refractive index 1.52 is under water of refractive index 1.33. A ray inside the glass meets the glass–water boundary at an angle of incidence of 55.0°. What happens to the ray?
Mission check

?An optical fibre has a glass core of refractive index 1.50, surrounded by glass of refractive index 1.40. On a straight stretch, a ray meets the core's wall at 75° to the normal. Where the fibre bends, the same ray meets the wall at 66° to the normal. What happens at each point?

In a fibre, light heads towards lower n and meets the wall at more than the critical angle, so it is totally internally reflected at every bounce, round gentle bends too.

Mission 4

Mission 4: A magnifying glass can show a scene upside down or upright. What decides which?

Make a guess: A magnifying glass can show a scene upside down or upright. What decides which?

Quick check
?This lens is thinner in the middle than at its edges. Predict before the next screen. The diagram shows a thin lens, its two focal points F, and two rays of a beam of light travelling parallel to the principal axis towards the lens from the left. Which statement describes the light after it passes through the lens?
Mission 4

Two kinds of lens

  • Converging lens (thicker in the middle): rays parallel to the principal axis meet at the principal focus F.
  • Diverging lens (thinner in the middle): parallel rays spread out as if from F on the side they came from.
  • Focal length f: lens centre to F.
  • A thin lens has a focal point on each side, the same distance from the lens.
Mission 4

Ray diagrams

  • Draw two (or three) rays from the top of the object:
  • 1. Parallel to the axis → refracted through F (converging) or as if from F (diverging).
  • 2. Through the centre of the lens → undeviated.
  • 3. Through F (or heading for F) → leaves parallel to the axis.
  • Rays actually meet: a real image. It can be caught on a screen.
  • Rays only appear to come from a point: a virtual image. No screen can show it.
Mission 4

Where the object is decides

  • Converging lens, object beyond 2F: real, inverted, smaller.
  • At 2F: real, inverted, same size (image at 2F).
  • Between F and 2F: real, inverted, larger.
  • At F: rays leave parallel: no image forms.
  • Inside F: virtual, upright, larger. A magnifying glass.
  • Diverging lens, anywhere: virtual, upright, smaller.
Quick check
?The ray diagram shows an object O near a thin converging lens with focal points F, two rays from the tip of O, and the image I that an observer on the right sees by looking through the lens. Which claim about the image I is supported by the diagram?
Mission 4

The lens equation

  • 1/u + 1/v = 1/f: u = object distance, v = image distance.
  • Real is positive: real distances +, virtual distances −. Converging f +; diverging f −.
  • Magnification m = image height/object height = v/u.
  • A negative image distance means a virtual image, on the same side as the object.
Mission 4

Worked example

  • Converging lens, f = 10.0 cm.
  • Object at u = 14.0 cm: 1/v = 1/10.0 − 1/14.0 → v = +35.0 cm. Real, inverted. m = 35.0/14.0 = 2.5.
  • Move it to u = 6.0 cm (inside F): 1/v = 1/10.0 − 1/6.0 → v = −15.0 cm. Virtual, upright. m = 15.0/6.0 = 2.5.
  • Same lens, both images 2.5 times larger, but different object distances: the image flips from inverted to upright.
Quick check
?An object is placed 24.0 cm from a thin diverging lens whose focal length has a magnitude of 12.0 cm. Using the real-is-positive convention, what is the image distance v?
Quick check
?An object 3.0 cm tall is placed 30.0 cm from a thin converging lens of focal length 20.0 cm. What is the height of the image?
Mission check

?A projector has a thin converging lens of focal length 8.0 cm. A slide is placed 9.0 cm from the lens, close to it. Which describes the image?

Object outside F → rays really meet → real, inverted image. Object inside F → rays only appear to meet → virtual, upright image.

Mission 5

Mission 5: Why do opticians describe a lens by its power rather than by its focal length?

Make a guess: Why do opticians describe a lens by its power rather than by its focal length?

Mission 5

Lens power

  • Power P = 1/f, with f in metres.
  • Unit: dioptre (D) = m⁻¹.
  • Converging lens: P positive. Diverging lens: P negative (f negative, real is positive).
  • f = 25 cm = 0.25 m → P = +4.0 D. f = −50 cm → P = −2.0 D.
  • More strongly curved lens → shorter f → larger power: it bends light more.
Mission 5

Lenses together

  • Thin lenses in contact: P = P1 + P2 + …
  • Example: +5.0 D with −2.0 D → +3.0 D, so f = 1/3.0 = 0.33 m.
  • Focal lengths do not add: you would need 1/f = 1/f1 + 1/f2. Powers just add.
Quick check
?A thin converging lens of focal length 40 cm is placed in contact with a thin diverging lens of focal length 20 cm. What is the power of the combination?
Mission 5

Power in use

  • Lens equation in powers: 1/u + 1/v = P.
  • Example: object 0.50 m from a +3.0 D lens: 1/v = 3.0 − 1/0.50 = 1.0 → v = +1.0 m, a real image.
  • Camera lenses often contain a doublet: two thin lenses glued in contact. Their powers add, so a weak diverging lens can correct a strong converging one.
  • (Lenses spaced apart do not simply add.)
Quick check
?An object is 0.20 m from a thin converging lens of power +8.0 D. How far from the lens is the image?
Mission check

?A camera lens must have a focal length of 50 mm. It is made of two thin lenses in contact. One is converging, with focal length 40 mm. What must the focal length of the other be?

Power is 1/f in dioptres. Lenses in contact simply add their powers, which is why opticians, and lens designers, use it.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Intensity I = P/A (W m⁻²), measured on an area perpendicular to the wave.
  • Point source, no absorption: I = P/(4πr²), so I ∝ 1/r².

Mission 2

  • Rays are perpendicular to wavefronts. Reflection: angle of incidence = angle of reflection, measured from the normal.
  • n = c/v. Refraction is caused by a change of speed; frequency is unchanged, wavelength changes.
  • n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2: towards the normal into larger n.

Mission 3

  • TIR: light heading into lower n at more than the critical angle is all reflected.
  • sin C = 1/n (boundary with air).

Mission 4

  • Converging lens: object outside F → real, inverted image; inside F → virtual, upright, magnified. Diverging lens → always virtual, upright, smaller.
  • 1/u + 1/v = 1/f (real is positive); m = v/u = image height/object height.

Mission 5

  • P = 1/f (f in m), unit dioptre; converging +, diverging −; thin lenses in contact: P = P1 + P2 + …
