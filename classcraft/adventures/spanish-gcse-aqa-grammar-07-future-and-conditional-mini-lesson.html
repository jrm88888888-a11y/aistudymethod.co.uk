The future & conditional

Two tenses for talking about what hasn't happened yet — the future ("will") and the conditional ("would"). They share a clever trick: instead of chopping the verb, you glue endings onto the whole infinitive. Learn one, and the other is almost free.

The simple future · "will"

Add endings to the whole infinitive

Unusually, the simple (inflectional) future does not chop the verb. You keep the whole infinitive and add one set of endings — the same for -ar, -er and -ir verbs:

infinitive + -é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -ánhablar → hablaré  ·  comer → comeré  ·  vivir → viviré

Accents everywhere but "we". Every form carries an accent except nosotros (hablaremos). Compare with ir a + infinitive from cluster 4 (voy a hablar = "I'm going to speak") — also a future, but more casual/near-term.

The conditional · "would"

Same trick, new endings

The conditional means "would". It uses the very same "add-to-the-whole-infinitive" trick — just a different set of endings, again shared across -ar / -er / -ir:

infinitive + -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -íanhablar → hablaría  ·  comer → comería  ·  vivir → viviría

nosotros Higherhablaríamos
vosotros Higherhablaríais
ellos / ustedes Higherhablarían

Every form has an accented í. The conditional is your go-to for hypotheticals and politeness: Me gustaría… ("I would like…"), ¿Podrías…? ("Could you…?"). Notice yo and él/ella are identical (hablaría) — context tells you which.

Foundation vs Higher

How much AQA needs

AQA lists the future and conditional at Foundation in the singular persons (yo, tú, él/ella/usted). The plural persons (nosotros, vosotros, ellos) are Higher. We teach the full paradigm — but the badge shows what's Higher-only:

persontier
yo / tú / él / ella / ustedFoundation
nosotros HigherHigher
vosotros HigherHigher
ellos / ellas / ustedes HigherHigher

Good news: the endings and the stem never change between tiers — the plural forms are just more of the same pattern. If you know the singular, the plural is a short step up.

Irregular stems

Same stem for BOTH tenses

A handful of common verbs won't use the plain infinitive — they have a special irregular stem. The magic: it's the same stem for the future and the conditional. Learn the stem once, then just add the endings.

Foundation stems (future / conditional, yo)
infinitive → stemfuture / conditional
tener → tendr-tendré / tendría
hacer → har-haré / haría
poder → podr-podré / podría
poner → pondr-pondré / pondría
haber → habr-habrá / habría

haber is special: its él-form gives habrá = "there will be" and habría = "there would be" — the future/conditional twins of hay.

Higher stems follow exactly the same rule — just more verbs to know:

Higher stems Higher
infinitive → stemfuture / conditional
salir → saldr-saldré / saldría
venir → vendr-vendré / vendría
decir → dir-diré / diría
querer → querr-querré / querría
saber → sabr-sabré / sabría

Using the future

Predictions, plans & ambitions

The future tense is your engine for what will happen — plans, predictions, ambitions and dreams. Perfect for talking about careers, the environment and life goals:

  • El año que viene estudiaré más. — Next year I will study more.
  • Trabajaré como médico. — I will work as a doctor. (ambition/career)
  • El planeta se calentará si no actuamos. — The planet will warm up if we don't act. (environment prediction)
  • Habrá más coches eléctricos. — There will be more electric cars.

Higher aside — future of probability: Spanish can use the future to guess about the present. Estará en casa can mean "he's probably at home" (not "he will be"). Keep it in your back pocket for Higher reading. Higher

Using the conditional

Wishes, hypotheticals & politeness

The conditional is for what would happen — dreams, hypotheticals, and being polite. It instantly lifts your writing above the present tense:

  • Me gustaría viajar por el mundo. — I would like to travel the world. (dream)
  • Sería genial tener mi propia empresa. — It would be great to have my own business.
  • ¿Podrías ayudarme, por favor? — Could you help me, please? (politeness)
  • Con más dinero, protegería el medio ambiente. — With more money, I would protect the environment.

Don't muddle the two: estudiaré = "I will study" (future); estudiaría = "I would study" (conditional). One letter of ending changes the whole meaning — mind the í.

Recap

Your future & conditional toolkit

Future ("will"): whole infinitive + -é, -ás, -á, -emos, -éis, -án. Accents on all but nosotros (hablaremos).

Conditional ("would"): whole infinitive + -ía, -ías, -ía, -íamos, -íais, -ían. Every form has í.

Irregular stems (same for both): tendr-, har-, podr-, pondr-, habr- + Higher saldr-, vendr-, dir-, querr-, sabr-.

Tier: singular persons Foundation; plural persons Higher (same endings/stem).

Future uses: predictions, plans, ambitions (estudiaré más).

Conditional uses: wishes, hypotheticals, politeness (me gustaría, ¿podrías?).
