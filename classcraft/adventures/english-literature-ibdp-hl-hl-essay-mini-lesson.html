HL Essay

The HL Essay is an independent, formal literary essay of 1200–1500 words on one work (or body of work) you have studied, developed from one of the three areas of exploration. It is written coursework, externally assessed, and worth 20% of the HL grade.

  • You choose the work, the area of exploration and the line of inquiry — this is the most independent piece in the course.
  • It rewards a sustained argument and close analysis in an academic register, with proper citation.
  • Only HL students write it; it is one of the two things (with more works) that distinguish HL from SL.

Work through each screen, answer the questions as you go and collect ⭐ stars. Any named work appears only as a swappable, illustrative example — the IB reading lists are chosen by your school and rotate, so this lesson teaches the skills and ideas, never a set text. Press Start when you are ready.

Parameters

The parameters

The parameters are strict — know them exactly.

  • 1200–1500 words — short enough that every paragraph must earn its place.
  • One work, or one body of work (for example, several poems by a single poet count as one “work”).
  • Developed from one area of exploration — Readers, writers and texts; Time and space; or Intertextuality.

Line of inquiry

The line of inquiry

Everything rests on a strong line of inquiry — a precise, arguable question about the work.

  • Move from a broad topic to a narrow, defensible claim a 1500-word essay can actually sustain.
  • Too broad: the theme of freedom. Descriptive: a summary of the plot. Arguable: how a work’s shifting narrative distance manufactures, then withdraws, the reader’s sympathy for a morally compromised figure.

The test: could a well-informed reader disagree with your line of inquiry? If not, it is a topic, not an argument.

Analysis

Thesis-led close analysis

The essay must be thesis-led and grounded in close analysis.

  • A controlling thesis answers your line of inquiry.
  • Each section develops one strand, built on specific textual evidence analysed for method.
  • Breadth of coverage matters less than depth: a few passages, read exactingly, beat a tour of the whole work.

📐 Academic practice

Academic practice

Academic practice

The HL essay is academic writing, and is assessed partly on academic practice.

  • Citation: reference quotations and any secondary sources accurately and consistently.
  • Works cited: include a bibliography of everything you consulted.
  • Integrity: the essay is your own; secondary critics may be used but must be acknowledged and never misquoted.

Record as you go. Log every source when you use it, not the night before submission.

Register

Register and metalanguage

Match the register to the task.

  • Formal, precise, critical register — no chattiness, no unearned assertion.
  • A metalanguage of literary analysis used accurately (verify every technical term before you deploy it).
  • Signposting that lets the reader follow a developing argument.

The rule

The no-double-dipping rule

The no-double-dipping rule applies here too.

  • The work you write on in the HL essay cannot be reused in Paper 2 or the Individual Oral.
  • So choose an HL-essay work you feel able to sustain an independent argument about — one with real interpretive depth.

Criteria

The four assessment criteria

Know the four criteria (20 marks total).

  • A — Knowledge, understanding and interpretation: a convincing, developed reading.
  • B — Analysis and evaluation: of the writer’s choices, sustained and precise.
  • C — Focus, organisation and development: a coherent, well-built argument.
  • D — Language: clear, accurate, formal, appropriately academic.

Workflow

From reading to submission

A workflow from reading to submission.

  1. Explore in the learner portfolio; notice what genuinely puzzles you in a work.
  2. Frame a line of inquiry within one area of exploration.
  3. Thesis and plan; select the passages that carry the argument.
  4. Draft, prune to 1500 words, and reference rigorously.

The mark of a top HL essay: a single, arguable idea, pursued deep into the text, in disciplined academic prose.

Recap

The big ideas to take away

The task: 1200–1500 word literary essay on ONE work, from one area of exploration (HL only, 20%)

You choose the work, the area and the line of inquiry — the most independent component

Line of inquiry: precise, arguable, sustainable in 1500 words

Depth over breadth: a few passages analysed exactingly, all serving one thesis

Academic practice: accurate citation, works cited, no misquotation

Register: formal, critical, accurate literary metalanguage

No double-dipping: the HL-essay work cannot reappear in Paper 2 or the IO
