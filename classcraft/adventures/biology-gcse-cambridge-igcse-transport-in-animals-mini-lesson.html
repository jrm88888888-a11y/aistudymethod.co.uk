Transport · the circulatory system

The circulatory system & double circulation

Mammals have a double circulation — blood passes through the heart twice for each complete trip around the body. There are two separate loops:

  • Pulmonary circulation — heart → lungs → heart. Here blood picks up oxygen and drops off carbon dioxide.
  • Systemic circulation — heart → rest of the body → heart. Here oxygen is delivered to the tissues.

Why double? After the lungs, blood pressure is low. Returning to the heart lets it be pumped out again at high pressure, so oxygen reaches body cells quickly and efficiently.

Transport · the heart

The heart

The heart is a muscular pump with four chambers: two atria (top) and two ventricles (bottom). The right side handles deoxygenated blood; the left side handles oxygenated blood — the two are kept apart by the septum.

  • Atria receive blood; ventricles pump it out.
  • Valves stop backflow so blood only flows one way.
  • The left ventricle wall is thicker than the right because it pumps to the whole body; the right only pumps to the nearby lungs.

Transport · blood vessels

Arteries, veins & capillaries

There are three types of blood vessel, each suited to its job:

  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart at high pressure. They have thick, elastic, muscular walls and a small lumen. (All carry oxygenated blood except the pulmonary artery.)
  • Veins carry blood back to the heart at low pressure. They have thin walls, a large lumen and valves to stop backflow. (All carry deoxygenated blood except the pulmonary vein.)
  • Capillaries are one cell thick, so substances (oxygen, glucose, CO₂) can diffuse quickly between blood and cells.

Exam trap: arteries carry blood away (A for Away), veins carry it back. Direction is defined by the heart, not by whether the blood is oxygenated.

Transport · the blood

The components of blood

Blood is made of a liquid called plasma carrying three kinds of cell/fragment:

  • Red blood cells — carry oxygen using the red pigment haemoglobin. They have no nucleus and a biconcave shape (more room + surface area for oxygen).
  • White blood cells — part of the immune system; some engulf pathogens (phagocytes), others make antibodies (lymphocytes).
  • Platelets — cell fragments that help blood clot at wounds.
  • Plasma — the straw-coloured liquid that transports CO₂, digested food, urea, hormones and heat.

Recap

The big ideas to know

Double circulation: pulmonary (heart↔lungs) + systemic (heart↔body); through the heart twice

Heart: 4 chambers; atria receive, ventricles pump; left ventricle wall thickest

Arteries: away from heart, high pressure, thick wall, small lumen

Veins: back to heart, low pressure, thin wall, valves

Capillaries: one cell thick — short diffusion distance

Blood: red cells (O₂/haemoglobin) · white cells (defence) · platelets (clotting) · plasma (transport)
