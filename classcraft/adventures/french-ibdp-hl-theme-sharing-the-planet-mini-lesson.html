Mission 1

The facts behind the debate

  • 12 December 2015: the Paris Agreement (l'Accord de Paris, COP21): keep warming well below 2 °C, aiming for 1.5 °C.
  • France's legal target: carbon neutrality by 2050 (la neutralité carbone).
  • In France, transport is the largest source of greenhouse gas emissions.
  • France's electricity in 2025: about 68% nuclear, about 95% low-carbon (nuclear + renewables).
  • Effects already felt: heatwaves (les canicules), droughts, floods, forest fires.
  • Watch out: l'environnement is spelt with a double n (-nne-); English "environment" has a single n there.
Mission 1

Vocabulary bank

  • le réchauffement climatique – global warming
  • les gaz à effet de serre – greenhouse gases
  • l'empreinte carbone (f) – carbon footprint
  • les énergies renouvelables · une éolienne – wind turbine · un panneau solaire
  • le nucléaire · une centrale – power station
  • le développement durable – sustainable development
  • la sobriété – using less, sufficiency
  • le gaspillage – waste (wasting) · les déchets – waste (rubbish) · le tri sélectif – sorting for recycling
  • la biodiversité · la transition écologique
  • Watch out: les déchets = rubbish; le gaspillage = the act of wasting.
Quick check
?Le gouvernement appelle à la sobriété énergétique. What is the government asking for?
Mission 1

What France has decided

  • 2005: the Charte de l'environnement becomes part of the Constitution (right to a healthy environment).
  • 2016: large supermarkets may not destroy unsold food; they must offer it to charities.
  • 2020: the anti-waste law (loi AGEC) bans destroying unsold non-food goods and plans the end of single-use plastic packaging by 2040.
  • 2019–2020: the Convention citoyenne pour le climat: 150 citizens drawn by lot make 149 proposals.
  • 2021: the loi Climat et résilience, e.g. no domestic flights where the train takes under 2 h 30; a phased ban on renting out the worst-insulated homes.
  • Many members of the Convention said their proposals had been watered down; the government said the law was ambitious.
  • 2021, l'Affaire du siècle: a Paris court rules that the State has failed to meet its own climate targets.
Mission 1

Beyond France: global cooperation

  • The Great Green Wall (la Grande Muraille verte): an African-led project, launched in 2007, to restore land across the Sahel, from Senegal to Djibouti.
  • Québec: almost all its electricity comes from hydropower.
  • Vanuatu (a Pacific state where French is an official language) led the request for an opinion from the International Court of Justice.
  • July 2025: the Court says states have legal obligations to protect the climate.
  • The tension: global cooperation vs national agendas (jobs, energy prices, growth).
  • la coopération internationale · un pays en développement · les engagements – commitments
Quick check
?Complete: Pour limiter le réchauffement, il faut que chacun ___ des efforts.
Mission 1

Individual choices and their limits

  • Housing: insulating, renovating, heating less (la rénovation énergétique).
  • Food: less meat, local and seasonal produce, less food waste.
  • Travel: train instead of plane, cycling, car-sharing (le covoiturage).
  • Consumption: second-hand, repairing (France shows a repairability score on many electronic goods since 2021).
  • The limits: in 2018 a planned rise in fuel tax helped spark the gilets jaunes protests.
  • A slogan set la fin du monde (the end of the world) against la fin du mois (making it to the end of the month).
  • Some politicians reject what they call l'écologie punitive (environmental policy that penalises ordinary people).
Mission 1

Argue both sides

  • Individuals or governments and industry?
  • Chaque geste compte : si tout le monde triait ses déchets, l'impact serait énorme. – Every action counts: if everyone sorted their waste, the impact would be huge.
  • Les gestes individuels ne suffisent pas ; ce sont les États et les grandes entreprises qui doivent agir. – Individual actions are not enough; states and big firms must act.
  • Nuclear power?
  • Supporters say: Le nucléaire produit une électricité presque sans CO₂. – Nuclear produces almost CO₂-free electricity.
  • Opponents say: Les déchets nucléaires restent dangereux pendant des milliers d'années. – Nuclear waste stays dangerous for thousands of years.
  • Linkers: Certes …, mais … · Il convient de nuancer – This needs qualifying · En revanche … – On the other hand
Mission 1

Model sentences

  • Il est indispensable que les gouvernements prennent des mesures contraignantes. (il est indispensable que + subjunctive; prendre → prennent)
  • Si nous ne réduisons pas nos émissions, les canicules deviendront plus fréquentes. (si + present → future)
  • Plus on consomme, plus on produit de déchets. (plus …, plus … – the more …, the more …)
  • Your turn: "The more we fly, the more we pollute."
    Show answer

    Plus on prend l'avion, plus on pollue.

  • Watch out: prendre l'avion – to fly (not voler, which is for birds and planes themselves, or "to steal").

Mission 2: More than one person in seven in France lives below the poverty line. Who, why, and what helps?

Make a guess: More than one person in seven in France lives below the poverty line. Who, why, and what helps?

Mission 2

Measuring poverty

  • France, like the EU, uses relative poverty: living on less than 60% of the median standard of living (le niveau de vie médian).
  • 2024 threshold for a single person: €1,337 a month.
  • 15.4% of people in metropolitan France were below it: about 9.8 million.
  • This is the highest rate since the series began in 1996 (INSEE, July 2026).
  • Groups most at risk: unemployed people (about 36%), single-parent families (34%), children (about 22%). Pensioners: about 10%.
  • le seuil de pauvreté – poverty line · le taux de pauvreté – poverty rate · le niveau de vie – standard of living · les inégalités (f)
Mission 2

Causes and consequences

  • Causes often cited:
  • unemployment and insecure jobs (la précarité, les contrats courts);
  • high housing costs;
  • family breakdown (one income instead of two);
  • low qualifications.
  • Consequences:
  • poor housing (le mal-logement), homelessness (les sans-abri, les SDF);
  • health problems, giving up care;
  • children's school results; isolation.
  • Social mobility: an OECD study (2018) estimated it could take six generations for a child from a low-income family in France to reach average income.
  • l'ascenseur social – the "social lift" (social mobility) · être en panne – to have broken down
Quick check
?Le taux de pauvreté atteint son plus haut niveau depuis 1996. What does this mean?
Mission 2

What helps: the State and associations

  • The State:
  • the RSA (revenu de solidarité active, 2009), a minimum income;
  • housing benefit (les APL), the prime d'activité for low-paid workers, family benefits;
  • since 2025, RSA claimants are registered with France Travail and expected to do about 15 hours of activity a week (training, placements).
  • Associations:
  • les Restos du Cœur (founded 1985 by the comedian Coluche): free meals;
  • le Secours populaire, le Secours catholique, Emmaüs, ATD Quart Monde.
  • une association caritative – charity · un(e) bénévole – volunteer · une aide sociale – benefit · l'exclusion (f)
Mission 2

Gender equality at work

  • INSEE (2024 data): in the private sector, women earn on average about 22% less than men.
  • At equal working time: about 14% less (women more often work part-time).
  • Same job, same employer: about 4% less.
  • Laws:
  • 2000: la parité, equal numbers of men and women on many electoral lists;
  • 2011: at least 40% women on the boards of large companies;
  • 2019: an equality index that companies must publish.
  • l'écart de salaire – pay gap · la parité · le plafond de verre – glass ceiling
Quick check
?Choose the correct French for: "Women earn 22% less than men."
Mission 2

Poverty in the world

  • 2015: the UN adopts 17 Sustainable Development Goals (objectifs de développement durable); Goal 1: end extreme poverty by 2030.
  • The World Bank's extreme poverty line: $3.00 a day (updated 2025).
  • Many of the poorest countries are French-speaking, e.g. in the Sahel and Haiti.
  • French-born NGOs: Médecins Sans Frontières (1971, Nobel Peace Prize 1999), Action contre la faim.
  • Fair trade (le commerce équitable): a guaranteed minimum price for producers.
  • l'aide humanitaire · l'aide au développement · une ONG – NGO · la malnutrition
Mission 2

Argue both sides

  • Should the State or associations fight poverty?
  • C'est à l'État de garantir à chacun un minimum pour vivre dignement. – It is the State's job to guarantee everyone enough to live with dignity.
  • Les associations, plus proches du terrain, aident souvent mieux les personnes en difficulté. – Associations, closer to the ground, often help people in difficulty better.
  • Should benefits come with obligations (the RSA reform)?
  • Supporters say: Une activité régulière aide à retrouver un emploi. – Regular activity helps people find work again.
  • Opponents say: Imposer des heures d'activité risque de stigmatiser les plus pauvres. – Imposing hours risks stigmatising the poorest.
  • Linkers: Les uns soutiennent que … tandis que d'autres … · Force est de constater que … – One has to recognise that …
Mission 2

Vocabulary and model sentences

  • la pauvreté · la précarité · le chômage · un chômeur / une chômeuse · un logement social · les sans-abri · la solidarité · l'égalité des chances – equal opportunities · la mobilité sociale · la discrimination · le commerce équitable · l'aide humanitaire
  • Bien que la France soit un pays riche, près de dix millions de personnes y vivent sous le seuil de pauvreté. (bien que + subjunctive; y = in France)
  • Ce qui me frappe, c'est que les enfants sont parmi les premières victimes de la pauvreté. (cleft)
  • Il faudrait que l'école donne les mêmes chances à tous. (conditional il faudrait que + subjunctive)
  • Your turn: "It is essential that everyone has a home."
    Show answer

    Il est essentiel que chacun ait un logement.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Paris Agreement 2015; neutrality by 2050; transport largest source of emissions; 2025 electricity about 68% nuclear, about 95% low-carbon.
  • Laws: Charte 2005, food waste 2016, AGEC 2020, Climat et résilience 2021; l'Affaire du siècle 2021.
  • Debate: individuals vs states and industry; fin du monde / fin du mois; nuclear for and against.

Mission 2

  • Relative poverty: below 60% of the median (€1,337/month); 15.4%, 9.8 million in 2024, highest since 1996.
  • Measures: RSA (activity requirement since 2025), benefits, associations (Restos du Cœur, 1985).
  • Equality: pay gap 22% overall, 14% equal hours, 4% same job; parity laws 2000, 2011, 2019. World: SDGs 2015, MSF.
