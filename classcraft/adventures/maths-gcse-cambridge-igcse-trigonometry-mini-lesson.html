Mission 1

Make a guess: Will a 1.2 m umbrella fit flat in a 1 m by 0.8 m box?

Geometry · Pythagoras' theorem

The hypotenuse and the theorem

  • In a right-angled triangle, the side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse. It is always the longest side.
  • Pythagoras' theorem: if the shorter sides are aa and bb and the hypotenuse is cc, then
a2+b2=c2a² + b² = c²
  • The theorem is about the squares of the sides, not the sides themselves.
  • It works the other way too: sides 6, 8 and 10 give 36+64=10036 + 64 = 100, so that triangle is right-angled. Sides 6, 8 and 11 do not.
Worked example Geometry · Pythagoras' theorem

Finding the hypotenuse

Question
A right-angled triangle has shorter sides 8 cm and 5 cm. Find the length of the hypotenuse to 3 significant figures.
  1. 1
    Square the two shorter sides and add: 82+52=64+25=898² + 5² = 64 + 25 = 89
  2. 2
    That is c2c², so square-root: c=89​=9.433…c = √89 = 9.433…
  3. 3
    Round to 3 significant figures and check it is longer than 8 cm.
Answer
9.43 cm
⚠️ Watch out
stopping at 89 forgets the square root. A "hypotenuse" of 89 cm next to sides of 8 cm and 5 cm is impossible.

Geometry · Pythagoras' theorem

Finding a shorter side

  • Rearrange the theorem: a2=c2−b2a² = c² − b².
  • Square, subtract, square-root. Subtract because the hypotenuse is the biggest square.
  • Check: your answer must be shorter than the hypotenuse.
  • First spot the hypotenuse: it is opposite the right angle, wherever the triangle is turned.
Worked example Geometry · Pythagoras' theorem

A ladder against a wall

Question
A 5 m ladder leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is 1.4 m from the wall on level ground. How high up the wall does it reach?
  1. 1
    The ladder is the hypotenuse (opposite the right angle between wall and ground): c=5c = 5
  2. 2
    Square and subtract: h2=52−1.42=25−1.96=23.04h² = 5² − 1.4² = 25 − 1.96 = 23.04
  3. 3
    Square-root: h=23.04​=4.8h = √23.04 = 4.8
Answer
4.8 m
⚠️ Watch out
adding the squares gives 26.96​≈5.19√26.96 ≈ 5.19 m, which is longer than the ladder itself.

Geometry · Pythagoras' theorem

Pythagoras in 3-D (Extended)

  • To find the longest diagonal of a cuboid, use Pythagoras twice.
  • First the diagonal across the base: d2=a2+b2d² = a² + b². Then the base diagonal and the height make a right angle: g2=d2+c2g² = d² + c².
  • Together: g2=a2+b2+c2g² = a² + b² + c², so g=a2+b2+c2​g = √(a² + b² + c²).
  • A box 1 m by 0.8 m by 0.5 m deep: g=1+0.64+0.25​=1.89​≈1.37g = √(1 + 0.64 + 0.25) = √1.89 ≈ 1.37 m.
Calculate
?A box is a cuboid 60 cm long, 40 cm wide and 30 cm high. Find the length of the longest straight rod that fits inside it. Give your answer to 1 decimal place.
cm
Mission check

?Will a 1.2 m umbrella fit flat in a box 1 m by 0.8 m?

The diagonal splits the box into two right-angled triangles with the diagonal as hypotenuse. 12+0.82=1.641² + 0.8² = 1.64 and 1.64​≈1.28√1.64 ≈ 1.28 m, so the 1.2 m umbrella fits corner to corner with about 8 cm to spare.

Geometry · trigonometry

Naming the sides and the three ratios

  • Mark the angle θθ you know or want. The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle, the opposite faces θθ, the adjacent is next to θθ (and not the hypotenuse).
  • sinθ=hypopp​cosθ=hypadj​tanθ=adjopp​sin θ = opp/hyp cos θ = adj/hyp tan θ = opp/adj
  • Memory aid: SOH CAH TOA. Pick the ratio that uses the side you have and the side you want.
  • Each ratio is fixed for a given angle because all right-angled triangles with that angle are similar.

Finding a side

Question

  1. 1
    Label from 35°: hyp = 12, opp = xx. These are O and H, so use sine.
  2. 2
    Write the ratio: sin35∘=12x​sin 35° = x/12
  3. 3
    Multiply both sides by 12: x=12sin35∘=6.883…x = 12sin 35° = 6.883…
Answer
6.88 cm
⚠️ Watch out
check your calculator is in degrees. In radians 12sin3512sin 35 gives −5.14−5.14, which is impossible for a length.

Geometry · trigonometry

Angles of elevation and depression

  • The angle of elevation is measured up from the horizontal to the line of sight.
  • The angle of depression is measured down from the horizontal.
  • Looking up from A to B and down from B to A gives equal angles (alternate angles between parallel horizontals).
  • Measure from where your eye is: add the eye height back on at the end.
  • Extended: elevation and depression; also, the shortest distance from a point to a line is the perpendicular one.

Worked example Geometry · trigonometry

The angle between a line and a plane (Extended)

Question
A cuboid ABCDEFGH has base ABCD with AB = 8 cm, BC = 6 cm, and height CG = 5 cm. Find the angle between the diagonal AG and the base ABCD, to 1 decimal place.
  1. 1
    Drop from G to the base: it lands on C, so the angle is ∠GAC∠ GAC in triangle GAC (right angle at C).
  2. 2
    Find AC across the base with Pythagoras: AC=82+62​=10AC = √(8² + 6²) = 10
  3. 3
    In triangle GAC, opp = CG = 5 and adj = AC = 10: tanθ=105​tan θ = 5/10
  4. 4
    Inverse: θ=tan−1(0.5)=26.56…∘θ = tan⁻¹(0.5) = 26.56…°
Answer
26.6°
⚠️ Watch out
using the edge AB = 8 instead of AC gives 32.0°. The angle with a plane uses the line's projection onto it.
Mission check

?Sol stands on level ground 20 m from a tree, eyes 1.5 m above the ground, and measures a 40° angle up to the top. How tall is the tree?

Measure your distance from the base along the ground and the angle up to the top. The height above your eyes is opposite that angle and the distance is adjacent, so height =dtanθ= dtan θ; add your eye height. No climbing needed.

Geometry · non-right-angled triangles

The sine rule (Extended)

  • Label angles AA, BB, CC and the opposite sides aa, bb, cc.
  • Sine rule: each side divided by the sine of its opposite angle gives the same value.
sinAa​=sinBb​=sinCc​orasinA​=bsinB​=csinC​a/(sin A) = b/(sin B) = c/(sin C) or (sin A)/a = (sin B)/b = (sin C)/c
  • Use it when you know a side and its opposite angle; use the second form for an angle.
  • These formulas are given on the formula sheet.
  • Obtuse angles: sinx=sin(180∘−x)sin x = sin (180° − x); sin−1sin⁻¹ gives only the acute one.
  • Ambiguous case: a=7a = 7, b=9b = 9, A=40∘A = 40°, sinB=79sin40∘​sin B = (9sin 40°)/7 gives B=55.7∘B = 55.7° or 124.3∘124.3°; both fit: two triangles.
Worked example Geometry · non-right-angled triangles

A bearings problem with the sine rule (Extended)

Question
Boat B is 12 km due east of boat A. A buoy C is on a bearing of 050° from A and 300° from B. Find the distance BC, to 3 significant figures.
  1. 1
    Angle at A: from east (090°) back to 050° is 90∘−50∘=40∘90° − 50° = 40°
  2. 2
    Angle at B: from west (270°) round to 300° is 30∘30°, so C=180∘−40∘−30∘=110∘C = 180° − 40° − 30° = 110°
  3. 3
    Pair AB = 12 with its opposite angle C=110∘C = 110°; BC is opposite A=40∘A = 40°: sin40∘BC​=sin110∘12​BC/(sin 40°) = 12/(sin 110°)
  4. 4
    Rearrange: BC=sin110∘12sin40∘​=8.208…BC = (12sin 40°)/(sin 110°) = 8.208…
Answer
BC = 8.21 km
⚠️ Watch out
using sin30∘sin 30° on top pairs BC with the wrong angle and gives 6.39 km, which is AC.

Geometry · non-right-angled triangles

The cosine rule (Extended)

  • Use the cosine rule when you know two sides and the angle between them, or all three sides.
a2=b2+c2−2bccosAcosA=2bcb2+c2−a2​a² = b² + c² − 2bccos A cos A = (b² + c² − a²)/2bc
  • aa is the side opposite the angle AA; bb and cc are the sides that make angle AA.
  • If A=90∘A = 90°, cosA=0cos A = 0 and it becomes Pythagoras.
  • For an obtuse angle cosAcos A is negative, so the calculator gives the obtuse angle directly.
Worked example Geometry · non-right-angled triangles

Finding an angle from three sides (Extended)

Question
A triangle has sides 5 cm, 7 cm and 10 cm. Find its largest angle, to 1 decimal place.
  1. 1
    The largest angle is opposite the longest side, so a=10a = 10, b=5b = 5, c=7c = 7
  2. 2
    Substitute: cosA=2×5×752+72−102​=70−26​cos A = (5² + 7² − 10²)/(2 × 5 × 7) = (−26)/70
  3. 3
    Evaluate: cosA=−0.3714…cos A = −0.3714…
  4. 4
    Inverse cosine: A=111.80…∘A = 111.80…°
Answer
111.8∘111.8°
⚠️ Watch out
the negative cosine is correct, not a mistake: it tells you the angle is obtuse.

Area of any triangle (Extended)

  • Two sides and the angle between them give the area:
Area=21​absinCArea = 1/2 absin C
  • The angle must be the included angle, between sides aa and bb.
  • Example: sides 6 cm and 9 cm with 30° between them: 21​×6×9×sin30∘=13.5 cm21/2 × 6 × 9 × sin 30° = 13.5 cm².
  • If you know all three sides, find an angle with the cosine rule first.

Mission check

?A ship at A sees a lighthouse L on a bearing of 040°. It sails 10 km due north to B, where L is on a bearing of 120°. How far is the ship at B from the lighthouse?

The bearings give the angles: 40° at A and 180∘−120∘=60∘180° − 120° = 60° at B, so 80° at L. The 10 km side is opposite 80° and BL is opposite 40°, so the sine rule gives BL=sin80∘10sin40∘​≈6.53BL = (10sin 40°)/(sin 80°) ≈ 6.53 km. No right angle needed.

Recap

Big ideas

  • The hypotenuse is opposite the right angle and is the longest side: a2+b2=c2a² + b² = c².
  • Hypotenuse: square, add, square-root. Shorter side: square, subtract, square-root.
  • Extended: in a cuboid use Pythagoras twice, g=a2+b2+c2​g = √(a² + b² + c²).
  • Label hyp, opp and adj from the angle; choose the ratio with SOH CAH TOA.
  • Unknown side: write the ratio and rearrange. Unknown angle: use sin−1sin⁻¹, cos−1cos⁻¹ or tan−1tan⁻¹.
  • Know the exact values for 0°, 30°, 45°, 60°, 90°; in 3-D, find a right-angled triangle inside the solid (Higher/Extended).
  • Label each side with the same letter as its opposite angle.
  • Sine rule for a side–angle pair; cosine rule for two sides with the included angle, or three sides.
  • Area =21​absinC= 1/2 absin C with the angle between the two sides.
