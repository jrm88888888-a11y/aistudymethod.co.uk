Statistics · sampling

Populations, samples and censuses

  • The population is the whole group you want to know about. A sample is the part of it you actually collect data from.
  • A census collects data from every member of the population.
  • A census is accurate and complete, but slow and costly, and impossible when testing destroys the item.
  • A sample is quicker and cheaper, but may not represent the population.
  • A sampling frame is a list of the population's members (the sampling units), used to choose a sample.

Statistics · sampling

Simple random sampling

  • In a simple random sample of size nn, every possible sample of size nn is equally likely to be chosen.
  • Method: number every member of the sampling frame, then use a random number generator (or table) to pick numbers. Ignore repeats and numbers that are out of range.
  • It is free from selection bias, but needs a full sampling frame.
Worked example Statistics · sampling

Choosing a simple random sample

Question
A club has 450 members, numbered 001 to 450. Use the random digits 382047915204382133, read in groups of three, to choose a simple random sample of 4 members.
  1. 1
    Split the digits into threes: 382, 047, 915, 204, 382, 133
  2. 2
    Keep 382 and 047 (both in range)
  3. 3
    Reject 915: there is no member 915
  4. 4
    Keep 204; reject the second 382 (already chosen); keep 133
Answer
members 382, 047, 204 and 133
⚠️ Watch out
don't stop when you meet an invalid number. Skip it and keep reading until you have the full sample.
Statistics · sampling

Opportunity and systematic sampling

  • Opportunity (convenience) sampling uses whoever is available at the time and place you collect.
  • It is quick and cheap but likely to be biased: the people easiest to reach may differ from the rest.
  • Systematic sampling takes every kkth member of an ordered list, k=sample sizepopulation size​k = (population size)/(sample size), from a random start between 1 and kk.
  • It is simple, but can be biased if the list has a pattern.
Calculate
?A systematic sample of 35 is taken from a list of 840 employees. The random starting point is employee 7. What is the number of the 5th employee chosen?
Calculate
?A town has 1,800 households. In a simple random sample of 50 households, 12 have an electric car. Estimate how many households in the town have an electric car.
Quick check
?Two students each take a simple random sample of 20 pupils from the same school and find the mean time spent on homework. Their means are 4.2 hours and 5.1 hours. What is the best explanation?
Mission check

?A survey finds 90% of people asked love going to the gym. It was done at a gym. What went wrong?

The population was "people in general", but the sample was whoever was at a gym: an opportunity sample biased towards gym fans. The 90% says something about gym-goers, not about everyone, and asking more people at the gym would not remove the bias. A simple random sample from a list of the whole population would.

Statistics · sampling methods

Stratified sampling

  • Split the population into strata: groups that don't overlap, such as year groups or age bands.
  • Sample each stratum in proportion to its size:
number from a stratum=population sizestratum size​×sample sizenumber from a stratum = (stratum size)/(population size) × sample size
  • Round sensibly so the total is still the sample size, then take a simple random sample within each stratum.
  • It needs a sampling frame for each stratum. It gives a representative mix of the groups.
Worked example Statistics · sampling methods

Rounding a stratified sample

Question
A company has 120 full-time staff, 80 part-time staff and 50 contractors. Find how many of each to include in a stratified sample of 30.
  1. 1
    Population size: 120+80+50=250120 + 80 + 50 = 250
  2. 2
    Full-time: 250120​×30=14.4120/250 × 30 = 14.4
  3. 3
    Part-time: 25080​×30=9.680/250 × 30 = 9.6; contractors: 25050​×30=650/250 × 30 = 6
  4. 4
    Round to whole people and check the total: 14+10+6=3014 + 10 + 6 = 30
Answer
14 full-time, 10 part-time and 6 contractors
⚠️ Watch out
an equal split (10, 10, 10) ignores the stratum sizes, so the large group is under-represented.

Statistics · sampling methods

Quota and cluster sampling

  • Quota sampling: the interviewer chooses people to fill a set number (a quota) from each group, e.g. 20 men and 20 women.
  • Quota sampling needs no sampling frame and is quick, but it is not random: the interviewer's choices can bring in bias.

Mission check

?A school of 1,200 wants 60 opinions, fairly split by year group. Year 7 has 260 pupils. How many Year 7 pupils should be asked?

Use a stratified sample. The sampling fraction is 120060​=201​60/1200 = 1/20, so Years 7 to 11 (260, 240, 240, 220, 240 pupils) give 13, 12, 12, 11 and 12, which total 60. Then choose that many at random from each year.

Recap

Big ideas

  • Population: everyone you want to know about; sample: the part you ask; census: everyone.
  • Simple random sampling gives every sample of size nn the same chance; opportunity sampling is quick but usually biased; systematic sampling takes every kkth member.
  • Different samples give different results; a larger random sample is more reliable, but no size fixes bias.
  • Your board's large data set is a source of real data for practising these ideas: know what it covers and how it was collected.
  • Stratified: number from a stratum =population sizestratum size​×= (stratum size)/(population size) × sample size, then sample at random within each stratum.
  • Quota sampling fills group targets but is not random; opportunity sampling is quick but usually biased.
  • Judge a method by whether it is random, whether it represents the population, and what sampling frame it needs.
