Imperfect and past narration

German has a second past tense — the Präteritum (imperfect). It's the tense of writing and story-telling, but a handful of everyday verbs use it even in speech: war (was), hatte (had), es gab (there was), and the modals (konnte, musste). This lesson gives you all of them.

What it does

The written past — plus a few must-knows

Two past tenses, two jobs:

  • Perfekt (cluster 6) — the normal spoken past: Ich habe gespielt.
  • Präteritum — used in writing and narration, and always for a short list of high-frequency verbs, even out loud.

Learn these in the Präteritum now: sein → war, haben → hatte, es gibt → es gab, and the modals. You'll say and write these constantly.

"there was / there were"

es gab and friends

The imperfect of es gibt is es gab — and, like es gibt, it takes the accusative:

  • Es gab einen großen Markt. — There was a big market.
  • Es gab viele Touristen. — There were many tourists.
  • Das Wetter war schön und wir hatten viel Spaß. — The weather was nice and we had lots of fun.

Your holiday toolkit: es war, es gab, wir hatten — three imperfects that let you describe any past trip fluently.

Regular (weak) verbs

Weak imperfect: stem + -te-

For a regular verb, slot -te- between the stem and the ending:

machen → machte (did/made)
ich machtewir machten
du machtestihr machtet
er/sie/es machtesie/Sie machten

Same for all weak verbs: spielen → spielte, wohnen → wohnte, kaufen → kaufte. The ich and er forms match again.

Strong verbs

Strong imperfect: change the vowel

Strong verbs take no -te-. Instead the stem vowel changes, and ich and er have no ending:

infinitiveer form
gehen (to go)er ging
kommen (to come)er kam
fahren (to go/drive)er fuhr
sehen (to see)er sah
essen (to eat)er aß
geben (to give)er gab

Note the ß: essen → er aß keeps its ß. These strong forms are what make a written narrative flow.

Modals in the past

konnte, musste, wollte…

Modals almost always use the Präteritum, even in speech. They add -te and drop the umlaut:

presentimperfect (er)
kanner konnte (could)
musser musste (had to)
willer wollte (wanted)
darfer durfte (was allowed)

Umlaut gone: können → konnte, müssen → musste — no dots. The second verb still goes to the end: Ich konnte nicht kommen.

Which past tense?

Perfekt vs Präteritum

A simple rule of thumb for GCSE:

  • Speaking / short accounts → Perfekt: Ich habe Fußball gespielt.
  • Writing / narrating a story → Präteritum: Er ging in die Stadt und kaufte ein Buch.
  • Always Präteritum (even in speech): sein, haben, es gibt and the modals → war, hatte, es gab, konnte, musste.

Mix them naturally: Ich war in Berlin und habe das Museum besucht. — war (imperfect) for "was", Perfekt for the action.

Recap

Your imperfect toolkit

Must-knows: sein → war, haben → hatte, es gibt → es gab — used even in speech.

Weak verbs: stem + -te- (machte, spielte, wohnte).

Strong verbs: change the vowel, no ending on ich/er (ging, kam, fuhr, sah, aß, gab).

Modals: add -te, drop the umlaut (konnte, musste, wollte, durfte).

Which tense: speak → Perfekt; write/narrate → Präteritum; the "must-knows" always Präteritum.
