Mission 1

Mission 1: Can an object's balance point be in empty space, where there is no material at all?

Make a guess: Can an object's balance point be in empty space, where there is no material at all?

Mission 1

Centre of mass

  • Centre of mass: the single point where the whole mass can be treated as concentrated.
  • A mass-weighted average position of all the bits of the object.
  • Uniform and regular (sphere, cylinder, cube, uniform beam): at the geometric centre.
  • If the mass is spread symmetrically, it lies on every line of symmetry.
  • Uniform ring: at the centre of the hole. No material there.
Quick check
?A cylindrical rod is made of a 0.50 m aluminium section joined end to end to a 0.50 m steel section of the same diameter. Each section is uniform, and the steel section has the greater mass. How does the location of the rod's centre of mass compare with the rod's midpoint?
Mission 1

Centre of gravity

  • Centre of gravity: the point where the whole weight of the body can be taken to act.
  • In a uniform gravitational field it is at the centre of mass.
  • Uniform beam: draw its weight at the middle. This is how you take moments for an extended body.
Mission 1

Finding it by experiment

  • Hang a flat card (a lamina) freely from a pin near its edge.
  • At rest, its centre of gravity is directly below the pin: otherwise its weight would turn it.
  • Hang a plumb line from the pin; draw along it.
  • Hang from a second point; draw again.
  • The centre of gravity is where the lines cross. Check with a third point.
Quick check
?A rectangular wooden sign hangs from a single hook through a hole at the middle of its top edge. A heavy metal letter is fixed near the sign's left end. How does the sign hang at rest?
Mission check

?A high jumper clears the bar by arching her back, so that her body is draped over the bar in an upside-down U shape, with her head and arms hanging on one side and her legs on the other. Which statement about her centre of mass, at the instant her hips are directly above the bar, can be correct?

Yes. The centre of mass is an average position of the mass. For a ring, a horseshoe or an arched jumper, that average lies in empty space.

Mission 2

Mission 2: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Make a guess: How can a child on a see-saw balance an adult three times as heavy?

Mission 2

The turning effect of a force

  • Moment of a force about a point = force × perpendicular distance from the point to the force's line of action. Moment = Fx.
  • Unit: N m.
  • Same force, longer perpendicular distance: bigger turning effect.
  • A force whose line of action passes through the pivot has zero turning effect.
  • Each moment is clockwise or anticlockwise.
Quick check
?A mechanic pulls with a 20 N force on the end of a tool handle, 0.25 m from the centre of a nut. The force is in the plane of the handle's turn, but at 30° to the handle instead of at 90°. What is the turning effect of the force about the centre of the nut?
Mission 2

Balanced turning effects

  • Principle of moments: for a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point = the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.
  • Full equilibrium needs both: resultant force = 0 and resultant moment = 0.
  • See-saw: child of weight W at 3d balances an adult of weight 3W at d. W × 3d = 3W × d.
Mission 2

Worked example

  • Uniform plank, 3.0 m long, weight 120 N, rests on a pivot 1.0 m from end A.
  • Its weight acts at its centre of gravity, the midpoint: 1.5 m from A, so 0.50 m beyond the pivot.
  • Moment of the plank's weight about the pivot = 120 × 0.50 = 60 N m.
  • Weight W hung at A balances it: W × 1.0 = 60, so W = 60 N.
  • Resultant force also zero: pivot pushes up with 120 + 60 = 180 N.
  • Tip: take moments about the point where an unknown force acts. Its moment there is zero.
Quick check
?A uniform beam of length 5.0 m and weight 400 N rests horizontally on two supports, one at each end, P and Q. A person of weight 600 N stands on the beam 1.0 m from Q. What is the upward force from support P?
Mission 2

Couples

  • A couple: two equal, opposite, parallel forces whose lines of action do not coincide.
  • Resultant force = zero, but they turn the body.
  • Torque of a couple = Fd: one force × perpendicular distance d between the lines of action.
  • The same about any point.
  • Steering wheel, diameter 0.36 m, 25 N from each hand: 25 × 0.36 = 9.0 N m.
Quick check
?A tap handle is turned by two equal and opposite forces of 12 N, applied at the two ends of a handle 0.050 m long, each perpendicular to the handle. What is the turning effect of this couple?
Mission check

?A uniform board of weight W rests horizontally on two supports: support P at its left end and support Q one-quarter of the board's length from its right end. The diagram shows a student's force diagram for the board: FP and FQ are the forces exerted on the board by the supports, and W is drawn at the board's centre. Which statement about the student's diagram is correct?

The support farther from the board's midpoint pushes up with the smaller force. On the see-saw, the child sits three times as far from the pivot: W × 3d balances 3W × d.

Mission 3

Mission 3: How could you test whether a crown is pure gold without damaging it?

Make a guess: How could you test whether a crown is pure gold without damaging it?

Mission 3

Density

  • Density = mass per unit volume: ρ = m/V.
  • SI unit: kg m⁻³. Also common: g cm⁻³.
  • 1 g cm⁻³ = 1000 kg m⁻³. Water: about 1.0 g cm⁻³ = 1000 kg m⁻³.
  • Density belongs to the material, not the object. Cut a block in half: half the mass, half the volume, same ρ.
  • Rearranged: m = ρV and V = m/ρ.
Quick check
?The density of aluminium is 2.7 g cm⁻³. What is the volume, in m³, of an aluminium block of mass 0.081 kg?
Mission 3

Worked example

  • A brass cylinder: radius 1.0 cm, height 5.0 cm, mass 133 g.
  • V = πr²h = π × 1.0² × 5.0 = 15.7 cm³.
  • ρ = m/V = 133 ÷ 15.7 = 8.5 g cm⁻³.
  • In SI: 8.5 g cm⁻³ × 1000 = 8.5 × 10³ kg m⁻³.
  • Convert at the end, or work in kg and m³ throughout. Never mix g with m³.
Mission 3

Measuring density

  • Mass: top-pan balance (zeroed).
  • Regular solid: measure dimensions with vernier callipers or a micrometer; repeat at several places; calculate V.
  • Irregular solid: sink it in a displacement can or measuring cylinder; V = volume of water displaced.
  • Liquid: mass of an empty measuring cylinder, then with liquid added; read V at the bottom of the meniscus, eye level.
  • Better: plot mass against volume for several readings. Gradient = ρ.
  • Cube of side L: % uncertainty in ρ = % in m + 3 × % in L.
Quick check
?A student pours a liquid into a beaker in steps. After each step she records the volume V of liquid in the beaker and the total mass m of the beaker and liquid. The graph shows her results. What is the mass of 400 cm³ of this liquid?
Mission check

?A coin that looks like silver has a mass of 26.9 g. Lowered into a measuring cylinder of water, it raises the reading by 3.0 cm³. The table gives densities in kg m⁻³: aluminium 2.70 × 10³; copper 8.96 × 10³; silver 10.5 × 10³; lead 11.3 × 10³. Which metal is the coin most likely made of?

Same idea for the crown: find its mass, find its volume by displacement, and compare ρ = m/V with gold's 19.3 × 10³ kg m⁻³. No damage needed.

Mission 4

Mission 4: Why can you lie safely on a bed of nails, when one nail on its own would pierce your skin?

Make a guess: Why can you lie safely on a bed of nails, when one nail on its own would pierce your skin?

Mission 4

Pressure

  • Pressure p = F/A: force per unit area, with F the force perpendicular to the surface.
  • Unit: pascal, 1 Pa = 1 N m⁻².
  • A force at an angle: use only its perpendicular component, F cos θ (θ measured from the normal).
  • Same force, larger area → lower pressure. Same pressure, larger area → larger force.
Quick check
?A force of 50 N acts on a flat plate of area 25 cm². The force is at 60° to the plate's surface. What pressure does the force exert on the plate?
Quick check
?A sealed container holds a gas at a pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. Which statement about this pressure is correct?
Mission 4

Where gas pressure comes from

  • Gas particles move randomly and keep colliding with the walls.
  • Each collision changes a particle's momentum: the wall exerts a force on it, and it exerts an equal and opposite force on the wall, perpendicular to the wall.
  • An enormous number of collisions every second add up to a steady force. Pressure = total perpendicular force ÷ area.
  • Pressure exists at every point in the gas, not just at the walls, and a surface placed anywhere feels it.
Quick check
?The diagram shows three open containers of water, with the volume of water in each labelled. Containers 1 and 2 have bases of area A, and container 3 has a base of area A/2. Which correctly compares the pressures p1, p2 and p3 due to the water at the bottoms of the containers?
Mission 4

Pressure in a liquid

  • A column of liquid, height h, base area A: weight = ρ(Ah)g.
  • Pressure from the column = weight ÷ A:
  • p = hρg
  • Depends only on depth and density. Not on the shape or width of the container.
  • A liquid is (nearly) incompressible: ρ is the same at every depth, so the pressure due to the liquid is proportional to depth.
  • At depth h in an open container: total pressure = atmospheric pressure + hρg.
Mission 4

Worked example

  • A diver is 12 m below the surface of sea water. ρ = 1030 kg m⁻³; atmospheric pressure 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa.
  • Pressure from the water: hρg = 12 × 1030 × 9.81 = 1.21 × 10⁵ Pa.
  • Total pressure: 1.01 × 10⁵ + 1.21 × 10⁵ = 2.22 × 10⁵ Pa: more than twice atmospheric.
Mission check

?A fluid at pressure P pushes on a circular piston of radius r and exerts a force of magnitude F on it, perpendicular to the piston. The same fluid pressure acts on a second circular piston of radius 2r. The magnitude of the force on the second piston is how many times F?

Force = pressure × area. On a bed of nails your weight is spread over hundreds of points, so the pressure at each point is hundreds of times smaller than on one nail.

Mission 5

Mission 5: A steel ship floats, but a steel bolt sinks. What decides which?

Make a guess: A steel ship floats, but a steel bolt sinks. What decides which?

Mission 5

Archimedes' principle

  • A fluid pushes up on an object in it: upthrust.
  • Archimedes' principle: it equals the weight of the fluid displaced.
  • Upthrust = ρVg: ρ = density of the fluid; V = volume of fluid displaced (the submerged volume).
Mission 5

Why the fluid pushes up

  • Pressure rises with depth. The bottom face of a submerged block is deeper than the top face.
  • Upward force on the bottom > downward force on the top. Side forces cancel.
  • Block of height h, face area A: net upward force = ρghA = ρVg, the weight of fluid displaced.
Quick check
?The diagram shows a solid cube held at rest under water with its top face horizontal. The arrows show the forces exerted by the water on the cube's faces, and the cube's dimensions and the pressures due to the water at its top and bottom faces are labelled. What is the magnitude of the upthrust on the cube?
Quick check
?A solid steel sphere and a solid aluminium sphere have the same volume. Both hang from threads, completely under water: the steel sphere 0.20 m below the surface and the aluminium sphere 0.50 m below it. Steel is about three times as dense as aluminium. How do the upthrusts on the two spheres compare?
Mission 5

Float or sink?

  • Fully submerged object, volume V: upward force ρfluid Vg; weight ρobject Vg.
  • ρobject > ρfluid: weight wins. It sinks.
  • ρobject < ρfluid: it rises until only part is submerged, then floats.
  • Floating: upward force = weight. It displaces its own weight of fluid.
  • Fraction submerged = ρobject / ρfluid.
  • A steel ship is mostly air inside: its average density is less than water's.
Mission 5

Worked example

  • A 2.0 kg stone, volume 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³, hangs from a spring balance, fully under water (ρ = 1000 kg m⁻³).
  • Upthrust = ρVg = 1000 × 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.81 = 7.8 N. Weight = 2.0 × 9.81 = 19.6 N.
  • Balance reading = 19.6 − 7.8 = 11.8 N.
  • The stone feels lighter in water, but its weight has not changed.
Quick check
?A foam ball of volume V would float, but it is held at rest completely under water by a light string tied to the bottom of a tank. The density of the foam is a quarter of the density ρ of water. Which expression gives the tension in the string?
Mission check

?A wooden block floats in water (density 1000 kg m⁻³) with 60% of its volume below the surface. It is then placed in oil of density 800 kg m⁻³. What fraction of its volume is below the surface of the oil?

Floating or sinking is set by density: the ship's steel hull encloses so much air that its average density is below water's, so it floats once it has displaced its own weight of water. A solid bolt cannot.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Centre of mass: the mass-weighted average position; at the centre of a uniform regular object, on every line of symmetry, and possibly outside the material.
  • Centre of gravity: where the whole weight acts.
  • Find it by hanging the body from two points with a plumb line.

Mission 2

  • Moment = force × perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action. In equilibrium: Σ clockwise moments = Σ anticlockwise moments, and resultant force = 0.
  • A couple: equal, opposite, parallel forces; its torque = one force × the perpendicular distance between them.

Mission 3

  • ρ = m/V, in kg m⁻³ (1 g cm⁻³ = 1000 kg m⁻³); density belongs to the material, not the object.
  • Measure m with a balance and V from dimensions or displacement; the gradient of m against V gives ρ.

Mission 4

  • p = F/A (perpendicular force); in a liquid, p = hρg, set by depth alone.

Mission 5

  • Upthrust = weight of fluid displaced = ρVg (ρ of the fluid, V displaced).
  • An object floats if its average density is less than the fluid's; a floating object displaces its own weight.
