Mission 1

Mission 1: What can you say about God in about twenty words, and why do Sikhs begin every day with them?

Make a guess: What can you say about God in about twenty words, and why do Sikhs begin every day with them?

Mission 1

The root of the scripture

  • Mool = root; Mantar = sacred words, a formula.
  • The Mool Mantar is the first passage of the Guru Granth Sahib (page 1).
  • Sikhs believe Guru Nanak (1469–1539) first spoke it, after his experience of God in the river at Sultanpur.
  • It opens Japji Sahib, recited every morning by many Sikhs.
  • It is a summary, the "root", of Sikh belief about God.
Mission 1Read the source

The Mool Mantar

Guru Granth Sahib 1, word by word (paraphrase):

  • Ik Onkar: there is one God (One Creator, all that is).
  • Satnam: Truth is God's name.
  • Karta Purakh: the Creator, present in all.
  • Nirbhau: without fear.
  • Nirvair: without hate.
  • Akal Murat: beyond time; never dying.
  • Ajuni: beyond birth; never born.
  • Saibhang: self-existent.
  • Gurprasad: known by the Guru's grace.
  • In Macauliffe's 1909 translation, in summary: there is one God, whose name is true, the Creator, without fear or enmity, immortal, unborn, self-existent, known by the Guru's grace (Guru Granth Sahib 1, paraphrase).

Q1 Which two words say what God is not affected by?

Show answer

Nirbhau, Nirvair: fear and hate.

Q2 Which word explains how people come to know God?

Show answer

Gurprasad: by the Guru's grace.

Mission 1

Ik Onkar: God is one

  • The numeral 1 (ੴ begins with it) stresses that God is one.
  • Your spec stresses God's oneness (Ik Onkar).
  • Onkar points to God as the whole of reality, present in everything.
  • One God for all people: not a Sikh God, a Hindu God or a Muslim God.
  • No rival gods, no partner of God.
  • The Ik Onkar symbol appears on gurdwaras, books and jewellery.
  • Importance: if one God made and lives in all, all people are equal.
Mission 1

True, Creator, without fear or hate

  • Satnam: God alone is eternal truth, real for ever. Sikhs remember God's name in nam simran (meditation on the Name), often repeating Waheguru.
  • Karta Purakh: God made everything and is present in creation.
  • Nirbhau: God fears nothing, because nothing is greater than God.
  • Nirvair: God hates no one and is the enemy of none.
  • Importance: Sikhs try to live truthfully, care for creation, act without fear (the Khalsa's courage) and without hatred of anyone.

Mission 1

Beyond time, beyond birth

  • Akal Murat: God is timeless; God's being does not age or die.
  • Sikhs also call God Akal Purakh: the Timeless Being.
  • Ajuni: God is never born, so God does not take human form.
  • So Sikhs do not believe in avatars (God born on earth), unlike many Hindus.
  • Saibhang: self-existent: nothing made God; God depends on nothing.
  • Importance: worldly things pass away; only God lasts, so Sikhs keep God at the centre of life.
Mission 1

Gurprasad: known by grace

  • Gurprasad: God is known only by the Guru's grace (nadar, God's gift), not by cleverness or ritual alone.
  • God is the Sat Guru, the True Guru: the teacher behind every Guru.
  • Sikhs believe God's word came through the ten human Gurus and is now in the Guru Granth Sahib.
  • Sikhs respond with humility, prayer and nam simran, trusting God's grace.
  • Importance: for Sikhs, no one can earn God; everyone can receive grace.

Mission 1Diversity

Where does the Mool Mantar end?

  • Most Sikhs end it at Gurprasad.
  • Some include the lines that follow: Jap ("meditate"), then a verse that God was true in the beginning, true through the ages, true now, and, Nanak says, will be true for ever (paraphrase).
  • The short form "Ik Onkar Satgur Prasad" (one God, known by the True Guru's grace) opens many sections of the Guru Granth Sahib.
  • All agree on its meaning: one, true, creating, timeless God, known by grace.
Mission 1

Why the Mool Mantar is significant

  • First words of the Guru Granth Sahib, the living Guru: everything else unfolds from them.
  • Guru Nanak's own summary of God.
  • Daily: recited at the start of Japji Sahib in morning prayers; many repeat it through the day.
  • Ceremonies: Japji Sahib, which opens with it, is one of the prayers recited when amrit is prepared at the Khalsa initiation.
  • Shapes life: equality (one God); truthfulness (Satnam); courage and peace (Nirbhau, Nirvair); humility (Gurprasad).
  • Identity: it marks out Sikh belief: one God, no avatars, no images of God.
Mission 1Evaluate

"Ik Onkar is the most important idea in the Mool Mantar."

  • For: it comes first; everything else describes the one God it names.
  • For: it grounds equality, the heart of Sikh life (langar, sangat).
  • Against: without Gurprasad, no one could know the one God at all.
  • Against: Satnam (Truth) shapes daily life most directly through nam simran and truthful living.
  • Another view: the Mool Mantar is one statement; its words explain each other and cannot be ranked.
  • Judge it by: Which idea is most needed for the others to make sense? Which most changes how Sikhs live? Can parts of one statement be ranked?

Mission 2

Karta Purakh: the Creator Being

  • The Mool Mantra opens the Guru Granth Sahib. Its third phrase is Karta Purakh: the Creator Being.
  • Your spec spells it Mool Mantar.
  • Sikhs believe one God made everything that exists, seen and unseen.
  • Sikhs believe that before creation only God existed, so nothing comes from anywhere else.
  • So everything depends on God, and nothing exists apart from God.
  • Sikhs often call God Waheguru ("Wonderful Lord") in worship.
Mission 2

How did it begin?

  • Sikhism has no step-by-step creation story like Genesis.
  • Before the universe, for countless ages, there was only darkness and stillness, with God alone (GGS 1035, paraphrase).
  • God made the vast universe with a single word or command (Japji Sahib, pauri 16, GGS 3, paraphrase).
  • Everything happens within God's hukam: God's will and order.
  • There are countless worlds below and above; no one can count them (Japji Sahib, pauri 22, GGS 5, paraphrase).
  • Guru Nanak teaches that only the Creator knows when and how creation began (Japji Sahib, pauri 21, GGS 4, paraphrase).
Mission 2Read the source

"You are the true Creator"

Guru Granth Sahib 12, from the evening prayer Rehras Sahib (paraphrase):

  • You, God, are the true Creator and my Master. Whatever happens is Your doing. There is no one except You.
  • On the same page begins Sohila, the bedtime prayer: one sun makes the seconds, hours, days and seasons; in the same way, the one Creator appears in many forms (GGS 12–13, paraphrase).

Guru Granth Sahib 94 (paraphrase):

  • The section of hymns in Raag Majh opens with the Mool Mantar, naming God Karta Purakh.
  • In the hymn of Guru Ram Das on this page, God is the singer's mind, body and breath of life.

Mission 2

In the universe, and beyond it

  • Immanent / sargun: God is present in and through everything created.
  • Guru Tegh Bahadur: as fragrance lives in a flower and a reflection in a mirror, God lives within all, so search within (GGS 684, paraphrase).
  • Transcendent / nirgun: God is also beyond creation: without form, beyond time, beyond all description.
  • Sikhs hold both. God is in the world, but God is not the same thing as the world.
  • If every star vanished, God would still be God.

Mission 2

Why it matters to Sikhs

  • Respect for nature: the world is God's work and God is present in it.
  • Respect for people: God's light is in every person, so no one is worthless.
  • The world is real and good, not a trap to escape. Japji Sahib calls the earth a place to practise dharam (right living) (pauri 34, GGS 7, paraphrase).
  • So Sikhs live in the world, as working householders, rather than withdrawing from it.
  • Trust: everything is within hukam, so Sikhs try to accept what happens without fear.
  • Purpose of the universe: it exists by God's will, and it is where each soul can come to know God and unite with God.
Mission 2Diversity

Creation and science

  • Most Sikhs see no clash between God as Creator and the Big Bang or evolution: they answer different questions (how vs who and why).
  • They point out the Guru Granth Sahib already speaks of countless worlds and ages, and says only God knows when it began.
  • Some Sikhs read creation "by one word" as a sudden beginning, which they link to the Big Bang.
  • Some stress God within creation (sargun) in devotion, such as singing hymns; others stress God beyond it (nirgun) in silent meditation. Most combine both.
  • Non-religious view (e.g. humanist): the universe needs no Creator; science can explain it without God.
Mission 2Worked answer

Belief, source, development

Question: Explain two Sikh beliefs about God as Creator. Refer to Sikh scripture.

  • Belief 1: God is Karta Purakh, the Creator Being. Source: the Mool Mantra. Development: everything depends on God, so Sikhs are grateful and respect all of creation.
  • Belief 2: God is present in creation and also beyond it. Source: God lives in all as fragrance lives in a flower (GGS 684). Development: Sikhs look for God within themselves and others, while knowing God is greater than anything made.

Mission 3

A rare chance

  • Sikhs believe the soul passes through many lives: Sikh teaching speaks of 8.4 million life forms (84 lakh joon).
  • Being born human is rare and precious.
  • Only humans can choose to remember God, live well, and unite with God.
  • So human life is not a test to pass and forget. It is an opportunity that can be used or wasted.
  • (Karma, rebirth and mukti in detail: a later mission.)
Mission 3Read the source

"This is your chance"

Guru Granth Sahib 12, a hymn of Guru Arjan (Raag Asa), recited daily in Rehras Sahib (paraphrase):

  • You have been given this human body. This is your chance to meet the Lord of the Universe.
  • Other tasks are of no use to you here. Join the company of the holy and remember only the Name.

Q1 What is the human body given for, in this hymn?

Show answer

To meet God: to unite with God.

Q2 Does "other tasks are of no use" mean Sikhs should stop working?

Show answer

No: work and family matter; the point is that they do not last unless joined with remembering God.

Mission 3

Body, mind and soul

  • Body (sarir): given by God, temporary; a home for the soul. Cared for, kept free of intoxicants; never worshipped.
  • Man (mind): thoughts, desires, will. It can be ruled by selfish desire or turned towards God.
  • Guru Nanak: conquer your mind and you conquer the world (Japji Sahib, pauri 28, GGS 6, paraphrase).
  • Atma (soul): a spark of God's light (jot). It is not born and does not die; it longs to return to its source.
  • Guru Amar Das: O my mind, you are a form of divine light; recognise your origin (GGS 441, paraphrase).
  • Your spec spells it atman.

Mission 3

Virtues: becoming like God

  • To unite with God, a Sikh grows in God's qualities: the virtues.
  • Five often taught together: sat (truth), santokh (contentment), daya (compassion), nimrata (humility), pyar (love).
  • Guru Nanak: truth is above everything, but higher still is truthful living (GGS 62, paraphrase).
  • Virtues are not just feelings. They show in honest work, fair dealing and courage to defend others.
Mission 3

Why unite with God, and what changes?

  • Why: the soul is separated from its source. Union ends the separation, ends rebirth (mukti) and brings bliss.
  • How it changes life (the three pillars of Guru Nanak):
  • Nam japna: remembering God's Name, e.g. daily prayers (Nitnem).
  • Kirat karni: earning an honest living.
  • Vand chhakna: sharing with others, e.g. langar and giving.
  • Also: sewa (service), joining the sangat, and resisting selfish desires.
  • Union is not only after death: many Sikhs believe it can begin in this life.

Belief, source, development

Question: Explain why Sikhs see human life as an opportunity to unite with God.

  • Point 1: Human birth is rare among millions of life forms. Source: "this human body is your chance to meet God" (GGS 12, paraphrase). Development: so Sikhs try not to waste time on things that do not last.
  • Point 2: The atma is a spark of God's light. Development: it longs to return to God, so Sikhs build virtues such as truth and humility to become more like God.

Mission 4

Karma: what you sow

  • Karma = actions, and the results they bring.
  • Sikhs believe good actions bring good results; selfish actions bring suffering, in this life or a future one.
  • For Sikhs, karma from past lives helps explain the circumstances a person is born into.
  • Karma is not fate: what you choose now still matters.
  • Sikhs believe karma works within God's hukam (will and order), and God's grace is greater than karma.
Mission 4

Rebirth: coming and going

  • Sikhs believe that at death the atma (soul) leaves the body and is reborn in another body.
  • This cycle of birth, death and rebirth is called samsara; the Guru Granth Sahib also calls it "coming and going".
  • The next birth depends on karma; it may be in any life form, not only human.
  • The cycle is driven by haumai (self-centredness) and attachment.
  • The aim is to end the cycle by uniting with God.
  • Death is not the end for the soul. Sikhs cremate the body and hold Sohila and Ardas prayers; death is accepted as God's will, and loud mourning is discouraged.
Mission 4Read the source

Robe, field and gate

Guru Nanak, Japji Sahib, pauri 4, GGS 2 (paraphrase):

  • By karma (our actions) we receive this "robe" (a body, a birth); by God's grace (nadar) we find the gate of liberation.

GGS 78, Sri Raag (paraphrase):

  • The body is a field: whatever you sow in this life, you will harvest.

Mission 4

Mukti: freedom from, union with

  • Mukti = liberation: the aim of Sikh life.
  • Negative aspect (freedom from): release from rebirth, from haumai and from the five thieves.
  • Positive aspect (union with): the soul merges with God, like a drop returning to the ocean.
  • This union brings anand: bliss or deep joy (the theme of Anand Sahib).
  • Jivan mukti: many Sikhs believe a person can be liberated while still alive: a jivan mukt lives in the world, free of ego and full of love for God.
Mission 4

Grace: the gift of God

  • Grace (nadar, kirpa): God's freely given favour.
  • The Mool Mantar ends gur prasad: God is known "by the Guru's grace".
  • Sikhs cannot force mukti. Effort (remembering God, living well) and grace work together.
  • Grace often comes through the Guru: the Guru Granth Sahib and the sangat.
  • Ways Sikhs open themselves to grace: nam simran (remembering God's Name) and sewa (service), and becoming gurmukh (next missions).
Mission 4

What blocks the way: maya and the five thieves

  • Maya: illusion; treating money, status and possessions as if they last, so God is forgotten. (The world is real; the illusion is thinking it is all there is.)
  • Haumai: self-centredness, the root problem (a later mission).
  • The five thieves (panj chor) steal the soul's attention from God:
  • Kaam (lust) · krodh (anger) · lobh (greed) · moh (worldly attachment) · ahankar (pride).
  • They are countered by virtues: e.g. contentment against greed, humility against pride.
Quick check

Mission 4

Freedom and suffering

  • Freedom: humans can choose good or selfish actions. That is why karma is fair.
  • Limits: everything happens within God's hukam; past karma shapes where a person starts; grace is God's gift, not ours to command.
  • Causes of evil and suffering: mostly human: haumai and the five thieves lead people to harm others.
  • Some suffering is linked to past karma, or is part of God's will that humans cannot fully understand.
  • Guru Nanak: suffering can be the medicine and comfort the disease (GGS 469, paraphrase): pain can turn people back to God.
  • Questions for believers: why do innocent people suffer? Is karma fair to a child? Sikh responses: accept God's will (bhana) and relieve suffering through sewa.
Mission 4Diversity

Different understandings today

  • Rebirth: most Sikhs take rebirth literally. Some Sikh writers read "coming and going" mainly as the restless cycle of an ego-centred life now.
  • Heaven and hell: the Guru Granth Sahib mentions them; many Sikhs see them as states of mind, not places, and not the final goal.
  • When is mukti? Some stress union after death; others stress jivan mukti now.
  • Why it matters: for some, karma motivates good actions and gives meaning to suffering. Others say Sikhs should love God for God's sake, not for reward.
Mission 4Evaluate

"Mukti depends more on God's grace than on what a person does."

  • Reasons for: Japji 4 (GGS 2): grace opens the gate of liberation. The Mool Mantar ends gur prasad. Grace can outweigh past karma.
  • Reasons against: the body is a field: what you sow, you harvest (GGS 78). The Gurus demand action: nam japna, honest work, sharing and sewa.
  • Another view: effort and grace are not rivals: living well opens a person to grace, and grace makes the effort possible.
  • Judge it by: What do the named sources stress? Could a person be liberated with no effort? Does grace make good actions pointless, or more meaningful?

For Sikhs, karma shapes each birth, but the goal is mukti: freedom from the cycle and union with God, by grace.

Mission 5

Two directions

  • Manmukh: "facing the self" (literally, the mind). Led by your own desires, comfort and image.
  • Gurmukh: "facing the Guru". God-centred: led by the Guru's teaching and remembering God.
  • These are directions, not fixed labels. Anyone can turn from manmukh to gurmukh.
  • The aim of Sikh life: become gurmukh, and so move towards mukti.
Mission 5

Haumai: "I, I"

  • Haumai = ego, pride, self-centredness. The word comes from "I, I" (hau, mai).
  • It makes a person feel separate from God and better than others.
  • It is the root of the five thieves (lust, anger, greed, attachment, pride) and keeps the soul in rebirth.
  • Guru Angad: haumai is a chronic disease, but its cure lies within it too, when God's grace and the Guru's word come (GGS 466, paraphrase).
  • Haumai and suffering: selfishness leads people to cheat, exploit and harm others; much evil and suffering grows from it.
Mission 5Read the source

Honoured or restless

GGS 125, Guru Amar Das (Raag Majh) (paraphrase):

  • The gurmukh is honoured in life and in death. The pain of ego is taken away, and the mind finds peace.

GGS 226, Guru Nanak (Raag Gauri) (paraphrase):

  • Caught in ego, people are born and die again and again.
  • Religious dress, high status and reciting scripture do not unite anyone with God while the mind stays self-centred.
  • The gurmukh overcomes ego by reflecting on the Guru's word.

Mission 5

What makes a gurmukh?

  • Nam japna / nam simran: keeping God's Name in mind through the day.
  • Kirat karni: honest work. Vand chhakna: sharing.
  • Sewa: selfless service, with no wish for reward or praise.
  • Sangat: the company of others who remember God.
  • Accepting hukam: trusting God's will in good times and bad.
  • Virtues: truth, contentment, compassion, humility, love.
  • For many Sikhs, taking amrit (Khalsa initiation) and keeping the Rahit is part of the path.
Mission 5

Nam simran: remembering the Name

  • Nam simran = remembering God's Name, so that God is never forgotten.
  • Repeating Waheguru ("Wonderful Lord"), silently or aloud, sometimes with a mala (string of beads).
  • Singing hymns (kirtan) and reflecting on the Guru Granth Sahib.
  • Many Sikhs rise early (amrit vela) to meditate before the day begins.
  • The aim: replace "I, I" (haumai) with God at the centre. Ego shrinks as God fills the mind.
  • It brings anand (bliss) and leads towards mukti.

Mission 5Diversity

Why does it matter today?

  • For mukti: being gurmukh breaks the cycle of rebirth.
  • For this life: peace of mind and anand; less anxiety about status, money or image.
  • For society: haumai fuels conflict, prejudice and caste pride; gurmukhs serve and see everyone as equal.
  • Amritdhari Sikhs often see the Khalsa discipline as the surest way to be gurmukh.
  • Sahajdhari Sikhs often stress the inner attitude: humility and remembering God.
  • Many Sikhs warn that outward identity, even the Five Ks, can itself become a source of pride if the heart is not humble.
Mission 5Evaluate

"Haumai is the greatest barrier to mukti."

  • Reasons for: Guru Angad calls it a chronic disease (GGS 466). It is the root of the five thieves. GGS 226: in ego, people are born and die again. It is what separates the soul from God.
  • Reasons against: maya (illusion: treating the world as all there is) is just as strong a pull. The thieves, such as lust or greed, can each block the way. Without grace, no effort is enough: the real issue is God's gift, not one barrier.
  • Another view: the barriers are linked; maya and the thieves feed haumai, and haumai feeds them.
  • Judge it by: Which barrier do the named sources stress most? Is one barrier the root of the others? Can any barrier be overcome without grace?

Gurmukh and manmukh are about direction: the same action can face towards God or towards the self.

Mission 6

What is sewa?

  • Sewa = selfless service to others, with no wish for reward, praise or payment.
  • Sikhs believe serving people is serving God, because God's light is in everyone.
  • Sewa is a mark of a gurmukh and a cure for haumai: it puts others before self.
  • It is for everyone: Sikh or not, friend or stranger.
  • Sikh teaching gives priority to service: helping others comes before one's own comfort.
  • With nam simran, sewa is a way to mukti: serving others dissolves ego and opens a person to God's grace.
  • Sewa goes back to Guru Nanak's first langar at Kartarpur. In Guru Gobind Singh's time, Bhai Ghanaiya (also spelled Kanhaiya) gave water to wounded soldiers on both sides; questioned, he said he saw only the Guru's light in each face.
Mission 6

Tan, man, dhan

  • Sikhs serve with body, mind and wealth (tan, man, dhan).
  • Tan (body): cooking and serving langar, washing dishes, cleaning the gurdwara, looking after shoes.
  • Man (mind): teaching Punjabi or gurbani, singing kirtan, giving advice, using your skills for others.
  • Dhan (wealth): giving money, food or goods; daswandh.
  • Many Sikhs see tan sewa as the humblest, and so the most powerful against ego: e.g. cleaning others' shoes.
  • All three are needed; what matters most is the attitude.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • The Mool Mantar (Guru Granth Sahib 1) opens the scripture: Ik Onkar, Satnam, Karta Purakh, Nirbhau, Nirvair, Akal Murat, Ajuni, Saibhang, Gurprasad.
  • God is one, true, creator, without fear or hate, timeless, never born, self-existent, known by grace; so no avatars and no images of God.
  • God is the Sat Guru (True Guru) and Akal Purakh (Timeless Being).

Mission 2

  • For Sikhs, God is Karta Purakh, the Creator Being: one God made everything by hukam, with no step-by-step creation story.
  • God is sargun (in and through creation) and nirgun (beyond it); Sikhs hold both.
  • So Sikhs respect nature and every person, and live in the world rather than withdrawing from it.

Mission 3

  • Human birth is rare: only humans can choose to unite with God (GGS 12). A human is body, man (mind) and atma (soul, a spark of God's light).
  • Virtues, nam japna, honest work and sharing change daily life.

Mission 4

  • Sikhs believe karma shapes each rebirth in samsara; the body is a field where we sow and harvest (GGS 78).
  • Mukti: freedom from rebirth (negative) and union with God (positive); by God's grace (Japji 4, GGS 2).
  • Maya, haumai and the five thieves (lust, anger, greed, attachment, pride) block the way.

Mission 5

  • Manmukh faces the self; gurmukh faces the Guru and God (GGS 125, 226).
  • Haumai ("I, I") is the chronic disease at the root of the five thieves; its cure is nam simran and the Guru's word, by grace (GGS 466).
  • A gurmukh's life shows in nam simran, honest work, sharing, selfless sewa, sangat and humility.

Mission 6

  • Sewa is selfless service to all, because God's light is in everyone; it shrinks haumai.
  • Three kinds: body, mind and wealth (tan, man, dhan, including daswandh); GGS 26: sewa in this world brings honour in God's court.
  • From Guru Nanak's langar to Bhai Ghanaiya, sewa shaped Sikhism; today it ranges from the gurdwara to disaster relief.
