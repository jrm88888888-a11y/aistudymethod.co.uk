Mission 1: Short answers first, then longer writing. How do you change gear between the two?

Make a guess: Short answers first, then longer writing. How do you change gear between the two?

Mission 1

Your paper

  • One written paper per unit: 1 hour 30 minutes.
  • Full Course: two units, each paper 50%. Short Course: one unit, 100%.
  • Units 4–7: Gospel of Matthew · Gospel of Mark · Christian Ethics · Philosophy of Religion.
  • Half the marks are AO1 (knowledge and understanding), half AO2 (analyse and evaluate).
  • Section A and Section B. Section B has extended writing questions worth 10 marks, marked for QWC as well as content.
Mission 1

Knowledge or judgement?

  • AO1 words ask what and why: e.g. state, describe, explain.
  • AO2 words ask how good, how far: e.g. evaluate, discuss, "Do you agree? Give reasons…".
  • Evaluation questions usually want more than one point of view.
  • Look at the marks: they tell you how many points and how much development.
  • One misread command word can turn a strong answer into a weak one.
Mission 1

Short knowledge answers

  • Answer exactly what is asked, in the first sentence.
  • Count: "two ways" means two clear, different ways.
  • Precise terms: e.g. "omniscient", not "God knows a lot"; "parable", not "a story".
  • Gospel units: name the passage and give the key detail (who, what, where).
  • No evaluation needed. No long introductions.
Quick check
?"State two features of the Parable of the Sower." Which answer is best?
Mission 1

Explanation answers

  • Give reasons, then develop each one: what it means → why it matters to believers.
  • Support with a text reference: a Gospel passage, a Church teaching, a scholar or thinker.
  • Show diversity within Christianity where it exists: Unit 6 asks you more than once to take account of "the diversity of ethical positions within Christianity".
  • E.g. Catholic, Church of Ireland and Presbyterian views can differ; say why (Scripture, tradition, conscience).
  • Unit 7: some topics are open to any religious or non-religious view; others need two world religions.

Mission 1

Evaluation: argue, don't list

  • State the issue in your own words.
  • One view with reasons and evidence → another view with reasons and evidence.
  • Weigh: which reason is stronger, and why? Answer the other side's best point.
  • Include different Christian views and, where relevant, non-religious views.
  • Conclude: a judgement that follows from your reasons. Either side can reach full marks.
  • "I agree because I just do" is not a reason.
Mission 1

Quality of written communication

QWC is marked in the Section B 10-mark answers. CCEA asks you to:

  • write legibly, with accurate spelling, punctuation and grammar, so meaning is clear;
  • choose a form and style that suit the purpose and the subject;
  • organise ideas clearly and coherently, using specialist vocabulary.
  • In practice: short paragraphs, one idea each; signpost words ("however", "therefore"); key terms spelled right.
Mission 1Worked answer

One weighed paragraph

Question: "The design argument proves that God exists." Do you agree? (Unit 7)

  • View: William Paley (1743–1805), Natural Theology (1802), argued that as a watch's working parts show a watchmaker, the complex order of nature shows a designer: God (paraphrase).
  • Evidence: many theists point to the fine balance of conditions that allows life.
  • Other view: David Hume (1711–1776), Dialogues Concerning Natural Religion (1779), asked whether the analogy with human-made machines really holds.
  • Weigh: evolution by natural selection (Darwin, 1859) explains much apparent design without a designer; some believers reply that God could work through evolution.
  • Judgement: the argument may show design is possible or reasonable; whether it proves God is the point to decide, with your reason.

Mission 1

Two world religions, where marked

  • Unit 7 marks some topics as needing two different world religions:
  • creation stories, and views on humanity's place in the created order;
  • explanations of evil and/or suffering;
  • experiencing God (through sacred texts, religious leaders and example, worship);
  • life after death (e.g. resurrection, reincarnation, final judgement), plus non-religious views of the soul and afterlife.
  • On those topics, one religion only means part of the question is unanswered.
Quick check

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Short answers: exact, counted, precise terms. Explanations: reasons, developed, with text references and Christian diversity.
  • Evaluation: more than one view, weighed, a judgement that follows.
  • Section B 10-mark answers are marked for QWC: clear, organised, accurate, specialist terms.
