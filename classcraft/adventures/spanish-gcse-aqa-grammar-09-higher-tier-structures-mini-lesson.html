Higher-tier structures

These are the structures that lift a Higher answer above the crowd: the present subjunctive (just five verbs), the passive, acabar de ("to have just…"), seguir + gerund ("to still…"), and desde hace for "how long".

⚠️ Higher tier only. Everything in this lesson is assessed at Higher tier in AQA GCSE Spanish (8692). If you're sitting Foundation, you don't need these — but recognising them still helps your reading.

The present subjunctive Higher

A mood, not a tense

The subjunctive is a special verb form for things that are wished for, hoped, wanted, or not yet real. AQA keeps the scope tight: you need it for five high-frequency verbs, in the singular persons only (yo / tú / él-ella-usted).

verbyo / tú / él
hacer (to do/make)haga / hagas / haga
ser (to be)sea / seas / sea
ir (to go)vaya / vayas / vaya
venir (to come)venga / vengas / venga
tener (to have)tenga / tengas / tenga

Formation tip: take the yo present, drop the -o, add the "opposite vowel" endings — -ar verbs take -e, and -er/-ir verbs take -a: hablar → hable, comer → coma, vivir → viva.

The present subjunctive Higher

The four times you use it

Only four triggers are in scope. Learn to spot each one:

  • 1. Future after cuando. When "when" points to the future: Cuando sea mayor, seré médico — When I'm older, I'll be a doctor.
  • 2. After wishing / wanting / command / emotion. Quiero que vengas (I want you to come); Espero que tengas suerte (I hope you have luck).
  • 3. After que for a wish. ¡Que tengas un buen día! — Have a good day!
  • 4. Purpose after para que. Te lo digo para que lo sepas — I'm telling you so that you know.

Stay in scope. AQA does not ask for the imperfect subjunctive, the plural persons, or other verbs. Just the five verbs, singular, in these four situations.

The passive Higher

The passive with ser

In a passive sentence the thing comes first and the action is done to it. Spanish builds it with ser + a past participle, and the participle agrees (like an adjective). Add por + the doer:

  • La casa fue construida por los obreros. — The house was built by the workers.
  • El libro es leído por muchos. — The book is read by many.

Agreement: construida is feminine (matching la casa); it would be construido for a masculine noun, construidos/as for plurals.

The passive Higher

The se passive — far more common

Spanish usually avoids the ser passive and uses se + the verb in the 3rd person instead. It's the everyday way to say something is done without saying who:

se + verb (3rd person)singular thing → verb singular  ·  plural things → verb plural
  • Se habla español aquí. — Spanish is spoken here.
  • Se venden casas. — Houses are sold (note: plural → venden).
  • Aquí se come bien. — You eat well / one eats well here.

Exam tip: the se passive is what markers see most. Reach for it before the wordier ser + participle version.

acabar de Higher

acabar de + infinitive = "to have just…"

Use acabar de (in the present) + an infinitive to say something has just happened:

acabar de + infinitive= "to have just (done something)"
  • Acabo de llegar. — I've just arrived.
  • Acaban de salir. — They've just left.

Note: conjugate acabar like a normal -ar verb (acabo, acabas, acaba, acabamos, acabáis, acaban), keep de, then the infinitive never changes.

seguir + gerund Higher

seguir + gerund = "to keep / still…"

Use seguir + the gerund (the -ando/-iendo form) to say an action keeps happening / is still going:

  • Sigo estudiando. — I'm still studying.
  • Sigue lloviendo. — It's still raining.

Watch the boot: seguir is an e→i stem-changer, so the singular forms shift: sigo, sigues, sigue, then seguimos, seguís, siguen.

Related: llevar + time + gerund tells you how long something's been going: Llevo dos años estudiando español — I've been studying Spanish for two years.

desde hace Higher

desde hace — "for how long"

To say you've been doing something for a length of time (and still are), Spanish uses the present tense + desde hace + the time:

present + desde hace + time= "have been (doing) for …"
  • Estudio español desde hace tres años. — I've been studying Spanish for three years.
  • Vivo aquí desde hace seis meses. — I've been living here for six months.

Present, not past: the verb stays in the present because the action is still going on. Don't use a past tense here. (The imperfect desde hacía is out of scope for GCSE.)

A few more Higher touches Higher

Extras that earn marks

Light-touch, but worth recognising and dropping into Higher writing:

  • Negation. ya no = no longer (ya no fumo, I no longer smoke); tampoco = neither/not either; ni… ni… = neither… nor… (ni carne ni pescado).
  • Pronouns after prepositions. Use mí, ti, él… — para mí (for me), según él (according to him). Two irregulars: conmigo (with me), contigo (with you): ¿vienes conmigo?
  • The relative lo que = "what / that which": Lo que dices es verdad — What you say is true.

Use them for opinions and topics: Para mí, lo que importa es el medio ambiente — For me, what matters is the environment. Great for the future-ambitions and social-issues questions in Higher writing.

Your Higher-tier toolkit

Subjunctive (5 verbs, singular): haga, sea, vaya, venga, tenga — after cuando + future, wishing/wanting, que for a wish, and para que.

Passive: ser + participle (agrees) — fue construida; or the far commoner se + verb — se habla español.

acabar de + infinitive = "to have just…": acabo de llegar.

seguir + gerund = "to still / keep…": sigo estudiando.

desde hace (with the present) = "for (how long)": estudio español desde hace tres años.

Extras: ya no / tampoco / ni…ni…; para mí, conmigo, contigo; lo que.
