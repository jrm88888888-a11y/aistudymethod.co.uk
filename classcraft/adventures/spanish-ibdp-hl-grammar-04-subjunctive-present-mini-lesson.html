The present subjunctive

In IB Spanish B the subjunctive stops being an optional flourish and becomes core. It is a mood, not a tense: you use it in a subordinate clause after a trigger of wishing, emotion, doubt, necessity or certain conjunctions. This lesson builds the present subjunctive: its formation, the irregulars, and exactly when to reach for it. In IB Diploma Spanish B HL, this grammar powers your work across the five prescribed themes — Identities, Experiences, Human ingenuity, Social organization and Sharing the planet — and the two literary works you study, especially when you voice opinions on Social organization.

Mood, not tense

Indicative vs subjunctive

The indicative states facts. The subjunctive appears when a main clause colours the second clause with wishing, doubt or emotion:

  • Sé que está aquí. — I know he is here. (fact → indicative)
  • Quiero que esté aquí. — I want him to be here. (wish → subjunctive)
  • Dudo que esté aquí. — I doubt he is here. (doubt → subjunctive)

The tell: two clauses, two different subjects, joined by que, with a trigger of wishing / emotion / doubt / necessity in the first clause.

The irregulars · DISHES

When to use it · WEIRDO

The triggers

Reach for the subjunctive after these families of trigger (mnemonic WEIRDO), whenever the subject changes across que:

  • Wishes / influence: querer que, esperar que, preferir que, pedir que, aconsejar que, es necesario que.
  • Emotion: alegrarse de que, es una pena que, temer que, sentir que.
  • Impersonal: es importante que, es posible que, es mejor que.
  • Requests / doubt / denial: dudar que, no creer que, no pensar que, negar que.
  • Ojalá (+ subjunctive) = "I hope / if only": Ojalá llueva.

Watch the flip: creer / pensar / es verdad que assert a fact → indicative. Negate or question them and the certainty collapses → subjunctive: No creo que sea verdad.

The contrast in action

One idea, two moods

Flipping the trigger flips the mood — same verb, opposite certainty:

  • Creo que tiene razón. → indicative (I believe it, as fact).
  • No creo que tenga razón. → subjunctive (doubt).
  • Es verdad que viene. → indicative.
  • Es posible que venga. → subjunctive (possibility, not fact).

Exam gold: deploying no creo que + subjunctive and es posible que + subjunctive in your essays is a quick, reliable way to show IB-level range.

Conjunctions

Triggers that are conjunctions

Some conjunctions always take the subjunctive; cuando and aunque take it only when the action is future / hypothetical, not habitual fact:

  • Always: para que (so that), antes de que (before), sin que (without), a menos que (unless), con tal de que (provided that).
  • Future only: Cuando llegues, llámame. (future) vs Cuando llego a casa, ceno. (habitual → indicative).
  • Hypothetical: Aunque llueva, saldré. (even if it rains) vs Aunque llueve, salgo. (even though it is raining).

Recap

Your present-subjunctive toolkit

Formation: yo-stem + opposite endings (-ar→e; -er/-ir→a). Irregular yo forms carry through (tenga, haga, conozca).

DISHES irregulars: dé, vaya, sea, haya, esté, sepa.

Stem-changers: keep the change; -ir boot verbs also shift nosotros/vosotros (durmamos, pidamos).

Triggers (WEIRDO): wishing, emotion, impersonal, doubt/denial, ojalá, and conjunctions (para que, antes de que, cuando + future).

The flip: creo que + indicative → no creo que + subjunctive.
