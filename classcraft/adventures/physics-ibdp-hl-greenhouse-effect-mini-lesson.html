Mission 1

Mission 1: Sunlight hitting Earth head-on is four times stronger than the figure climate models start from. Why?

Make a guess: Sunlight hitting Earth head-on is four times stronger than the figure climate models start from. Why?

Mission 1

Power in, power out

  • Energy is conserved. A planet gains energy by absorbing radiation and loses it by emitting radiation.
  • Power absorbed = power emitted → equilibrium: steady mean temperature.
  • Absorbs more than it emits → the extra is stored as internal energy → it warms.
  • Warmer → emits more → the gap closes. A new equilibrium.
  • Climate models compare intensities, in W m⁻², averaged over the whole planet.
Mission 1

The solar constant, S

  • S: intensity of the Sun's radiation above the atmosphere, on a surface perpendicular to the rays, at Earth's mean distance from the Sun.
  • S ≈ 1.36 × 10³ W m⁻², measured by satellites.
  • Not a property of the Sun alone: intensity follows the inverse-square law, I = L/(4πd²).
  • Planet twice as far from its star: S is ¼ as large.
Quick check
?Four detectors measure the intensity of the Sun's radiation. Which reading is closest to the solar constant?
Mission 1

Why S/4

  • From the Sun, the planet looks like a disc of area πR². That is its projected area.
  • Power intercepted = S × πR².
  • That power is shared over the whole sphere: area 4πR² (day side and night side).
  • Mean incoming intensity = SπR² ÷ 4πR² = S/4.
  • Earth: 1360 ÷ 4 = 340 W m⁻².
  • The factor of 4 is pure geometry: half the surface is in night, and on the day side the rays strike at a slant everywhere except the point directly beneath the Sun.
Quick check
?A spherical satellite of radius 0.80 m orbits above the Earth's atmosphere. There, sunlight has an intensity of 1.36 × 10³ W m⁻² on a surface facing the Sun. What is the total power of the sunlight the satellite intercepts?
Mission 1

Albedo

  • Albedo, α = total scattered power ÷ total incident power.
  • A ratio: no unit, between 0 and 1.
  • Scattered = sent back without being absorbed (reflection by cloud, ice, air, ground).
  • Fraction absorbed = 1 − α.
  • Earth's mean albedo ≈ 0.30.
  • Mean absorbed intensity = (1 − α)S/4 = 0.70 × 340 = 238 W m⁻².
Mission 1

Albedo is not fixed

  • Cloud: thick cloud scatters much of the sunlight back. Cloud cover changes daily.
  • Latitude: snow and ice near the poles scatter strongly; sunlight arrives at low angles, and is scattered more.
  • Open ocean, high Sun: very little scattered (≈ 0.06).
  • Fresh snow: up to ≈ 0.8–0.9.
  • Earth's 0.30 is a long-term global mean.
Quick check
?Sunlight delivers a total power of 4.0 × 10¹⁶ W to a planet. The planet absorbs 3.0 × 10¹⁶ W of it. What is the planet's albedo?
Quick check
?A satellite measures the albedo of the same area of Arctic sea ice in spring and in late summer. In spring the ice is covered in snow and the albedo is about 0.8. In late summer it is about 0.5. Which is the best explanation?
Mission check

?A moon with no atmosphere orbits a star. Near the moon, the star's radiation has an intensity of 2.0 × 10³ W m⁻² on a surface facing the star. The moon's albedo is 0.20. What is the mean intensity absorbed, averaged over the moon's whole surface?

Same geometry as Earth: 1360 ÷ 4 = 340 W m⁻² arrives on average, and with albedo 0.30 only 238 W m⁻² is absorbed.

Mission 2

Mission 2: How warm must a planet get before it radiates away all the sunlight it absorbs?

Make a guess: How warm must a planet get before it radiates away all the sunlight it absorbs?

Mission 2

Emissivity

  • Every surface radiates because of its own temperature.
  • A black body radiates the most possible: σT⁴ per square metre (σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴).
  • Emissivity, e = power radiated per unit area ÷ σT⁴ (black body, same temperature).
  • No unit; between 0 and 1. Black body: e = 1.
  • Power radiated: P = eσAT⁴.
Quick check
?A surface at 27 °C radiates 380 W m⁻². Using σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, what is its emissivity?
Mission 2

Albedo or emissivity?

  • Albedo: about incoming sunlight. What fraction is scattered back?
  • Emissivity: about outgoing infrared. How well does the body radiate at its own temperature?
  • Different radiation, different property. Snow: albedo high (visible), emissivity ≈ 1 (infrared).
  • Earth's emission is infrared (T ≈ 288 K). Sunlight is mostly visible and near-infrared.
Mission 2

Equilibrium temperature

  • Balance per square metre of the whole planet: absorbed = emitted.
  • (1 − α)S/4 = eσT⁴
  • Solve: T = [(1 − α)S / (4eσ)]^(1/4).
  • Higher albedo → cooler. Lower emissivity → warmer (it must get hotter to radiate the same power).
Mission 2

Worked example: Earth with no greenhouse effect

  • Model: black body (e = 1), α = 0.30, S = 1.36 × 10³ W m⁻².
  • Absorbed: 0.70 × 1360 ÷ 4 = 238 W m⁻².
  • σT⁴ = 238 → T⁴ = 238 ÷ 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ = 4.20 × 10⁹ K⁴.
  • T = 255 K (−18 °C).
  • Observed mean surface temperature ≈ 288 K. The 33 K gap is the greenhouse effect.
Quick check
?In a simple model of Earth's global mean energy flows, 340 W m⁻² of solar radiation arrives at the top of the atmosphere and 100 W m⁻² of this is scattered back to space by clouds, air and the surface. The surface emits 390 W m⁻² of infrared radiation. Earth is in energy equilibrium. What intensity of infrared radiation leaves Earth at the top of the atmosphere?
Mission 2

One-number model: effective emissivity

  • Seen from space, Earth emits 238 W m⁻², but its surface (288 K) emits σ(288)⁴ = 390 W m⁻².
  • Treat Earth as one surface at 288 K with an effective emissivity: e = 238 ÷ 390 = 0.61.
  • Check: T = [238 ÷ (0.61 × 5.67 × 10⁻⁸)]^(1/4) = 288 K.
  • The atmosphere is what makes e < 1: it absorbs most of the surface's infrared.
Mission 2

Surface and atmosphere

  • Two layers: the surface and the atmosphere (mostly transparent to sunlight, absorbs most infrared).
  • The atmosphere radiates equally up (to space) and down (to the surface).
  • Surface: in = absorbed sunlight + downward atmospheric radiation; out = σTs⁴ (+ convection, evaporation).
  • Atmosphere: absorbs a fraction e of the surface's infrared (the rest escapes to space), plus convection and evaporation; out = up + down.
  • Top of atmosphere: in = absorbed sunlight; out = infrared leaving to space.
  • Each layer in equilibrium: in = out.
Mission 2

Worked example: surface temperature

  • Surface absorbs (1 − α)S/4 = 238 W m⁻² of sunlight. Treat it as a black body.
  • Atmosphere: T = 243 K, e = 0.80. Downward intensity = 0.80 × 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 243⁴ = 158 W m⁻².
  • Surface (radiation only): σTs⁴ = 238 + 158 = 396 W m⁻².
  • Ts = (396 ÷ 5.67 × 10⁻⁸)1/4 = 289 K.
  • Check the top of the atmosphere: out = 158 + 0.20 × 396 = 237 ≈ 238 W m⁻² in. Balanced.
  • Without the downward radiation: 255 K. The atmosphere's radiation adds to the surface's input.
Quick check
?In a simplified model of Earth's global mean energy flows, the surface absorbs 161 W m⁻² of solar radiation and 333 W m⁻² of infrared radiation emitted by the atmosphere. It loses 17 W m⁻² by convection and 80 W m⁻² by evaporation of water; the rest of its energy loss is by radiation. At the top of the atmosphere, 239 W m⁻² of infrared radiation leaves Earth. The surface is in equilibrium and can be treated as a black body. Using σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, estimate the mean surface temperature.
Mission check

?A planet orbits a star where the intensity of the star's radiation (its equivalent of the solar constant) is 900 W m⁻². The planet's albedo is 0.25 and its emissivity is 0.90. Using σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴, estimate the planet's equilibrium temperature.

A planet warms until eσT⁴ equals (1 − α)S/4. For Earth as a black body that is 255 K, well below freezing; the atmosphere's downward radiation lifts the surface to about 288 K.

Mission 3

Mission 3: Air is mostly nitrogen and oxygen. Why do traces of carbon dioxide and water vapour absorb Earth's infrared?

Make a guess: Air is mostly nitrogen and oxygen. Why do traces of carbon dioxide and water vapour absorb Earth's infrared?

Quick check
?Which statement about the main greenhouse gases in Earth's atmosphere is correct?
Mission 3

The main greenhouse gases

  • Water vapour, H2O: natural: evaporation. Human: small direct sources; its amount is set mainly by temperature.
  • Carbon dioxide, CO2: natural: respiration, decay, volcanoes, the oceans. Human: burning fossil fuels, cement, deforestation.
  • Methane, CH4: natural: wetlands. Human: livestock, rice paddies, landfill, leaks from gas and coal extraction.
  • Nitrous oxide, N2O: natural: soil and ocean bacteria. Human: nitrogen fertilisers, some industry.
  • Every one has natural and human sources. A molecule absorbs the same way whatever its source.
Mission 3

Molecular energy levels

  • A molecule's vibrations are quantised: only certain vibrational energy levels exist.
  • Level spacings ≈ 10⁻²⁰ J: photons of infrared.
  • A photon of frequency f carries energy E = hf (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, the Planck constant).
  • A photon is absorbed only if hf = ΔE, a level spacing.
  • Earth's surface (≈ 288 K) emits most strongly near 10 μm: infrared.
  • CO2 absorbs strongly near 15 μm: E = hc/λ = 1.33 × 10⁻²⁰ J.
  • Sunlight is mostly visible: these gases let most of it through.
Quick check
?A carbon dioxide molecule in the atmosphere absorbs an infrared photon emitted by Earth's surface. What happens?
Mission 3

Absorb, then emit in all directions

  • The excited molecule soon loses the energy:
    • by emitting an infrared photon in a random direction, or
    • by collisions, sharing the energy with the surrounding air (warming it).
  • The warmed air also emits infrared, up and down.
  • Roughly half is emitted downwards, towards the surface: the downward radiation that adds to the surface's input.
  • Less infrared escapes directly to space; the surface ends up warmer than 255 K.
Mission 3

The resonance model

  • Model the bonds as springs: each mode of vibration has a natural frequency.
  • Infrared at that frequency drives the vibration at resonance: large amplitude, strong absorption.
  • CO2 bending mode: natural frequency 2.0 × 10¹³ Hz. Matching wavelength: λ = c/f = 15 μm.
  • Same answer as the energy-level model: hf = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 2.0 × 10¹³ = 1.33 × 10⁻²⁰ J.
  • Two models, one absorption: frequency = natural frequency ⇔ photon energy = level spacing.
Quick check
?The bending vibration of a water molecule has a natural frequency of 4.8 × 10¹³ Hz. According to the resonance model, which radiation does this vibration absorb strongly?
Mission 3

Why not N2 and O2?

  • Infrared is an oscillating electric field. It can only drive a vibration that makes the molecule's charge distribution oscillate.
  • N2 and O2: two identical atoms. Stretching them leaves the charge symmetric: infrared cannot drive it.
  • CO2 (bending), H2O, CH4, N2O: some of their vibrations shift charge, so infrared drives them.
  • So 99% of dry air is almost transparent to Earth's infrared.
Mission 3

The enhanced greenhouse effect

  • Greenhouse effect: natural; it lifts Earth's mean surface from 255 K to ≈ 288 K.
  • Enhanced greenhouse effect: the augmentation of that effect by human activities that add greenhouse gases.
  • Primary cause: burning fossil fuels (CO2). Also deforestation, farming (CH4, N2O).
  • CO2 at Mauna Loa: ≈ 316 ppm (1959) → ≈ 421 ppm (2023). Ice cores: ≈ 280 ppm before industrialisation.
Quick check
?A planet is in equilibrium. The concentration of a greenhouse gas in its atmosphere then rises and is held at the new value; the albedo does not change. Compared with the original equilibrium, which describes the new equilibrium the planet eventually reaches?
Mission check

?Argon makes up about 0.9% of dry air, more than twenty times as much as carbon dioxide. Yet argon absorbs almost none of Earth's infrared. Which is the best explanation?

Absorption needs a match: photon energy = level spacing (or frequency = natural frequency). CO2 and H2O have vibrations that match Earth's infrared; N2 and O2 have none that infrared can drive.

Recap

The big ideas

Mission 1

  • Equilibrium: power absorbed = power emitted. An imbalance warms or cools the planet until they match.
  • Mean incoming intensity = S/4 (disc πR² intercepts, sphere 4πR² shares). Absorbed: (1 − α)S/4.
  • Albedo = scattered power ÷ incident power; Earth ≈ 0.30, varying with cloud, latitude and day.

Mission 2

  • Emissivity = power radiated per unit area ÷ σT⁴; P = eσAT⁴. Albedo is about incoming sunlight; emissivity about outgoing infrared.
  • Equilibrium: (1 − α)S/4 = eσT⁴. Earth as a black body: 255 K.
  • Surface balance: absorbed sunlight + downward atmospheric radiation = surface emission (+ convection and evaporation).

Mission 3

  • Main greenhouse gases: H2O, CO2, CH4, N2O; each has natural and human sources.
  • A molecule absorbs infrared when hf equals a vibrational level spacing (resonance: f equals a natural frequency); it re-emits in all directions, so some returns to the surface.
  • Enhanced greenhouse effect: human additions, chiefly CO2 from burning fossil fuels, increase absorption; the planet warms until balance is restored.
