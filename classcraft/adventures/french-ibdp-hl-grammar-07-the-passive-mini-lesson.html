The passive & how to avoid it

In the passive, the subject has the action done to it: La maison a été construite. French forms it with être + past participle — and the participle agrees. But French uses the passive less than English, so you also learn the neat ways to avoid it.

passive = être (any tense) + past participle… and the participle agrees with the subject

Formation

être + agreeing past participle

Put être into whatever tense you need, then add the past participle — which agrees with the subject like an adjective:

  • La maison a été construite. — The house was built. (fem sing → -e)
  • Les livres seront vendus. — The books will be sold. (masc pl → -s)

Agreement is the tell: because être drives the passive, the participle always matches the subject's gender and number.

The tense lives in être

One verb, every tense

The past participle stays put; you shift the tense onto être:

present
La loi est votée
perfect
La maison a été construite
future
Les livres seront vendus

Agent: the doer is introduced by par — écrit par Hugo. With verbs of state or feeling you may see de instead (respecté de tous).

IB French B key point

French avoids the passive

English reaches for the passive constantly ("French is spoken here"). French often prefers an active dodge. Three go-to strategies:

  • Use on: On parle français ici. — "French is spoken here."
  • Use a reflexive verb: Ça ne se dit pas. — "That isn't said." · Ça se fait. — "That's done."
  • Recast as active: On vend des maisons. — "Houses are sold."

Why it matters: a well-placed on or reflexive reads as more natural, idiomatic French than a string of être + participle.

Reflexive dodge

The reflexive passive

Many "it is done / it is said" ideas come out as a reflexive verb with ça or a thing as subject:

  • Ça ne se dit pas. — That isn't said.
  • Ça se fait. — That's done / that's the done thing.
  • Les journaux se vendent ici. — Newspapers are sold here.

Feel: the reflexive passive is everyday, natural French — reach for it before a full être-passive when there's no named agent.

The agent

Naming the doer with "par"

When you do want the passive and want to name the doer, introduce the agent with par:

  • Ce roman a été écrit par Hugo. — This novel was written by Hugo.
  • La ville a été détruite par l'incendie. — The town was destroyed by the fire.

Contrast: verbs expressing a feeling or lasting state can take de — Elle est aimée de tous. ("She is loved by all.")

Recap

Your passive toolkit

Formation: être (in any tense) + past participle, which agrees with the subject — La maison a été construite; Les livres seront vendus.

Agent: introduced by par (or de for states/feelings) — écrit par Hugo.

Avoid it: use on (On parle français ici), a reflexive (Ça ne se dit pas), or an active recast.
